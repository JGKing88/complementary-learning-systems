Freedom to choose a speed is worth nothing if the policy just picks the maximum. Across three settings the pattern is the same: permitted [0.5, 2.0] it picks ~1.8; permitted [0.5, 1.0] it picks 0.975; pinned at 1.0 it has no choice and performs identically to the capped-at-1.0 case. Step count tracks the cap, not the freedom.

This deflates the headline P10 comparison. The learned-speed arm reaching the goal in 7.17 steps against the frozen arm's 10.95 looked like evidence that choosing speed helps. It was permission to move 80% faster. The two arms walk paths of near-identical directness (1.08× and 1.04× of the straight line) — one simply covers it in longer strides.

Caveat: this arm is not the clean test. It also carries the curriculum and the reduced time penalty, so it answers "capped speed plus both treatments" rather than "capped speed alone". The uncontaminated version, p12_lo, is still running and reads 13.1 steps at u1050 — tracking this arm closely.
