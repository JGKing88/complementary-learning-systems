Cells per step, against the strategy ladder

A uniform random walk manages 0.36 in this arena, a billiard 0.775, a lawnmower sweep 0.955. This run reaches billiard by u350 and holds it — but only after a collapse to 0.14 at u200, deeper than anything the learned-speed arm produced.

The collapse at u200

Coverage ran 0.207 → 0.274 → 0.125 → 0.072 across four consecutive evals before recovering to 0.322. Training reward fell alongside it, so this was a real regression, not an eval artifact — and it recovered without intervention.

Heading noise, and the gauge

Directional spread falls from 27° to 13° — a far gentler collapse than the learned-speed arm's 29° → 5°. With speed pinned, the policy has one fewer way to sharpen its trajectory, and it ends markedly blunter while covering the same ground.

Freezing the speed costs nothing here. Final cells-per-step is 0.770 frozen against 0.721 learned, and both arms oscillate across roughly 0.61–0.78 for their whole plateau, so the difference is inside the noise. What matters is the direction: in the exploit task the same freeze costs a third of the step count. Coverage does not reward speed the way goal-reaching does.

The u200 collapse was called wrong twice. At u150 one declining point looked like nothing; at u200, three declining evals plus a falling training reward looked like a genuine failure. It recovered on its own by u250. Four directional calls were reversed in this project by reading too few eval points — this run produced two of them.
