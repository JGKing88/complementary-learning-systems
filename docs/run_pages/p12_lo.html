The answer to the control: choosing does not buy steps. Capped at 1.0 the policy reaches the goal in 11.79 steps against the pinned arm's 10.95 — slightly worse, not better. All four settings walk paths within 4–8% of the straight line. The learned-speed arm's headline 7.17 steps came from being allowed 1.8 cells per step, nothing else. Step count tracks the speed cap.

But choosing does buy stability, and that was not predicted. This arm's worst eval after its breakthrough is 0.844; the pinned arm collapses to 0.490. Same cap, same everything else — the only difference is that the speed is a learned quantity rather than a constant, and the run stops falling apart. A plausible reading is that an adjustable step size gives PPO a low-risk way to respond to a bad update, where a pinned policy can only change heading. That is a hypothesis this run suggests rather than tests.
