The reference values are what idealized strategies achieve in this arena. A uniform random walk manages 0.36; a billiard — straight lines with wall bounces — 0.775; a lawnmower sweep 0.955. The policy crosses random-walk by u150 and sits on billiard for the rest of the run.

How it got there: heading noise collapses

Directional spread, derived from the von Mises concentration. It falls from 29° to 5° — the policy makes its heading almost deterministic. Straight lines are the whole billiard strategy, and this is the policy building one.

And speed saturates near the cap

Commanded step magnitude, permitted range [0.5, 2.0]. It rises to ~1.8 within 300 updates and stays. Notably it does not pin at 2.0 — coverage is non-monotonic in step size, since longer steps skip cells between samples.

The plateau is a ceiling, not a stall. Thirty eval points span u50–u1500 and every one after u150 sits in 0.61–0.78. Reaching the next rung is not more of the same strategy executed better — a lawnmower requires remembering which cells you have already visited, and nothing in this policy's inputs carries that. The novelty reward that shapes coverage is computed by the environment, not observed by the agent.

Frozen speed costs nothing here. The companion run with speed pinned at 1.0 reaches the same 0.75 plateau. Whatever the learned speed of 1.77 is buying, it is not coverage — which is the opposite of the exploit case, where speed buys a third off the step count.

Read on the recorded split — the run's own six validation environments, not a fresh draw.
