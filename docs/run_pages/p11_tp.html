Cheaper-Failure Run

Dropping the per-step time penalty from 0.05 to 0.02 makes a failed episode cost −4 instead of −10. The policy finds goals twenty-four times more often in its first fifty updates — and still takes longer to settle than the curriculum did.

The opening is transformed: 0.760 at u50 where the control manages 0.031, and 0.854 at u100. But the instability survives — 0.365 at u150 and 0.792 at u450 — and the run does not hold a ceiling until u500.

The early jump is real and it is not the thing that mattered. A 24× improvement at u50 is the largest single-knob effect in this series. It also reaches 0.85 earlier than any other arm — u100 — and is then the least stable of the four: it falls to 0.365 immediately afterward, and is still dropping below 0.9 as late as u450, where the curriculum arm was done wobbling by u250. The reward asymmetry was genuinely suppressing early goal discovery, and that part of the diagnosis holds. Early goal discovery is simply not the bottleneck on convergence.

On the stability measure used here. Earlier drafts of these pages counted "collapses below 0.55", which was applied inconsistently — the control's count included its pre-breakthrough updates while the treatment arms' did not, which flattered the treatments. Replaced with two facts that need no threshold: when the run first reaches 0.85, and the minimum it touches after that.

control u150 / 0.490 · curriculum u300 / 0.979 · cheaper-failure u100 / 0.365 · both u200 / 0.646

What the step counts show. 59 steps to reach a goal at u50 is a policy stumbling onto the target within a 200-step budget, not one steering toward it. The cheaper penalty simply lets it survive long enough to stumble. That is why the success number moves so far while the settling time barely does.
