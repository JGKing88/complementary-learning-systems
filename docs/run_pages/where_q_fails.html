What was measured

no policy involved

The agent navigates by q, the tangent-plane projection of the displacement from where it stands to whatever pattern the Hopfield recalls. Phase 1 reported that at ten distractors q points about 46° wrong, and concluded the encoder was the binding constraint on exploitation.

That number came from a mean over a single evaluation world of eight environments. This measures every cell of 64 fresh worlds per seed, and splits the failure into the two things it conflated: whether the recall settled on the wrong pattern, and how far off q points given that it settled on the right one. Those have different fixes — the first needs a better encoder, the second is the projection's own noise floor.

This overturns the phase-1 headline

The recall locks onto the goal 99.07% of the time at ten distractors, and onto a distractor 0.15% of the time. When it locks correctly the direction error is 15.5°, essentially flat from one distractor to ten.

Worlds as bad as the one phase 1 drew do exist — the worst of the 192 environments here has 12% of its cells below cos 0.5 against a median of 0.78%. But it was the tail, and an eight-environment mean cannot tell you which draw you got.

Two things only the figures showed

not visible in the summary table

1. Direction error is governed by recall fidelity, not geometry

An earlier version of this page said the bad readout was a halo around the goal, and explained it as a one-cell displacement projecting to mostly noise. That explanation was backwards — a short displacement is where the tangent-plane linearization is at its best. Worked out properly, the driver is how closely the Hopfield settles onto the exact stored pattern:

cos(settled state, goal pattern)share of cells% with dir_cos < 0.5
≥ 0.9950.1%0.01%
0.90 – 0.9949.1%2.87%
0.50 – 0.900.8%21.8%

99.7% of all bad-direction cells have cos_goal below 0.99. When the recall settles exactly, the direction is essentially never wrong. The failure is imperfect recall — the settled state carrying a slight admixture of other patterns — and nothing else.

Distance enters only as a lever arm. |q| scales with distance to the goal (median 0.041 at one cell, 0.393 beyond twelve), so a fixed amount of recall error becomes a larger angular error when the true displacement is short. Hence an 11.7% failure rate within 1.5 cells of the goal against 0.44% beyond twelve.

And the halo was overstated. The near-goal rings have a much higher rate but hold few cells: only 24.8% of sub-0.5 cells lie within 2.5 cells of the goal, and the 5–8 cell ring contributes more than any other. The rate is highest near the goal; the mass is spread across the arena.

What survives unchanged is the wall result, which was measured rather than reasoned: dir_cos is flat against distance-to-wall. That is a negative result for the readout half of H-wall — failures piling up at walls are not a degraded signal there.

A prediction this hands to P3

If direction error tracks recall fidelity, the agent has an observable proxy for it. cos_goal is not available to the policy — but a pattern that has settled exactly is a fixed point of the recall map, and one sitting at cos 0.94 is not. That is precisely what ‖recall(x) − recall²(x)‖ measures, and the multistep iterates are already computed and already fed to the policy.

So it should predict dir_cos, giving the policy a per-step confidence signal on its own readout. Cheap to test, and it runs early in P3.

2. A wrong lock is not a random direction

All three lock categories hug cos 1.0 in the cumulative plot, where a randomly-directed q would trace the diagonal. Cells classified as spurious mixtures are mostly recalls sitting near the goal pattern without clearing the 0.9 threshold, so they still carry most of the direction.

So the honest statement is stronger than the summary table supported: there is no sizeable population of catastrophically misdirected q at ten distractors at all. The entire sub-0.5 tail is 1.58% of cells, spread across all three categories rather than concentrated in the wrong-lock ones.

What is still open

running now

Phase 1's 23.3% sits above the worst of all 64 environments measured here, which is more than sampling noise from n=2 should produce. That measurement replayed a trained model's recorded evaluation world; these draw fresh placements. Two jobs are re-measuring both recorded worlds with this tool. If recorded placements are systematically worse, the fresh-world distribution means something different and this page gets an update.
