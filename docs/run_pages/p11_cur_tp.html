The cross is not additive, and it is not synergistic — it is intermediate. On both stability measures it sits precisely between its parents, and on final efficiency it is worse than either. That is the signature of two knobs pushing the same underlying quantity in opposite directions: the curriculum delays the first breakthrough to buy stability, the cheaper failure rushes it at stability's expense, and running both leaves you partway along each.

What this settles about the original question. All four arms finish within 10.95–12.09 steps and 0.875–0.958 success at ten distractors. None of these knobs changes what the policy becomes. They change only how turbulently it gets there, and on that axis the curriculum alone is the clear pick — earliest to stop wobbling (u250), highest post-breakthrough floor (0.979), earliest lock (u400).

The uncrossed arm. p11_eps was meant to complete this set but is a no-op: exploit.py hard-codes epsilon=0.0, so --epsilon_explore on an exploit-only schedule is accepted, echoed and discarded.
