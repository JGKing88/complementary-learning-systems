Spurious attractors in the readout

p10_pol_v1 · 10 distractors · 192 memories · the field is computed with no policy and no rollouts

The recalled direction q is a vector field over cells. For a given memory it can be evaluated everywhere at once, then integrated with the environment’s own update rule — fixed step, snapped sampling, clipped at the arena — from all 400 cells. For most memories every cell flows to the goal. For some, the field folds into a vortex somewhere else and swallows almost the whole arena.

A sink is necessary for failure

Zero failures in 155 episodes whose field was clean. Every one of the 24 failures had a sink. A sink does not guarantee failure — the agent escaped 13 of 37 — but it is present every time the agent misses. The median share of cells flowing to the goal was 1.00 on reached episodes and 0.09 on failed ones.

The agent orbits where the field says it will

Arrows are q. The teal ring is the capture ball, the red dot is the field’s sink, the amber cross is where the agent actually settled. Nothing about the agent went into computing the red dot.

Median agreement across these is 0.61 cells. Two of them match to 0.06. Chance agreement for points placed at random in a 20×20 arena would be about 7.7 cells.

The same goal is clean under most distractor draws and trapped under others, so the attractor is a property of the particular set of stored patterns rather than of the goal or the arena. That is consistent with §13.2: interference degrades the recall margin without the goal ever losing the competition, and a contaminated blend does not need to flip the winner to bend the field into a vortex.
