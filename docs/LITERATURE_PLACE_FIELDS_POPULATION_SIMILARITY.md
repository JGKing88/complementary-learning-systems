# Hippocampal fields stretch far, and population similarity stays local except in ventral CA3

Very large place fields are real, but a single field rarely covers a large share of the space around it. The absolute record is a **32 m field in dorsal CA1 of an Egyptian fruit bat flying a 200 m tunnel**, about 17% of the 185 m flight path. In that dataset the median field was about 4.8 m, and fields of one neuron differed up to 20-fold in size. Fields covering a third or more of an environment appear in four places: ventral rat CA3 on an 18 m track, where fields averaged 5.52 m, or 31%, few exceeded 10 m, and one drawn field ran about 15.7 m, or 87%; marmoset hippocampus, with a mean of 1.42 m on a 2.44 m track, or 58%; dorsal CA1 in small rooms and short tracks, at 25 to 42%; and the subiculum, whose cells spread 95% of their spikes over 56% of a 1.5 m box against 26% for CA1, through diffuse firing rather than one compact field. When the environment grows, dorsal CA1 fields grow sublinearly and cells add fields instead, so the largest single fields in the 200 m tunnel, an 18.6 m² arena and a 48 m track cover only 17%, 19% and about 7% of the space. **On the second question the answer is a qualified yes, resting on one study.** On an 18 m track, Kjelstrup et al. correlated population vectors at three levels of rat CA3. Each vector pooled cells recorded in different rats and sessions, not a simultaneously recorded ensemble. In ventral CA3 the correlation fell to 0.5 about 3.3 m from a position and then declined smoothly, reaching about zero near 10.7 m, about 65% of the 16.5 m analysed. Intermediate CA3 reached zero near 7 m, while dorsal CA3 fell below 0.1 within about 1 m. No one has replicated the result, and the track ends may truncate ventral fields. Every other published curve of population-vector similarity against distance comes from dorsal CA1, CA3, subiculum or retrosplenial cortex in environments no longer than a 4 m loop. Where widths are reported, these curves fall to near zero within about one field width, 30 to 40 cm or 15 to 22% of a 1 to 2 m environment, and then stay flat. Other hints of broader kernels come only from altered self-motion, an abstract-only comparison of dorsal and middle CA1, and human fMRI of remembered distances. No one has published this curve for dorsal CA1 in an environment longer than a 4 m loop, or for any region in an environment longer than 18 m, although two public datasets, the 48 m track and a 3.5 × 2.5 m arena, would allow it.

Each finding carries a tag that says how it was checked. **[full text]** means the value was read in the paper or its supplement. **[abstract]** means only the abstract was read. **[secondary]** means a later paper, review, commentary, thesis summary or press item reports it. **[unverified]** means it came from a search snippet or aggregator page that could not be checked. **[preprint]** marks work that is not peer reviewed. **[derived]** marks my own arithmetic on reported numbers, mostly conversions into a fraction of the environment. **[figure-read]** means I read the value off a published figure against its axis ticks, so it is approximate. "Figure only" means the paper shows the value in a figure that could not be read numerically.

## Very large fields exist, but few cover much of their environment

The largest single fields come from dorsal CA1 of Egyptian fruit bats flying a 194 m tunnel, 185.3 m between the two landing balls. In 5 wild-born bats, 196 of 235 putative pyramidal cells were place cells, with 1,629 fields in total. **Field size ran from 0.6 to 32 m** and fit a log-normal distribution whose median is about 4.8 m and mean about 5.7 m [full text; median and mean derived] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). The fields of one neuron differed up to 20-fold in size, with a mean largest-to-smallest ratio of 4.4, and the 12.2% of neurons with a single field had fields averaging 5.9 ± 3.5 m [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). The authors bounded each field at 20% of its peak and measured size as the span from the 5th to the 95th percentile of in-field spike positions. The size distribution held across nine detection variants, although a stricter 20th-to-80th percentile rule ended the tail near 20 m instead of 32 m [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). Rats in large environments have smaller fields in absolute terms. On a novel 48 m track, dorsal CA1 fields averaged **65 ± 43 cm**, and the largest reached about 3.3 m, read from a scatter plot [full text, supplement] ([Rich et al. 2014 supplement](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf)). In an 18.6 m² arena, dorsal CA1 subfields ranged from 0.023 to 3.46 m², with 78% at 1 m² or less [full text, accepted manuscript] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)). Ventral rat CA3 is the one rodent site with fields near 10 m. On an 18 m track, ventral fields averaged 5.52 ± 0.54 m, the authors write that "few fields were longer than 10 m", and the longest field drawn in the paper, from a ventral cell, ran about 15.7 m in one running direction [full text; 15.7 m figure-read] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)).

The fraction falls in large spaces because field size grows more slowly than the environment. From their 6 m and 200 m tunnels, Eliav et al. fitted mean field size as rising with tunnel length to the power **0.36** and mean per-cell coverage as falling with length to the power -0.24 [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). In rats, a 5.8-fold larger floor made CA1 fields only 30% larger [full text] ([Fenton et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2695947/)), and the fraction of the arena covered per cell was the same in 8.2 and 18.6 m² arenas, Tukey P = 0.92 [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)). Eliav et al. also tested whether small environments hide whole-environment fields among cells that look non-spatial. They argued against it because the share of active cells that were place cells was the same in the 6 m and 200 m tunnels, **83.3% and 83.4%** [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). Detection rules still bias the record against the broadest cells. Tanni et al. rejected candidate fields larger than half the rate map [full text] ([Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)), Mankin et al. kept only cells with fields under 25% of the enclosure in at least one session [full text] ([Mankin et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4392894/)), Tingley and Buzsáki capped field width at 120 cm, which is 37.5% of their 3.2 m maze [full text, derived] ([Tingley & Buzsáki 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605060/)), and spatial-information cutoffs such as 1 bit/spike in macaques remove diffuse cells by construction [full text] ([Mao et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571030/)).

The answer to the first question is therefore yes in absolute terms and conditional in relative terms. **No source reports a single dorsal field covering a third or more of an environment larger than about 6 m or 2 m².** Fields that large relative to the space are reported only in small arenas, in ventral rodent hippocampus, in pooled marmoset hippocampus, and as diffuse subicular or lateral septal firing that is not a thresholded field. The per-level means for the 18 m track, 0.98 ± 0.03, 3.73 ± 0.43 and 5.52 ± 0.54 m in dorsal, intermediate and ventral CA3, are stated in the paper itself on p. 140. They come from 5, 3 and 4 of the 21 rats recorded, 12 in all, and count as fields "spatially stable contiguous regions with firing above 20% of the peak rate". The ventral fields are bounded by the 18 m track, and the authors note that "fields at the end of the track may be truncated", so the ventral scale in a longer space is unknown [full text; 12 of 21 derived] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). A cell can also cover a large share of an environment through many fields. Bat CA1 cells covered 15% of the tunnel on average, and the coverage histogram reaches roughly 80% for a few cells, read from the figure [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)).

Population-vector (PV) similarity has been plotted against the distance between two positions in only a few real datasets. Outside intermediate and ventral CA3, each one shows a peak that falls to near zero within about one field width, then a flat tail. In dorsal CA1 of rats on a 1.8 m track, z-scored PVs built from all 209 active pyramidal cells fell to near zero within less than 40 cm, with a spatial scale of about 30 cm, which is **17 to 22% of the track** [full text, derived] ([Green et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9245695/)). On a 76 cm circular track, Wang et al. state that beyond the average field size "the representations are maximally decorrelated with no further relationship to longer distances". Median field size there was 60° in CA1 and 73° in CA3, about 17 and 20% of the loop [full text, derived] ([Wang, Monaco & Knierim 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259364/)). Mouse retrosplenial cortex on a 150 cm treadmill belt gave a curve that "steeply falls to zero over a short distance", about 35 cm or 23% of the belt according to a paraphrase of the Discussion, and close to CA1 imaged the same way [full text; 35 cm not confirmed verbatim] ([Mao et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557927)). The subiculum, whose single cells fire over twice the area of CA1 cells, has a **narrower** PV autocorrelation peak than CA1 in a 150 cm box [full text, widths figure only] ([Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)). In the subiculum, then, broad single-cell tuning did not produce a broad population kernel. Along the CA3 long axis, broader fields did come with a broader kernel, as the next paragraph shows.

The one direct exception comes from CA3 on an 18 m linear track. Kjelstrup et al. stacked the rate maps of all pyramidal cells recorded at each longitudinal level, combining "data from multiple recordings", so each population vector pools cells recorded in different rats and sessions rather than a simultaneously recorded ensemble. They cut the track into 5 cm bins, which gave 330 vectors per running direction once the turning points were removed, and computed the Pearson correlation between every pair of vectors. The band of high correlation was 0.81, 3.71 and 6.66 m wide at r = 0.50, which the paper calls the half-width, and 1.41, 8.65 and 13.59 m wide at r = 0.20, in dorsal, intermediate and ventral CA3 [full text, p. 141] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). The marker lines in the figure span both sides of zero separation, so these are full widths across the diagonal. In ventral CA3 the correlation therefore falls to 0.5 about 3.3 m from a position [derived; 3.4 to 3.6 m figure-read]. Read from the figure, ventral correlation was about 0.70 at 2 m, 0.44 at 4 m, 0.27 at 6 m and 0.15 at 8 m, reached about zero near 10.7 m and stayed between about -0.03 and -0.13 out to 16 m. Intermediate correlation reached about zero near 7 m. Dorsal correlation fell below 0.1 by about 1 m and then stayed between about -0.05 and 0.13 [full text, figure-read] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). The ventral curve declines without a plateau between 1 and 8 m, and its fall to zero spans about 65% of the 16.5 m analysed. At both the intermediate and the ventral level the zero crossing sits at about twice the mean field size [derived]. The authors trust this population width more than single-field sizes, because their detector sometimes split fields and because fields at the track ends may be truncated [full text]. The result has four limits. It comes from one study that no one has replicated. The vectors are a pseudo-population pooled across rats and sessions. The 18 m track cuts off fields that run into its ends, so the track may set part of the ventral width. The main text gives neither the number of cells behind each level's vectors nor any rate normalization.

The remaining candidate exceptions are weak or indirect. Maurer et al. report that dorsal CA1 PVs "became decorrelated over substantially shorter distances" than PVs in the middle hippocampus, but no distances are available [abstract] ([Maurer et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16145692/)). A study from the same group measured the distance at which PV overlap drops to 0.5 on a 130 cm diameter ring track and found it **about three times larger** when rats drove a small vehicle than when they walked. When the rat sat still and the room moved, "most cells that were active on the track fired over most of the track" [full text, distances figure only] ([Terrazas et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6725460)). The claim that ventral PVs "remain correlated over much larger distances" comes from an introduction citing four papers [full text] ([Keinath et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627/)). Of these, Kjelstrup et al. measured PV correlation against distance in ventral CA3, as above, and Maurer et al. compared the dorsal pole with the middle hippocampus. Human fMRI shows hippocampal pattern similarity graded with remembered distance from 100 m to 30 km on a log scale [full text] ([Nielson et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4568259)) and across a virtual city about 390 m wide [full text] ([Deuker et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5053807)), but these are voxel patterns during memory retrieval, not the current position. A reanalysis of mouse medial entorhinal Neuropixels data from a virtual track run over a hundred metres found a general decreasing trend that was "not truly monotonic" [preprint] ([Dasbehera, Dogra & Redman 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08292)). In bats in 174 to 194 m tunnels, PVs for the same place reached after different routes stayed distinguishable for more than 90 m, but that measures history, not spatial distance, and the PVs were smoothed with a 10 m window [full text] ([Maimon et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)).

The three datasets from larger environments, the 48 m track, the 200 m tunnel and the 18.6 m² arena, contain no such curve. The Rich et al. supplement has 18 figures, none a PV or decoding analysis [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014 supplement](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf)). The only population analysis in Eliav et al. ran decoders on synthetic data [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)), and Harland et al. inferred that "the population discharges at each location were unique" from field layout without measuring PV similarity [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021, published PDF](https://cenl.ucsd.edu/pubs/Harland-contreras-souder-fellous+CA1-Multiscale-Megaspace+CurrBio+2021.pdf)). The answer to the second question is a qualified yes. **One real-data curve declines gradually across most of an environment under normal locomotion, in ventral rat CA3 on an 18 m track, and intermediate CA3 on the same track declines over about 7 m.** It rests on one unreplicated study, a pseudo-population pooled across rats and sessions, and a track short enough to truncate ventral fields. Every dorsal curve is local, including dorsal CA3 on the same track. The test has not been run on dorsal CA1 in environments longer than a 4 m loop, or on any region in an environment longer than 18 m. Two cautions apply to anyone who runs it. The far-distance floor depends on how PVs are built. Per-cell z-scoring pulls the mean correlation toward zero, while unnormalized rates that include silent cells leave a positive floor. Multi-field cells in large spaces could also raise the far-distance floor without creating a slope, since field positions of different cells on the 48 m track were uncorrelated at scales of 1 to 16 m [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf)). Both points are my reading of the methods, not reported results.

## Field size runs from 25 cm in lab arenas to 32 m in a bat tunnel

In standard laboratory spaces, dorsal CA1 fields are a few tens of centimetres across, and CA3 fields are smaller. The largest same-animal comparison recorded 3,670 CA1 cells in 13 rats and 2,203 CA3 cells in 6 of them. Median open-field fields were **1,775 cm² in CA1 and 1,275 cm² in CA3**, equal-area circles of 47.5 and 40.3 cm, and 5.5 and 3.9% of a 180 × 180 cm box. On a 250 cm track the values were 75 cm and 50 cm, on 75 cm radial arms 38.3 and 33.8 cm, and on a zigzag maze 48 and 34 cm [full text; diameters and fractions derived] ([Mizuseki et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3718552/)). In a 200 cm cylinder, proximal, intermediate and distal CA1 fields averaged 2,208, 2,688 and 1,931 cm², 6 to 9% of the floor [full text, derived] ([Henriksen et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093538/)). Mouse CA1 fields imaged on an 84 cm track had a median width of 24 cm [full text] ([Ziv et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3784308)), and two-photon fields on a 180 cm virtual track averaged 50 ± 19 cm [full text] ([Dombeck et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2648)). Rat CA1 fields on a T maze averaged 29.1 ± 14.7 cm [full text] ([Wirtshafter & Wilson 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7274787/)). A narrow definition gives smaller numbers. Counting only bins at least 1.5 SD above a cell's mean rate, mouse dorsal CA1 fields on a 1.9 m virtual track were 9.4 ± 2.0 cm per session [full text] ([Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021](https://www.eneuro.org/content/8/5/ENEURO.0211-21.2021)).

One-dimensional and two-dimensional sizes are not interchangeable, even in one lab. Mizuseki's CA1 open-field median corresponds to a 47.5 cm circle, yet the same region's track fields were 75 cm [derived] ([Mizuseki et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3718552/)). Track fields are also counted per running direction, while open-field fields are counted per cell.

In large spaces, absolute size depends on species and on position within the space. The bat fields of 0.6 to 32 m, the rat fields averaging 0.65 m on the 48 m track and the subfields of up to 3.46 m² in the 18.6 m² arena are described above. Bat field peak rate rose with field size, ρ = 0.29 over 1,629 fields, with mean in-field and out-of-field rates of 5.01 and 0.24 Hz [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). On the 48 m track, field size did not correlate with location along the track or with how many other fields the cell had, ρ = 0.05 for both [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014 supplement](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf)). In the 18.6 m² arena, cells with more subfields had smaller subfields, but each cell's summed area did not depend on subfield count, r = 0.027 [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)). Position matters in two dimensions. Fields grew with distance from the nearest wall in arenas up to 8.75 m², H = 10.5, p = 0.015 in the largest, mostly through width perpendicular to the wall, while field density was highest near walls [full text] ([Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)). Subfield size correlated with distance to the nearest wall at r = 0.36 in the 18.6 m² arena [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)). In the bat tunnel, fields within 4 m of the food-baited landing balls were smaller and denser, with size rising with distance from the ball at ρ = 0.64, while field size bore no relation to the 13 landmarks [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)).

## Larger spaces add fields faster than they enlarge them

Every study that enlarged an environment found more fields per cell, and most found larger fields, but field size grew far more slowly than the space. The classic rescaling experiments showed mixed behaviour within one population. When a 76 cm cylinder was doubled in diameter and height, which quadruples floor area, **36% of 25 cells recorded in both kept a field at the same angular and relative radial position, 52% had a field in only one cylinder or unrelated fields, and 12% were nearly silent in both**, counts of 9, 13 and 3. The authors had selected cells with a clear field in the small cylinder or small rectangle. Field area did not quadruple. For the 9 scaling cells, the ratio of large to small field size averaged 0.87 and ranged from 0.24 to 2.06. It rose to 1.73 when the authors equalized dwell time per unit area and to 2.49 when they merged 2 × 2 pixels, and they concluded that "sampling problems may be obscuring an interesting relationship between field sizes". Doubling a 40 × 56 cm rectangle gave scaled fields in 7 of 19 cells [full text] ([Muller & Kubie 1987](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.07-07-01951.1987)). The companion paper notes that "a single cell may have more than one field in a given apparatus" [abstract] ([Muller, Kubie & Ranck 1987](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:3612225&resultType=core&format=json)). O'Keefe and Burgess recorded 28 fields from 27 CA1 and CA3 complex-spike cells in 6 rats in four boxes, 61 × 61, 61 × 122, 122 × 61 and 122 × 122 cm, with 61 cm walls. In 38 of 56 field-by-axis cases the peak sat at a fixed distance from one wall or a fixed proportion of the distance between two, and 28 of those 38 were distances [full text; split derived from Table 1b]. Peaks in the illustrated cells sat 2.7 to 36.5 cm from the walls that fixed them. Of 112 rate maps in Table 1a, 16 had no field, 69 had one compact peak, 11 had one stretched peak, 13 had two peaks and 3 had more, and 9 of 28 fields showed a second peak in at least one larger box. In 22 of 24 stretched or double-peaked maps, from 14 of 15 cells, the peak moved with running direction. The paper reports no field areas, and its bins were 1/24 of each box's area, so bin size grew with the box [full text] ([O'Keefe & Burgess 1996](https://doi.org/10.1038/381425a0)). Shortening a linear track from 170 to 100 cm in 3 rats kept 1,147 of 1,750 CA1 and CA3 fields, which shrank from **52 to 42 cm**, a 19% drop for a 41% shorter track, p < 10⁻¹² [full text] ([Diba & Buzsáki 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2656938/)).

Open arenas of 2 to 19 m² show the same pattern with larger numbers. In 4 rats, dorsal CA1 cells averaged 1.3 ± 0.03 fields in a 68 cm cylinder and 3.4 ± 0.11 on the floor of a 150 × 140 cm chamber, or 4.05 counting its stairs. Single-field place cells fell from 72% to 11%, fields grew by about 30%, and the per-field share of the floor fell from 0.27 to 0.073. Multiple fields were irregular, and only 7.6% of chamber cells had autocorrelogram angles in the 50 to 70° range of a hexagonal grid [full text] ([Fenton et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2695947/)). In a 1.8 × 1.4 m box, 5.6 times the area of a 76 cm cylinder, fields per cell were **5.89 in CA1, 4.96 in CA3 and 5.35 in DG**, and single-field cells fell from 60.7 to 17.9% in CA1, 81.8 to 29% in CA3 and 63.9 to 8.2% in DG. The largest field per cell grew, but pooled field size did not, F(1,1316) = 0.01, p = 0.93. CA3 and DG each came from one rat [full text] ([Park, Dvorak & Fenton 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0022349)). Across four arenas from 1.09 to 8.75 m² in 5 rats with 629 CA1 place cells, fields per cell rose from **1.28 to 2.28**, field count rose linearly with area, r = 0.983, as did summed field area, r = 0.996, and 84.8% of cells had more than one field across the four arenas. A gamma-Poisson distribution fit field counts better than a Poisson distribution with equal rates, and its extrapolation puts 99% of place cells at one or more fields above 51.8 m² [full text] ([Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)). Across arenas of 0.54, 2.16, 8.2 and 18.6 m², 94% of cells had a single field in the smallest arena, 91% had one or two subfields at 2.16 m², and **82% had two to five subfields** at 18.6 m². Subfield count rose linearly with area, R² = 0.978, and summed area per cell was 1.99 to 2.15 m², or 10.7 to 11.6% of the largest arena [full text, fractions derived] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)).

The 48 m track and the 200 m tunnel give the cleanest statistics because both are long and one-dimensional. On the track, extended in stages to 3, 10, 22 and 48 m and analysed only on novel sections, 253 putative pyramidal cells from 5 rats averaged 2.29 to 4.33 fields per rat. Field counts were overdispersed and fit a negative binomial with r = 0.57 and p = 0.14, which equals a Poisson process per cell whose rate is gamma distributed with shape 0.57 and rate 7.75, in fields per metre. Some cells had close to 30 fields. For the 61 cells with six or more fields, field positions were uniform and gaps exponential, with 0 of 61 deviating from a Poisson process, and positions of different cells were uncorrelated at bins of 1 to 16 m. Field density did not vary with track angle, distance to corners or running speed, and slow-wave-sleep firing before exposure predicted later field count, r = 0.45 [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf); [Rich et al. 2014 supplement](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf)). In the bat tunnel, cells averaged **4.9 fields per flight direction**, some more than 10, with uniform positions, exponential gaps and no spatial periodicity in any bat. A 6 m section of the same tunnel gave one or two fields per cell, and a 130 m section gave intermediate values. The multiscale code was present on the first day of exposure, with no trend over 30 days, and in 3 lab-born bats raised in a 5.3 × 5.0 m room [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). A later study recorded CA1 and CA3 in 15 bats in tunnels up to 200 m. In tunnels of 130 to 200 m, **29.6% of CA1 cell-directions had one field against 73.7% in CA3**, and five or more fields occurred in 31.9% of CA1 place cells against 0.7% of CA3 place cells. CA1 field number rose with tunnel length, CA3 field number stayed low, and field size grew in both [full text] ([Maimon et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)). In mice imaged across several large virtual environments, each cell's field propensity varied about 10-fold between neighbouring cells and was preserved across environments and months [abstract] ([Lee et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33035454/)).

Putting these on a common scale needs arithmetic that the papers do not do. The rat gamma fit implies a mean of 0.57 / 7.75 = 0.074 fields per metre across all cells, silent ones included, while 4.9 bat fields over about 185 m is 0.026 fields per metre among place cells only [derived]. The spread of field sizes relative to the mean is similar in the two datasets, a coefficient of variation of 0.66 for the rats and about 0.63 implied by the bat log-normal [derived]. The bat value is approximate, because the log field sizes in that dataset are skewed, about -0.72 where a log-normal gives 0 [full text, figure-read] ([Mainali, Azeredo da Silveira & Burak 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.01.017)). Per-cell coverage was 15% of the tunnel in bats [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)), about 11% of the 18.6 m² arena [full text, derived] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)), and about 5% of the 48 m track, from 3.5 fields of 0.65 m [derived]. Implied growth exponents are 0.36 for bat field length against tunnel length, about 0.15 for rat field area against floor area from Fenton's 30% for 5.8 times the area, and about 0.4 for field length against track length from Diba and Buzsáki [derived]. Muller and Kubie's fourfold larger cylinder gives field-area exponents of -0.10 from the raw ratio, 0.40 with dwell time equalized and 0.66 with merged pixels [derived].

The studies disagree on several points. Fenton found pooled fields 30% larger in a bigger room while Park found no change in pooled size after a similar enlargement, and the zero-rate field definition both labs used may fragment or merge fields differently as rooms grow [inference] ([Fenton et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2695947/); [Park, Dvorak & Fenton 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0022349)). Fenton and Park's 3.4 to 5.9 fields per cell in boxes of about 2 m² exceed Tanni's 2.28 in 8.75 m², again most likely because of thresholds [inference]. Harland et al. argue that the 48 m track's novelty, single dimension and smaller floor may hide multi-scale structure in rats, and Rich et al. did not report within-cell size ratios, so whether rat cells are multiscale in the bat sense on a long track is untested [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)). Eliav et al. write that rodent field counts were "much smaller" than theirs, which holds against the 1D rodent literature but not against the 2D box counts [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). Eliav et al. also note that the 48 m track was a zigzag of roughly 1 m segments and that the 18 m track of Kjelstrup et al. passed through several small rooms, so before their study no place-cell recording had used a single open compartment longer than about 10 m [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). Familiarity and speed matter less than size. Megaspace maps were less stable between two visits than small-arena maps, t(920) = 8.33, and speed-matched sessions removed a small difference in summed subfield area between robot-following and foraging rats [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326)). In bats, the within-cell field-size ratio did not track the speed ratio at those fields, ρ = 0.03 [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)).

## Field scale rises five- to eightfold toward the ventral pole, in one study

The dorsoventral gradient rests on one study. Kjelstrup et al. recorded CA3 in 21 rats running back and forth on an 18 m linear track and grouped recording sites by position along the long axis, dorsal at 7 to 22%, intermediate at 40 to 70% and ventral at 70 to 100%. Fields were "spatially stable contiguous regions with firing above 20% of the peak rate". Mean field size, ± SEM, was 0.98 ± 0.03 m for 111 dorsal cells from 5 rats, 3.73 ± 0.43 m for 37 intermediate cells from 3 rats and 5.52 ± 0.54 m for 61 ventral cells from 4 rats, or 5, 21 and 31% of the track [full text; fractions derived]. The legend of Fig. 4 calls this measure "the average width of the largest place field of individual cells", so the means may describe each cell's largest field. "Few fields were longer than 10 m", and one ventral cell drawn in the paper had a single field from about 1.2 to 16.9 m in one running direction, 87% of the track [full text; length figure-read]. The central peak of each cell's spatial autocorrelation, measured at 20% of its height, was 1.12, 3.07 and 4.10 m wide at the three levels. Theta phase precession appeared at every level. Its slope fell from -102° ± 33° per m dorsally to -18° ± 9° per m in intermediate and ventral cells combined, and in ventral CA3 "the phase advance often occurred over distances as large as 10 m". The authors read this as showing that ventral firing regions "are part of one place field rather than multiple overlapping fields" [full text] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)).

The population estimate scales the same way. The PV band was 0.81, 3.71 and 6.66 m wide at r = 0.5 across the diagonal, and in ventral CA3 the correlation reached zero only about 10.7 m from a position, as described in the second section [full text; zero crossing figure-read]. The authors trust this population width more than single-field sizes, because their detector "occasionally divided fields as a consequence of local rate variations" and because "fields at the end of the track may be truncated". Their summary is that the map "expands approximately linearly from a scale of <1 m near the dorsal pole to ~10 m near the ventral pole" [full text] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). Left-run and right-run fields were more correlated in ventral than in dorsal CA3, yet for discrete environments ventral CA3 "still had strongly orthogonalized place maps", a result the paper leaves to its supplement, which was not read [full text, note 33]. A same-year commentary describes ventral neurons that "fire almost everywhere in an environment in one room, and nowhere in an identical environment in another room" [secondary] ([Hasselmo 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2590634)). The main text does not describe the track's layout, which Eliav et al. say passed through several small rooms [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). A later paper reports that fewer than 10% of ventral CA3 cells in that study showed reliable phase precession [secondary] ([Royer et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2825159/)), a share the Kjelstrup main text does not give. Because the track was only 18 m long, a 10 m ventral field cannot be independent of the track's ends, so the ventral scale in a longer open space is unknown [inference].

Other rat studies agree that ventral cells fire more broadly but disagree on how spatial they are. In a square chamber, a significantly smaller share of ventral than dorsal CA1 complex-spike cells had place fields, and ventral spatial selectivity was "of significantly lower resolution" [abstract] ([Jung, Wiener & McNaughton 1994](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:7996180&resultType=core&format=json)). In a cylinder, ventral cells had positional firing "very similar" to dorsal place cells [abstract] ([Poucet, Thinus-Blanc & Muller 1994](https://doi.org/10.1097/00001756-199410270-00014)). Kjelstrup et al. point out that this study did not verify recording sites histologically and found fields of dorsal size, so "it is hard to compare the results" [full text, note 29] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). Recording CA1 at the extreme dorsal pole and in the middle hippocampus, Maurer et al. found smaller fields and higher rates dorsally and a falling probability of a field toward the temporal pole [abstract] ([Maurer et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16145692/)). Kjelstrup et al. place the intermediate recordings of Maurer et al. and of Jung et al. at about 30 to 45% of the dorsal-to-ventral axis [secondary, Kjelstrup note 29] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). Royer et al. recorded 1,546 dorsal and 351 ventral CA3 pyramidal cells in 6 rats in a 1 × 2 m open field, a seven-arm radial maze and a zigzag maze. Ventral cells "rarely showed continuous two-dimensional place fields", most "lacked the defining criteria of place cells", single-arm fields were **39% larger** in ventral CA3, p < 0.01, and ventral cells typically fired on several arms. Left and right journey field sizes correlated at r = 0.81 in ventral CA3 against -0.02 dorsally. Absolute sizes are figure only [full text] ([Royer et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2825159/)). In a context task with two 37 × 37 cm boxes, ventral CA3 fields "usually extended only to areas adjacent to the location of one object", and after overtraining, ventral ensembles of 2 to 5 cells generalized across locations within a context more than dorsal ensembles did [full text] ([Komorowski et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3667351)).

Mouse data show smaller differences and conflict on precision. In 35 and 70 cm cylinders, ventral CA1 fields were larger than dorsal, F(1,197) = 5.9, and grew in the larger cylinder, P < 0.001. The authors describe ventral cells as "broadly tuned" and say they "frequently span the entirety of the environment". Field count per cell did not differ by region, and population location reconstruction showed no region effect, F(1,70) = 2.1, with 89 dorsal cells from 6 mice and 65 ventral cells from 5 mice [full text] ([Keinath et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627/)). On a 1.9 m virtual track, head-fixed mice gave ventral CA1 fields of 11.9 ± 6.3 cm against 9.4 ± 2.0 cm dorsally per session, not significantly different, but ventral maps were "coarser and less precise" with lower spatial information, and dorsal activity had higher linear dimensionality [full text] ([Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021](https://www.eneuro.org/content/8/5/ENEURO.0211-21.2021)). The ventral site in that study, 4.25 mm deep, may be intermediate rather than the ventral pole [inference].

Long-axis gradients appear in other species too, though homology is uncertain. In marmosets, field width "significantly decreased the further posterior the recording was conducted" [full text] ([Courellis et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922474/)). Human fMRI shows more similar voxel time courses and higher autocorrelation in anterior than posterior hippocampus [abstract] ([Brunec et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.05.016)) and coarse-grained position encoding anteriorly [abstract] ([Evensmoen et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22357)). A six-scale distance task shifted activity from posterior to anterior hippocampus as scale grew, but only 6 of 19 individuals showed it and a reviewer disputed the hippocampal result [full text] ([Peer et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6739872)). In tufted titmice, place-cell incidence rose from under 10% at the posterior pole to over 70% at the anterior pole [full text] ([Payne, Lynch & Aronov 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8503942/)).

The studies disagree in three ways. Ventral precision is "comparable" to dorsal at matched population size in one mouse study and coarser in another, while rats show lower resolution. Ventral field counts are lower per environment in Maurer, equal in Keinath and spread over many arms in Royer, while Kjelstrup et al. read ventral phase precession on the 18 m track as showing one long field per cell. Whether large fields are a long-axis property or a per-cell property is also contested, because Harland et al. and Eliav et al. both find a wide range of field sizes within single dorsal CA1 cells. Harland et al. write that the dorsal-small, ventral-large concept "is challenged" by this, while Eliav et al. call the within-cell multiscale code "a fundamentally different phenomenon" from the gradient [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10294326); [Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). A review proposes gradients superimposed on discrete domains defined by gene expression [abstract] ([Strange et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3785)). No study gives field counts per cell in ventral hippocampus in a large environment.

## CA3 fields are smaller and sparser than CA1, and CA2 fields larger

Within dorsal hippocampus, same-animal comparisons give a consistent ordering. CA3 fields are more compact than CA1 fields and carry more information per spike, 1.77 against 1.31 bits/spike in the open field and 1.57 against 0.83 on the track, and fewer CA3 cells have fields on a track, 16.8% against 48.0% with a coherence criterion [full text] ([Mizuseki et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3718552/)). Along the CA3 transverse axis, proximal CA3 near the dentate gyrus has smaller, sharper fields than distal CA3 and CA2 [abstract] ([Lu et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.07.007)). In 80 × 80 cm and 50 cm radius enclosures, **CA2 fields were 24.5% larger than CA1 fields**, z = -2.54, P = 0.034, CA2 cells had more fields than CA1 and CA3 cells, and CA2 carried less spatial information, P < 0.001. Active fractions per session were 59.2% in CA1, 68.2% in CA2 and 48.1% in CA3 [full text] ([Mankin et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4392894/)). Bats show the CA3-CA1 contrast at a much larger scale, with CA3 maps sparser, higher in spatial information and lower in coverage than CA1 maps in 130 to 200 m tunnels, p = 10⁻⁷² for information [full text] ([Maimon et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)).

The dentate gyrus contains two cell types with opposite statistics. In rats recorded in four rooms with platforms about 107 to 117 cm across, granule-cell-layer cells had **1.04 fields per active cell** and were active in 9% of environments on average, while hilar putative mossy cells had 2.26 fields and were active in 88%, and CA3 cells had 1.19 fields and were active in 29%. All 8 juxtacellularly identified granule cells had a single field [full text] ([GoodSmith et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5300955/)). In 16 mice with 586 putative granule cells and 500 putative mossy cells, only 6.6% of recorded granule cells had two or more fields, against 57.8% of mossy cells and 36.1% of CA3 cells, and granule cells had the lowest spatial information [full text] ([Senzai & Buzsáki 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5293146/)). Earlier work had separated single-field cells, proposed as mature granule cells, from multi-field cells below the granule layer [abstract] ([Neunuebel & Knierim 2012](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.6038-11.2012)). The 5.35 fields per DG cell in Park et al.'s large box probably include mossy cells, though Park et al. did not separate the types [inference] ([Park, Dvorak & Fenton 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0022349)).

CA1 itself varies along two axes. Along the proximodistal axis in a 200 cm cylinder, distal cells had more fields on the first trial, 2.13 against 1.57 proximally, and fewer single-field cells, 33.8% against 57.0%, without larger fields, P = 0.19, while spatial information fell distally [full text] ([Henriksen et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3093538/)). Deep-layer CA1 cells were "more likely to have place fields" and fired at higher rates [abstract] ([Mizuseki et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2894)). Superficial maps were more stable while deep maps stabilized during goal learning [abstract] ([Danielson et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.06.020)), and superficial cells were more active in cue-poor environments while deep cells were more active in cue-rich ones [abstract] ([Sharif et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.10.034)). These abstracts do not report field size by sublayer.

Structures outside the hippocampus proper carry weaker or differently formatted spatial codes. In rat caudodorsal lateral septum on a T maze, 26.5% of cells were place cells against 63.0% in simultaneously recorded CA1, with field length **28.8 ± 16.6 cm against 29.1 ± 14.7 cm**, about 1.7 and 1.5 fields per place cell, and 0.73 against 1.54 bits/spike [full text, field counts derived] ([Wirtshafter & Wilson 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7274787/)). A larger silicon-probe study found place fields in 5.3% of 4,393 lateral septum rate maps against 31.7% of 5,735 hippocampal maps. Lateral septal neurons carried position mainly in theta phase, which decoded position better than rate in 786 of 840 neurons [full text] ([Tingley & Buzsáki 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605060/)). About 95% of medial entorhinal cells passed spatial cutoffs, and of the spatial cells 17.7% were grid cells, 9.3% border cells and 67.5% non-grid spatial cells, whose field sizes were not reported [full text] ([Diehl et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444540/)). Lateral entorhinal neurons showed weak spatial specificity [abstract] ([Hargreaves et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1110449)). In mouse retrosplenial cortex on a 150 cm treadmill, 297 of 2,256 imaged neurons, about 13%, were place cells with narrow fields [full text] ([Mao et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557927)). Rostral thalamic nuclei including reuniens contain place-like, head-direction and border-like units [abstract] ([Jankowski et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2015.00256)). Medial prefrontal studies disagree, with a "substantial proportion" of prelimbic and infralimbic cells having goal-biased fields in one lab [abstract] ([Hok et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0407332102)) and "very few" place-specific deep-layer cells in another [abstract] ([Jung et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/8.5.437)). No source gives field sizes in metres for nucleus reuniens, retrosplenial cortex or prefrontal cortex.

## Bats, primates and birds share place coding at very different rates

Bats give the clearest rodent-like code outside rodents, and the highest place-cell rates. In the 200 m tunnel, 83.4% of dorsal CA1 putative pyramidal cells were place cells [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)), and pooled over 130 to 200 m tunnels, 76.3% of CA1 and 58.6% of CA3 pyramidal cells were [full text] ([Maimon et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)). In three-dimensional flight, over 90% of neurons encoded all three axes with similar resolution, so fields were close to isotropic [abstract] ([Yartsev & Ulanovsky 2013](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1235338)). In crawling bats, 47 of 64 non-diffuse cells, 73%, were place cells [unverified, snippet] ([Ulanovsky & Moss 2007](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/neurobiology.labs.ulanovsky/files/uploads/ulanovsky_moss_natneurosci2007.pdf)). In a flight room, 27% of 304 CA1 neurons were spatially selective for current position but 46% were selective at some time lag, and for 42% of those the current-position information was negligible, so analyses of current position miss nonlocal coding [full text] ([Dotson & Yartsev 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg1278)).

Primates show fewer place cells and stronger coding of view or facing direction. In 2 freely moving marmosets, 289 of 2,054 single and multi-units, **14.1%**, were place cells, with about 1.22 directional fields per place cell and 1.60 ± 1.61 bits/spike. Theta came in bouts of about 0.4 s and was not consistently coupled to place-cell firing [full text, fields per cell derived] ([Courellis et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922474/)). In 3 rhesus macaques foraging in a 3.30 m diameter arena, 26% of hippocampal units passed a classical place test at 1 bit/spike, but only 7% remained place-tuned once facing location, head direction and other variables were fit. Facing location tuned 24% of cells and spatial view 11%, and grid tuning was about 1 to 2% [full text] ([Mao et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571030/)). In a virtual X-maze, macaque hippocampal position codes did not generalize across two tasks in the same environment [abstract] ([Gulli et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0548-3)). Earlier macaque work emphasized spatial view cells, whose responses depend on where the monkey looks [abstract, review] ([Rolls & Xiang 2006](https://doi.org/10.1515/REVNEURO.2006.17.1-2.175)).

Human single units come only from epilepsy patients navigating virtual environments, so no human field has a physical size. Location-specific cells were found mainly in hippocampus among 317 neurons in a virtual town [abstract] ([Ekstrom et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01964)). Secondary sources report 24% of hippocampal neurons as pure place cells with about 1.7 fields each, which could not be checked [secondary, unverified]. In another virtual town, 95 of 371 units, 25.6%, were place-responsive [full text, annotated copy] ([Miller et al. 2013](https://www.scienceintheclassroom.org/research-papers/original-gps-how-we-remember-what-happened-where)). Entorhinal "path cells" coded direction of travel around a virtual square road [abstract] ([Jacobs et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0911213107)).

Birds have mammal-like place cells whose prevalence tracks ecology. In tufted titmice, a food-caching species, 321 of 538 excitatory cells, **60%**, were place cells, against 48 of 179, 27%, in non-caching zebra finches [full text] ([Payne, Lynch & Aronov 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8503942/)). In flying barn owls in a 4 × 2.2 × 2.4 m room, 27% of hippocampal units and 48% of hyperpallium apicale units were place cells, with field size not differing across regions [full text] ([Agarwal et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9945993/)). A chickadee study reportedly found 56% place cells among 4,366 units [unverified] ([Chettih et al. 2024](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(24)00235-6)). Field sizes in metres were not available for marmosets on the L-track, for macaques, for birds or for 3D bat recordings. Cross-species comparisons of place-cell rates are weak because bat and bird papers report fractions of cells that fired, primate studies often do not control for view, and humans navigate on screens [inference].

## The subiculum spreads firing over half the space yet decodes as well as CA1

The subiculum is the main output of the hippocampus and the clearest real-data case of broad single-unit spatial tuning in rodents. Its cells fire at several times the CA1 rate, carry far less information per spike, spread their firing over roughly twice the area, and still support population decoding at least as good as CA1.

### Subicular cells fire faster and more broadly, with less information per spike

The early descriptions are qualitative. Barnes et al. found that neither entorhinal cortex nor subiculum "exhibited the highly localized patterns of spatial firing observed in the CA fields", that rates there were "substantially higher", and that some subicular cells showed "spatially consistent, but dispersed, firing … suggestive of the convergence of a number of CA1 place cells" [abstract] ([Barnes et al. 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2392566/)). Sharp and Green reported that "most subicular cells showed a robust locational signal" but "tended to fire throughout much of the environment", with "graded, location-related rate modulation" rather than compact fields. They recorded 99 dorsal subicular cells in 21 rats chasing pellets in a 76 cm cylinder and kept every cell they could isolate. On average a cell fired at least one spike in 84% of the visited 2.9 cm pixels, a figure that includes 10 putative interneurons firing in 99%, and bursting and non-bursting cells did so in 70% and 79%. Spatial coherence was significant for 83 of the 99 cells. Its mean was 0.48, against 0.80 for hippocampal place cells recorded in the same apparatus in an earlier study that kept only cells with visible fields, and 11% of subicular cells reached 0.80. Head direction was significant for 60 of 98 cells but explained 1% of rate variance on average and 13% at most. The authors recorded no hippocampal cells, did not count fields and did not compute spatial information [full text] ([Sharp & Green 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-04-02339.1994)). Later studies that recorded subiculum and CA1 in the same animals put numbers on this.

Kim et al. say their spatial activity fraction "can be interpreted as the proportion of the environment over which a neuron fires". On the 350 cm track that is about 242 cm for distal subiculum against 91 cm for CA1 [derived]. Ledergerber's 56% of a 150 cm box is an area equal to a square about 113 cm on a side, against 76 cm for CA1 [derived]. These measures differ from each other and from a thresholded field, so they should not be ranked as one quantity. Taken together, subicular information per spike is **3 to 8 times lower** than in CA1 while information per second is about equal, so subicular cells spend more spikes for the same information. The broad-tuning picture holds most strongly in rats and in middle and distal subiculum. A mouse study sampling mostly proximal subiculum found no difference in field size [full text] ([Abad-Perez et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833)), and 68.5% of mouse subicular neurons were place cells in arenas of 30 to 35 cm [full text] ([Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)). No study reports subicular field size in centimetres with a stated threshold alongside CA1, and none gives a distribution of fields per cell.

### Distal subiculum is broadest and proximal subiculum is close to CA1

Kim et al. found a strong proximodistal gradient, with spatial activity fraction rising from 0.31 to 0.69 and rate from 2.5 to 10.3 Hz, p < 10⁻⁴, and describe proximal cells as "more similar to canonical, sparsely firing hippocampal place cells" [full text] ([Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)). Sharp and Green had seen only "slightly higher" rates, spatial signalling and field size distally. Distal against proximal cells, 52 against 34, averaged 11.2 against 6.3 Hz, spatial coherence 0.59 against 0.38, and firing in 88% against 78% of visited pixels. With cells as samples, the coherence, field-size and in-field rate differences were significant, at p < 0.001, < 0.01 and < 0.05, and the overall rate difference was not. With the means of 16 distal and 9 proximal electrode tracks as samples, none was significant [full text] ([Sharp & Green 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-04-02339.1994)). Lee et al. found the opposite on one property, with multi-field units more common proximally, p = 0.033 [full text] ([Lee, Lee & Lee 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)). The two need not conflict, because a cell can fire at graded rates almost everywhere without forming discrete fields [inference]. Vector trace cells were 36% of cue-responsive cells distally against 5% proximally [full text] ([Poulter et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739)). Gene expression splits the subiculum into proximal and distal classes with different projection targets, and distal subiculum was needed for encoding in a spatial working memory task [secondary] ([Cembrowski et al. 2018, news summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/04/180419131055.htm)). Burst firing does not predict broader tuning. Sparsely bursting cells were more often spatial than dominantly bursting cells, 67% against 33%, p = 0.022, and all 17 boundary cells were sparsely bursting [full text] ([Simonnet & Brecht 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6510334)). In a 76 cm cylinder, bursting and non-bursting cells had the same rate, about 4 Hz, and similar coherence, 0.44 and 0.46, but non-bursting cells fired over more of the floor, 79% against 70% of pixels, a significant difference [full text] ([Sharp & Green 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-04-02339.1994)). Ventral subicular fields were reported larger than CA1 fields in one study known only from a catalogue record [unverified].

### Boundary, trace, corner and axis cells shape subicular firing

Boundary vector cells are the best-documented subicular type. In 6 rats foraging in a 62 × 62 cm box with 50 cm walls, Lever et al. found 36 such cells and put an upper bound of 24% on their share, from 45 boundary-cell recordings among 186 recorded firing fields. The 45 include 9 cells recorded again on later days, so the ratio counts recordings, not distinct cells. A second field appeared along an inserted barrier in all 3 cells tested, and 3 cells kept their firing in complete darkness. Changing the square for a 79 cm walled circle did not disrupt boundary firing, and 14 of 17 cells were "primarily insensitive" even to a 90 cm raised platform with no walls. Firing carried 0.25 bits/spike about location against 0.05 about head direction, and directional selectivity was 1.56 against 2.87 for CA1 place cells analysed the same way in an earlier study. Mean rate was 5.0 Hz, and the peak rates printed on the 36 maps run from 1.6 to 28.6 Hz, median about 11 Hz. The authors say short-range cells appear to outnumber long-range ones and that cells they grouped as far-tuned had broader fields, but they give no counts and did not measure field area [full text; peak rates figure-read] ([Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)). In a 150 cm walled circle, peaks sat 70.74 ± 1.26 cm from the centre, about 4.3 cm from the wall, and 42 of 57 cells with wall-adjacent fields gained a second field along an inserted barrier [full text, distance derived] ([Stewart et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866438)). A developmental study classified 187 of 517 adult subicular neurons, 36%, as boundary vector cells, with a mean preferred distance of 7.5 ± 0.6 cm and preferred directions within ±12° of wall orientations in square boxes [full text] ([Muessig et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10834499)). Mouse imaging with a border-score criterion found 10.7% in a square and 7.2% in a rectangle [full text] ([Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)), so prevalence ranges from about 10% to 36% depending on species and criterion. "Boundary-off" cells fire everywhere except where a matching boundary cell fires, but only a few have been recorded [full text] ([Stewart et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866438)). Vector trace cells, 73 of 253 cue-responsive cells or 29%, kept a field at a vector offset after a cue was removed, for 0.4 to 2.5 h, and were tuned 18.8 cm from the cue against 12.4 cm for other cue cells [full text] ([Poulter et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739)). Concave corner cells were 7.0 to 7.2% of mouse subicular neurons in triangles and squares and about 0% in circles, against 0.6% of CA1 neurons in a square [full text] ([Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)). Axis-tuned cells fire for travel in either direction along one axis of a multi-path track, so one cell fires on separate segments that share that axis. In 3 rats on a 160 × 125 cm track with 8 cm paths, 47 of 542 subicular neurons, 8.7%, met the criteria. Excluding one outlier, their firing at the two tuning peaks averaged 4.65 ± 2.23 times their firing at the minima. No neuron met the criteria in a 60 cm wall-less arena above the track. When the track was rotated 90°, all 26 tested axis cells kept their tuning fixed to the room, with tuning curves correlating at 0.76 in room coordinates against -0.41 in track coordinates. The paper's 73.9 ± 12.5% is not track coverage. It is a selection score averaged over the 47 cells. A cell's score is the share of well-sampled 2 cm track positions where, while the rat faced a preferred direction, it fired at least half its mean rate for that direction, and a cell needed over 50% to qualify. In the arena, about 29% of subicular cells had place-like maps and about 21% boundary-like maps by correlation with idealized templates at r > 0.4, and 7 of the 47 axis cells were boundary-like there [full text; percentage derived; supplementary figures not read] ([Olson, Tongprasearth & Nitz 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4464)). No dedicated report of subicular object-vector cells was found, and no boundary, trace or corner cell paper reports field area.

### Subicular populations generalize across rooms, decode well and have a narrower similarity peak

Subicular maps change less between environments than CA1 maps, but they do change. Sharp reported that subicular cells "showed very similar firing patterns in the two chambers", a cylinder and a square, "regardless of whether they were visually similar or different", while hippocampal cells remapped [abstract] ([Sharp 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9095343/)). In the earlier study, Sharp and Green moved rats directly from a 76 cm cylinder into a 39 × 55.5 cm rectangle while recording 8 cells. Each cell fired over much of both boxes, and 5 of the 8 appeared by eye to repeat their cylinder pattern in the same room orientation, with no quantitative test. The authors note that the hippocampal comparison studies used a home-cage interval rather than direct transfer [full text] ([Sharp & Green 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-04-02339.1994)). Subicular patterns in a large square were "expanded versions" of those in a smaller square inside it, but the expansion did not cross barriers [abstract] ([Sharp 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10495074/)), and Sharp described a "rigid framework of interrelated firing fields" fitted to each enclosure [abstract] ([Sharp 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859764/)). On two identical-looking tracks, all 39 testable subicular cells had higher same-track than cross-track similarity, so the maps did differ. Yet mean rates across tracks correlated at **r = 0.87 in subiculum against 0.19 in CA1**, and only 4 of 59 subicular cells, 7%, were active on just one track against 7 of 15 CA1 cells, 47% [full text] ([Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)). Lever et al. concluded that boundary vector cells "remap less than PCs in response to the same environmental differences". CA1 maps from 5 rats correlated at 0.803 between two trials in the square, 0.053 between the square and a walled circle, and -0.049 between the square and a wall-less platform, values the paper labels an unpublished observation. The paper gives no correlation for the boundary cells, and the change to the wall-less platform "elicited remapping in many of the non-BVC subicular cells" recorded alongside them, with data not shown [full text] ([Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)). Subicular firing was "more schematic" in a visual-scene task, with higher within-epoch and lower between-epoch population correlations, and it did not change under scene blurring while CA1 rate remapping did [full text] ([Lee, Lee & Lee 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)). Tuning on the left and right arms of a T-maze correlated at 0.41 in subiculum against 0.26 in CA1, P = 0.0016 [full text] ([Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)). In bats switching between vision and echolocation, subicular neurons turned on or off while CA1 neurons shifted their fields [abstract] ([Geva-Sagiv et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27239936/)).

Within one environment, the subicular population code changes faster with distance than the CA1 code. The mean PV autocorrelation peak was narrower in subiculum than in CA1, which the authors call "a faster change of the population code between nearby spatial locations" [full text, widths figure only] ([Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)). Decoding matches. With a 400 ms window and 5 cm bins, subiculum and CA1 did not differ at 5, 20 or 50 neurons, p = 0.66, 0.61 and 0.32, but subiculum was better at **100 neurons, p = 0.005, and 150 neurons, p < 0.001** [full text] ([Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)). On a linear track, errors were comparable at all population sizes, P > 0.2, thinning subicular spikes to CA1 rates made subicular decoding worse, and subicular error grew less under added noise [full text] ([Kitanishi, Umaba & Mizuseki 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)). With 10 units, position R² was 0.25 in subiculum against 0.13 in CA1, P = 0.010 [full text] ([Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)). Population information grew by 0.064 bits per added distal subicular neuron against 0.036 for CA1 [full text] ([Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)). Neighbouring subicular cells have unrelated maps. Pairs recorded on the same wire correlated at 0.06 on average for 4 simultaneous pairs and 0.0003 for 37 pairs from different sessions, while one cell's maps from repeated sessions correlated at 0.49, n = 7 [full text] ([Sharp & Green 1994](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-04-02339.1994)). Subicular population activity also has higher intrinsic dimension, a correlation dimension of **5.24 against 3.07 in the T-maze and 5.84 against 3.61 in the open field**, and its manifold structure was more similar across tasks than CA1's, P = 0.0057 [full text] ([Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)). A conference poster reports that dimensionality rises with remapping less in subiculum than in CA1 and CA3 [unverified, conference abstract] ([FENS 2024 poster](https://world-wide.org/eposter/fens-24/dimensionality-neural-sub-populations-t851pjkv)).

### No subicular recording exists in a large space

The largest environment with subicular recordings is a 3.30 m macaque arena. There, 5% of 110 subicular-complex units passed a classical place test, 12% were spatially tuned in a model-based analysis, 74% were head-direction tuned, and subiculum had "the least sparsity" of the three regions sampled. The sample mixed prosubiculum, subiculum and presubiculum [full text] ([Mao et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571030/)). The largest rodent environments are a 350 cm U-track [full text] ([Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)), a 234 cm track [full text] ([Kitanishi, Umaba & Mizuseki 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)), a 150 × 190 cm box [full text] ([Poulter et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739)) and a 150 cm circle [full text] ([Stewart et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866438)). In human intracranial recordings, theta during encoding was stronger for targets near boundaries, an effect specific to the subiculum, F(2,34) = 4.71, p = 0.016 [full text] ([Lee et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5884460)). The 56 to 69% coverage in 1.5 to 3.5 m spaces therefore cannot be extended to tens of metres, and whether subicular firing grows, fragments or stays fixed in a large space is untested.

## A third to a half of CA1 fires in any one environment

In a standard small environment, roughly 20 to 50% of dorsal CA1 pyramidal cells have place fields and most of the rest are silent or nearly so. The estimate holds across methods but depends heavily on the denominator and the criterion. Thompson and Best isolated pyramidal cells under anaesthesia and then recorded them awake. **Nearly two thirds were silent in the test environment**, and over 92% of the spontaneously active pyramidal cells were place cells in at least one environment. Reanaesthesia and antidromic stimulation confirmed that the silent cells were still being recorded [abstract] ([Thompson & Best 1989](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-07-02382.1989)). In novel and familiar environments, "less than half of the neurons in areas CA1 and CA3 are active whereas the rest are essentially silent", and CA1 fired about twice as many spikes in novel environments [abstract] ([Karlsson & Frank 2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4261-08.2008)). An early estimate was only an impression, that about 65% of well-isolated complex-spike units behaved as place cells, and its authors wrote that "a proper estimate will require additional work" [full text] ([Muller & Kubie 1987](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.07-07-01951.1987)). In tracks under 5 m or arenas under 1 m², about 20 to 50% of dorsal CA1 pyramidal cells become place cells, according to the literature summary of Rich et al. [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf)). The criterion alone can double the answer. In the same open-field data, **74.7% of CA1 cells had fields under a rate-only criterion and 37.3% when spatial coherence was added**, and on a 250 cm track the figures were 64.2% and 48.0% for CA1 and 31.0% and 16.8% for CA3 [full text] ([Mizuseki et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3718552/)). A 2025 analysis says published estimates range from 20% to 90% and argues that in a familiar environment all active pyramidal cells are place cells [abstract] ([Talpir et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.112489)).

Other methods agree roughly. Arc expression marked about 40% of CA1 pyramidal cells after exploration [abstract] ([Chawla et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20091)) and about 35% of CA1 against 18% of CA3 for one context [abstract] ([Vazdarjanova & Guzowski 2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0350-04.2004)). In mice imaged on an 84 cm track, 31 ± 1% of tracked cells were active per session, about 20% had a field in either direction and 12 ± 1% per direction, and each field was active on only 17 ± 14% of passes [full text] ([Ziv et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3784308)). Two-photon imaging on a virtual track found 15 to 20% place cells, 91% of them with one field [full text] ([Dombeck et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2648)). Of 9 classifiable whole-cell recordings in a novel arena, 4 cells had place fields and 5 were silent [full text] ([Epsztein, Brecht & Lee 2011](https://www.inmed.fr/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/11-epsztein_brecht_lee_2011.pdf)).

The regional ordering is consistent. Per environment, about 88% of hilar mossy cells, 29% of CA3 cells and **9% of granule cells** were active in rats, and 25 of 37 granule cells, 6 of 28 CA3 cells and 1 of 23 hilar cells were silent in all four rooms. Sleep rates were 0.10, 0.13 and 0.70 Hz for granule, CA3 and hilar cells. Cells had to fire in both baseline sleep sessions to be counted, so silent cells were included [full text] ([GoodSmith et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5300955/)). In rat granule cell layer recordings, 88% of cells fired below 0.5 Hz [abstract] ([Jung & McNaughton 1993](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450030209)). Arc mapping across many environments suggests that "possibly 90-95%" of granule cells are effectively retired [abstract] ([Alme et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20810)), and imaged mouse granule cells produced about one calcium event every 2 to 5 min [abstract] ([Pilz et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3065-15.2016)). CA2 had 68.2% of cells active per session against 59.2% in CA1 and 48.1% in CA3 [full text] ([Mankin et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4392894/)).

Recruitment grows with environment size without a clear ceiling, but slowly. On the 48 m track, with silent cells counted through flanking sleep sessions, **recruitment reached only about 65% of CA1 cells at 48 m**, 46 to 77% across rats. The fitted distribution matched the recruitment curve, KS = 0.05, P = 0.48, and its extrapolation reaches 90% at about 500 m [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf)). The fitted parameters predict 0.14^0.57 = 0.33 of cells with no field on 48 m, close to the observed 35% [derived]. Some cells silent on the track formed fields in a second novel environment [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf)). In a box 5.6 times the area of a cylinder, the active subset grew in every region, p < 0.001 [full text] ([Park, Dvorak & Fenton 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0022349)). In bats, only 36 of 67 CA1 cells, 53.7%, were active in flight in a 6 m tunnel, while all 235 cells in the 200 m dataset were [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)), and more than 80% of both CA1 and CA3 cells were place cells in the 200 m tunnel [full text] ([Maimon et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)). The bat denominators may exclude cells that never fired, which would make them larger than Rich's sleep-verified fractions [inference].

The share of cells active at any one location is small. Across four arenas up to 8.75 m², about **15% of place cells fired above 1 Hz at a given location**, with a mean rate of 0.52 Hz per place cell, and both values were constant across location and environment [full text] ([Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)). Multiplying place-cell fraction by field coverage gives about 3% of imaged CA1 cells per point and direction on the 84 cm track, 4 to 6% on the virtual track, and about 14% of CA1 against 3% of CA3 on the 250 cm track, ignoring multiple fields and directionality [derived]. Granule cells must be well under 1% [inference].

Silence belongs to a cell in an environment, not to the cell permanently. In whole-cell recordings, silent directions still received a subthreshold depolarization of 2.9 ± 0.3 mV against 12.8 ± 2.8 mV in place-field directions, place cells had lower spike thresholds, and future place cells fired more of their spikes in bursts in response to current steps before the animal entered the maze, 0.80 against 0.24 [full text] ([Epsztein, Brecht & Lee 2011](https://www.inmed.fr/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/11-epsztein_brecht_lee_2011.pdf)). A small uniform depolarization of a silent CA1 cell during behaviour made a place field appear, reversibly [abstract] ([Lee, Lin & Lee 2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1221489)). Slow-wave-sleep rate before exposure predicted later field count, r = 0.45 [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf)), and the same neurons' log rates correlated between two mazes at r = 0.65 in CA1 and 0.59 in CA3 [full text] ([Mizuseki & Buzsáki 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2013.07.039)). Across 11 rooms, 342 CA3 cells were analysed, of which 210 were active in at least one room, 6% in six or more rooms and under 3% in eight or more, from search snippets of the paper and a reanalysis preprint [unverified; preprint] ([Alme et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421056111); [eleven-room reanalysis](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.15.623794v3.full)). If each cell activated independently in each room at the mean rate, about 80% would be active somewhere and 0.15% in six or more rooms, so the observed counts show both more never-active cells and more multi-room cells than independence predicts [derived from unverified counts]. Within one familiar environment imaged over 45 days, 57 ± 1% of cells ever active were active in only one or two of ten sessions, 2.8% in all ten, and the place-cell sets of two sessions overlapped by about 25% at 5 days and 15% at 30 days [full text] ([Ziv et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3784308)).

## Dorsal similarity curves reach baseline within one field width, ventral CA3 only after about two mean field widths

A population vector lists every recorded cell's rate at one position, and the similarity between two positions is usually a Pearson correlation or a normalized dot product of their vectors. Most papers show the full matrix of position pairs, where a diagonal band marks high similarity and the band's width is the length scale. Few report the curve against separation, and fewer report the value far from the diagonal. The table lists every within-environment measurement found.

The kernel is not one width per environment. Its band narrows where local cues change, "especially in CA3" [full text] ([Wang, Monaco & Knierim 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259364/)), on cue-rich tracks [full text] ([Battaglia, Sutherland & McNaughton 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6729394/)), and near walls, where population change on runs perpendicular to the wall peaked close to it, H = 12.2, p = 0.007, with no effect on runs parallel to it. Across a virtual replica of the largest arena, the rate of population change correlated with the rate of visual change at r = 0.60, partial r = 0.51, and with running speed at r = -0.30 [full text] ([Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)). One global length fitted to a whole environment therefore averages over narrow and wide regions.

The case for broader kernels in ventral hippocampus rests on one measurement. The sentence usually cited, that dorsal population activity "becomes decorrelated over small distances" while ventral activity "remains correlated over much larger distances", comes from an introduction citing Maurer 2005, Kjelstrup 2008, Royer 2010 and Komorowski 2013 [full text] ([Keinath et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627/)). Two of them measured population decorrelation. Kjelstrup et al. measured it in intermediate and ventral CA3 on an 18 m track, as in the table above [full text] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)), and Maurer et al. compared the dorsal pole with the middle hippocampus [abstract] ([Maurer et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16145692/)). A 2021 review says ventral place-cell activity "will change more slowly", but it presents this as following from the authors' own proposal and cites no measurement, and for field size it cites only Jung et al. 1994 [full text, author manuscript] ([Maurer & Nadel 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2020.12.007)). A Europe PMC full-text search for PV correlation with ventral or intermediate hippocampus and distance returned no other such study, and no other ventral or intermediate PV-versus-distance curve was found in any species [full text search] ([Europe PMC query](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(%22population%20vector%20correlation%22%20OR%20%22population%20vector%20correlations%22)%20AND%20(%22ventral%20CA1%22%20OR%20%22ventral%20CA3%22%20OR%20%22intermediate%20hippocampus%22%20OR%20%22temporal%20hippocampus%22%20OR%20%22ventral%20hippocampal%20place%22)%20AND%20distance&format=json&pageSize=60&resultType=lite)). The ventral ensembles that do show generalization are tiny and organized by context. After overtraining, ventral CA3 ensembles of 2 to 5 cells were more similar across the two locations within a context than dorsal ensembles of 6 to 16 cells, t(6) = -7.41 [full text] ([Komorowski et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3667351)), and ventral CA3 cells fired alike toward shared goals and on open against closed arms [full text] ([Royer et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2825159/)). On the 18 m track the ventral curve declines smoothly between 1 and 8 m rather than staying flat, which looks graded by distance, but the whole track was one context, so that dataset cannot separate metric distance from context membership [inference].

Altered self-motion is the only real-data route found to a broad dorsal kernel. On a ring of about 408 cm, the distance at which PV overlap fell to 0.5 was about three times larger when rats rode a small vehicle than when they walked, and when rats sat still while the room rotated, "most cells that were active on the track fired over most of the track". The same cells gave the narrow kernel under normal walking [full text, values figure only] ([Terrazas et al. 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6725460)). This study's overlap measure is not mean-subtracted, so its far-distance floor cannot reach zero while cells fire anywhere [inference].

Other regions show the same short kernel or have not been measured. Retrosplenial cortex resembled CA1 [full text] ([Mao et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557927)). For medial entorhinal and retrosplenial border cells, PV correlation decayed at similar rates over 0 to 20 cm of distance from the wall, but that axis is distance from a wall, not separation between two positions [full text] ([van Wijngaarden et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7609058)). A reanalysis of mouse medial entorhinal Neuropixels data on a virtual track run over a hundred metres found a decreasing trend that was "not truly monotonic", without grid-module identity [preprint] ([Dasbehera, Dogra & Redman 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08292)). In macaques, a 2026 study plotted PV correlation against task progression rather than distance [full text] ([Yan et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13276072)), and a full-text search of the marmoset paper found no PV-distance analysis [full text search] ([Courellis et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922474/)). No lateral septum, lateral entorhinal or CA2 curve was found.

Among environments longer than a few metres, only the 18 m track has a measured PV curve, the Kjelstrup result in the table above, and its dorsal CA3 curve is as local as the short-track curves. The 48 m, 200 m and 18.6 m² studies leave the question open. None of the 18 supplementary figures of the 48 m study is a population analysis; its only cross-cell correlation figure shows that field counts of cell pairs across track segments are uncorrelated [full text] ([Rich et al. 2014 supplement](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf)). The bat tunnel paper decoded only synthetic data [full text] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)), and the 18.6 m² study has no PV analysis [full text] ([Harland et al. 2021, published PDF](https://cenl.ucsd.edu/pubs/Harland-contreras-souder-fellous+CA1-Multiscale-Megaspace+CurrBio+2021.pdf)). In bats in tunnels of 130 to 194 m, PVs for the same position after a straight approach and after a turn reached a correlation of 0.8 only 91.5 m past the junction in one bat, and the history effect "sometimes reached as far as 130 m". Those PVs were peak-normalized per cell and smoothed with a 10 m boxcar, which would hide any spatial kernel narrower than a few metres [full text] ([Maimon et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)). On a 200 cm track in developing rats, PV matrices were analysed for offsets and displacement, not for a decorrelation length [full text] ([Bjerknes et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5816199)).

Human data show graded similarity over very large distances, but for remembered places. In 9 participants viewing lifelogging images taken 100 m to 30 km apart, left anterior hippocampal pattern distance scaled with log distance, β = 0.71, t = 5.55, corrected P = 0.021, and no other region survived correction [full text] ([Nielson et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4568259)). In 26 participants who learned objects in a virtual city about 390 m across, the change in hippocampal pattern similarity correlated with remembered distance, Z = -3.72, and with true distance, Z = -2.85 [full text] ([Deuker et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5053807)). Left hippocampal fMRI adaptation tracked real distances between campus landmarks [abstract] ([Morgan et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4667-10.2011)). In human single units, ensemble similarity between recall and navigation was highest near the recalled item's location, intermediate at middle distances and lowest far away, F(2,300) = 7.6, with only three distance bins [full text, annotated copy] ([Miller et al. 2013](https://www.scienceintheclassroom.org/research-papers/original-gps-how-we-remember-what-happened-where)). An intracranial EEG study found the opposite sign, with cue representations for subjectively similar goal locations more distinct, t21 = -2.09, P = 0.049 [full text] ([Kunz et al. 2019](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6609163/fullTextXML)). These studies test retrieval with log-distance regressors and voxel-level pooling, so they cannot separate one broad kernel from many narrow kernels of different widths [inference].

Three methodological points govern any comparison. The far-distance floor depends on construction. Per-cell z-scoring, as in Green et al., forces each cell's rates to sum to zero and pulls the floor to zero or just below, while peak-normalized or raw rates leave a positive floor when cells differ in mean rate [inference]. On the 18 m track, the intermediate and ventral curves dip below zero far from the diagonal, to about -0.03 to -0.13 beyond 12 m, and the main text states no rate normalization [full text, figure-read] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). Between-environment values are not the far tail of a within-environment kernel. Including silent cells, median PV correlations at the same location were 0.91 in CA3 and 0.83 in CA1 for repeated conditions and **0.03 in CA3 and 0.25 in CA1 between two rooms** [full text] ([Leutgeb et al. 2005](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb05.pdf)), and within-environment correlations two weeks apart stayed higher than same-day correlations between environments [full text] ([Rubin et al. 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4749549/fullTextXML)). Smoothing widens the measured peak, so scales below about two smoothing SDs cannot be resolved [inference]. No paper found fits a functional form, Gaussian or exponential, to a measured hippocampal PV-versus-distance curve, and none reports a numeric far-distance value with its construction stated.

## CA3 maps for different rooms overlap no more than random draws

Different environments get codes that are close to independent in CA3 and the dentate gyrus and partly shared in CA1. The classic single-cell work set this up. Firing in a circular enclosure "could not be predicted from a knowledge of the firing pattern" in a rectangular one. Of 18 to 22 cells recorded in each cylinder-rectangle pair, only 1, in the pair of small enclosures, kept a related field, although the text calls such cells "missing". Removing the cue card rotated fields to unpredictable angles while their size and shape "most often appeared unchanged", but peak rotational correlation with the standard session fell from 0.884 for repeated sessions to 0.680, P < 0.01 [full text] ([Muller & Kubie 1987](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.07-07-01951.1987)). Whether two similar enclosures get separate maps depends on experience. When a white cue card was swapped for a black one, most cells at first kept their fields, but once a rat's maps had diverged, every cell recorded from that rat afterwards fired differently under the two cards [abstract] ([Bostock, Muller & Kubie 1991](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450010207)). A later review describes each environment as recruiting an apparently random subset of about 20% of pyramidal cells, with subsets for two environments behaving as independent samples [secondary] ([Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160)).

Ensemble recordings quantified the independence. In 10 rats with 146 CA3 and 244 CA1 cells, active-set overlap between two familiar rooms in CA3 was **0.11 to 0.14, against 0.12 to 0.14 expected for independent firing**, and a novel room gave 0.16 against 0.14. CA1 overlap between identical boxes in two rooms was 0.55 against 0.63 and 0.66 for repeat tests in the same room, fell to 0.43 for squares of different size, and reached 0.32 against 0.28 expected for boxes differing in size and shape. In a novel room CA1 overlap was 0.38 against 0.24 expected. The population carried 0.54 bits about room identity in CA3 and 0.30 in CA1 [full text] ([Leutgeb et al. 2004](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/~kempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb04.pdf)). With silent cells included, median PV correlations at the same location between two rooms were 0.03 in CA3 and 0.25 in CA1. Changing the box while keeping its location changed rates, often by more than tenfold, while fields stayed put [full text] ([Leutgeb et al. 2005](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb05.pdf)). The largest test recorded CA3 in 11 boxes of nearly identical geometry in 11 rooms, giving 55 room pairs per animal. Maps were "highly correlated between repeated tests in the same room but remained orthogonal across all combinations of different rooms", and cells active in many rooms had "completely uncorrelated" firing locations [abstract] ([Alme et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421056111)). Sources disagree on this dataset's details. The paper reports 342 CA3 cells from 7 rats, a reanalysis says 5 rats, and the public deposit describes CA1 [secondary] ([NIRD archive record](https://archive.sigma2.no//dataset/D30E6693-C9A3-4D2E-AFBF-BD046054DF83)). In four rooms with differently shaped platforms, rate overlap was 0.15 for granule cells and 0.13 for CA3, both at shuffle level, and 0.31 for hilar mossy cells, above all shuffles. Only 4.4%, 4.2% and 3.1% of granule, hilar and CA3 cell pairs had rate-map correlations above the shuffled 95th percentile, so even mossy cells active in every room placed their fields at unrelated locations [full text] ([GoodSmith et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5300955/)).

Calcium imaging and virtual reality give the same picture for CA1. Between a 400 cm and a 250 cm virtual track, the CA1 PV correlation was **0.099**, 95% CI 0.094 to 0.103, and when reward moved on one track, 7.9% of all cells had fields in both conditions that moved to "apparently random locations" [full text] ([Gauthier & Tank 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.06.008)). On 450 cm virtual tracks morphed between two extremes, cells with significant spatial information in both extremes were 6.07% against 4.78% expected by chance in mice rarely shown intermediate morphs, and 1.98% against 1.06% in mice often shown them [full text] ([Plitt & Giocomo 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00816-6)). Between a straight and an L-shaped track, PV correlations were low and stable over 15 days, and only about 7% of cells were active on all days [full text] ([Rubin et al. 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4749549/fullTextXML)). Ensembles for two contexts overlapped more when explored 5 h apart than 7 days apart, and an immediate-early-gene tag showed above-chance overlap at 5 h but not at 7 days [full text, overlap values figure only] ([Cai et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17955)).

The "same environment" baseline is itself well below 1 and falls with time. Overlap of CA1 place-cell sets between two imaging sessions in one environment was about 25% at 5 days and 15% at 30 days [full text] ([Ziv et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3784308)). CA1 activity for a repeated event grew more different over hours to days, while CA3 stayed reproducible [abstract] ([Mankin et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1214107109)). The CA2 same-shape PV correlation fell with elapsed time to a plateau of about 0.35 after 18 h, and CA2 did not distinguish the two shapes at all [full text] ([Mankin et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4392894/)). In small arenas, some session pairs within one arena showed global remapping, up to 36% of pairs in one mouse, and about half of each population typically rotated coherently between sessions [full text] ([Kinsky et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.09.037)). Mouse CA1 can keep several distinct stable maps of one familiar context, switching only between visits [abstract] ([Sheintuch et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.02.018)).

Regions differ in how far they orthogonalize. Along the CA3 transverse axis, the ability to form uncorrelated maps for different environments falls toward CA2, with the sharpest drop in distal CA3 [abstract] ([Lu et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.07.007)), and proximal CA3 resembled dentate pattern separation under cue conflict while distal CA3 and CA2 stayed coherent [abstract] ([Lee et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.07.012)). In the dentate gyrus, small shape changes altered correlated activity among granule cells, while more different environments recruited new CA3 cells but not new dentate cells [abstract] ([Leutgeb et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135801)). In mice, a remapping index of at least 0.2 marked 36.7% of granule place cells, 61.3% of mossy cells and 55.6% of CA3 cells, and population remapping was smallest for granule cells, P < 0.00001 [full text] ([Senzai & Buzsáki 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5293146/)). Imaged granule-cell maps were more similar between two virtual contexts than CA1 or CA2/3 maps and stayed stable over 5 days, while CA2/3 correlations fell below chance within two days [full text] ([Hainmueller & Bartos 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0191-2)). Another imaging study reported "robust remapping" of mature granule cells [abstract] ([Danielson et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.02.019)). Granule cells therefore seem to separate environments mainly by which few cells are active, rather than by moving fields within a shared set [inference]. Upstream, rate remapping in the hippocampus came with stable grid fields, and global remapping came with a coherent shift and rotation of co-localized grid cells [abstract] ([Fyhn et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05601)). Non-grid medial entorhinal cells reorganized completely after shape or colour changes while grid cells kept their alignment [full text] ([Diehl et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444540/)). Lateral entorhinal lesions impaired CA3 rate remapping [abstract] ([Lu et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3462)). About a third of lateral septal cells encoded matching locations on separate days in one arena, and their patterns in two arenas became more similar with experience, while hippocampal cells kept distinct fields [abstract] ([Leutgeb & Mizumori 2002](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0306-4522(02)00101-X)). The subicular results are described in the subiculum section. In human high-resolution fMRI, left CA3/DG distinguished two virtual cities differing only in two store locations, while CA1 distinguished only a fully distinct city [full text] ([Kyle et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.10499)). Kjelstrup et al. state that ventral CA3 "still had strongly orthogonalized place maps" for discrete environments, though left and right runs on the 18 m track were more correlated ventrally than dorsally, and the values are in a supplement that was not read [full text, note 33] ([Kjelstrup et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)). No ventral hippocampal study reporting a cross-environment PV or rate-map correlation in its main text was found, and no bat, macaque or bird study.

What tips remapping from rate or partial to global is mostly known. Moving the same box to a new place gave global remapping, and changing the box in place gave rate remapping [full text] ([Leutgeb et al. 2005](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb05.pdf)). Enclosures differing in shape, colour and texture together drove remapping in 92% of CA1 cells, 48 of 52, and in one rat 17 of 20 cells switched together at the same step of a morph sequence, p < 10⁻¹¹ [full text] ([Wills et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1108905)). Shape alone produced gradual divergence over repeated exposure [abstract] ([Lever et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/416090a)), and stepwise morphing between a familiar square and circle gave a "gradual noncoherent progression" in CA3 and CA1 [abstract] ([Leutgeb et al. 2005, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.09.007)). Labs disagree on protocols that look alike: 18 days of alternating square and circle training gave complete remapping in one lab and partial remapping in another, and a 6-day protocol gave complete remapping in Wills et al. but partial remapping in a replication [secondary] ([Sanders, Wilson & Gershman 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51140); [Colgin et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00202.2010)). Mice rarely shown intermediate morphs kept one map until a morph value of about 0.25 to 0.5 and then switched together, while mice often shown them changed gradually [full text] ([Plitt & Giocomo 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00816-6)). Compound colour and odour contexts gave heterogeneous partial remapping [abstract] ([Anderson & Jeffery 2003](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-26-08827.2003)), and visually identical compartments produced repeated fields above chance [abstract] ([Skaggs & McNaughton 1998](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-20-08455.1998); [Spiers et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bht198)).

Codes for different environments still share structure at the level of cells. The same neurons' log firing rates correlated between two mazes at **r = 0.65 in CA1 and 0.59 in CA3**, and burst index at 0.63 and 0.57 [full text] ([Mizuseki & Buzsáki 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2013.07.039)). Field propensity is preserved across environments and months [abstract] ([Lee et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33035454/)). Reward coding "consistently arose from a single pool of cells" across virtual environments, 0.8% of all recorded cells in the reward-shift condition [full text] ([Gauthier & Tank 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.06.008)). A decoder trained in one environment identified the recording day from the other environment's data in 65% of sessions against 12.5% chance, so slow drift is shared [full text] ([Rubin et al. 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4749549/fullTextXML)). In small arenas, mice "often employed a coherent map to represent both arenas", and some apparent global remapping may be a coherent rotation of one map [full text] ([Kinsky et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.09.037)). A review notes that "many studies reporting global remapping report low but non-zero correlations" [secondary] ([Sanders, Wilson & Gershman 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51140)). PV correlations computed on raw rates will pick up conserved rates even when field positions are random, so "near zero" and "non-zero" reports often differ in method more than in biology [inference]. The most maps shown in one animal is 11, all mutually orthogonal, with no sign of saturation, but about 30 active cells per animal limits the power to detect small residual correlations [abstract; inference] ([Alme et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421056111)). A reanalysis preprint supports independence across rooms but suggests possible clusters of cells by activity level [preprint] ([Jain & Elliott 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14559)).

## Decoders reach 5 to 12 cm whatever the environment size

Across rodent CA1 studies in spaces from 0.5 m to 10.3 m, median or root-mean-square position errors cluster at about 5 to 12 cm with tens to hundreds of cells, so relative error shrinks as the environment grows.

Error falls with ensemble size in every study that varied it, and the largest imaging dataset suggests a floor. Fitting inverse mean squared error against ensemble size, Hazon et al. found **saturation at 346 to 1,402 CA1 neurons** depending on the animal, which bounds error at about 10 cm, "the size of a mouse". With noise correlations removed by trial shuffling, "the error decreased without apparent bound". Sessions held at most about 500 neurons, so saturation is an extrapolation, and the error floor was lower at the end of the track than at its start, p = 2 × 10⁻²⁹ [full text] ([Hazon et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31254-y)). In an open field the sign reversed. Destroying correlations increased error in 4 of 6 animals, mostly in CA1, and position could be decoded from non-place cells alone [full text] ([Stefanini et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7442694)). The two studies differ in decoder, shuffle and environment, so the effect of correlations is unsettled [inference]. On the same rat data, a recurrent network beat the best Bayesian decoder by 17.9% in median error, and error rose for windows shorter or longer than about 1 to 1.4 s [full text, percentage derived] ([Tampuu et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006822)).

Regional comparisons depend on the decoder and on firing rates. Subiculum matches or beats CA1 at 100 or more cells, as described in the subiculum section. Dorsal and ventral mouse CA1 gave equal reconstruction at matched population size in one study [full text] ([Keinath et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627/)) and coarser ventral maps in another [full text] ([Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021](https://www.eneuro.org/content/8/5/ENEURO.0211-21.2021)). Lateral septal position decoded better from theta phase than from rate in 62 of 62 sessions, and in hippocampal place fields phase carried more information at scales below 6 cm while rate carried more above 10 cm [full text] ([Tingley & Buzsáki 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6605060/)). In marmosets, decoded trajectories explained 64.0 ± 13.8% of variance, and decoding was significant in 34 of 37 sessions [full text] ([Courellis et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922474/)). Macaque position decoding in a virtual maze did not generalize across tasks [abstract] ([Gulli et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0548-3)), and human fMRI discriminated four virtual positions above chance in posterior hippocampus [full text] ([Hassabis et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.02.033)). In flying bats, 42% of spatially selective CA1 neurons carried negligible information about current position and were tuned to past or future positions, which current-position decoders miss [full text] ([Dotson & Yartsev 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg1278)). No study reports how often place-cell decoders jump to distant positions in multi-field or very large environments.

## Spatial activity lies on curved manifolds of one to six dimensions

Nonlinear estimates of intrinsic dimension for hippocampal spatial activity are low, while linear methods need many more components. In mouse CA1 on a linear track, activity split into discrete states, running in each direction, turning and drinking, and within each running state the intrinsic dimension was 1, with "one component, no holes, and no spaces". PCA did not reveal these states, and the structure was conserved across mice [full text] ([Rubin et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12724-2)). With both directions included, linear-track activity forms a ring, with Betti numbers 1, 1 and 0 [full text] ([Schneider, Lee & Mathis 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06031-6)), or "three-dimensional ring manifolds" whose deep-layer and superficial-layer parts transform differently when the track is rotated [abstract] ([Esparza et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.01.022)). A reanalysis of 225 CA1 cells on a circular track found intrinsic dimension "around 2" under seven embedding methods, with the linear ones highest [full text] ([Mitchell-Heggs et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10827-022-00839-3)).

Two-dimensional and task-rich settings give 3 to 6 dimensions. The Grassberger-Procaccia correlation dimension was **3.07 ± 1.32 for CA1 and 5.24 ± 0.70 for subiculum in a T-maze, and 3.61 ± 1.10 and 5.84 ± 0.79 in an open field**, on manifolds "homoeomorphic to external space" whose geometry was shared across rats [full text] ([Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)). In 7 mice with 449 ± 64 CA1 neurons per session in a virtual evidence-accumulation T-maze, the number of manifold states grew with radius at exponent **5.4, interval 4.8 to 6.0**, held-out reconstruction saturated at 5 to 6 dimensions, and PCA needed 29, 40 and 47 components to match 4, 5 and 6 nonlinear dimensions [full text] ([Nieh et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03652-7)). On rat CA1 data, manifold distances correlated with behavioural distances at 0.70 ± 0.06 in random foraging and 0.55 ± 0.16 in a sound task, against 0.23 for PCA, and the manifold carried an extra dimension beyond the measured behavioural variables [preprint] ([Low et al. 2018](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/418939v1.full)). Pairwise-correlation structure in CA1, including on the 48 m track, matched points sampled from a three-dimensional hyperbolic geometry and not a Euclidean one, and the hyperbolic size grew with the logarithm of exploration time [full text] ([Zhang, Rich, Lee & Sharpee 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01212-4)). Clique topology found geometric organization in CA1 correlations during open-field exploration, wheel running and REM sleep alike [abstract] ([Giusti et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1506407112)). Dorsal CA1 had higher PCA dimensionality than ventral CA1 on a virtual track [full text] ([Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021](https://www.eneuro.org/content/8/5/ENEURO.0211-21.2021)).

Activity in an open arena is therefore organized by position and homeomorphic to it, but measured intrinsic dimension is above 2. Non-spatial variables, estimator bias from noise and finite sampling, and curved geometry could each explain the excess, and the data cannot yet separate them [inference]. For contrast, grid modules of 66 to 189 Neuropixels-recorded cells lie on a two-dimensional torus that six principal components suffice to carry [full text] ([Gardner et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04268-7)), and head-direction populations lie on a one-dimensional ring in waking and REM sleep [abstract] ([Chaudhuri et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0460-x)). No participation ratio or cross-validated eigenspectrum exponent of the kind reported for mouse visual cortex [unverified] ([Stringer et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1346-5)) was found for hippocampal spatial activity. No dataset measures how linear dimension grows with environment size or number of positions, though a theory preprint argues that it is bounded by task extent divided by the smoothness of the population response [preprint] ([Gao et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1101/214262)).

## Theory ties place-like units to non-negative, energy-limited firing

This section summarizes what theoretical papers claim. It does not test any of them against the data above.

The central theoretical point is that an objective defined on the population leaves the single-unit basis undetermined. Tuning determines representational geometry, but geometry does not determine tuning, because different sets of tuning curves can give the same pairwise distances, and geometry alone fixes Fisher information, mutual information and ideal-observer performance [abstract] ([Kriegeskorte & Wei 2021](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2104.09743)). One paper that learned place-like units from an objective mapping closeness in space to closeness in representation states that the objective is invariant to orthogonal transformations, and that rotating a trained map gives a new map with the same similarity structure that is decorrelated from the original, which the authors equate with global remapping [abstract] ([Pettersen, Rogge & Lepperød 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96470)).

Theory papers break this freedom with constraints on single units. Whittington et al. argue that non-negative activity together with energy efficiency in activity and weights makes each neuron represent a single factor of variation. Mixing two factors in one neuron needs a larger bias to keep it non-negative over all factor values, which costs more energy. They report that sparsity alone does not produce this, and that factors entangled in the world stay entangled in the representation [full text, first half read] ([Whittington et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01768)). A follow-up argues that the condition for single-variable modules concerns the range of the variables' joint support, which is much weaker than statistical independence [abstract] ([Dorrell et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06232v2)). With rectifying neurons, a similarity-preserving objective on data from a symmetric manifold has localized receptive fields that tile the manifold as its optimum, which the authors present as a model of place cells; reviewers questioned how far this generalizes [abstract and reviews] ([Sengupta et al. 2018](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/hash/ee14c41e92ec5c97b54cf9b74e25bd99-Abstract.html)). Dorrell et al. combine a separation loss weighted by a spatial kernel, non-negativity, bounded activity and a requirement that movement act on the code as a fixed matrix. The kernel term alone favours the lowest-frequency, place-like code, and adding a finite occupancy range pushes toward a ring of frequencies whose densest packing is a hexagonal grid. They predict module orientation offsets of about 3 to 8° and a neuron count per module that scales with the square of lattice scale over field width [full text, first half read] ([Dorrell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15563)). An orthogonal rotation of a non-negative code generally produces negative rates, so non-negativity removes most rotational freedom while leaving permutation and positive rescaling [inference].

The same constraints recur in work deriving grid cells from place-like inputs. Non-negative principal component analysis of place-cell-like inputs gave hexagonal grids, and removing non-negativity gave square ones [abstract] ([Dordek et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4841785)). Pattern-formation analyses of networks trained to path integrate identify non-negativity as the symmetry-breaking constraint for hexagons [abstract] ([Sorscher et al. 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/6e7d5d259be7bf56ed79029c4e621f44-Abstract.html)) and report that centre-surround place-cell readouts are also needed [abstract] ([Sorscher et al. 2023](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10513491)). A critique found that grid-like units appear only under a narrow set of design choices [abstract] ([Schaeffer, Khona & Fiete 2022](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/66808849a9f5d8e2d00dbdc844de6333-Paper-Conference.pdf)), and a later preprint calls the unified account "at best an occasionally suggestive description" of trained networks [preprint] ([Schaeffer et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.03954)).

Normative accounts make different claims about field size and number. The successor-representation account holds that a place cell's firing is proportional to expected discounted future occupancy, so fields skew backward along habitual routes, spread within well-connected regions but not past barriers, and could span a range of scales if discounting varies along the long axis [abstract and author interview] ([Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman 2017](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/097170v2.full.pdf)). A compression account produces place-like units from compressing correlated sensory input and predicts that field fluctuations carry recent history [abstract] ([Benna & Fusi 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10338047)). Coding analyses state that the resolution of a single-field place code grows only linearly with neuron number, while nested periodic codes grow exponentially [abstract] ([Mathis, Herz & Stemmler 2012](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/14814/)), and that Fisher information per neuron scales with tuning width to the power D minus 2, so narrow fields help in one dimension, width does not matter in two, and broad fields help above two [abstract and secondary] ([Zhang & Sejnowski 1999](https://mlanthology.org/neco/1999/zhang1999neco-neuronal)). A preprint casts subicular boundary, corner and trajectory responses as successor features with a longer predictive horizon than CA1, a horizon inferred from fitting, not measured [preprint] ([Bennett et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11580870)).

Multi-field statistics have their own theory. One account gives each cell an independent realization of a random Gaussian process and makes the cell fire wherever its realization exceeds a threshold, with two parameters per dataset, a correlation length and a threshold. It reports matching field statistics in bats and rodents in one, two and three dimensions, all from dorsal CA1. It suggests that synaptic projections into CA1 are predominantly random, on the strength of the fit and a central-limit argument, with no connectivity data. Its fitted correlation length grows sublinearly with environment size, with log-log slopes of 0.24 in bat tunnels and 0.53 in rat arenas, and its fitted threshold falls. The paper has no population-level analysis [full text; slopes printed in a figure; supplement not read] ([Mainali, Azeredo da Silveira & Burak 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.01.017)). Eliav et al. decoded synthetic populations and report that the multiscale code is more precise than single-field codes in very large spaces but not in small ones [full text; numeric advantages unverified] ([Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)). Press coverage adds that a single-field code would need 10¹³ to 10¹⁵ neurons for kilometre-scale routes [secondary, press only] ([News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20210601/Study-reveals-a-novel-neuronal-code-for-spatial-perception.aspx)). On capacity, one analysis argues that remapping is limited by place-code sparseness rather than by the capacity of the grid-to-place projection [abstract] ([Kammerer & Leibold 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003986)). Four disagreements remain open: whether grid emergence in trained networks is principled or tuned, whether non-negativity plus energy or rectification plus similarity matching is the minimal recipe for interpretable units, what sets field width in two dimensions where Fisher information is indifferent, and whether multi-field codes are optimized or random. Mainali et al. do not treat the last as a strict choice, because random Gaussian-process codes can have capacity that grows exponentially with neuron number [full text]. No theory paper found predicts the shape of the population similarity kernel and compares it with measured data.

## Conclusion

The two questions have different evidentiary standing. Very large fields are well documented in absolute terms, but "fraction of the environment" is not a fixed property of a cell. It shrinks as the space grows, because dorsal fields grow roughly as tunnel length to the 0.36 power while cells add fields, so a field's share of the space says as much about the room as about the neuron. The cases where tuning covers a third or more of the space come with a different measured quantity each time, a ventral field length on a track barely longer than the field, a sparseness index, a spike-spread area, a theta-phase field. They are also exactly the cells that field caps and spatial-information thresholds tend to discard. The subiculum adds a result that changes how single-cell breadth should be read. Its cells fire over twice the area of CA1 cells, yet its population similarity peak is narrower and its decoding at least as good at 100 or more cells. Broad single-unit tuning does not imply a broad population kernel in the subiculum. Along the CA3 long axis, field size and kernel width grew together, so single-cell field size tracks the population kernel in one region and fails to in another.

One study supports a population kernel that declines gradually across most of an environment under normal movement. In ventral rat CA3 on an 18 m track, PV correlation fell to 0.5 about 3.3 m from a position and reached zero only near 10.7 m, and in intermediate CA3 it reached zero near 7 m. No one has replicated that result, its vectors pool cells across rats and sessions, and the track's ends may truncate the ventral fields. No dorsal recording shows such a kernel, including dorsal CA3 on the same track, and no bat recording has measured one. The best indirect hints, abnormal self-motion and human memory retrieval, test different things. The missing measurement is concrete. A PV-against-separation curve on the 48 m track and the 8.75 m² arena, with the construction stated, should report z-scored against raw rates, whether silent cells are included, the smoothing used, and the numeric far-distance value. Simultaneous ventral recordings on tracks longer than 18 m, analysed away from the ends, would show whether the ventral kernel is set by the region or by the track. Large-environment recordings in subiculum would test the other region where broad kernels are assumed. Until those exist, gradual fall-off across metres is an observation for intermediate and ventral CA3 on one track and an extrapolation everywhere else.
