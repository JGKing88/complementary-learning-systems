# Changelog: full-text corrections to the place-field review

Applied on 2026-11-15 to `docs/LITERATURE_PLACE_FIELDS_POPULATION_SIMILARITY.md`. The changes come from `fulltext_large_fields.md` (Kjelstrup et al. 2008, Muller & Kubie 1987, O'Keefe & Burgess 1996, Maurer & Nadel 2021; 33 proposed changes plus Part 3), `fulltext_subiculum.md` (Sharp & Green 1994, Olson et al. 2017, Lever et al. 2009; 11 changes) and `fulltext_mainali.md` (Mainali et al. 2025; 3 changes and one note). Section names below are the new headings. Values read off figures carry the new tag [figure-read], which the tag legend now defines.

## (a) Changes to the two key answers

### Second question: does a population kernel decline gradually across most of an environment?

The answer changed from "no" to a qualified yes that rests on one study. Kjelstrup et al. 2008 Fig. 3 is a PV autocorrelogram on an 18 m track. The authors stacked the rate maps of all CA3 pyramidal cells recorded at each longitudinal level, combining "data from multiple recordings", so each vector is a pseudo-population pooled across rats and sessions. Vectors were 5 cm bins, 330 per running direction, compared by Pearson correlation. The stated widths at r = 0.50, which the paper calls the half-width, are 0.81, 3.71 and 6.66 m for dorsal, intermediate and ventral CA3, and the widths at r = 0.20 are 1.41, 8.65 and 13.59 m (p. 141). The figure's marker lines span both sides of the diagonal, so these are full widths, and ventral correlation falls to 0.5 about 3.3 m from a position. Figure-read ventral values are about 0.70 at 2 m, 0.44 at 4 m, 0.27 at 6 m and 0.15 at 8 m, with zero near 10.7 m, about 65% of the 16.5 m analysed. Intermediate CA3 reaches zero near 7 m, and dorsal CA3 falls below 0.1 by about 1 m.

Every place that stated the old answer now agrees with the new one:

- The H1 headline, now "Hippocampal fields stretch far, and population similarity stays local except in ventral CA3".
- The opening paragraph.
- The section formerly "No measured population kernel declines gradually across a whole environment", now "Only one study, in ventral CA3, shows a population kernel declining across most of an environment". The first paragraph is restricted to curves outside intermediate and ventral CA3, a new paragraph describes the Kjelstrup analysis, the Keinath sentence is corrected, and the answer sentence is rewritten.
- The PV similarity row of the twelve-measures table.
- The section formerly "Every measured similarity curve reaches baseline within one field width", now "Dorsal similarity curves reach baseline within one field width, ventral CA3 only after about two". It has a new Kjelstrup table row and source link, a rewritten ventral paragraph, a rewritten opening for the large-environment paragraph, and a note on the negative far tail.
- The dorsoventral section, which now has a paragraph on the population estimate.
- The Conclusion.

The caveats appear explicitly wherever the answer is stated. The result comes from one study and has not been replicated. The vectors are a pooled pseudo-population across rats and sessions. The track ends may truncate ventral fields, so the track may set part of the ventral width. The main text gives no cell count per level and no rate normalization. Dorsal kernels are local everywhere else, including dorsal CA3 on the same track.

### First question: do single fields cover a large share of the environment?

The form of the answer is unchanged, yes in absolute terms and conditional in relative terms, but the ventral CA3 evidence is now full text. The per-level means, 0.98 ± 0.03, 3.73 ± 0.43 and 5.52 ± 0.54 m, are verbatim on p. 140. The study recorded 21 rats, and the means come from 5, 3 and 4 of them, 12 in all. Fields are "spatially stable contiguous regions with firing above 20% of the peak rate". The paper says "few fields were longer than 10 m". The longest field drawn in the paper, from a ventral cell at 83% of the long axis, ran about 15.7 m in one running direction, about 87% of the track [figure-read]. The aggregator-page citation is gone. The "about 10 m scale, about 56% of the track" entry is no longer presented as a field measure, because it is the authors' summary of scale.

## (b) Corrections of values or claims that were wrong

- The statement that no ventral or intermediate PV-against-distance curve exists was wrong (Kjelstrup Fig. 3). The Europe PMC search sentence now says "no other such study".
- Twelve-measures PV row and second-question text. "No curve for spaces above a 4 m loop" was wrong, because Kjelstrup has dorsal, intermediate and ventral curves on an 18 m track. The notes' proposed replacements (changes 2, 13 and 16) said no dorsal curve exists beyond a 4 m loop, which is also wrong for dorsal CA3, so the report now says "dorsal CA1".
- The Keinath et al. 2014 trace said only Maurer 2005 among the four cited papers measured population decorrelation. Kjelstrup 2008 did too, in ventral CA3.
- "Broad single-cell tuning does not produce a broad population kernel" was stated generally. It now applies to the subiculum, and the text notes that field size and kernel width grew together along the CA3 long axis. The Conclusion changed to match.
- First table. The row "Rat CA3, ventral pole, 'Scale' about 10 m, about 56%" treated the authors' summary as a field measure. It is replaced by the largest drawn field, about 15.7 m, 87% [figure-read].
- The dorsoventral heading "about tenfold" is now "five- to eightfold" (mean field 5.6-fold, PV half-width 8.2-fold, derived). The quoted abstract wording "approximately 10 meters" is printed as "~10 meters" in the PDF. The paragraph now quotes the Discussion, "<1 m ... to ~10 m".
- Muller & Kubie 1987. "52% changed their firing completely" was looser than the paper. The 52% combines 9 cells with a field in only one cylinder and 4 with unrelated fields, out of 25. "Without changing their size or shape" after card removal is now "most often appeared unchanged", with peak rotational correlation falling from 0.884 to 0.680, P < 0.01. Of the cylinder-rectangle pairs, Table 2 lists 1 related cell where the text says such cells are "missing".
- Lever et al. 2009. "Up to 24% of cells, 45 of 186" counts recordings of firing fields, not distinct cells, and the authors call it an upper bound. Barrier duplication rests on 3 cells, darkness on 3 cells and the wall-less platform on 14 of 17. The short-range and far-tuned statements carry no counts.
- Lever et al. 2009. The quoted phrase "showed much less remapping than CA1 place cells" does not occur in the paper. The closest sentence is "remap less than PCs in response to the same environmental differences". The CA1 values are an unpublished observation inside the paper, 0.803 within the square, 0.053 square to circle and -0.049 square to platform, not "about 0" in general. The paper gives no boundary-cell correlation and says many non-boundary subicular cells remapped.
- Olson et al. 2017. The press figure "about 10%" is 47 of 542, 8.7%. "73.9% of the track" is a selection score for the 47 axis-tuned cells, not the share of the track a cell fires over. The press framing "groups separate locations" is dropped.
- Mainali et al. 2025. "A single random Gaussian process" is now an independent realization per cell. "Concludes that inputs are predominantly random" is now "suggests", resting on the fit and a central-limit argument with no connectivity data.

## (d) New findings added

- Kjelstrup PV curve. The values listed in (a), the four limits, and a note that the ventral curve declines with no plateau between 1 and 8 m. At the intermediate and ventral levels the zero crossing sits at about twice the mean field size [derived]. Beyond 12 m the intermediate and ventral tails dip to about -0.03 to -0.13 [figure-read], which bears on the review's point that PV construction sets the far-distance floor.
- Kjelstrup single cells. Spatial autocorrelation widths of 1.12, 3.07 and 4.10 m. Precession slopes of -102° ± 33° per m dorsally against -18° ± 9° per m for intermediate and ventral combined, with ventral precession over up to 10 m read by the authors as one field per cell. The Fig. 4 legend calls the single-cell measure "the average width of the largest place field", so the per-level means may describe each cell's largest field. A new intermediate-group row (3.73 m, 21%) in the first table. A field-definitions row. The detector-cleaving example: the 15.7 m field was split into fields of about 2.4 and 9.6 m in the other running direction [figure-read].
- Kjelstrup notes 29 and 33. Poucet et al. 1994 lacked histology and found dorsal-sized fields. The Maurer and Jung intermediate recordings sat at about 30 to 45% of the axis [secondary]. Ventral CA3 "still had strongly orthogonalized place maps" across discrete environments, added to the dorsoventral and remapping sections.
- Muller & Kubie 1987. Large-to-small field-size ratios of 0.87, 1.73 and 2.49, and the derived field-area exponents -0.10, 0.40 and 0.66. Rectangle scaling in 7 of 19 cells. Cell selection. The 65% place-cell impression, in the active-fraction section. A field-definitions row.
- O'Keefe & Burgess 1996. 28 fields from 27 cells in 6 rats. 38 of 56 peak determinants, 28 of them wall distances [derived]. Table 1a counts of compact, stretched, two-peak and multi-peak maps. Direction dependence in 22 of 24 maps. Bins that grow with the box. A field-definitions row.
- Sharp & Green 1994. A first-table row, any spike in 84% of visited pixels over all cells and 70 and 79% for bursting and non-bursting cells. The same value in the twelve-measures row. 99 cells in 21 rats, coherence 0.48 against 0.80 for hippocampal cells, head direction explaining 1% of variance. Proximodistal statistics with cells and with electrode tracks as samples. Bursting against non-bursting cells. The 5 of 8 cross-environment repeats judged by eye. Neighbouring-cell map correlations of 0.06 and 0.0003 against 0.49 for one cell across sessions.
- Lever et al. 2009. 0.25 against 0.05 bits/spike for location and direction, directional selectivity 1.56 against 2.87 for CA1, mean rate 5.0 Hz, and map peak rates of 1.6 to 28.6 Hz [figure-read].
- Olson et al. 2017. Track and arena sizes, peak-to-minimum ratio 4.65, room-fixed tuning after track rotation (0.76 against -0.41), and arena place-like and boundary-like shares of about 29% and 21%.
- Maurer & Nadel 2021. The review's claim that ventral activity "will change more slowly" follows from the authors' own proposal and cites no measurement.
- Mainali et al. 2025. All data are dorsal CA1. The fitted correlation length grows with log-log slopes of 0.24 in bat tunnels and 0.53 in rat arenas. The paper has no population-level analysis. Random Gaussian-process codes can have exponentially growing capacity, added as a note on the open disagreement about optimized against random codes. The bat log field sizes are skewed, about -0.72 [figure-read], which qualifies the derived coefficient of variation of 0.63.

## Deviations from the notes

- No model comparisons were added. The Mainali full-version paragraph was not used because it adds model-against-data fit statistics (ΔAIC, predicted against measured exponents). The minimal version was used, without parentheses. The note on the CV of 0.63 is phrased as a property of the data, the skew of log sizes, not as a model comparison.
- The Kjelstrup Fig. 4 four-level values were not added. The figure regroups the non-dorsal data in a way that does not match the text's three groups, and its legend and caption disagree.
- Change 6 proposed two links to the same Kjelstrup DOI in the first table's source list. One is kept.
- Change 29's closing inference was reworded. The ventral curve declines smoothly between 1 and 8 m, which looks graded by distance, but the whole track was one context, so the dataset cannot separate distance from context membership.
- Change 22 was split into a single-cell paragraph and a population paragraph, and it now refers to the second section for the PV analysis rather than to a later section.
- The Kjelstrup PV analysis is a separate paragraph in the second section instead of a replacement for the first sentence of the following paragraph, which now opens "The remaining candidate exceptions are weak or indirect".
