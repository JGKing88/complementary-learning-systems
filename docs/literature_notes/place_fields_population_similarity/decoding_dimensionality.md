# Decoding accuracy and dimensionality of hippocampal and related spatial population codes

Conventions used in these notes:
- "[verified]" means I read the number in the paper's own text (full text via the NCBI BioC/PMC API, or the abstract via Europe PMC). Unmarked abstract-level claims were also read from the abstract.
- "[unverified]" means the claim comes from a search-engine summary, a secondary source, or my own prior knowledge, and I could not check it against the source text. Treat these as leads, not facts.
- Relative error = reported error divided by the environment's linear size (track length, arena side or diameter). I computed these myself. They are inferences, listed under Inferences.
- Corrections to the brief: Rubin et al. 2019 is in Nature Communications, not eLife. Tampuu et al. 2019 decodes from extracellular single units, not calcium imaging. The main text of Stevenson & Kording 2011 does not analyze hippocampal position decoding (details below).

## Question 1. How accurately can position be decoded from hippocampal and related populations, in absolute terms and relative to environment size, across N cells, region, environment size and method?

### Takeaway
Across rodent CA1 studies spanning 0.5 m arenas to a 10.3 m track, median or RMS position-decoding errors cluster at about 5-12 cm. Ensembles were tens to hundreds of cells, with time windows of 0.25-2 s. Relative error therefore shrinks as the environment grows: about 8-10% of the arena for small boxes and tracks, under 1% for a 10 m track. Primate, human, bat and bird data mostly show significant decoding, not centimetre-level errors, and the multi-metre bat results are theoretical decoding analyses on recorded field statistics.

### Cited Findings

Classic rat CA1 electrophysiology
- Wilson & McNaughton 1993. Ensembles of 73 to 148 rat hippocampal neurons, recorded simultaneously, predicted the animal's movement through the environment. The ensemble code was less robust in a novel space and improved rapidly with exploration [verified, abstract]. Source: [Wilson MA, McNaughton BL (1993) Dynamics of the hippocampal ensemble code for space. Science. doi:10.1126/science.8351520](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.8351520)
- Wilson & McNaughton 1993, method. The actual rate vector over each 1-s epoch was compared with expected rate vectors (template or maximum-correlation matching), and the best match gave the decoded location [unverified; from a search snippet of a McNaughton/Wilson slide deck, not the paper]. The arena size, the error in cm, and any extrapolation of the number of cells needed for a given accuracy were not found in any source I could read. Source: [McNaughton & Wilson slides, Stanford](https://web.stanford.edu/~jlmcc/Presentations/McNaughtonWilson.pdf)
- Brown et al. 1998. CA1 place cells, 2 rats, open circular environment, 10 min of foraging. A Bayesian recursive (causal) filter used an inhomogeneous Poisson encoding model with position and theta phase, plus a Gaussian random-walk path prior. Median decoding error was 8.0 cm for animal 1 (34 place cells) and 7.7 cm for animal 2 (33 place cells). Position modulated spiking 3 to 5 times more strongly than theta phase [verified, abstract]. The arena diameter (I recall about 70 cm) and the errors of the comparison decoders are [unverified]. Source: [Brown EN, Frank LM, Tang D, Quirk MC, Wilson MA (1998) A statistical paradigm for neural spike train decoding applied to position prediction from ensemble firing patterns of rat hippocampal place cells. J Neurosci. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-18-07411.1998](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-18-07411.1998)
- Zhang et al. 1998 compared population vector, optimal linear estimator, template matching, and one- and two-step Bayesian methods on multi-electrode rat place-cell data. Bayesian methods were most accurate when a continuity constraint was enforced (the two-step method). The best errors were within a factor of two of the information-theoretic (Cramér-Rao type) minimum and comparable to the error of position tracking itself. The reconstructed trajectory tended to jump erratically when the animal stopped running [verified, abstract]. The errors in cm, the N cells and the arena size are [unverified]: the PDF uses LZW compression and I could not extract its text. Source: [Zhang K, Ginzburg I, McNaughton BL, Sejnowski TJ (1998) Interpreting neuronal population activity by reconstruction: unified framework with application to hippocampal place cells. J Neurophysiol. doi:10.1152/jn.1998.79.2.1017](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1998.79.2.1017)
- Davidson, Kloosterman & Wilson 2009. Dorsal CA1 tetrodes/octrodes, 5 rats, a 10.3 m linear track (linearized, A = 0 m, B = 10.3 m). There were 47, 34, 23, 32 and 21 spatially tuned units in rats 1-5. Interneurons (>5 Hz mean rate) and units with peak tuning <3 Hz were excluded. Less-well-isolated units with stable amplitude signatures were included. A Bayesian decoder of position x running direction during RUN (>15 cm/s), with 500 ms windows, gave median errors of 7, 9, 8 and 8 cm for rats 1-4, "with good performance across the entire environment". Replay decoding used non-overlapping 20 ms bins [verified, full text]. Source: [Davidson TJ, Kloosterman F, Wilson MA (2009) Hippocampal replay of extended experience. Neuron. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2009.07.027](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.07.027)
- Tampuu et al. 2019. CA1 single units from 5 rats (fewer than 10% interneurons), 2-D 1 m x 1 m square arena. Neurons per rat in 2-D: 63, 33, 48, 43, 26. Recordings lasted 1081-1500 s. In 1-D sessions rat R2192 had 72 neurons. Windows were varied from 200 to 4000 ms in 200 ms steps, and the RNN saw 100 consecutive windows. All decoders used 10-fold CV. For the best rat (R2192, 63 neurons), the RNN reached a median error of 10.18 ± 0.23 cm (1200 ms window) and a mean error of 12.50 ± 0.39 cm (1400 ms). The simple Bayesian decoder's best median was 12.00 cm and its best mean 15.83 cm (2800 ms window). A Bayesian decoder with a memory prior reached a best median of 11.31 cm (1400 ms) and a best mean of 15.46 cm (2000 ms). Errors rose for shorter windows (noisy counts) and longer windows (activity less specific to location) [verified, full text]. Source: [Tampuu A, Matiisen T, Ólafsdóttir HF, Barry C, Vicente R (2019) Efficient neural decoding of self-location with a deep recurrent network. PLoS Comput Biol. doi:10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006822](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006822)
- Fenton et al. 2008. Rat CA1, a 68-cm-diameter cylinder versus a 150 cm x 140 cm chamber. Rate maps used 4.7 cm pixels. A template-matching decoder (maximum projection of the rate vector onto location templates) worked on recorded ensembles of only 5-25 cells. To extrapolate, the authors simulated ensembles of up to 322 cells from the recorded fields. The optimal decoding interval was 250 ms in both environments, and error fell as N grew. Decoding error was "similarly small (< 5 cm) in the cylinder and chamber when the ensemble was approximately 200 cells or more". Place cells in the larger chamber had multiple, irregular, enlarged fields [verified, full text]. Caveat: the 200-cell result comes from simulated Poisson spike trains, not recorded ensembles. Source: [Fenton AA et al. (2008) Unmasking the CA1 ensemble place code by exposures to small and large environments. J Neurosci. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008)
- Schneider, Lee & Mathis 2023 (CEBRA). On a public rat hippocampus dataset (1.6 m linear track, position binned into 100 bins per direction), they embedded the data with CEBRA-Behaviour (position + direction labels) and decoded with a kNN regressor. Median absolute error was 5.8 cm (R² = 73.35%), versus 44.7 cm with shuffled labels. They cite 12 cm for a prior conv-pi-VAE method and 11 cm for their time-windowed baseline. The embedding had ring topology with Betti numbers (1,1,0) [verified, full text]. The dataset is, I believe, Grosmark & Buzsáki 2016 [unverified attribution]. Source: [Schneider S, Lee JH, Mathis MW (2023) Learnable latent embeddings for joint behavioural and neural analysis. Nature. doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06031-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06031-6)

Calcium-imaging decoders (mouse)
- Ziv et al. 2013. Miniscope Ca²⁺ imaging of 515-1040 CA1 pyramidal cells per mouse on an 84 x 4.5 cm elevated linear track, 10 sessions over 45 days. Rate maps used 3.5 cm bins, and the last 7 cm at each end (reward) were excluded. Mean place-field size was about 27% of the track. Bayesian decoding, trained and tested on the same day, gave a median error "nearly always <7 cm". Decoders trained 30 days earlier still performed far above chance (P = 10⁻²⁷), using the 15-25% of cells shared between days. The track ends were represented more densely than the interior [verified, full text]. N mice [unverified]. Source: [Ziv Y et al. (2013) Long-term dynamics of CA1 hippocampal place codes. - Stefanini et al. 2020. Miniscope imaging of DG granule cells and CA1 in freely moving mice, in a 50 x 50 cm arena (DG mice) and a 50 x 28 cm arena (CA1 mice). Position was discretized into an 8 x 8 grid (64 classes) and decoded with a linear classifier, with immobility (<2 cm/s for >1 s) removed. Median decoding error was "comparable to the animal size" in both areas. DG and CA1 accuracy were comparable after matching cell numbers, and position could be decoded from a few tens of granule cells. Non-place cells carried decodable position information. Destroying noise correlations by shuffling increased decoding error in 4 of 6 animals, mostly in CA1 [verified, full text]. Exact cm values and the per-region N mice are [unverified]. Source: [Stefanini F et al. (2020) A distributed neural code in the dentate gyrus and in CA1. Neuron. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2020.05.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.05.022)
- Hazon et al. 2022. Miniscope imaging of 12 mice, 151-497 CA1 neurons per session (median 235), 27,898 neurons over 110 sessions, on a 120 cm linear track decoded into 20 equal bins (6 cm each). RMS error was 9.38 ± 4.06 cm with real (correlated) data and 5.15 ± 2.40 cm after trial-shuffling removed noise correlations (median ± IQR, n = 110 sessions). Tracking error was 0.28 cm. Pairwise noise correlations were mostly 0.01-0.2 [verified, full text]. Source: [Hazon O, Minces VH, Tomàs DP, Ganguli S, Schnitzer MJ, Jercog PE (2022) Noise correlations in neural ensemble activity limit the accuracy of hippocampal spatial representations. Subiculum and other output regions
- Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012. 5 rats on a U-shaped track (350 cm long, 6 cm wide), plus a geometrically identical novel track. Their measure was information-theoretic, not decoding error in cm. Median single-neuron spatial mutual information was 0.057 bits in subiculum versus 0.021 bits in distal CA1. Proximal, middle and distal subiculum had 0.030, 0.062 and 0.062 bits. Population information grew per added neuron at 0.037, 0.059 and 0.064 bits/neuron in proximal, middle and distal subiculum, versus 0.036 bits/neuron for CA1 plus the CA1/subiculum transition zone. Context information grew at 0.012-0.022 bits/neuron in subiculum versus 0.015 in distal CA1. The authors warn that "the number of bits of information that we calculated cannot be interpreted literally" [verified, full text]. Source: [Kim SM, Ganguli S, Frank LM (2012) Spatial information outflow from the hippocampal circuit: distributed spatial coding and phase precession in the subiculum. J Neurosci. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5942-11.2012](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5942-11.2012)
- Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024. 256-channel silicon probes in rats. The T-maze dataset had 302 CA1 and 282 subiculum principal neurons (13 and 15 sessions; 8 and 9 rats). The open-field dataset had 265 CA1 and 282 subiculum neurons (10 and 15 sessions; 6 and 9 rats). Manifolds were "homoeomorphic to external space". They "conveyed position, speed, and future path information with higher decoding accuracy in the SUB than in the CA1" [verified, abstract and full text]. Error values in cm [unverified, not extracted]. Source: [Nakai S, Kitanishi T, Mizuseki K (2024) Distinct manifold encoding of navigational information in the subiculum and hippocampus. Sci Adv. doi:10.1126/sciadv.adi4471](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adi4471)
- Tingley & Buzsáki 2018 (lateral septum). Rats (2 female, 3 male). LS rate maps had lower peak information than hippocampal maps (0.4 vs 1.7 bits/cm). Only 5.3% (232/4,393) of LS rate maps had place fields, versus 31.7% (1,820/5,735) in hippocampus. A maximum-correlation classifier (60/40 train/test, 10-fold CV, ~30 cm smoothing) decoded position better from spike theta phase than from rate for 786/840 LS neurons and in 62/62 sessions. For hippocampal place fields, phase carried more information at spatial scales <6 cm (about one theta cycle) and rate at >10 cm [verified, full text]. Source: [Tingley D, Buzsáki G (2018) Transformation of a spatial map across the hippocampal-lateral septal circuit. Entorhinal cortex
- Hardcastle et al. 2017. 794 layer II/III MEC neurons from 14 mice foraging in open arenas, fit with LN models. Position decoded from mixed-selective cells was more accurate than from single-variable cells (n = 269 cells each): position error 2.44 ± 0.05 cm versus 4.51 ± 0.07 cm, and head-direction error 15 versus 29.8 degrees. A maximum-likelihood decoder over 60-200 ms integration windows decoded better from a "fast" population than a "slow" one during fast running [verified, full text]. Arena size, and whether the cells were pooled across sessions rather than recorded simultaneously, are [unverified]. Pooled pseudo-populations would make these errors optimistic. Source: [Hardcastle K, Maheswaranathan N, Ganguli S, Giocomo LM (2017) A multiplexed, heterogeneous, and adaptive code for navigation in medial entorhinal cortex. Neuron (PMC5498174)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5498174)
- Redman et al. 2025 (eLife). In one recorded grid module (N = 168 putative grid cells, about 130 min of open-field data), grid spacing ranged from 65 to 90 cm and orientation from 2 to 9 degrees among high-scoring cells. In synthetic grid populations, within-module variability of that size significantly lowered decoding error of local position, even for a single module [abstract verified; numbers from search snippet, unverified]. Source: [Redman WT, Acosta-Mendoza S, Wei XX, Goard MJ (2025) Robust variability of grid cell properties within individual grid modules enhances encoding of local space. eLife. doi:10.7554/eLife.100652](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.100652)
- Mathis, Herz & Stemmler 2012 (theory). They computed position-estimation error for place and grid codes with Fisher information (asymptotic) and maximum-likelihood estimation (low spike counts). For resolution, the best grid code uses nested modules. For maximal range, it uses pairwise incommensurate periods. Grid codes can outperform place codes in resolution [verified, abstract]. - Absolute error stays at about 5-12 cm while environments range from 0.5 m to 10 m, so relative error falls about tenfold with environment size. Within this range, error seems set by place-field width, firing rates, N and the integration window more than by environment size. This is an inference across heterogeneous studies with different decoders, windows and error metrics (median vs mean vs RMS).
- Methods matter by roughly 15-30%. On the same data, a RNN using about 100 s of temporal context beat the best Bayesian decoder by 17.9% in median error (Tampuu). Continuity priors also improve Bayesian decoding (Zhang 1998). Comparisons across papers should therefore control for decoder and window.
- For subiculum, two independent methods (information per neuron, Kim 2012; manifold decoding, Nakai 2024) both find it at least as informative as CA1 per neuron. Lateral septum carries position mainly in theta phase, not in rate.

## Question 2. How does decoding error scale with ensemble size, where does it saturate, and where do errors concentrate (local error vs aliasing between distant positions)?

### Takeaway
Error falls with N in every study that varied N. The largest calcium-imaging dataset (Hazon 2022) finds that inverse mean squared error saturates at about 350-1,400 CA1 neurons, at an error floor of about 10 cm on a 120 cm track. The authors attribute the floor to noise correlations, and decorrelated data keep improving without apparent bound. Evidence on where errors concentrate in place-cell data is thin: reported effects are jumps when the animal stops, position dependence along the track, and the theoretical point that ambiguity errors in periodic or multi-field codes are far from the true position.

### Cited Findings
- Hazon et al. 2022 fit inverse mean squared error versus ensemble size with IMSE(n) = I0·n / (1 + n/N). IMSE grows linearly for small n (as for independent noise) and plateaus at I0·N. Saturation occurred at 346-1,402 neurons depending on the animal. With noise correlations removed by trial shuffling, "the error decreased without apparent bound". Noise correlations bound error at about 10 cm, "the size of a mouse". The asymptotic SNR was significantly higher at the end of a run than at its start (p = 2 x 10⁻²⁹, n = 110 sessions), so the error floor varies along the track. Saturation was inferred by extrapolating fits, because sessions held at most about 500 neurons. The authors validated the procedure on artificial populations of 500 and 2,000 neurons [verified, full text]. Source: [Hazon et al. 2022, Nat Commun](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31254-y)
- Stefanini et al. 2020 point the other way on correlations. Shuffling to destroy correlations increased decoding error in 4 of 6 animals, mostly in CA1, so correlations helped decoding there [verified, full text]. Source: [Stefanini et al. 2020, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.05.022)
- Fenton et al. 2008. Error fell monotonically with simulated ensemble size. It reached <5 cm at about 200 or more cells in both a 68 cm cylinder and a 150 x 140 cm chamber, though the larger chamber needed larger ensembles to match the cylinder [verified, full text]. Simulated cells were independent, so this extrapolation cannot show correlation-limited saturation. Source: [Fenton et al. 2008, J Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008)
- Courellis et al. 2019 (marmoset). Decoded-trajectory variance explained and correlation rose with the number of place cells per session [verified, full text]. Source: [Courellis et al. 2019, PLoS Biol](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000546)
- Kim et al. 2012. Population spatial information rose roughly linearly with neuron count over the sampled range, at 0.036 bits/neuron in CA1 and up to 0.064 bits/neuron in distal subiculum [verified, full text]. Source: [Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012, J Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5942-11.2012)
- Stevenson & Kording 2011. The log N growth they report is for spike-prediction accuracy of interaction models in cortex, not for position-decoding error [verified, full text]. Source: [Stevenson & Kording 2011, Nat Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2731)
- Zhang et al. 1998. The reconstructed trajectory showed "erratic jumps" when the rat stopped running. Bayesian decoding with a continuity constraint came within a factor of two of the theoretical minimum error [verified, abstract]. Source: [Zhang et al. 1998, J Neurophysiol](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1998.79.2.1017)
- Ziv et al. 2013. Track ends were represented more densely than the interior [verified, full text]. Hazon et al. 2022 report error per spatial bin and different IMSE curves for end versus centre bins [verified, full text]. Sources: [Ziv et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329); [Hazon et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31254-y)
- Davidson et al. 2009 report "good performance across the entire environment" of a 10.3 m track, with 23-47 units [verified, full text]. Source: [Davidson et al. 2009, Neuron](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.07.027)
- Theory on aliasing. For grid codes in environments larger than the largest grid period, ambiguity errors dominate, and such errors "will typically be extremely large (i.e. decoded locations will be far from the correct location)". The same paper contrasts single-field place cells, where such ambiguity is absent [from search snippet; authors not verified, I believe Wei, Prentice & Balasubramanian]. Mathis et al. 2012 likewise treat range (ambiguity) and resolution as separate design constraints for grid codes [verified, abstract]. Sources: [The sense of place: grid cells in the brain and the transcendental number e, arXiv:1304.0031](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0031); [Mathis et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00319)
- Redman et al. 2025. Identical spacing and orientation within a grid module make population patterns repeat across space. Small within-module heterogeneity breaks this symmetry and lowers decoding error in synthetic populations [abstract verified]. Source: [Redman et al. 2025, eLife](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.100652)
- Fenton et al. 2008. Multiple, irregular fields per cell in the larger chamber did not degrade ensemble decoding once ensembles were large. The authors present this as support for an ensemble code over a dedicated single-field code [verified, full text]. Source: [Fenton et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008)

### Inferences
- The two calcium-imaging studies disagree on whether noise correlations hurt (Hazon, linear track, 20 bins, RMS error) or help (Stefanini, open field, 64-class linear classifier) position decoding. Their shuffle procedures, decoders and environments differ, so the sign of the correlation effect may depend on method. Neither result alone settles whether information saturates in CA1 generally.
- The 1/(1 + n/N) form in Hazon implies that RMS error falls roughly as 1/√n for n much smaller than N, then levels off. With N of about 350-1,400, recordings of fewer than about 100-200 cells (most tetrode studies) sit in the regime where error still falls steeply with N.
- I found no direct empirical measurement of how often place-cell decoders make large "aliasing" errors to distant positions in multi-field or very large environments. The claim that multi-field and periodic codes risk distant errors rests on theory (grid-code papers) and on decoding analyses of recorded field statistics (Eliav), not on reported error distributions from recorded ensembles.

### Gaps
- No verified saturation estimate from electrophysiology, and none for 2-D open fields or large environments.
- No study found that reports the full decoding-error distribution (local versus distant errors) for place cells in very large or multi-field environments. Eliav 2021 may contain this, but I could not read the full text.
- Wilson & McNaughton's 1993 error-versus-N curve and extrapolation not verified.

## Question 3. What are the linear and nonlinear dimensionality estimates of hippocampal spatial population activity?

### Takeaway
Nonlinear (intrinsic) dimension estimates for hippocampal spatial activity are low, about 1-6. Specific values: about 1 per running direction on a linear track; 3-D rings, or a ring topology, when both directions are included; about 3-3.6 for rat CA1 in a T-maze and open field; about 5-6 for subiculum; about 4-6 for CA1 in a VR evidence-accumulation task. Linear methods need many more components: 29-47 PCs to match a 4-6 dimensional MIND embedding in CA1. No hippocampal power-law eigenspectrum or participation ratio, comparable to Stringer et al.'s V1 analysis, was found.

### Cited Findings
- Rubin et al. 2019 (Nature Communications, not eLife). Mouse CA1 calcium imaging on a linear track. Laplacian eigenmaps revealed discrete network states (running in each direction, turning, drinking), and PCA did not reveal them. Within each running-direction state, the intrinsic dimension was 1. They estimated it from the log-log slope of the number of neighbouring points versus radius (a correlation-dimension estimate) and found "one component, no holes, and no spaces", the topology of a line. The internal structure was conserved across mice, so one animal's data could decode another's behaviour. The same method found trajectory-phase coding in ACC (N = 3 mice) and head-direction tuning in anterior thalamus [verified, full text]. Open-field (2-D) results [unverified, not extracted]. Source: [Rubin A et al. (2019) Revealing neural correlates of behavior without behavioral measurements. Nat Commun. doi:10.1038/s41467-019-12724-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12724-2)
- Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024. Rat CA1 and subiculum, silicon probes ("dozens of neurons" per population). Grassberger-Procaccia correlation dimension in the T-maze was CA1 3.07 ± 1.32 and SUB 5.24 ± 0.70, rising to 3.01 ± 1.30 and 5.73 ± 0.87 with z-scored rates. In the open field it was CA1 3.61 ± 1.10 and SUB 5.84 ± 0.79 (mean ± SD). Subiculum was significantly higher. Manifold geometry was shared across rats [verified, full text]. Source: [Nakai et al. 2024, Sci Adv](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adi4471)
- Nieh et al. 2021. 7 mice, 2-photon imaging of dorsal CA1, 3,144 neurons total (449 ± 64 per session), in a VR accumulating-towers T-maze (stem 0-300 cm). MIND distances gave state counts growing as r^d with d = 5.4 [4.8, 6.0]. Cross-validated reconstruction of held-out activity saturated at about 5-6 embedding dimensions. A simpler task (cues on one side only) had significantly lower dimension. PCA needed 29, 40 and 47 components to match the decoding indices of 4-, 5- and 6-dimensional MIND embeddings [verified, full text]. Position and accumulated evidence were jointly and smoothly mapped on the manifold, and the geometry was similar across mice [verified, abstract]. Source: [Nieh EH et al. (2021) Geometry of abstract learned knowledge in the hippocampus. Nature. doi:10.1038/s41586-021-03652-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03652-7)
- Low et al. 2018 (bioRxiv preprint, flagged). MIND applied to rat hippocampal recordings during spatial and non-spatial tasks found low-dimensional manifolds that smoothly encoded task variables, plus an extra dimension carrying information beyond the measured behavioural variables. This extra dimension let trajectories differ across repeated behavioural conditions [verified, abstract]. Specific dimension numbers [unverified]. I recall a roughly 3-D manifold for 2-D foraging and 2-D for a 1-D task, but the bioRxiv full text returned rate-limit errors. Source: [Low RJ, Lewallen S, Aronov D, Nevers R, Tank DW (2018) Probing variability in a cognitive map using manifold inference from neural dynamics. bioRxiv. doi:10.1101/418939](https://doi.org/10.1101/418939)
- Mitchell-Heggs et al. 2023 (review with re-analysis). They re-analysed 2-photon CA1 data from a mouse on a circular track (225 cells, 92 classified as place cells; data from Go et al.) with PCA, MDS, Isomap, LLE, Laplacian eigenmaps, t-SNE and UMAP. All manifolds had intrinsic dimensionality "around 2" by a correlation-dimension estimator. The linear embeddings (PCA, MDS) had the highest intrinsic dimensionality. Decoding with an optimal linear estimator was near maximal with only the first manifold dimension. The authors note that any dimensionality measure depends strongly on timescale and population size [verified, full text]. Source: [Mitchell-Heggs R, Prado S, Gava GP, Go MA, Schultz SR (2023) Neural manifold analysis of brain circuit dynamics in health and disease. J Comput Neurosci. doi:10.1007/s10827-022-00839-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10827-022-00839-3)
- Esparza et al. 2025. Single- and dual-colour miniscope imaging of deep and superficial CA1 pyramidal cells in mice on a linear track. Population activity formed "three-dimensional ring manifolds" encoding position and running direction. Rotating the track rotated and translated deep-cell manifolds, while superficial-cell manifolds were more stable [verified, abstract]. Source: [Esparza J, Quintanilla JP, Cid E, Medeiros AC, Gallego JA, de la Prida LM (2025) Cell-type-specific manifold analysis discloses independent geometric transformations in the hippocampal spatial code. Neuron. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2025.01.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2025.01.022)
- CEBRA (Schneider et al. 2023). The rat CA1 linear-track embedding had ring topology, Betti numbers (1,1,0), which the authors call "as one would expect from a linear track for place cells" given direction selectivity [verified, full text]. Source: [Schneider et al. 2023, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06031-6)
- Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021. On a VR linear track, dorsal CA1 had higher PCA dimensionality than ventral CA1, measured as the fraction of PCs needed for 80% variance after 1 s Gaussian smoothing. Each session had 42-80 dorsal and 43-62 ventral units [verified, full text; numeric dimensionality values not extracted]. Source: [Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021, eNeuro](https://doi.org/10.1523/ENEURO.0211-21.2021)
- Zhang, Rich, Lee & Sharpee 2023. Rat dorsal CA1, including a 48 m linear track (example: 264 fields from 63 cells) and a square environment. Place-field sizes followed an approximately exponential distribution. Betti curves of pairwise-correlation matrices matched points sampled from 3-D hyperbolic geometry and differed from 3-D Euclidean geometry, in both linear and square environments. Shuffled spike trains broke the match. The hyperbolic representation's size expanded with the logarithm of exploration time [verified, full text]. Source: [Zhang H, Rich PD, Lee AK, Sharpee TO (2023) Hippocampal spatial representations exhibit a hyperbolic geometry that expands with experience. Nat Neurosci. doi:10.1038/s41593-022-01212-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01212-4)
- Giusti et al. 2015. Clique topology (Betti curves of ordered correlation matrices, invariant to monotone nonlinearities) detected geometric rather than random organization in rat hippocampal pyramidal-cell correlations during 2-D exploration. It found the same during wheel running and REM sleep. The authors warn that eigenvalue-based methods can mislead when nonlinearities are present [verified, abstract]. Source: [Giusti C, Pastalkova E, Curto C, Itskov V (2015) Clique topology reveals intrinsic geometric structure in neural correlations. PNAS. doi:10.1073/pnas.1506407112](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1506407112)
- Method contrast, head direction (Chaudhuri et al. 2019). Mouse anterodorsal thalamic HD population states form a 1-D ring with nontrivial topology. The ring is isometric and the same in wake and REM sleep, and it can be decoded blind (SPUD, a spline parameterization for unsupervised decoding) [abstract verified; SPUD name from search snippet]. Cell counts and the dimension estimator [unverified]. Source: [Chaudhuri R, Gerçek B, Pandey B, Peyrache A, Fiete I (2019) The intrinsic attractor manifold and population dynamics of a canonical cognitive circuit across waking and sleep. Nat Neurosci. doi:10.1038/s41593-019-0460-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0460-x)
- Method contrast, grid cells (Gardner et al. 2022). Neuropixels recordings from rat MEC, 4 sessions with 140-544 grid cells each, six modules of 66-189 grid cells. Method: PCA to 6 components (which carried "a particularly large fraction of the variance" per module in the open field), then UMAP to 3-D for visualization, then persistent cohomology on the 6-D representation. Barcodes showed one prominent H0 bar, two H1 bars and one H2 bar, the cohomology of a torus. Example modules held 93, 149 and 145 cells. Position on the torus mapped to physical position, and cells kept their toroidal phases across environments and sleep [verified, full text]. Source: [Gardner RJ et al. (2022) Toroidal topology of population activity in grid cells. Nature. doi:10.1038/s41586-021-04268-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04268-7)
- Method contrast, power-law spectra (Stringer et al. 2019). About 10,000 mouse V1 neurons responding to thousands of natural images. Cross-validated PCA gave variance of the nth component decaying as n^-α with α ≈ 1. Theory: for a d-dimensional stimulus ensemble, spectra decaying slower than α = 1 + 2/d imply a non-differentiable representation. Lower-dimensional stimulus ensembles gave steeper spectra, as predicted [from search summaries of the paper and lab page, unverified against full text]. A 2024 preprint (Pospisil & Pillow) argues that V1 is better fit by a broken power law with faster decay of later modes [verified, abstract; preprint]. Sources: [Stringer C, Pachitariu M, Steinmetz N, Carandini M, Harris KD (2019) High-dimensional geometry of population responses in visual cortex. Nature. doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1346-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1346-5); [Pospisil DA, Pillow JW (2024) Revisiting the high-dimensional geometry of population responses in visual cortex. bioRxiv preprint. doi:10.1101/2024.02.16.580726](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.02.16.580726)
- Method contrast, persistent cohomology robustness (Kang, Xu & Morozov 2021). On simulated grid, head-direction and conjunctive populations, they measured how dataset size, tuning variability and noise affect topological discovery and trajectory decoding [verified, abstract]. Source: [Kang L, Xu B, Morozov D (2021) Evaluating state space discovery by persistent cohomology in the spatial representation system. Front Comput Neurosci. doi:10.3389/fncom.2021.616748](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2021.616748)

### Inferences
- Intrinsic-dimension values for a 1-D track range from about 1 per direction (Rubin) to about 2 for a circular track (Mitchell-Heggs) to a 3-D ring embedding (Esparza). The variation tracks how running direction, non-running states and the estimator are handled, more than biology. Both directions together form a ring-like or two-branch structure. Each direction alone is a line.
- In CA1, linear dimension (PCs needed) exceeds intrinsic dimension by roughly an order of magnitude: 29-47 PCs versus 4-6 MIND dimensions in Nieh 2021. In Mitchell-Heggs 2023, linear embeddings had the highest correlation dimension. Both fit a curved low-dimensional manifold embedded in many linear dimensions.
- The subiculum's higher intrinsic dimension (about 5-6 vs about 3 in CA1, Nakai 2024) and its higher information per neuron (Kim 2012) are consistent with each other. Both papers describe a denser, more distributed code downstream of CA1.

### Gaps
- No participation ratio or cvPCA eigenspectrum exponent for hippocampal CA1 or subiculum spatial activity was found. Hazon 2022 discusses the "potential dimensionality of the spatial neuronal code" via noise principal components, but I did not extract numbers.
- Low 2018 dimension values unverified (bioRxiv full text rate-limited).
- Chaudhuri 2019 cell counts and dimension estimator details unverified.
- Numeric PCA dimensionality values from Chockanathan 2021 and Keinath 2014 not extracted.

## Question 4. Does population activity for a 2-D environment lie on a 2-D manifold, how is it embedded, and how does linear dimension scale with environment size or number of positions?

### Takeaway
Population activity in 2-D environments is organized by position: it is geometric, homeomorphic to the arena, and decodable from unsupervised embeddings. But measured intrinsic dimensions are above 2. Rat CA1 in an open field gives a correlation dimension of 3.6 ± 1.1, Betti curves fit a 3-D hyperbolic geometry, and MIND adds an extra non-behavioural dimension. The manifold is embedded nonlinearly and needs many more linear dimensions than intrinsic ones. I found no empirical study measuring how linear dimension scales with environment size or the number of positions. Theory says such dimension is bounded by task extent relative to tuning smoothness.

### Cited Findings
- Nakai et al. 2024. Open-field CA1 correlation dimension was 3.61 ± 1.10, and subiculum 5.84 ± 0.79. Manifolds were "homoeomorphic to external space" [verified, full text]. Source: [Nakai et al. 2024, Sci Adv](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adi4471)
- Zhang et al. 2023. In both linear and square environments, CA1 correlation structure matched 3-D hyperbolic geometry and not 3-D Euclidean geometry [verified, full text]. Source: [Zhang, Rich, Lee & Sharpee 2023, Nat Neurosci](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01212-4)
- Low et al. 2018 (preprint). Hippocampal manifolds contained "an extra dimension reflecting information beyond the measured behavioral variables" [verified, abstract]. Source: [Low et al. 2018, bioRxiv](https://doi.org/10.1101/418939)
- Giusti et al. 2015. CA1 correlations during 2-D exploration had geometric organization, and so did correlations during wheel running and REM sleep, so the geometric structure is not only a consequence of position coding [verified, abstract]. Source: [Giusti et al. 2015, PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1506407112)
- Nieh et al. 2021. A 4-6 dimensional nonlinear manifold required 29-47 linear PCs for equivalent decoding. The authors call it an "intricately shaped low-dimensional manifold that can only be identified with nonlinear dimensionality reduction" [verified, full text]. Source: [Nieh et al. 2021, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03652-7)
- Gao et al. 2017 (bioRxiv preprint). Neural task complexity (NTC) upper-bounds the dimensionality of trial-averaged neural trajectories. NTC grows with the extent of each task parameter and shrinks with the population autocorrelation length (smoothness) along that parameter. For a simple, repeated task, measured dimensionality becomes independent of the number of recorded neurons [abstract-level, from search summary]. The exact formula, which I recall as proportional to the product over task parameters of range divided by autocorrelation length, is [unverified]. Source: [Gao P, Trautmann E, Yu B, Santhanam G, Ryu S, Shenoy K, Ganguli S (2017) A theory of multineuronal dimensionality, dynamics and measurement. bioRxiv. doi:10.1101/214262](https://doi.org/10.1101/214262)
- Stringer et al. 2019 smoothness bound. For a d-dimensional input manifold (d = 2 for an open arena), a differentiable representation requires the eigenspectrum to decay faster than n^-(1 + 2/d), that is faster than n^-2 for d = 2 [from search summary, unverified against full text]. Source: [Stringer et al. 2019, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1346-5)
- Gardner et al. 2022 (method contrast). A grid module's activity lies on a 2-D torus. Six linear PCs sufficed to carry the toroidal structure for UMAP and persistent cohomology [verified, full text]. Source: [Gardner et al. 2022, Nature](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04268-7)
- Rich et al. 2014 and Eliav et al. 2021. The number of active cells grows logarithmically with track length (rats, 48 m). Field sizes span 0.6-32 m within single bat CA1 neurons in a 200 m tunnel [verified, abstracts]. These field statistics set how many distinct population states a large environment uses. Neither paper reports a dimensionality measurement [not checked in supplements]. Sources: [Rich et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255635); [Eliav et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg4020)

### Inferences
- "On a 2-D manifold" holds in the topological sense: activity is organized by, and homeomorphic to, the arena. It does not hold for estimated intrinsic dimension, which exceeds 2 in the studies found. The excess may reflect non-spatial variables (Low 2018's extra dimension, speed or head direction), estimator bias from noise and finite sampling (Mitchell-Heggs note dependence on timescale and N), or genuinely curved geometry (Zhang 2023, hyperbolic). The available data do not separate these.
- Localized place fields of width w make population vectors at positions more than about w apart nearly uncorrelated. The trial-averaged code then needs about (L/w)^d roughly orthogonal directions for an environment of linear size L. Linear dimension should therefore grow with environment size and number of distinct positions until it reaches N, while intrinsic dimension stays near d. This is my inference from the NTC and smoothness arguments above. No hippocampal dataset I found tests it.
- Multiscale or multi-field codes in large environments (Eliav; Harland; Fenton) would change this count: large fields add smooth, low-frequency components, and small fields add high-frequency ones. This also goes untested empirically.

### Gaps
- No empirical measurement of hippocampal linear dimension (PCA, participation ratio, cvPCA exponent) as a function of environment size or number of positions.
- No persistent-homology confirmation of a 2-D sheet (Betti numbers 1,0,0 for a disk) for place-cell activity in an open arena. Gardner 2022 covers grid cells only. Spreemann et al. 2018, a persistent-homology analysis of place-cell data that I recall, was not retrieved.
- Low 2018's open-field intrinsic dimension and Rubin 2019's open-field analysis not verified.
