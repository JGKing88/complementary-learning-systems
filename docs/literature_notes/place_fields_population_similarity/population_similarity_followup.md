# Population similarity against spatial distance: follow-up on gaps from the first pass

Scope note for the report writer. This file adds to `population_similarity_vs_distance.md` and does not repeat it. The first pass already covers Green et al. 2022, Wang, Monaco & Knierim 2020, Battaglia et al. 2004, Rubin et al. 2015, Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022, Leutgeb 2004 and 2005, Stefanini 2020, Nagelhus 2023, Kjelstrup 2008 (single-cell scale only), Komorowski 2013, Royer 2010, Jimenez 2018, and the grid-cell theory preprint. I did not re-check Tanni 2022 here.

Method of checking. For each paper I read the PMC or publisher full text with targeted prompts, or I pulled the PDF and searched its extracted text for "population vector", "ensemble", "decod" and "correlat". Where a fetch was truncated, blocked or paywalled, I say so. "Figure only" means the text says a curve exists but gives no number, so the value has to be read from the figure image, which I could not do.

## Do the named papers contain a PV correlation, ensemble overlap or rate-map similarity curve against distance, and what does it show?

### Takeaway
Only one of the eleven named papers has a population-similarity-versus-distance curve. Ledergerber et al. 2021 (Fig. S7H) reports that the mean PV autocorrelation peak is narrower in subiculum than in CA1 in a 150 cm box, and gives no widths in the text. The large-environment papers (Rich 2014, Eliav 2021, Harland 2021) contain no population-similarity or real-data decoding analysis, so no one has published a measured PV kernel over tens of meters from those datasets.

### Cited findings

#### Ledergerber et al. 2021, subiculum vs CA1 (the only named paper with a curve)
- The paper plots "the average population vector autocorrelation as a function of distance between spatial bins" (Figure S7H). The text says it "shows that the peak in SUB is narrower than that in CA1" and calls this "a faster change of the population code between nearby spatial locations in SUB than that in CA1". Figure S7G shows the spatial autocorrelations of all recorded neurons. [Ledergerber et al. 2021, PMC8170370](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- The main text and STAR Methods do not give peak width, half-width, the lag where correlation reaches zero, any statistic, or the cell count for this analysis. I also found no description there of PV construction (bin size, smoothing, normalization, 1D or 2D lag). The Figure S7 legend is in the supplemental PDF (Document S1), which I could not read. [Ledergerber et al. 2021, PMC8170370](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Context from the same paper (already in `subiculum.md`). Rats explored an open field, a "150 × 150 cm square box". The dataset is 8 rats with tetrodes, 421 SUB and 325 CA1 putative principal cells. 95% of spikes fell in 56.3% of bins for SUB and 25.7% for CA1. Subicular cells therefore fire over about twice as much of the box, yet the PV autocorrelation peak is narrower. [Ledergerber et al. 2021, PMC8170370](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Citation: Ledergerber D, et al. (2021). Task-dependent mixed selectivity in the subiculum. Cell Reports 35:109175. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2021.109175.

#### Rich, Liaw & Lee 2014, 48 m track (no population analysis)
- I extracted the text of the supplementary PDF and listed all 18 supplementary figure titles. None is a population vector, ensemble similarity or decoding analysis. "Population vector" and "decod" occur zero times in the extracted text. The only pairwise-correlation figure is Fig. S12, "The field locations of pairs of cells are uncorrelated", which shows Pearson correlations between the numbers of fields two cells have across track segments. That is a cell-pair statistic, not a similarity-versus-distance curve. [Rich et al. 2014 supplement PDF](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf)
- Citation: Rich PD, Liaw HP, Lee AK (2014). Large environments reveal the statistical structure governing hippocampal representations. Science 345(6198):814-817. doi:10.1126/science.1255635.

#### Eliav et al. 2021, bats in a 200 m tunnel (decoding on synthetic data only)
- The paper's only population analyses run on synthetic data. The authors "generated synthetic data for each coding scheme and then used maximum-likelihood and population-vector decoders to test their decoding performance" for six coding schemes. They used a Bayesian maximum-likelihood decoder (main Fig. 7) and a population-vector decoder (fig. S18), with integration windows of 500 ms (Fig. 7) and 200 ms (fig. S19), "for progressively larger environments". I found no PV correlation or decoding of recorded neurons against distance. [Eliav et al. 2021 PDF, Ulanovsky lab](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/brain-sciences.labs.ulanovsky/files/2024-11/Eliav2021.pdf)
- Citation: Eliav T, Maimon SR, Aljadeff J, Tsodyks M, Ginosar G, Las L, Ulanovsky N (2021). Multiscale representation of very large environments in the hippocampus of flying bats. Science 372:eabg4020. doi:10.1126/science.abg4020.

#### Harland et al. 2021, 18.6 m² megaspace (no population similarity analysis)
- The extracted text of the published PDF contains no "PV" string and no decoding analysis. Population-level statements rest on the distribution of subfields. For example, "the population discharges at each location were unique", and subfield centers were "spread out within the megaspace, with no evidence of clusters". Figure 3A plots subfields from 125 well-isolated cells in seven area bands, from 0.023-0.091 m² to 1.23-3.46 m². [Harland et al. 2021 PDF, Fellous lab](https://cenl.ucsd.edu/pubs/Harland-contreras-souder-fellous+CA1-Multiscale-Megaspace+CurrBio+2021.pdf)
- The uniqueness claim is an inference from field layout. The paper does not measure PV similarity between locations.
- Citation: Harland B, Contreras M, Souder M, Fellous JM (2021). Dorsal CA1 hippocampal place cells form a multi-scale representation of megaspace. Current Biology 31(10):2178-2190.e6. doi:10.1016/j.cub.2021.03.003.

#### Ziv et al. 2013 (no curve)
- Neither the paper nor its supplementary legends contain PV correlation against position offset, within a session or across days. Related measures are present. Overlap of active subsets is plotted against time between sessions (Fig. 3c). Median centroid shift across days was 3.5 cm against a median field width of 24 cm (Fig. 3d). Bayesian decoding gave median errors of about 7-13 cm (Fig. 3h-j). [Ziv et al. 2013, PMC3784308](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3784308)
- Construction details for reference: an 84 cm linear track, 3.5 cm bins, Gaussian smoothing σ = 8.75 cm, last 7 cm at each end excluded. 4 mice in the longitudinal analyses, 807-1000 cells per day. [Ziv et al. 2013, PMC3784308](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3784308)
- Citation: Ziv Y, Burns LD, Cocker ED, Hamel EO, Ghosh KK, Kitch LJ, El Gamal A, Schnitzer MJ (2013). Long-term dynamics of CA1 hippocampal place codes. Nature Neuroscience 16:264-266. doi:10.1038/nn.3329.

#### Sheintuch et al. 2017 (no curve found)
- I searched the full text from Europe PMC (73,350 characters) for "population vector" or "PV" within 200 characters of "distance", "lag", "separation", "apart", "adjacent" or "neighbor", and found no match. [Sheintuch et al. 2017, PMC5670033](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5670033)
- Citation: Sheintuch L, et al. (2017). Tracking the same neurons across multiple days in Ca2+ imaging data. Cell Reports 21(4):1102-1115. doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.10.013.

#### Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012, subiculum (no curve in the portion read)
- In the first 100,000 of 183,287 characters, the similarity measures (cosine similarity, normalized overlap) are "within-neuron comparisons only". Information is indexed by ensemble size ("ensembles of K neurons at a time, for K = 1,…,7"), not by spatial separation. Spatial scale appears only as the kernel bandwidth hx = 20 cm, chosen "to match the scale of features in the firing-rate maps". [Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012, PMC3458125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Design. A U-shaped track "6 cm wide, 350 cm long", with positions within 20 cm of either food well excluded. 5 rats, 91 subicular units, of which 59 were stable across both environments. [Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012, PMC3458125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Citation: Kim SM, Ganguli S, Frank LM (2012). Spatial information outflow from the hippocampal circuit: distributed spatial coding and phase precession in the subiculum. J Neurosci 32(34):11539-11558. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5942-11.2012.

#### Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024, subiculum manifolds (no distance curve)
- In the 100,000 characters read, there is no analysis of PV similarity or neural-state distance against physical distance. The PV analysis (Fig. 3C) correlates left-turn and right-turn trial PVs at each linearized position. Its x-axis is position, not distance. SUB had higher left/right correlation than CA1 in the arms. [Nakai et al. 2024, PMC10830115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)
- Design. A T-maze in "a square arena (118 cm by 118 cm)", with 302 CA1 and 282 SUB putative principal neurons from 8 and 9 rats. [Nakai et al. 2024, PMC10830115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)

### Inferences
- In SUB, single cells fire over about twice the area of CA1 cells (56% vs 26% of bins in Ledergerber 2021), yet the population autocorrelation peak is narrower. Broad single-cell tuning therefore does not produce a broad population kernel here. One likely reason is that Pearson-type PV correlation removes the component shared across positions, leaving the sharper position-specific part. This is my reading, and the paper does not test it.
- The three large-environment datasets (48 m, 200 m, 18.6 m²) contain no measured population similarity curve. Multi-field cells there could raise far-distance similarity above zero, but that remains a prediction and has not been measured. Rich 2014 Fig. S12 (uncorrelated field placement across cells) suggests any such floor would not depend on distance.

### Gaps
- The Ledergerber Figure S7H widths and the PV construction need the supplemental PDF (Document S1), which I could not access.
- Wills et al. 2005 (Science 308:873-876, doi:10.1126/science.1108905, PMC2680068) and Colgin et al. 2010 could not be checked. Europe PMC returned HTTP 500 for both full texts. Europe PMC matches the Colgin title to J Neurophysiol 104:35-50 (doi:10.1152/jn.00202.2010, PMC2904215), not Neuron as the brief states. The first pass describes both as between-shape morph correlations.
- Sheintuch et al. 2020 (Curr Biol, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2020.02.018) has no PMC full text and was not checked.
- The last 83,287 characters of Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012 and 47,132 of Nakai 2024 were not read. Both fetches covered the main Results and Methods but not every supplement.

## Ventral hippocampus: the source of "remains correlated over much larger distances", and any ventral or intermediate PV-versus-distance curve

### Takeaway
Keinath et al. 2014 cites four papers for the claim. Only one of them, Maurer et al. 2005, measured population-vector decorrelation against distance, and it compared extreme dorsal with middle CA1, not ventral. I found no PV-versus-distance curve for ventral hippocampus in any species.

### Cited findings
- Keinath et al. 2014 writes that "population activity in the DH becomes decorrelated over small distances" whereas "in the VH population activity remains correlated over much larger distances". Both fragments come from one introduction sentence citing Maurer et al., 2005; Kjelstrup et al., 2008; Royer et al., 2010; and Komorowski et al., 2013. The paper itself reports no PV correlation or similarity against distance. Its population analyses are PCA (1 s bins, z-scored) and location reconstruction. The environments were a 35 cm and a 70 cm diameter cylinder, with 89 dorsal cells from 6 mice and 65 ventral cells from 5 mice in the visuospatial condition. [Keinath et al. 2014, PMC4447627](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627)
- Citation: Keinath AT, Wang ME, Wann EG, Yuan RK, Dudman JT, Muzzio IA (2014). Precise spatial coding is preserved along the longitudinal hippocampal axis. Hippocampus 24(12):1533-1548. doi:10.1002/hipo.22333.
- Maurer et al. 2005 is the original population-level source. The abstract states that rats carried ensemble recordings of CA1 pyramidal cells and interneurons "from the extreme dorsal and middle hippocampus". It also states that "pyramidal cell population vectors representing successive locations became decorrelated over substantially shorter distances in the dorsal than in the middle hippocampus". Dorsal pyramidal cells had smaller fields and higher rates. Theta amplitude, but not frequency, rose more with running speed in dorsal than in middle hippocampus, and the authors attribute the scale gradient to the speed response. I could not access the full text, so the decorrelation distances, environment, PV construction and N are unverified. [Maurer et al. 2005, PubMed 16145692](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16145692/)
- Citation: Maurer AP, VanRhoads SR, Sutherland GR, Lipa P, McNaughton BL (2005). Self-motion and the origin of differential spatial scaling along the septo-temporal axis of the hippocampus. Hippocampus 15(7):841-852. doi:10.1002/hipo.20114.
- Terrazas et al. 2005, from the same group and year, shows how such a scale is measured and how it moves. Recordings were from dorsal CA1 on a 130 cm diameter circular track. Rate maps went into an "N cells × 128 bin matrix (3.2 cm/bin)", with "1° = 1.13 cm". Similarity was the "overlap matrix for population vectors… (normalized dot products)", with no mean subtraction. The text defines the scale as "the point at which the average overlap falls to 0.5". Cells were all pyramidal cells "firing at least 40 spikes", 152 in WALK and 96 in CAR, plus 217 active cells in WORLD from different subjects. Six male Fischer 344 rats were used, and the number contributing to this analysis is not stated. [Terrazas et al. 2005, PMC6725460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6725460)
- Terrazas et al. 2005 results (Fig. 9). The scale was "approximately three times larger during CAR [rat driving a small vehicle] than during WALK". In WORLD, with the rat stationary and the environment rotated, "most cells that were active on the track fired over most of the track". The 0.5-crossing distances themselves are figure only. Arithmetic: circumference = π × 130 cm ≈ 408 cm (consistent with 128 × 3.2 cm = 409.6 cm). The largest separation on the ring is half of that, about 204 cm. In WORLD, the overlap therefore stays high out to a large fraction of 204 cm. [Terrazas et al. 2005, PMC6725460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6725460)
- Terrazas et al. 2005 also notes that on a ring, the function "can be distorted by sampling noise and by the periodic boundary conditions", and in their simulations it reaches its minimum at 180°. It cites Maurer et al. 2005 (then in press) only for "absolute place field size is invariant over changes in track size", with no dorsal-versus-middle numbers. [Terrazas et al. 2005, PMC6725460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6725460)
- Citation: Terrazas A, Krause M, Lipa P, Gothard KM, Barnes CA, McNaughton BL (2005). Self-motion and the hippocampal spatial metric. J Neurosci 25(35):8085. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0693-05.2005.
- I ran a full-text Europe PMC query for "population vector correlation(s)" AND ("ventral CA1" OR "ventral CA3" OR "intermediate hippocampus" OR "temporal hippocampus" OR "ventral hippocampal place") AND distance. It returned eight records, and none is, by title, a ventral or intermediate PV-versus-distance study (Dudok 2021, Kinsky 2025, Ma 2023, Zemla 2022, Zutshi 2022, Huszár 2022, Gauthier 2018, an ACNP abstract book). [Europe PMC query results](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=(%22population%20vector%20correlation%22%20OR%20%22population%20vector%20correlations%22)%20AND%20(%22ventral%20CA1%22%20OR%20%22ventral%20CA3%22%20OR%20%22intermediate%20hippocampus%22%20OR%20%22temporal%20hippocampus%22%20OR%20%22ventral%20hippocampal%20place%22)%20AND%20distance&format=json&pageSize=60&resultType=lite)

### Inferences
- The ventral part of the Keinath sentence rests on single-cell scales (Kjelstrup 2008, fields over 10 m in ventral cells) and small ventral ensembles (Royer 2010, Komorowski 2013, 2 to 5 cells per ensemble). The only population measurement behind it is Maurer 2005, dorsal versus middle. "Ventral PVs stay correlated over larger distances" is therefore an extrapolation from single cells, not a measured ventral kernel.
- Maurer 2005 and Terrazas 2005 come from the same group in the same year, so Maurer probably used the same overlap measure (normalized dot product, scale at 0.5). If so, its far-distance floor is above zero by construction, because non-mean-subtracted overlap of non-negative rates cannot go to zero while cells fire anywhere. I have not verified the method in Maurer.
- Terrazas 2005 is the only checked source where a dorsal CA1 population kernel spans a large part of the environment, and only under abnormal self-motion (WORLD, and CAR at about 3 times WALK). The same cells under normal walking give the narrow kernel. In this dataset the kernel width depends on self-motion input, not on region alone.

### Gaps
- Maurer 2005 numbers (decorrelation distances for dorsal and middle CA1, track type and length, N rats, N cells) need the full text, which is paywalled at Wiley. The decorrelation distances are unverified.
- The Terrazas 2005 WALK, CAR and WORLD 0.5-crossing distances are figure only.
- I found no ventral or intermediate PV-versus-distance curve from Kjelstrup 2008 (18 m track), Royer 2010, Komorowski 2013, Jimenez 2018 or Ciocchi 2015. Ciocchi 2015 was not checked directly.
- A web search for ventral CA1 PV correlation against distance, with calcium imaging on linear tracks, also returned no such study. The results were dorsal CA1 or unrelated.

## Other regions: MEC, LEC, lateral septum, retrosplenial cortex, subiculum, primate hippocampus

### Takeaway
Retrosplenial cortex has a measured curve. RSC PV correlation on a 150 cm treadmill "steeply falls to zero over a short distance", about 35 cm, close to CA1 recorded the same way. For MEC I found only a distance-from-wall analysis of border cells. For LEC, lateral septum and primate hippocampus I found no PV-versus-distance measurement.

### Cited findings

#### Retrosplenial cortex
- Mao, Kandler, McNaughton & Bonin 2017. Mice, two-photon calcium imaging of superficial agranular RSC, head-fixed on a 150 cm linear treadmill with tactile cues. Each cell's position map was scaled to 0-1. "A population vector was constructed using the occupancy-normalized trial-averaged activity of all place cells." Similarity was the "pairwise Pearson's linear correlation coefficient". The track was split into 100 bins (1.5 cm each), smoothed with a Gaussian of 4.5 cm SD. Only place cells were included (SI above the 95th percentile of shuffles, plus field criteria). [Mao et al. 2017, PMC5557927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557927)
- Mao et al. 2017 result. Fig. 3d plots "population vector correlation (Pearson correlation coefficient) as a function of distance for RSC (black) and CA1 (red) place cells", and "the periodicity occurred because of the periodicity of the track". The text reports "a steep drop in correlation with distance", and the Discussion says it "steeply falls to zero over a short distance". According to the fetch summary, the Discussion puts that distance at about 35 cm, which I could not confirm verbatim. The paper says "the correlation structure of RSC activity closely resembled that of CA1", and the three RSC subregions showed "a similar steep drop" (Fig. 4b). [Mao et al. 2017, PMC5557927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557927)
- Arithmetic: 35 cm / 150 cm = 0.23 of the track. On a 150 cm loop the largest separation is 75 cm, so 35 / 75 = 0.47 of the maximum separation.
- Mao et al. 2017 sample. RSC had 2,256 neurons from 4 wild-type mice in 14 sessions, of which 297 (about 13%) were place cells. Thy1-GP4.3 mice (n = 3) gave 1,416 of 2,544. CA1 had 1,758 imaged neurons (2 wild-type, 1 Thy1) plus 337 electrophysiology neurons from 8 mice, for 611 place cells in total. The legend does not say whether the Fig. 3d CA1 curve uses imaging, electrophysiology or both, and no far-distance value appears in the text. [Mao et al. 2017, PMC5557927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5557927)
- Citation: Mao D, Kandler S, McNaughton BL, Bonin V (2017). Sparse orthogonal population representation of spatial context in the retrosplenial cortex. Nature Communications 8:243. doi:10.1038/s41467-017-00180-9.

#### Medial entorhinal cortex and RSC border cells
- van Wijngaarden et al. 2020 (rats, tetrodes, some RSC sessions with a 64-channel silicon probe, 1 m² open field) plots "Population vector correlation of firings rates, binned according to the wall distance for border cells in RSC (left) and MEC (right)" (Figure 6, figure supplement 1A,B). "Population vector correlations decay from the diagonal to distal bins at a similar rate for MEC and RSC border cells" over 0-20 cm. "In the larger distance range, decay is stronger for MEC". The distance axis is distance from the wall (bins up to 50 cm), not the separation between two positions. Values, cell counts and construction are not in the text I could read. RSC came from 8 rats (5,415 neurons, 82 sessions). [van Wijngaarden et al. 2020, PMC7609058](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7609058)
- Citation: van Wijngaarden JB, et al. (2020). Entorhinal-retrosplenial circuits for allocentric-egocentric transformation of boundary coding. eLife 9:e59816. doi:10.7554/eLife.59816. Co-authors are not verified.
- Diehl et al. 2017 (grid and non-grid MEC cells) computes population-vector cross-correlations for grid cells across environment changes. A search summary indicates no distance analysis for non-grid cells, which I did not verify in the full text. [Diehl et al. 2017, PMC5444540](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5444540)

#### Lateral septum
- Wirtshafter & Wilson 2020 recorded rat CA1 and caudodorsal lateral septum together during a rewarded task. LS place cells are rarer than in CA1 but "similar to the hippocampus in field size and number of fields per cell", with fields skewed toward reward. The search summaries mention no PV-versus-distance analysis, and I did not open the full text. [Wirtshafter & Wilson 2020, eLife 9:e55252](https://elifesciences.org/articles/55252v1)

#### Primate hippocampus
- Yan, Huang, Cao & Mao 2026 (macaques, physical vs virtual navigation) has no PV correlation against physical distance. Its PV correlation (Fig. 4c) is plotted against "differential bin count (Δ progression)" between task blocks. Position is binned 20 × 20 but used only for spatial information and tuning. [Yan et al. 2026, PMC13276072](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13276072)
- Citation: Yan W, Huang Y, Cao X, Mao D (2026). Divergent neural representations of space and task between physical and virtual navigation in macaques. Nature Communications 17:5365. doi:10.1038/s41467-026-72141-0.
- Courellis et al. 2019 (freely moving marmosets) came back with no match when I searched its Europe PMC full text (157,994 characters) for "population vector" or "PV" within 200 characters of a distance term. A different similarity analysis could still be present under other wording. [Courellis et al. 2019, PMC6922474](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6922474)

#### Subiculum
- Covered above (Ledergerber 2021 narrower peak than CA1, figure only; Kim 2012 and Nakai 2024 no curve).

### Inferences
- The RSC kernel on a 150 cm treadmill (about 35 cm, or 23% of the loop) is in the same range as the dorsal CA1 values in the first pass (30-40 cm, 15-22% of 1-2 m tracks). Both reach about zero within about one field width. In this dataset the RSC curve gives no sign of a long tail.
- The van Wijngaarden curves measure how the population code changes with distance from a wall. They cannot answer the question of similarity between two arbitrary positions.

### Gaps
- No empirical PV-versus-separation curve for MEC grid or non-grid populations was found. The first pass's grid result is a theory preprint.
- I found no LEC PV-versus-distance measurement. I did not search LEC separately beyond the combined Europe PMC query.
- Mao et al. 2021 (macaque, 3.3 m arena, Neuron) was not checked for a PV analysis in this pass.
- The ~35 cm RSC figure comes from a model summary of the Discussion and is not a verbatim quote. Fig. 3d CA1 and RSC far-distance values are figure only.

## Recent Neuropixels or large-scale imaging studies (2018-2026) with PV similarity against distance across a full or large environment

### Takeaway
I found no 2018-2026 study that plots PV similarity against separation across a whole environment larger than about 2 m. The closest large-scale study is Maimon et al. 2026 (Nature, bats, about 174-194 m tunnels). It correlates population vectors between trajectories with different histories, not between positions, and finds history-dependent differences lasting over 90 m.

### Cited findings
- Maimon et al. 2026 recorded dorsal CA1 and CA3 in Egyptian fruit bats with a wireless neural logger in tunnels of 200 m (effective 194 m), 130 m, 180 m (effective 174 m) and 193 m (multicompartment). 15 bats were recorded, 5 with both CA1 and CA3. [Maimon et al. 2026, PMC13421320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)
- Maimon et al. 2026 PV construction. Each cell's map was "normalized separately to a firing-rate range of 0-1, by dividing the smoothed map by its peak firing rate". "The population vectors were then smoothed by averaging over a sliding window of 10 m (boxcar smoothing), with 0.2 m steps". Pearson correlation was computed between pairs of PVs at each position, using "the entire place cells population… pooled over all of the recording days of the bat". Confidence intervals come from 50% draws of neurons, 1,000 repetitions. [Maimon et al. 2026, PMC13421320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)
- Maimon et al. 2026 results. "The retrospective signal lasted >90 m after the bat has left the junction". In bat 2 the correlation between from-straight and from-turn PVs reached 0.8 at x = 88.5 m, 91.5 m from the junction (Extended Data Fig. 13a), and the effect "sometimes reached as far as 130 m". CA3 has "much fewer fields than in CA1", and its retrospective curve was less robust after matching cell numbers (Fig. 5g, 50 cells, 1,000 repetitions). Cells per analysis included 316 forward and 334 returning CA1 place cells pooled across bats, and 145 and 126 CA3. [Maimon et al. 2026, PMC13421320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13421320)
- Arithmetic: 91.5 m / 174 m effective length = 0.53 of the tunnel. This is the distance over which two codes for the same place stay distinguishable by history. It is not a spatial similarity length.
- Citation: Maimon SR, Eliav T, Aljadeff J, Shalev A, Gronich Y, Finger NM, Eveland KE, Moss CF, Las L, Ulanovsky N (2026). Sparse-to-dense coding transformation between hippocampal areas CA3 and CA1. Nature 655(8125):1242-1251. doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0.
- Bjerknes et al. 2018 (developing and adult rats, CA1, 200 cm linear track, 5 cm bins, unsmoothed maps for PV, bins with fewer than 10 cells omitted) reports PV-matrix offsets from the start and end diagonals and displacement in bins. It does not report a decorrelation length. Adult lights-on displacement was 5 bins (25 cm), and lights-off was 2 bins. 31 rats on the track, 76 adult place cells with lights on. [Bjerknes et al. 2018, PMC5816199](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5816199)
- A web search for Neuropixels hippocampal PV correlation against distance in large environments (2023-2025) returned only the Nagelhus et al. 2023 object-distance analysis already in the first pass (150 × 150 cm, 2 of 16 sessions Neuropixels). [Nagelhus et al. 2023, Neuron](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(23)00270-2)
- A Europe PMC query for "population vector correlation" with a distance phrase and a non-dorsal region term returned 15 records. Beyond the papers covered here, it listed Farooq 2024 (Nat Commun), Resnik 2012 and Cabral 2014 (J Neurosci), Widloski 2022 (Neuron), Bladon 2019, Duan 2025, Petersen 2020, Wolcott 2025 and Attinger 2025 (bioRxiv). By title, all are dorsal CA1 or cortical studies in small environments, and I did not open them. ### Inferences
- In this follow-up, the only population similarity measured over distances of tens of meters is history-dependent (Maimon 2026), and it was smoothed over 10 m. A 10 m boxcar would hide any kernel narrower than a few meters, so this measure cannot show the within-trajectory spatial kernel at those scales.
- Across the checked literature, no rodent or bat dataset under normal locomotion shows a population similarity curve that declines gradually across the whole environment. The two candidates for broad similarity are Terrazas 2005 under altered self-motion and the untested ventral extrapolation from single cells.

### Gaps
- I could not find a Neuropixels or large-field imaging study that plots PV similarity against separation in an open field or a track longer than 2 m. Large public datasets (Rich 2014, Eliav 2021, Harland 2021, Tanni 2022, Maimon 2026) could support such an analysis, but none publishes it.
- "A hippocampal digital twin reveals latent structure in freely flying bats" (bioRxiv 2026, PMC13742630) appeared in a Europe PMC query and was not checked. It is a preprint.
- Gonzalez et al. 2019 (Science, doi:10.1126/science.aav9199) has no PMC full text in Europe PMC and remains unchecked, as in the first pass.
