# How independent are hippocampal population codes across environments? Global, partial and rate remapping, quantified

Notes on sourcing. Values marked "full text" were read from the paper text (PMC/Europe PMC XML or a PDF copy). Some full-text reads went through an automated page reader that stops near 100,000 characters, so values shown only in figures or supplements were often not reached; these are listed under Gaps. Values marked "abstract only" come from the PubMed/Europe PMC abstract. Values marked "secondary" were taken from a later paper or review and were not checked against the primary source. I could not get the full text of Alme et al. 2014, Leutgeb et al. 2005 (Science), Fyhn et al. 2007, Colgin et al. 2008, Kubie & Muller 1991, Lu et al. 2015 or Lee et al. 2020 (Cell). For those, only the abstract or significance statement was verified.

Abbreviations used below: PV is population vector, MEC/LEC are medial/lateral entorhinal cortex, GC is dentate granule cell, MC is mossy cell, VR is virtual reality.

## Q1. PV correlations, rate-map correlations and shared active cells: between environments vs the same environment on different occasions

### Takeaway
Between different rooms, CA3 codes are at chance on every measure reported: active-set overlap 0.11 to 0.14 against 0.12 to 0.14 expected for independence (Leutgeb 2004), rate-map correlations above the shuffled 95th percentile in about 3% of cell pairs (GoodSmith 2017), and maps uncorrelated across all 55 room pairs (Alme 2014). Different VR tracks give a CA1 PV correlation of about 0.1 (Gauthier & Tank 2018). The same environment is far from a correlation of 1 when revisited. CA1 overlap on repeat tests in the same room was 0.63 to 0.66 (Leutgeb 2004), only 15 to 25% of active CA1 cells overlap between imaging days (Ziv 2013), and CA2 PV correlation falls to about 0.35 within 18 h.

### Cited findings

Classic single-cell era (rat, dorsal hippocampus, single electrodes or stereotrodes, foraging in enclosures)
- Muller & Kubie 1987. Rat, dorsal hippocampal complex-spike cells. Standard apparatus was a gray cylinder 76 cm in diameter with 51 cm walls and a white cue card covering 100 degrees of arc. When the cylinder size was doubled and the same cells were recorded in both, 36% of cells had fields that scaled (same angular and relative radial position), 52% showed very different firing patterns, and 12% were virtually silent in both. Between a circular and a rectangular floor plan, "the firing pattern of a cell in an apparatus of one shape could not be predicted from a knowledge of the firing pattern in the other shape." Removing the cue card left field size, shape and radial position unchanged but rotated fields to unpredictable angles. Abstract only; N rats, N cells and the rectangle dimensions were not in the available text. Source: [Muller RU, Kubie JL (1987) The effects of changes in the environment on the spatial firing of hippocampal complex-spike cells. J Neurosci 7:1951-1968](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.07-07-01951.1987)
- Bostock, Muller & Kubie 1991. Rat, 36 place cells, cylinder with a white cue card replaced by a black card of the same size and shape. On the first black-card exposures most cells kept similar fields. On later exposures the white-card and black-card patterns became distinct. Once this differentiation had happened in a rat, every cell recorded afterwards from that rat had different firing patterns under the two cards. This shows that whether two environments get independent maps depends on experience. Abstract only; the number of exposures and rats needed was not in the available text. Source: [Bostock E, Muller RU, Kubie JL (1991) Experience-dependent modifications of hippocampal place cell firing. Hippocampus 1:193-205](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450010207)
- Kubie & Muller 1991, as summarized by Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020 (secondary). Each environment recruits a small, apparently random subset of place cells, about 20% of pyramidal cells (citing Thompson & Best 1989). The active subsets for two environments behave as independent samples. "Complete remapping" was defined as no relationship between the firing patterns of cells active in both environments. The original 1991 text (Hippocampus 1:240-242) was not available to me, and Europe PMC lists no abstract. Sources: [Kubie JL, Muller RU (1991) Multiple representations in the hippocampus. Hippocampus 1:240-242](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450010305); [Kubie JL, Levy ERJ, Fenton AA (2020) Is hippocampal remapping the physiological basis for context? Hippocampus 30:851-864](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160)
- O'Keefe & Conway 1978, as cited in Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020 (secondary). Of 34 units recorded on both a platform and an elevated T-maze, 15 had fields in both. Kubie & Ranck 1983 (also secondary via the same review) recorded cells in a home cage, an operant chamber and an eight-arm maze and found that field location in one setting did not predict location in the others. Source: [Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160)

Ensemble tetrode era (rat, CA3 vs CA1)
- Leutgeb et al. 2004. Rat, tetrodes, 146 CA3 and 244 CA1 pyramidal cells in 10 rats, including 64 CA3 and 76 contralateral CA1 cells recorded simultaneously. Enclosures were a large square, a small square and a small circle, tested in three rooms (A, B, C). Cells counted as active above 0.25 Hz. Overlap was measured with an active-set measure corrected for the different sparsity of CA3 and CA1, and compared with the value expected for independent firing.
  - CA3, familiar rooms A vs B: measured overlap 0.11 to 0.14 across the four A/B comparisons, against 0.12 to 0.14 expected for independent firing (t between 0.1 and 1.2, n.s.).
  - CA3, novel room C vs A or B: 0.16 vs 0.14 expected (t(83) = 0.79, n.s.), so the new map was decorrelated from the first trial. The new CA3 map only stabilized after 20 to 30 min.
  - CA1, identical square boxes in rooms A and B (high similarity): overlap 0.55 for AB, against 0.63 for AA and 0.66 for BB on repeated tests in the same room.
  - CA1, squares of different size (medium similarity): 0.43.
  - CA1, boxes differing in size and shape (low similarity): 0.32 vs 0.28 expected from random permutations, no longer distinguishable from independence. Per-animal values: high similarity 0.56 and 0.54; medium 0.53, 0.45 and 0.32; low 0.41, 0.34 and 0.27. F(2,140) = 6.0, P < 0.005.
  - CA1, novel room C vs A/B: 0.38 vs 0.24 expected (t(104) = 4.6, P < 0.001).
  - Decoding: the PV distribution carried 0.54 ± 0.06 bits about room identity in CA3 vs 0.30 ± 0.05 bits in CA1.
  - Full text: Science Express version, 22 July 2004, read from a journal-club PDF copy. Source: [Leutgeb S, Leutgeb JK, Treves A, Moser MB, Moser EI (2004) Distinct ensemble codes in hippocampal areas CA3 and CA1. Science 305:1295-1298](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1100265); PDF copy at [hu-berlin.de](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/~kempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb04.pdf)
- Leutgeb et al. 2005 (Science). In the "variable cue, constant place" condition (the box is changed but stays in the same location), active cells kept their firing locations while their rates changed, often by more than an order of magnitude. This is rate remapping. In the "variable place, constant cue" condition (an identical box in different places/rooms), both location and rate changed, "so that population vectors for a given location in the chamber were statistically independent." This is global remapping. Abstract only; the numerical PV correlations were not retrievable. Source: [Leutgeb S, Leutgeb JK, Barnes CA, Moser EI, McNaughton BL, Moser MB (2005) Independent codes for spatial and episodic memory in hippocampal neuronal ensembles. Science 309:619-623](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1114037)
- Alme et al. 2014. Rat, dorsal CA3, tetrodes, 7 rats, 342 well-isolated cells, an average of 30 active cells per animal. Rats foraged in 11 square boxes of nearly identical geometry, each in a different room (1 familiar, 10 novel), over two days, which gives 55 room pairs per animal. Representations were "highly correlated between repeated tests in the same room but remained orthogonal across all combinations of different rooms, with minimal overlap in the active cell samples from each environment." "A low proportion of cells had activity in many rooms but the firing locations of these cells were completely uncorrelated." Verified from the abstract and significance statement only. A search snippet (not verified in the text) gives an activity threshold of 0.10 Hz. A reanalysis preprint reports that 210 of the 342 cells were active in at least one room. Sources: [Alme CB, Miao C, Jezek K, Treves A, Moser EI, Moser MB (2014) Place cells in the hippocampus: eleven maps for eleven rooms. PNAS 111:18428-18435](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421056111); [Jain B, Elliott S (2020) Correlation across environments encoded by hippocampal place cells. arXiv:2012.14559, PREPRINT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14559)
- GoodSmith et al. 2017. Rat, tetrodes plus juxtacellular recordings. Four environments, each in a different room: a rough black square 109 x 109 cm, a smooth gray square 107 x 107 cm, a textured green octagon 109 cm across and a smooth black circle 117 cm in diameter. A cell counted as active with at least 75 spikes per 15 to 20 min session, spatial information above 0.5 bits/spike and p < 0.01 against a shuffle. The initial dataset had 37 GCL, 23 hilus and 28 CA3 cells; the expanded dataset had 112 GCL, 33 hilus and 97 CA3 cells.
  - Fraction active per environment: GCL 9%, hilus 88%, CA3 29%.
  - Mean number of environments, out of 4, in which a cell had a field: GCL 0.35 ± 0.09, hilus 3.52 ± 0.21, CA3 1.14 ± 0.18. 25 of 37 GCL cells were silent in all four environments, and only one GCL cell was active in more than one room. 17 of 23 hilus cells were active in all four.
  - Rate overlap between environments: GCL 0.15 ± 0.02 and CA3 0.13 ± 0.01, neither different from shuffles (GCL p = 0.45, CA3 p = 0.43). Hilus 0.31 ± 0.02, above all shuffles.
  - Rate-map correlation, taken as the maximum over 36 rotations in 10 degree steps: the fraction of cell pairs above the shuffled 95th percentile was GCL 4/90 (4.44%), hilus 8/189 (4.23%) and CA3 7/227 (3.08%). All are at or below the 5% expected by chance, so even the hilar cells that fired in every room had unrelated field locations.
  - Full text. Source: [GoodSmith D, Chen X, Wang C, Kim SH, Song H, Burgalossi A, Christian KM, Knierim JJ (2017) Spatial representations of granule cells and mossy cells of the dentate gyrus. Neuron 93:677-690](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026)

Calcium imaging (mouse CA1)
- Gauthier & Tank 2018. Mouse, CA1 and subiculum, two-photon imaging in VR. Track A was 400 cm with reward at 366 cm; track B was 250 cm with reward at 229 cm. 12 mice were used (5 in the A/B condition).
  - PV correlation between tracks A and B: 0.099 (95% CI 0.094 to 0.103). The paper contrasts this with 0.22 reported by Danielson et al. 2016 (secondary; I did not check which Danielson 2016 paper or measure).
  - In a same-track condition where reward moved between 366 and 166 cm, 7.9% of all recorded cells had fields in both conditions that "remapped to new, apparently random locations."
  - Full text read by the automated reader. Source: [Gauthier JL, Tank DW (2018) A dedicated population for reward coding in the hippocampus. Neuron 99:179-193](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.06.008)
- Plitt & Giocomo 2021. Mouse, CA1, two-photon imaging, 12 mice (6 "rare morph", 6 "frequent morph"), 161 to 3,657 putative pyramidal cells per session. VR tracks were 450 cm long, with wall texture, background color and tower color morphed together between two extremes (morph values 0 to 1). Between the two extreme environments, 6.07% (rare) and 1.98% (frequent) of cells had significant spatial information in both, against chance levels of 4.78% and 1.06% computed for independent assignment. The authors call the two maps "largely independent." Full text (NIH manuscript). Source: [Plitt MH, Giocomo LM (2021) Experience-dependent contextual codes in the hippocampus. Nat Neurosci 24:705-714](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00816-6)
- Rubin et al. 2015. Mouse, CA1, head-mounted miniscope, 5 mice, more than 1,000 cells per mouse. Environment A was a straight 96 cm track; environment B was an L-shaped track with two 48 cm arms. They differed in shape, floor texture, visual cues, odor and reward flavor, and were imaged every other day for 15 days. Between-environment PV correlations were described as "low" and stable over time, but numerical values were not in the text that was read. Only 7.4% (A) and 7.2% (B) of cells were active on all days. Within-environment correlations from sessions two weeks apart stayed higher than same-day across-environment correlations. Full text, partly read. Source: [Rubin A, Geva N, Sheintuch L, Ziv Y (2015) Hippocampal ensemble dynamics timestamp events in long-term memory. eLife 4:e12247](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.12247)
- Cai et al. 2016. Mouse, CA1, miniscope, 8 young mice. Three novel contexts of about 800 cm² each, differing in shape, odor and visual cues. More CA1 cells were reactivated in the same context than in a different one (t(3) = 6.305, p = 0.0081, n = 4). Ensemble overlap between two different contexts was higher when they were explored 5 h apart than 7 days apart (t(7) = 3.83, p = 0.0065). In a TetTag immediate-early-gene experiment, overlap normalized to chance was above chance at 5 h (p = 0.0043) and not above chance at 7 days. Overlap percentages appear only in figures and were not read. Source: [Cai DJ, Aharoni D, Shuman T, et al. (2016) A shared neural ensemble links distinct contextual memories encoded close in time. Nature 534:115-118](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17955)

Same environment on different occasions (baseline for "no remapping")
- Ziv et al. 2013. Mouse, CA1, miniscope, familiar environment imaged over weeks. Each day the environment was represented by a different subset of cells, and the overlap between any two daily subsets was about 15 to 25%. Cells in the overlap kept their place fields. Abstract only. Source: [Ziv Y, Burns LD, Cocker ED, et al. (2013) Long-term dynamics of CA1 hippocampal place codes. Nat Neurosci 16:264-266](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329)
- Mankin et al. 2012. Rat. When the same event is repeated hours to days apart, CA1 population activity becomes progressively more different, while CA3 firing patterns stay highly reproducible regardless of the interval. Abstract only. Source: [Mankin EA, Sparks FT, Slayyeh B, Sutherland RJ, Leutgeb S, Leutgeb JK (2012) Neuronal code for extended time in the hippocampus. PNAS 109:19462-19467](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1214107109)
- Kinsky et al. 2018. Mouse, CA1, miniscope, 4 mice analyzed (Methods say 5, with 1 excluded), 194 to 548 neurons per 10 min session. Arenas were a 25 x 25 cm square and an octagon about 28 cm across. Even within the same arena, some session pairs showed global remapping: per mouse, 36%, 14%, 0% and 0% of pairs in the square and 21%, 7%, 14% and 0% in the octagon. Full text. Source: [Kinsky NR, Sullivan DW, Mau W, Hasselmo ME, Eichenbaum HB (2018) Hippocampal place fields maintain a coherent and flexible map across long timescales. Curr Biol 28:3578-3588](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.09.037)
- Sheintuch et al. 2020. Mouse, CA1, calcium imaging. Neurons globally remapped between separate visits to the same familiar environment, forming multiple distinct maps of one context. Switching happened only between visits, and each map stayed spatially informative and persisted for weeks. Abstract only. Source: [Sheintuch L, Geva N, Baumer H, Rechavi Y, Rubin A, Ziv Y (2020) Multiple maps of the same spatial context can stably coexist in the mouse hippocampus. Curr Biol 30:1467-1476](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.02.018)
- Law et al. 2016, as cited in Sanders et al. 2020 (secondary). The spatial correlation of hippocampal maps across repeated presentations of the same environment starts low and rises over training days. Source: [Sanders H, Wilson MA, Gershman SJ (2020) Hippocampal remapping as hidden state inference. eLife 9:e51140](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51140)

### Inferences
- Measured with active-set overlap, as in Leutgeb 2004 and GoodSmith 2017, CA3 codes for different rooms are indistinguishable from independent random draws. CA1 can stay well above chance (0.55 vs 0.63 to 0.66 for repeat tests in the same room) when the enclosures are identical.
- The "same environment" baseline is itself far below 1 and drops with elapsed time, especially in CA1 and CA2 and in calcium-imaging data. A between-environment correlation therefore has to be read against both a shuffle floor and a time-matched same-environment ceiling.

- I could not get the numeric PV and rate-map correlations of Alme 2014 (per-pair values, same-room repeat values, the active-cell overlap distribution), Leutgeb 2005 Science or Fyhn 2007, because the full text was not accessible.
- Rubin 2015, Cai 2016 and Kinsky 2018 show their key correlation and overlap values in figures that the text extraction did not reach.

## Q2. Regional differences: CA1, CA3, DG, CA2, subiculum, entorhinal cortex, ventral hippocampus, lateral septum, and species

### Takeaway
Proximal CA3 orthogonalizes most strongly. CA1 keeps more shared structure when sensory input is shared. CA2 and distal CA3 remap less between environments, and CA2 tracks elapsed time more than context. Dentate granule cells are so sparse that cell sets barely overlap, while mossy cells fire in every environment with unrelated field positions. Studies disagree on how much granule-cell maps change between contexts. Subiculum (Sharp 1997) and MEC grid cells keep their structure: grids shift and rotate coherently rather than scrambling. Human fMRI shows environment-specific patterns in CA3/DG more than in CA1. I found no quantitative cross-environment remapping data for bats, macaques or birds.

### Cited findings

CA3 vs CA1
- Leutgeb 2004: CA3 overlap between rooms was at the level expected for independence, while CA1 overlap rose with enclosure similarity (numbers in Q1). The authors note that this may explain why remapping in visually identical boxes was complete in a study that recorded mostly CA3 cells (Tanila 1999) but partial in one that recorded CA1 (Skaggs & McNaughton 1998). Source: [Leutgeb et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1100265)
- Skaggs & McNaughton 1998. Rat, CA1, 4 rats, 10 to 39 cells per population, two nearly identical boxes joined by a corridor. A higher-than-chance fraction of cells had similarly shaped fields in both boxes while others had completely different fields. The level of correlation "differed greatly across rats and, for three of the four rats, across recording sessions." Abstract only. Source: [Skaggs WE, McNaughton BL (1998) Spatial firing properties of hippocampal CA1 populations in an environment containing two visually identical regions. J Neurosci 18:8455-8466](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-20-08455.1998)

CA3 transverse axis and CA2
- Lu et al. 2015. Rat. Along the CA3-to-CA2 axis there is a progressive loss in the ability to distinguish configurations of the same environment and "a reduction in the extent to which place cells formed uncorrelated representations for different environments." The change is nonlinear, with the sharpest drop in distal CA3. Abstract only; numeric PV values not retrieved. Source: [Lu L, Igarashi KM, Witter MP, Moser EI, Moser MB (2015) Topography of place maps along the CA3-to-CA2 axis of the hippocampus. Neuron 87:1078-1092](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.07.007)
- Lee et al. 2015. Rat, local vs global cue conflict. Proximal CA3, near the DG, showed degraded representations resembling DG pattern separation. Distal CA3 and CA2 kept coherent representations. Abstract only. Source: [Lee H, Wang C, Deshmukh SS, Knierim JJ (2015) Neural population evidence of functional heterogeneity along the CA3 transverse axis: pattern completion versus pattern separation. Neuron 87:1093-1105](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.07.012)
- Mankin et al. 2015. Rat, tetrodes, 8 rats. Boxes were an 80 x 80 cm square and a 16-sided polygon of 50 cm radius, in the same room, run in two daily blocks for 2 days. Two-shape paradigm: CA2 62 cells (5 rats), CA1 43 + 46 cells, CA3 42 + 29 cells. Proportion of cells active per session: CA1 59.2%, CA2 68.2%, CA3 48.1%. CA2 shape-preference scores did not differ from shuffled shape labels (only 67.9% of shuffles were lower than the real mean), so CA2 did not code the circle/square difference. The CA2 same-shape PV correlation fell monotonically with elapsed time up to 18 h and then plateaued at about 0.35. In CA2 the change after 6 h already exceeded the change caused by switching shape with no time lag. Full text read by the automated reader; CA1 and CA3 numeric PV values were not reached. Source: [Mankin EA, Diehl GW, Sparks FT, Leutgeb S, Leutgeb JK (2015) Hippocampal CA2 activity patterns change over time to a larger extent than between spatial contexts. Neuron 85:190-201](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.001)

Dentate gyrus
- Leutgeb et al. 2007. Rat. Small changes in enclosure shape substantially altered correlated activity among place-modulated dentate cells. When environments were made more different, CA3 recruited new cell populations but the DG did not, which suggests two decorrelation mechanisms: changed coincidence patterns in DG and recruitment of nonoverlapping assemblies in CA3. Abstract only. Source: [Leutgeb JK, Leutgeb S, Moser MB, Moser EI (2007) Pattern separation in the dentate gyrus and CA3 of the hippocampus. Science 315:961-966](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135801)
- GoodSmith 2017 (rat, numbers in Q1). Granule cells were very sparse: 9% active per environment, and most were silent in all four rooms. Mossy cells (hilus) were active in most environments (88% per environment, 3.52 of 4) but their fields were in unrelated locations. The paper describes two mechanisms: "sparse coding in granule cells and changes in firing field locations in mossy cells." Source: [GoodSmith et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026)
- Neunuebel & Knierim 2012. Rat. Cells with multiple fields found below the granule layer were proposed to be hilar cells or newborn granule cells, and single-field cells to be mature granule cells. Abstract only. Source: [Neunuebel JP, Knierim JJ (2012) Spatial firing correlates of physiologically distinct cell types of the rat dentate gyrus. J Neurosci 32:3848-3858](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.6038-11.2012)
- Senzai & Buzsaki 2017. Mouse, silicon probes, 16 mice (6 in open-field mazes), 586 putative GCs and 500 putative MCs. Mazes A, B and A' (a repeat of A) were in the same room and differed in floor, wall cues and reward. Remapping index = corr(A, A') minus the mean of corr(A, B) and corr(B, A'), using pixel-wise rate-map correlation. Cells with an index of at least 0.2 counted as remapping: 36.7% of GC place cells (11/30), 61.3% of MCs (68/111) and 55.6% of CA3 pyramidal cells (80/144). Population remapping, from 1,000 random draws of 20 cells, was smaller for GCs than for MCs or CA3 (P < 0.00001). Full text read by the automated reader. Source: [Senzai Y, Buzsaki G (2017) Physiological properties and behavioral correlates of hippocampal granule cells and mossy cells. Neuron 93:691-704](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.011)
- Danielson et al. 2016. Mouse, two-photon imaging of the DG. When mice were presented with different contexts, "mature granule cells underwent robust remapping of their spatial representations," and the few spatially tuned adult-born cells remapped to a similar degree. Abstract only. Source: [Danielson NB, Kaifosh P, Zaremba JD, et al. (2016) Distinct contribution of adult-born hippocampal granule cells to context encoding. Neuron 90:101-112](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.02.019)
- Hainmueller & Bartos 2018. Mouse, head-fixed VR, chronic two-photon imaging of DG, CA1 and CA3 (possibly including some CA2), about 500 neurons per field of view. A familiar 4 m track was compared with a visually distinct novel track. Place-cell map correlations between the two contexts were higher in DG than in CA1 and CA2/3. PV correlations between contexts were lower than within the familiar context in CA1 and CA2/3 (P = 0.004) but not in DG (P = 0.051). The authors state that CA2/3 activity discriminated contexts most. Over 5 days, DG fields in the familiar context stayed stable, while CA1 and CA2/3 correlations decayed fast, with CA2/3 dropping below chance after more than two days. Full text read by the automated reader. Source: [Hainmueller T, Bartos M (2018) Parallel emergence of stable and dynamic memory engrams in the hippocampus. Nature 558:292-296](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0191-2)

Subiculum
- Sharp 1997. Rat, cylinder vs square of equal area, painted either similar or very different for different rats. "The subicular cells showed very similar firing patterns in the two chambers, regardless of whether they were visually similar or different." Simultaneously studied hippocampal place cells remapped. Abstract only. Source: [Sharp PE (1997) Subicular cells generate similar spatial firing patterns in two geometrically and visually distinctive environments: comparison with hippocampal place cells. Behav Brain Res 85:71-92](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-4328(96)00165-9)
- Lever et al. 2009 reported boundary vector cells in the rat subiculum. The abstract does not quantify cross-environment stability. Source: [Lever C, Burton S, Jeewajee A, O'Keefe J, Burgess N (2009) Boundary vector cells in the subiculum of the hippocampal formation. J Neurosci 29:9771-9777](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1319-09.2009)
- Gauthier & Tank 2018 imaged CA1 and subiculum. Across VR environments most cells remapped randomly, but a dedicated reward-cell pool was reused (Q5). Source: [Gauthier & Tank 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.06.008)

Entorhinal cortex
- Fyhn et al. 2007. Rat, MEC grid cells recorded with hippocampal cells. Rate remapping in the hippocampus came with stable grid fields. Global remapping "is always accompanied by a coordinate shift in the firing vertices of the grid cells." Grid fields of co-localized MEC cells "move and rotate in concert," so local grid ensembles keep a constant spatial phase structure across environments. Abstract only; shift and rotation magnitudes and N were not retrieved. Source: [Fyhn M, Hafting T, Treves A, Moser MB, Moser EI (2007) Hippocampal remapping and grid realignment in entorhinal cortex. Nature 446:190-194](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05601)
- Diehl et al. 2017. Rat, MEC. Nearly all of the two-thirds of MEC cells that are not grid, border or head-direction cells are spatially selective ("nongrid spatial cells"). These cells completely reorganized their spatial firing when box shape or color changed, while grid cells kept their spatial alignment and mainly redistributed firing rates across their fields. Abstract only. Source: [Diehl GW, Hon OJ, Leutgeb S, Leutgeb JK (2017) Grid and nongrid cells in medial entorhinal cortex represent spatial location and environmental features with complementary coding schemes. Neuron 94:83-92](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.03.004)
- Lu et al. 2013. Rat. Lesions of the LEC regions that project most densely to the CA3 recording site impaired CA3 rate remapping induced by changes in box shape or color. Rate remapping was not observed in LEC itself. Abstract only. Source: [Lu L, Leutgeb JK, Tsao A, et al. (2013) Impaired hippocampal rate coding after lesions of the lateral entorhinal cortex. Nat Neurosci 16:1085-1093](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3462)
- Schlesiger et al. 2015 (rat, CA1 after MEC lesions) is cited by Sanders 2020 among studies reporting near-zero between-context correlations. I verified only the abstract, which concerns disrupted theta phase precession with partly preserved spatial firing. Source: [Schlesiger MI, Cannova CC, Boublil BL, et al. (2015) The medial entorhinal cortex is necessary for temporal organization of hippocampal neuronal activity. Nat Neurosci 18:1123-1132](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4056)

Ventral hippocampus
- Kjelstrup et al. 2008. Rat, CA3 at multiple dorsoventral levels, 18 m linear track. The place-field scale increased almost linearly from under 1 m at the dorsal pole to about 10 m at the ventral pole. Abstract only. Source: [Kjelstrup KB, Solstad T, Brun VH, et al. (2008) Finite scale of spatial representation in the hippocampus. Science 321:140-143](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086)
- Komorowski et al. 2013. Rat, CA3 along the dorsoventral axis. Dorsal CA3 neurons rapidly associated events with specific locations. More ventral CA3 neurons gradually formed representations that "generalize across related events within a spatial context and distinguish events across contexts." Abstract only. Source: [Komorowski RW, Garcia CG, Wilson A, Hattori S, Howard MW, Eichenbaum H (2013) Ventral hippocampal neurons are shaped by experience to represent behaviorally relevant contexts. J Neurosci 33:8079-8087](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5458-12.2013)

Lateral septum
- Leutgeb & Mizumori 2002. Rat, two arenas on alternating days. About one third of lateral septal cells encoded matching locations on separate days in the same arena. Cells were usually location-selective in only one arena and at first showed unrelated patterns in the other. On the second test in each arena the patterns became more similar between arenas, while simultaneously recorded hippocampal cells kept distinct fields for each arena throughout. Abstract only. Source: [Leutgeb S, Mizumori SJ (2002) Context-specific spatial representations by lateral septal cells. Neuroscience 112:655-663](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0306-4522(02)00101-X)
- Tingley & Buzsaki 2018. Rat. Lateral septal neurons carry spatial information in theta phase while their firing rates are uninformative. Cross-environment remapping was not covered in the abstract. Source: [Tingley D, Buzsaki G (2018) Transformation of a spatial map across the hippocampal-lateral septal circuit. Neuron 98:1229-1242](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.04.028)

Humans (fMRI)
- Kyle et al. 2015. Human, high-resolution fMRI, 19 participants, four virtual cities. Cities 1 and 2 had the same stores and geometry with two store locations swapped. City 3 (interference) had the same stores in a new geometry and new locations. City 4 was entirely distinct. A searchlight classifier cluster in left CA3/DG and CA1 classified cities 1, 2 and 4 above chance (t(18) > 3.2) and city 3 below chance (t(18) = -3.2). The remapping index (within-city minus between-city pattern similarity) was significant in left CA3/DG for cities 1 and 2, and in left CA1 only for the fully distinct city 4. City 3 retrieval accuracy correlated with city 3 classifier accuracy, r(17) = 0.61. Full text read by the automated reader. Source: [Kyle CT, Stokes JD, Lieberman JS, Hassan AS, Ekstrom AD (2015) Successful retrieval of competing spatial environments in humans involves hippocampal pattern separation mechanisms. eLife 4:e10499](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.10499)
- Stokes, Kyle & Ekstrom 2015 (human fMRI of subfields) frame DG/CA3 as rapidly differentiating competing spatial contexts, with CA1 integrating. Abstract only; results numbers not retrieved. Source: [Stokes J, Kyle C, Ekstrom AD (2015) Complementary roles of human hippocampal subfields in differentiation and integration of spatial context. J Cogn Neurosci 27:546-559](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_00736)
- Steemers et al. 2016. Human fMRI. Participants learned object locations in two distinct VR environments, then saw morphs between them; hippocampal patterns showed nonlinear, attractor-like responses. Abstract only. Source: [Steemers B, Vicente-Grabovetsky A, Barry C, et al. (2016) Hippocampal attractor dynamics predict memory-based decision making. Curr Biol 26:1750-1757](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2016.04.063)

### Inferences
- Within the DG, the result depends on cell type. Before cell-type identification, "DG" recordings that showed multi-field firing in all environments (for example Leutgeb 2007) may have included mossy cells; GoodSmith 2017 and Senzai & Buzsaki 2017 both find mossy cells active across environments. This is my reading of the two abstracts, not a claim I verified as stated in either paper.
- Granule cells remap less than CA3 in the same-room maze comparison of Senzai & Buzsaki (36.7% vs 55.6%) and discriminate VR contexts less than CA1/CA3 in Hainmueller & Bartos. Danielson 2016 reports "robust" GC remapping. Taken together, these suggest that granule cells differ between environments mainly through which cells are active (sparse, nearly disjoint sets), not through field relocation within a shared active set.

### Gaps
- Numeric PV values for distal CA3 and CA2 between rooms (Lu 2015) were not retrieved.
- I found no paper that quantifies remapping across distinct environments in the subiculum with modern ensemble recordings. Kitanishi et al. 2021 (Sci Adv, doi 10.1126/sciadv.abf1913) studied subicular place coding but its abstract does not address remapping.
- I found no ventral hippocampus study reporting a cross-environment PV or rate-map correlation.
- Bats, macaques and birds: the searches found place coding papers (for example Mao et al. 2021 Neuron, macaque, doi 10.1016/j.neuron.2021.09.032; Payne et al. 2021 Science, titmouse, doi 10.1126/science.abg2009) but no quantified cross-environment remapping. Human single-unit remapping data were not found in this search.

## Q3. What makes remapping global vs partial or rate, and how does active-set overlap compare with chance?

### Takeaway
Changing the room or location gives global remapping, with population vectors that are statistically independent (Leutgeb 2005). Changing local features such as color, cue identity or shape within a fixed location often gives rate remapping or partial remapping. The outcome depends on experience, the number of sensory dimensions changed, the subregion and the lab protocol. Active-set overlap between rooms is at chance in CA3 and granule cells but above chance in CA1 for similar boxes, in hilar mossy cells, and for contexts experienced close in time.

### Cited findings
- Location vs local cues. Leutgeb 2005: a changed box at a fixed location gave rate remapping, with rates changing often by more than tenfold at fixed field positions. The same box in different places gave global remapping with statistically independent population vectors. Source: [Leutgeb et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1114037)
- Multimodal vs unimodal change. Wills et al. 2005, rat CA1. A square morph box and a wooden circle differing in shape, colour and texture produced rapid remapping in 92% of cells (48/52) after the first day's six trials. 40 of 46 remapped cells transferred their circle pattern to the morph-box circle. In octagonal intermediate shapes, 28 of 33 simultaneously recorded cells switched abruptly between the square-like and circle-like patterns. In one rat, 17 of 20 cells remapped between square and circle (12 by turning on or off, 5 by moving fields), and all switched at the same morph step (p < 1e-11 for 17 cells under independent switching). The authors contrast this with earlier work in which shape alone, with no colour or texture change, produced slow, cell-by-cell divergence. Data from 4 of 6 rats. Full text (author manuscript). Source: [Wills TJ, Lever C, Cacucci F, Burgess N, O'Keefe J (2005) Attractor dynamics in the hippocampal representation of the local environment. Science 308:873-876](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1108905)
- Shape alone, with experience. Lever et al. 2002, rat. With repeated exposure to differently shaped enclosures, place-cell representations diverged gradually. The divergence was specific to shape, needed no explicit reward, persisted for at least one month and transferred to new enclosures of the same shape. Abstract only. Source: [Lever C, Wills T, Cacucci F, Burgess N, O'Keefe J (2002) Long-term plasticity in hippocampal place-cell representation of environmental geometry. Nature 416:90-94](https://doi.org/10.1038/416090a)
- Morph experiments in CA3/CA1 that found gradual change. Leutgeb et al. 2005 (Neuron) morphed a familiar square into a familiar circle in steps. CA3 and CA1 showed "a gradual noncoherent progression," with hysteresis in CA3. Abstract only. Source: [Leutgeb JK, Leutgeb S, Treves A, et al. (2005) Progressive transformation of hippocampal neuronal representations in "morphed" environments. Neuron 48:345-358](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.09.007)
- The two morph results conflict. Sanders et al. 2020 reviews it: alternating square and circle training gave complete remapping after 18 days in the O'Keefe lab (Lever 2002) but partial remapping after comparable training in the Moser lab (Leutgeb 2005 Neuron). A 6-day protocol gave complete remapping in Wills 2005 but partial remapping in Colgin 2010. Sanders et al. attribute this to unreported differences in training that made animals move from partial to global remapping more slowly in one lab. Source: [Sanders, Wilson & Gershman 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51140); [Colgin LL, Leutgeb S, Jezek K, et al. (2010) Attractor-map versus autoassociation based attractor dynamics in the hippocampal network. J Neurophysiol 104:35-50](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00202.2010)
- The remapping categories are not exclusive. Sanders 2020 argues that rate remapping "is always accompanied by partial remapping": manipulations that cause rate remapping affect only part of the population, and other cells keep or lose their fields (citing Wood 2000 and Leutgeb 2005a). Allen et al. 2012 is the only report the authors know of with rate remapping and no partial remapping. Source: [Sanders et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51140)
- Prior experience of context statistics. Plitt & Giocomo 2021, mouse CA1, VR. Mice that had rarely seen intermediate morphs (fewer than 4% of trials before session 8) kept one map until morph value S of about 0.25 to 0.5 and then switched coherently. Mice that had frequently seen intermediates (60% of trials) changed gradually. The authors relate this to an optimal estimate of context identity. Source: [Plitt & Giocomo 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00816-6)
- Partial remapping with conflicting or compound cues. Anderson & Jeffery 2003, rat, compound contexts of black or white color with lemon or vanilla odor: some cells responded to color, some to odor and some to combinations, a heterogeneous partial remapping. Abstract only (truncated). Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020 summarize double-rotation experiments (Shapiro, Tanila & Eichenbaum 1997; Knierim 2002; Lee et al. 2004) as typically giving partial remapping, with one subset of cells following floor cues and another following wall cues (secondary). Sources: [Anderson MI, Jeffery KJ (2003) Heterogeneous modulation of place cell firing by changes in context. J Neurosci 23:8827-8835](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-26-08827.2003); [Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160)
- Visually identical compartments. Skaggs & McNaughton 1998 (CA1) found a higher-than-chance fraction of cells with repeated fields, varying across rats and sessions. Spiers et al. 2015, rat CA1, 4-compartment environment, found "a high degree" of place-field repetition across compartments. They also found remapping that stayed local to the changed compartment. Abstract only, truncated. Sources: [Skaggs & McNaughton 1998](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-20-08455.1998); [Spiers HJ, Hayman RM, Jovalekic A, Marozzi E, Jeffery KJ (2015) Place field repetition and purely local remapping in a multicompartment environment. Cereb Cortex 25:10-25](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bht198)
- Upstream correlates. Global remapping comes with a coherent grid shift and rotation; rate remapping comes with stable grids (Fyhn 2007). Rate remapping in CA3 needs LEC input (Lu 2013). Sources: [Fyhn et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05601); [Lu et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3462)
- Overlap vs chance, summary of measured cases:
  - CA3, different rooms: overlap equals the independence expectation (0.11 to 0.14 vs 0.12 to 0.14; novel room 0.16 vs 0.14). Source: [Leutgeb 2004](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1100265)
  - CA3 and GCL, four rooms: rate overlap not different from shuffle (CA3 0.13, GCL 0.15). Source: [GoodSmith 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026)
  - Hilar mossy cells: rate overlap 0.31, above all shuffles. Source: [GoodSmith 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026)
  - CA1, identical boxes in two rooms: 0.55 vs 0.63 to 0.66 for the same room repeated. CA1, novel room: 0.38 vs 0.24 expected. Source: [Leutgeb 2004](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1100265)
  - CA1, extreme VR morphs: cells with spatial information in both environments were 6.07% vs 4.78% expected (rare condition) and 1.98% vs 1.06% (frequent), near chance. Source: [Plitt & Giocomo 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00816-6)
  - CA1, contexts 5 h apart: overlap above chance. Contexts 7 days apart: not above chance (TetTag). Source: [Cai 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17955)

### Inferences
- The variables that predict global over rate or partial remapping across these studies are: a change of spatial location or room, a change in several sensory dimensions at once, longer or interleaved training that favors discrete switching, and recording in CA3 (especially proximal CA3) rather than CA1, distal CA3 or CA2.
- Both "chance" overlap and "global remapping" are defined against a shuffle or independence model fitted to each region's sparsity. Raw overlap fractions are not comparable across regions or methods without that correction, as Leutgeb 2004 explicitly controls for.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full Wood et al. 2000, Hayman et al. 2003, Allen et al. 2012 or Jeffery 2011 papers, so their quantitative partial-remapping data are not included.
- No single dataset was found that crosses all manipulation types (room, shape, color, odor, task) in the same animals with matched metrics.

## Q4. Capacity: how many near-orthogonal maps have been shown in one animal, and what limits are argued?

### Takeaway
The largest direct demonstration is 11 mutually orthogonal CA3 maps in each rat, one per room, with all 55 pairs uncorrelated (Alme 2014). Theory papers argue capacity is large and depends on sparsity and connectivity, but none of the sources I could verify gives a number for the rat. Calcium imaging adds that one familiar environment can have several stable maps (Sheintuch 2020), so the number of maps is not tied to the number of environments.

### Cited findings
- Alme et al. 2014: 11 rooms per rat, 55 pairwise comparisons, all orthogonal in CA3. The authors conclude "the number of independent spatial representations stored in hippocampal area CA3 is large, with minimal recurrence of spatial firing patterns," and that the data "speak against extensive recurrence of spatial motifs across experiences." Ten of the 11 rooms were novel, tested over 2 days, so this shows orthogonal encoding of 11 recent maps, not long-term storage of 11 maps. Source: [Alme et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421056111)
- Jain & Elliott 2020 (arXiv, PREPRINT, not peer reviewed) reanalyzed the Alme data. They support independence across rooms but suggest possible "activity-related clusters" of cells; their own same- vs different-room similarity test was not significant (p = 0.666). They describe the upper bound on CA3 capacity as unresolved. Source: [Jain & Elliott 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14559)
- Sheintuch et al. 2020: multiple distinct, stable maps of the same familiar environment in mouse CA1, persisting for weeks. Source: [Sheintuch et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.02.018)
- Samsonovich & McNaughton 1997 (theory). In the multi-chart continuous-attractor model, the same units take part in many charts, and "the number of uncorrelated charts that can be encoded in the same recurrent network is potentially quite large." Abstract only. Source: [Samsonovich A, McNaughton BL (1997) Path integration and cognitive mapping in a continuous attractor neural network model. J Neurosci 17:5900-5920](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.17-15-05900.1997)
- Battaglia & Treves 1998 (theory). Storage and information capacity for multiple charts were derived in the extremely diluted and fully connected limits, with capacity set mainly by the sparsity of the spatial representations and the degree of connectivity, as for discrete autoassociative memories. The abstract gives no numerical rat estimate. Source: [Battaglia FP, Treves A (1998) Attractor neural networks storing multiple space representations: a model for hippocampal place fields. Phys Rev E 58:7738-7753](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.58.7738); [arXiv cond-mat/9807101](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9807101)
- Monasson & Rosay 2014 (theory). In a place-cell attractor model storing several environments, crosstalk between stored maps pins the activity bump. Pinning depends on the number of environments stored, noise, activity level and connectivity. Abstract only. Source: [Monasson R, Rosay S (2014) Crosstalk and transitions between multiple spatial maps in an attractor neural network model of the hippocampus: collective motion of the activity. Phys Rev E 89:032803](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.89.032803)
- Kammerer & Leibold 2014 (theory, grid-to-place feedforward). Global remapping arises from grid phase shifts. Spatial decoding acuity survives many remappings far better than place-code sparseness does. Since the hippocampal place code is sparse, the authors conclude the grid-to-place projection "is not using its full capacity," so the binding limit is sparseness, not capacity. Abstract only. Source: [Kammerer A, Leibold C (2014) Hippocampal remapping is constrained by sparseness rather than capacity. PLoS Comput Biol 10:e1003986](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003986)
- Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020 (review) argue that the hippocampus tends to orthogonalize pairs of maps, which could store a very large number of independent environment representations. They also say they are "not yet convinced" that maps are the neural basis of context, and they cite Jeffery et al. 2003 showing spatial task performance survived complete remapping. Source: [Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160)
- Active-set sparsity sets how many random subsets can be drawn with low overlap. About 20% of cells are active per environment (Kubie & Muller 1991 via the 2020 review). Per-environment active fractions were 29% in CA3 and 9% in GCs (GoodSmith 2017), and 48.1% in CA3, 59.2% in CA1 and 68.2% in CA2 per session (Mankin 2015). Sources: [Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160); [GoodSmith 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026); [Mankin 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.001)

### Inferences
- With an active fraction f per environment and independent draws, the expected fraction of cells active in both of two environments is f squared. Leutgeb 2004 and GoodSmith 2017 find CA3 at this independence level, and Alme 2014 at 11 rooms is consistent with it. The data show no saturation up to 11 maps. They do not set an upper bound.
- Map counts from tetrode data rest on about 30 active cells per animal (Alme 2014), which limits the power to detect small residual correlations.

### Gaps
- I found no experiment testing more than 11 distinct environments with ensemble recordings in one animal. Calcium-imaging studies with many VR environments (for example Lee et al. 2020 Cell) were not checked for an explicit count of orthogonal maps.
- Numeric capacity estimates (maps per CA3 network) from Battaglia & Treves 1998, Treves & Rolls 1992 or Samsonovich & McNaughton 1997 were not retrieved from the full texts.

## Q5. Are codes for different environments fully uncorrelated, or is there residual shared structure?

### Takeaway
Measured pairwise, spatial maps in CA3 and the DG are at chance levels. But several kinds of shared structure carry across environments. Each cell's firing rate and burstiness (r of about 0.6 for log rate between mazes) and its place-field propensity are conserved. A small reward-coding population keeps its role. Ensembles drift together with time. CA1 keeps similarity when local cues are shared. Some apparent global remapping in CA1 may be a coherent rotation of one map. Sanders 2020 notes that "many studies reporting global remapping report low but non-zero correlations."

### Cited findings
- Sanders et al. 2020 (theory and review). "Although studies report correlations near zero between place fields in different contexts (Leutgeb et al., 2004; Muller and Kubie, 1987; Schlesiger et al., 2015), there are reasons to believe that correlations are not actually zero." They cite "low but non-zero correlations" in Leutgeb 2004, Skaggs & McNaughton 1998 and Spiers 2015. They add that experiments showing no remapping never report perfect overlap, and that even within one context the spatial firing varies as if more than one map is used (Fenton & Muller 1998; Kay et al. 2019; Kelemen & Fenton 2016). In their hidden-state model, remapping reflects inferred context: weak evidence for a new state gives rate or partial remapping, strong evidence gives global remapping. Source: [Sanders, Wilson & Gershman 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.51140)
- Coherent rotation in place of random remapping. Kinsky 2018, mouse CA1. Mice "often employed a coherent map to represent both arenas despite their distinct visual and geometric cues." Within-arena PV similarity was still higher than between-arena similarity (p = 1.4e-8 for rate-only PVs). About half of each population typically rotated coherently between sessions, coherence being rotation within 30 degrees of the population mean. Sanders 2020 summarizes the paper as suggesting that "previous observations of global remapping might be artifacts of misalignment of maps between contexts." Kinsky's arenas were small (25 cm square, about 28 cm octagon), and the rotation-aligned analysis is the key methodological difference from classic studies. Source: [Kinsky et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.09.037)
- Single-cell firing rates are conserved across environments. Mizuseki & Buzsaki 2013, rat, silicon probes. Correlations of the same neurons' rates between maze A and maze B: log rate r = 0.65 (CA1) and 0.59 (CA3); linear rate r = 0.59 (CA1) and 0.26 (CA3); burst index r = 0.63 (CA1) and 0.57 (CA3); all P < 0.00001, n = 6 rats. Familiar vs novel maze, CA1 log rate r = 0.68. Rates in slow-wave sleep before and after correlated with running rates (r about 0.46 to 0.65). Full text read by the automated reader; neuron counts per comparison were not reported in the text read. Source: [Mizuseki K, Buzsaki G (2013) Preconfigured, skewed distribution of firing rates in the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex. Cell Rep 4:1010-1021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2013.07.039)
- Place-field propensity is a cell-specific trait. Lee et al. 2020, mouse CA1, longitudinal imaging in several large VR environments. Each cell's field propensity (fields per unit length) "is preserved across distinct environments and over months." Propensity varied about 10-fold between neighboring cells. Abstract only; the cross-environment correlation value was not retrieved. Rich et al. 2014, rat, 48 m track: each cell had its own propensity to form fields at random positions. Sources: [Lee JS, Briguglio JJ, Cohen JD, Romani S, Lee AK (2020) The statistical structure of the hippocampal code for space as a function of time, context, and value. Cell 183:620-635](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.024); [Rich PD, Liaw HP, Lee AK (2014) Large environments reveal the statistical structure governing hippocampal representations. Science 345:814-817](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255635)
- Which cells become place cells may be partly set in advance. Epsztein et al. 2011, whole-cell recording in rats in a novel maze. Future place cells had lower spike thresholds and higher burst propensity before exploration, so "internal settings appear to predetermine which cells will represent the next novel environment." Abstract only. Source: [Epsztein J, Brecht M, Lee AK (2011) Intracellular determinants of hippocampal CA1 place and silent cell activity in a novel environment. Neuron 70:109-120](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.006)
- A dedicated reward population is shared across environments. Gauthier & Tank 2018, mouse CA1 and subiculum. "Across different virtual environments, most cells remapped randomly, but reward encoding consistently arose from a single pool of cells." In the reward-shift condition, reward-associated cells were 0.8% of all recorded cells (4.2% of cells with fields in both conditions). The reader also returned 4.4% (CA1) and 5.7% (subiculum) for the two-track condition; I could not confirm whether these are reward-cell fractions or fractions with fields on both tracks, so check before use. Source: [Gauthier & Tank 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2018.06.008)
- Shared time component. Rubin 2015, mouse CA1. A decoder trained in one environment identified the recording day from data in the other environment in 65% of sessions and 58% of trials (chance 12.5%), so slow ensemble drift is common to both environments even though their spatial maps differ. Cai 2016: CA1 ensembles for two contexts overlap more when the contexts are experienced 5 h apart than 7 days apart. Sources: [Rubin et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.12247); [Cai et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17955)
- Shared local geometry or cues. CA1 keeps substantial overlap for identical boxes in different rooms (0.55 vs 0.63 to 0.66 within a room; Leutgeb 2004). Fields repeat in visually identical compartments (Skaggs & McNaughton 1998; Spiers 2015). Sources: [Leutgeb 2004](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1100265); [Spiers et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bht198)
- Upstream structure is kept. MEC grid modules keep their internal phase relations across environments and shift and rotate coherently (Fyhn 2007). Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020 summarize evidence that head-direction cells keep their pairwise cofiring across conditions including sleep. Sources: [Fyhn et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05601); [Kubie, Levy & Fenton 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.23160)
- Evidence for full independence. CA3 active-set overlap at the independence level (Leutgeb 2004). CA3, GC and hilar field locations no more correlated than shuffles (GoodSmith 2017). Alme 2014: cells active in many rooms had "completely uncorrelated" firing locations. Plitt & Giocomo 2021: cells informative in both extreme VR environments at near-chance rates. Sources as above.

### Inferences
- Two levels need to be kept apart. Field locations and cofiring among place cells are near zero in CA3 and the DG, while cell-level traits (rate, bursting, propensity, reward-cell identity) persist across environments. A PV correlation computed on raw rates will therefore pick up some shared structure from conserved rates even when field positions are random. That shared structure is removed when rate maps are normalized or when only field position is compared.
- The size of residual correlation depends on method: whether maps are rotation-aligned (Kinsky 2018), which cells are included (all cells vs active-in-both), and how long apart the sessions are (Rubin 2015; Cai 2016; Mankin 2015). Studies reporting "near zero" and "non-zero" between-environment correlations often differ on these choices more than on biology.

### Gaps
- I did not find a study reporting a bootstrapped confidence interval for the between-room CA3 PV correlation that excludes or includes zero, such as the per-pair values in Alme 2014.
- I could not verify the numeric cross-environment correlation of field propensity in Lee et al. 2020 or the numeric "low but non-zero" values in Leutgeb 2004 for spatial correlations. Leutgeb 2004 Fig. 2D shows them but the values were not in the extracted text.
