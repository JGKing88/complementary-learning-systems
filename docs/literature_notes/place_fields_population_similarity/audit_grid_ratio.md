# Audit of the field-width over grid-spacing tables

Audited on 2026-11-20 against the draft `ratio_tables_draft.md`, its input `ratio_spec.json` and the generator `ratio_tables.py` (all in `/home/jackking/.claude/jobs/2c8b1c8e/tmp/`). I checked every number against the papers, not against the four `grid_ratio_*.md` notes. Real neural data only.

How I checked:

- Text values were read in the paper's full text where I could reach it. Mizuseki 2012 came from NCBI efetch (PMC3718552), since Europe PMC returned HTTP 500 three times. Mallory 2018's Supplementary Information PDF (Springer MOESM1) was fetched new for this audit. The rest came from the downloads the earlier agents saved, which I re-read myself.
- Figure values were re-read independently. Stensola Supp. Fig. 18b and Lee 2025 Fig. 4 are vector graphics, so I took marker and bar coordinates from the PDF with PyMuPDF and calibrated them against the axis frame. Keinath Fig. 1I and 1J, Dong Supp. Fig. 2e, Hainmueller Extended Data Fig. 3c and Park Fig. 4B are raster images. I rendered them at 6 to 8 times zoom, found tick-label centres and bar or curve pixels by colour, and converted.
- Every ratio cell was recomputed with a separate script.
- In quotes, minus signs and range dashes are written as plain hyphens where the source uses other dash characters. Everything else in a quote is verbatim.

Verdicts: "confirmed" means the number and label match the paper. "Confirmed, note needed" means the number is right but the range mixes measures or the label omits something the reader needs. "Corrected" gives new text. "Unverifiable" says why.

## The ventral track column (item 3)

What the sources say:

- Brun et al. 2008 abstract, confirmed word for word: "approximately 50 cm in the most dorsal part to approximately 3 m at the most ventral recording positions." The recordings covered "approximately three quarters of the dorsoventral extent". So "most ventral" means about 75% of the MEC axis, not the ventral pole.
- Kerdels & Peters 2018 (arXiv 1810.07429, Table 1, p. 8), confirmed at the survey: median spacing 171 ± 13, 301 ± 23 and 370 ± 46 cm and minimum spacing 91 ± 12, 202 ± 24 and 269 ± 47 cm for dorsal (0 to 25%, 55 cells), intermediate (25 to 50%, 59 cells) and ventral (50 to 75%, 29 cells) groups. The survey adds: "the algorithm used to detect the firing fields missed a substantial number of visually discernable fields due to low firing rates. Thus, the median values may overestimate the true grid spacing."
- Solstad 2009 thesis abstract (NVA record): "inter-peak distance ranged from less than 1m to at least 8m."
- The Brun full text is unverifiable. Wiley returns HTTP 403 behind a Cloudflare check, the paper is not in PMC, and Unpaywall rejects the anonymous API call. I could not see Brun's own table, so I cannot say whether the survey copied it correctly.

The conflict. The survey's dorsal median (171 cm) and even its dorsal minimum (91 cm) are 2 to 3 times the abstract's "approximately 50 cm". The survey's numbers therefore look like detected-peak statistics that run high. Its ventral group's minimum to median, 2.7 to 3.7 m, brackets the abstract's 3 m. So the abstract value is plausible for the ventral group, but its method and spread are unknown.

Advice. Keep the column, but only for the three Kjelstrup 18 m track rows. Kjelstrup et al. used the same 18 m track as Brun et al., so those cells compare like with like. For the box and short-track rows, leave the cells blank or mark them "not comparable". There a 1D interpeak distance on an 18 m track would sit next to 2D box spacing, and the M1 to M5 columns already cover those rows. Show the column's value as "about 3 m (abstract)" with a footnote giving the survey's 2.7 to 3.7 m. If the footnote range were used instead, the cells would be 0.26 to 0.42 (dorsal CA3), 0.83 to 1.4 (intermediate CA3) and 1.1 to 2.1 (ventral CA3).

## The mouse M1 conflict (item 6)

Both sides check out.

- Clark & Nolan 2024 (eLife 12:RP89356, Results, first section): "we identified 103/1881 neurons as grid cells (11.4±17.4 grid cells/mouse, range 0.4-9.5%, Supplementary file 1) ... These grid cells had field sizes of 7.5±3.3 cm and grid spacing of 72.5±13.7 cm and were found in dorsomedial parts of the MEC." Methods: "Spatial frequencies >5 were discarded from further analysis as no grid cells were found with grid spacings <40 cm." That is 9 mice. The ± is probably SD, since 11.4 ± 17.4 grid cells per mouse only makes sense as SD. Tetrodes were "lowered 1.2-1.4 mm into the right hemisphere of the brain, beginning at 3.4 mm lateral from Bregma and along the lambdoid suture", then advanced 50 to 100 µm per session. The paper gives no distance from the dorsal MEC border. The arena is "a metal box with a square floor area" with no size given. The same lab's Gerlei et al. 2020 states "a floor area of 1 m2" for the same frame parts.
- On the other side are Zong 2022 (30 cm at the dorsal edge of one imaged area), Mallory 2018 (36.26 ± 9.27 cm, intercept 31.97 cm, cells 386 ± 222 µm from the dorsal border) and Wen 2024 (a 25 cm module, VR-inferred).

Advice. Keep M1 at 30 to 36 cm. Three labs with different methods report dorsal mouse grids in this range, and Mallory places them within about 0.4 mm of the dorsal border. Clark & Nolan's population lacks such grids, most likely because of where their tetrodes sampled. Their paper does not report a dorsoventral position that would test this. Present the conflict as a footnote to M1. "One lab (Clark & Nolan 2024, 103 grid cells in 9 mice) found no grid cell below 40 cm and a mean of 72.5 ± 13.7 cm in 'dorsomedial' MEC, without a distance from the dorsal border." Do not use Clark & Nolan as a column end, since it is a mean over an unknown mix of modules. Its mean falls inside M3 and can be cited there as a cross-check. The same sentence reports field sizes of 7.5 ± 3.3 cm, which is small next to a 72.5 cm spacing. The text I read does not define either measure, so I could not check how they relate.

## Exclusions and missing rows (item 5)

- Diba & Buzsáki 2008, pooled CA1 and CA3. Fair to keep out of the region rows. Confirmed through the PMC article page: "No intrahippocampal regional differences could be seen for the measures we calculated", so CA1 and CA3 were pooled. Field size "decreased significantly from a mean of 52 to 42 cm" when the 170 cm track was shortened to 100 cm, with size taken as "the portion of the track with >2 Hz firing". NCBI efetch returns no body text for this paper (the publisher blocks XML). It could go in as a separate "dorsal CA1 and CA3 pooled, familiar 1.7 m track, 52 cm" row if wanted.
- Chockanathan & Padmanabhan 2021. Fair to exclude. Their width is the run of bins "at least 1.5 SDs above the mean", over all units, and gives dCA1 9.4 ± 2.0 cm against Harvey's 41 cm on a similar VR track. Keep their null result ("dCA1 = 9.4 ± 2.0 cm, vCA1 = 11.9 ± 6.3 cm, p = 0.84", Fig. 3H legend) as a note beside the mouse ventral CA1 row. It is the only same-study VR comparison and it disagrees with Keinath.
- Mouse M4 (derived only). Fair to exclude. No measured value exists.
- Rat ventral CA1 gap. Fair. I found no rat paper with a ventral CA1 field width. Jung et al. 1994 is the nearest candidate, but its sites are intermediate by Kjelstrup's account (note 29). The full text stayed blocked: NCBI efetch reports the publisher "does not allow downloading of the full text in XML form", and the Europe PMC PDF returned 403.
- Rows that could be added. Mizuseki open-field medians (1,775 and 1,275 cm², so 47.5 and 40.3 cm) fall inside the existing arena rows, so they add nothing new. The rat CA1 arena rows rest on one figure (Park) and could cite Mizuseki as a second source. No row needs to come out.
- Columns. Gardner et al. 2022 modules (about 58 to 126 cm) fall inside M2 to M4. No column is missing.

## Ratio check (item 4)

I recomputed all 78 cells (54 rat, 24 mouse) as (row low / column high) to (row high / column low), with a separate script that rounds with Decimal half-up to two significant figures.

- 77 of 78 cells match.
- One cell differs in display only. Rat dorsal CA1 (29 to 75 cm) against the 3 m column gives 29/300 = 0.0967. The draft shows "0.10 to 0.25". Two significant figures give "0.097 to 0.25". The cause is `fmt()` in the generator, which prints two decimals for any value below 1 and so drops a digit below 0.1. No other cell is below 0.1.
