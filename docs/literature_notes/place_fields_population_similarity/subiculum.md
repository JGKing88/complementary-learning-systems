# Spatial tuning in the subiculum and prosubiculum, single unit and population level

Notes on conventions used below:
- "SUB" is subiculum, "CA1" is hippocampal area CA1. "SI" is spatial information. Skaggs sparsity runs from 0 to 1, and a higher value means less sparse, broader firing.
- "Fraction of environment" arithmetic is mine, shown step by step, and is only as good as the measure the paper reports. Most papers do not report field size in cm or cm², so the arithmetic often uses a coverage measure instead of a field measure. Each case says which.
- "Unverified" marks anything I could not read in the source text itself, such as numbers seen only in a search snippet or press release.
- Citation corrections relative to the brief: Barnes et al. 1990 is in Progress in Brain Research, not Exp Brain Res. Poulter et al. 2021 is in Nature Neuroscience, not Nature Communications. The "similar firing across two different environments" result is Sharp 1997 (Behav Brain Res). Sharp 1999 has two empirical papers (Behav Neurosci on size scaling, Hippocampus on timing) and a model paper (Hippocampus). "Matsumoto et al. 2019" is a review (Matsumoto, Kitanishi & Mizuseki, Neurosci Res), which I did not read.

## 1. Single-unit field properties: field size, fields per cell, SI, sparsity, rates, and comparison with CA1

### Takeaway
Across rats recorded with tetrodes or silicon probes, subicular principal cells fire at 2 to 7 times the CA1 rate. They carry 3 to 8 times fewer bits per spike, and their firing covers about twice the fraction of the environment that CA1 firing covers: 56% vs 26% of bins hold 95% of spikes in a 150 cm box, and Skaggs sparsity is 0.78 vs 0.43 on a T-maze. They often have multiple irregular fields. Bits per second are about equal to CA1. The broad-tuning picture holds most strongly for distal and middle subiculum in rats. Proximal subiculum is close to CA1, and one mouse study found no difference in field size at all.

### Cited findings

Early descriptive work (rat, dorsal SUB)
- Barnes et al. 1990 compared entorhinal cortex, CA3, CA1 and SUB. Neither entorhinal cortex nor SUB showed the highly localized firing of the CA fields, and average discharge rates were "substantially higher" in entorhinal cortex and SUB. Some subicular cells fired in a spatially consistent but dispersed way, which the authors suggested may reflect convergence of several CA1 place cells. They concluded that hippocampal output is "a much more highly distributed spatial code" and that selectivity is not progressively refined through the circuit. Only the abstract was readable. Species was F344 rats per MeSH. Methods, N and numbers are unverified. [Barnes, McNaughton, Mizumori, Leonard & Lin 1990, Prog Brain Res 83:287-300, PMID 2392566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2392566/)
- Sharp & Green 1994 recorded dorsal SUB during open-field pellet chasing. In their words, "most subicular cells showed a robust locational signal". The cells "tended to fire throughout much of the environment" with graded, location-related rate modulation rather than compact fields. Distal cells had "slightly higher average firing rates, spatial signaling, and firing field size" than proximal cells. Bursting and nonbursting cells did not differ detectably in spatial firing. The authors describe a "robust, highly distributed spatial signal". Only the abstract was readable, because the PMC scan (PMC6577112) would not load. Cylinder size, N, coherence and field-size values are unverified. [Sharp & Green 1994, J Neurosci 14:2339-2356, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.14-04-02339.1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8158272/)
- Anderson & O'Mara 2003 recorded dorsal SUB in male Wistar rats with an 8-electrode microdrive in a small restricted enclosure. They report bursting, regular-spiking and fast-spiking classes, and about 32% of recordings were theta phase-locked. Abstract only. Spatial numbers are unverified. [Anderson & O'Mara 2003, J Neurophysiol 90:655-665, doi:10.1152/jn.00723.2002](https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/873047955/1/5)
- Sharp 1999 (Hippocampus) reports that subicular cells "appear to anticipate future locations by approximately 50 to 70 msec, on average". Abstract snippet only. [Sharp 1999, Hippocampus 9:158-172, PMID 10226776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10226776/)

Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012 (rat, intermediate SUB, tetrodes, same animals as distal CA1)
- Design: 5 male Long-Evans rats. 91 subicular single units (88 putative pyramidal, 3 fast-spiking). Table 2 counts are proximal 21, middle 35, distal 32, plus 24 distal CA1 or CA1/SUB transition units (21+35+32+24 = 112). Two geometrically identical U-shaped elevated tracks, 6 cm wide and 350 cm long, one familiar and one novel. [Kim, Ganguli & Frank 2012, J Neurosci 32:11539-11558](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Map definitions: 2 cm position bins and 6 degree theta-phase bins. Local quadratic kernel regression smoothing with bandwidth 20 cm in position and 90 degrees in phase. Speed of at least 10 cm/s, positions within 20 cm of food wells excluded, at least 5 passes per direction. Field segmentation needed a peak of at least 10 spikes/s and an H-maxima contrast of 0.3. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- The "spatial activity fraction" is a Skaggs-type sparseness, which the authors say "can be interpreted as the proportion of the environment over which a neuron fires". Medians with IQR: proximal SUB 0.31 (0.19-0.43), middle 0.54 (0.32-0.68), distal 0.69 (0.59-0.74), distal CA1 0.26 (0.21-0.37). The proximodistal gradient has p < 10^-4 and SUB vs CA1 has p < 10^-10. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
  - Arithmetic, approximate: on the 350 cm track, distal 0.69 × 350 = 242 cm, middle 0.54 × 350 = 189 cm, proximal 0.31 × 350 = 109 cm, CA1 0.26 × 350 = 91 cm. If the 2 × 20 cm food-well zones are dropped (310 cm usable), distal is 0.69 × 310 = 214 cm and CA1 is 0.26 × 310 = 81 cm. Caveat: the measure is a sparseness index on position-by-phase maps, not a thresholded field length.
- Mean rates (median, IQR): proximal 2.5 spikes/s (1.4-3.7), middle 6.3 (4.1-11.4), distal 10.3 (6.6-14.1), CA1 1.4 (1.1-1.7). SUB vs CA1 p < 10^-9. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Fields per cell: "a majority of neurons in the subiculum have multiple, irregularly spaced unitary place fields". Middle and distal maps "resembled the multipeaked maps from entorhinal grid cells in linear environments". The paper gives no count of fields per cell and no field lengths in cm. 143 unitary fields were segmented for the phase precession analysis, and larger fields had shallower precession slopes (Spearman 0.65, p < 10^-9). [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- SI was model-based, in bits per 20 ms bin, not bits/spike. SUB median 0.057 bits (IQR 0.036-0.080) vs CA1 0.021 (0.0081-0.056), p < 0.001. By location: proximal 0.030, middle 0.062, distal 0.062. Firing rate and information correlated (Spearman r = 0.66). The authors state that subicular neurons "sacrifice spatial selectivity for higher information capacity". [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Kim et al. note that their proximodistal sparseness difference is larger than the one in Sharp & Green 1994, and attribute this to methods. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)

Ledergerber et al. 2021 (rat, dorsal SUB and CA1, tetrodes)
- Design: 8 male Long-Evans rats. 421 SUB and 325 CA1 putative principal cells. Of the SUB cells, 292 were proximal and 136 distal (these sum to 428, not 421, as the fetched text reports). Open-field foraging in a 150 × 150 cm box, plus a goal-directed "spatial task" in the same box. Rate maps used 5 × 5 cm bins smoothed with σ = 1.5 bins. Units were excluded if their mean rate was below 0.5 Hz. [Ledergerber et al. 2021, Cell Rep 35:109175, doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2021.109175](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Mean rate: SUB 4.44 ± 0.14 Hz vs CA1 1.7 ± 0.12 Hz. [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Spatial spread: 95% of CA1 spikes fell in 25.7% ± 1.1% of spatial bins, and 95% of SUB spikes fell in 56.3% ± 0.7% of bins. [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
  - Arithmetic: the box is 150 × 150 = 22,500 cm². SUB 0.563 × 22,500 = 12,668 cm², the area of a square about 113 cm on a side. CA1 0.257 × 22,500 = 5,783 cm², a square about 76 cm on a side. Caveat: this is the area holding 95% of spikes, not a thresholded field.
- SI: CA1 0.84 ± 0.57 bits/spike vs SUB 0.11 ± 0.16 bits/spike, about 7.6 times lower in SUB (p = 1.1e-70). Rates in bits/s were close: CA1 0.47 ± 0.27 vs SUB 0.42 ± 0.26 (p = 0.02). [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- The paper reports no field size, field count, sparsity or classification threshold for "spatial" cells. One spatial-analysis speed filter appears in the fetched text as "above 2 m/s", which is almost certainly a transcription error for 2 cm/s (unverified). [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)

Kitanishi, Umaba & Mizuseki 2021 (rat, dorsal SUB and CA1, 256-channel silicon probes)
- Design: 11 male Long-Evans rats. 319 SUB and 315 CA1 putative principal cells. Tasks: open field 118 × 118 cm, linear track 234 × 6.5 cm, alternating T-maze, zigzag maze. Linear-track maps used 2 cm bins with Gaussian σ = 2 cm, and cells with peak below 1 Hz were excluded. A place cell needed bits/s above the 99th percentile of 1,000 shuffles. Projection targets were identified by optogenetic tagging: AV 3.4%, MMB 8.8%, NAC 5.6%, RSC 5.0% of SUB principal cells. [Kitanishi et al. 2021, Sci Adv 7:eabf1913, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abf1913](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)
- Linear track, mean ± SD: bits/spike CA1 0.94 ± 0.77 vs SUB 0.34 ± 0.46. Mean rate CA1 3.7 ± 5.4 vs SUB 9.8 ± 12.9 Hz. Peak rate CA1 9.3 ± 9.0 vs SUB 17.1 ± 17.0 Hz. Bits/s did not differ (CA1 1.43 ± 1.69 vs SUB 1.39 ± 1.87, P = 0.33). Mutual information did not differ (0.26 vs 0.29 bits). The proportion of place cells did not differ (χ² = 0.02, P = 0.88), but the proportions themselves are not in the text. SUB maps were more temporally stable than CA1 maps (figure only). [Kitanishi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)
- Spatial peak and centroid did not differ among projection classes (P > 0.1). Field size, field count and sparsity are not reported. [Kitanishi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)

Lee, Lee & Lee 2018 (rat, dorsal SUB and CA1 recorded simultaneously, tetrodes, visual-scene T-maze)
- Design: 7 male Long-Evans rats in the main task. 242 SUB and 129 CA1 units analyzed. Stem 73 × 8 cm (47 cm in one rat) and arms 38 × 8 cm. Field boundaries were set where bins fell below 40% of peak. [Lee, Lee & Lee 2018, J Neurosci 38:7392-7408, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0156-18.2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)
- Mean ± SEM, CA1 vs SUB: SI 1.12 ± 0.06 vs 0.25 ± 0.02, Skaggs sparsity 0.43 ± 0.02 vs 0.78 ± 0.01, coherence 1.81 vs 1.34, selectivity 4.34 vs 2.14. SUB mean rate was higher (Z = 6.83), and CA1 bursting was higher. [Lee et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)
  - Arithmetic, approximate: track length is 73 + 2 × 38 = 149 cm. Sparsity 0.78 × 149 = 116 cm for SUB and 0.43 × 149 = 64 cm for CA1. Caveat: Skaggs sparsity matches field coverage only for a binary field.
- Fields per cell: over 80% of CA1 units were single-field. SUB was split roughly equally between single-field and multi-field units (χ² = 49.69, p < 0.001). Subicular single fields were larger than CA1 single fields (Z = 8.09). No cm values are given. [Lee et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)

Simonnet & Brecht 2019 (rat, dorsal SUB, juxtacellular)
- Design: 40 male Long-Evans rats. 102 principal cells, of which 84 had at least 60% arena coverage and entered the spatial analysis. Arena 70 × 70 × 50 cm. Bins 2.5 × 2.5 cm, Gaussian σ = 5 cm. A field needed at least 100 cm² (16 bins) above one third of the rate range, with a peak of at least two thirds of the range. [Simonnet & Brecht 2019, J Neurosci 39:3651-3662](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6510334)
  - Arithmetic: the minimum field of 100 cm² is 100 / 4,900 = 2.0% of the arena.
- 51/84 (63%) were spatially modulated by a 95th-percentile shuffle. Mean rates were high: sparsely bursting cells 13.6 Hz and dominantly bursting cells 20.1 Hz. SI medians: sparsely bursting 0.36 bits/spike for significant cells (0.19 for all), dominantly bursting 0.16 (0.06 for all). Field sizes, field counts and sparsity are not reported. [Simonnet & Brecht 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6510334)

Frost et al. 2021 (rat, dorsal SUB, tetrodes, controls for an anterior thalamic lesion study)
- 7 control rats, 82 units, arenas 60 × 60 cm and 105 × 105 cm. Place cells needed SI > 0.5 bits/spike, coherence > 0.25 and rate > 0.25. Results: 47/82 (57%) spatial, made up of 11 place cells, 20 head-direction cells, 5 border cells and 11 grid cells. Mean rate across all control units was 2.93 ± 4.31 spikes/s. After anterior thalamic lesions, 0 of 21 SUB units were spatial while CA1 place fields "appeared intact". [Frost et al. 2021, J Neurosci 41:6511, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2868-20.2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8318085)
  - Caveat: the SI > 0.5 bits/spike threshold is above the mean subicular SI in Ledergerber (0.11) and Kitanishi (0.34), so this criterion counts only the sharpest subicular cells as place cells.

Brotons-Mas et al. 2010 and 2017 (rat, dorsal SUB, tetrodes)
- 2010: across repeated light-dark-light transitions during pellet chasing, 45% of pyramidal units kept stable locational firing. Responses to the transitions were heterogeneous. Abstract only. [Brotons-Mas, Montejo, O'Mara & Sanchez-Vives 2010, Eur J Neurosci 32:648-658, doi:10.1111/j.1460-9568.2010.07308.x](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22subiculum%22%20AND%20(AUTH:%22Brotons-Mas%22)&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=10)
- 2017: "size" and "barrier" experiments, with barriers dividing a familiar open field into four sub-chambers. The authors found heterogeneous coding by place cells, barrier-related units and BVCs, and grid-like patterns in narrow-spike units that they suggest may be axonal. The abstract gives no counts. Full text not accessed, so field sizes are unverified. [Brotons-Mas, Schaffelhofer, Guger, O'Mara & Sanchez-Vives 2017, Neuroscience 343:174-189, doi:10.1016/j.neuroscience.2016.11.042](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22subiculum%22%20AND%20(AUTH:%22Brotons-Mas%22)&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=10)

Mouse data
- Abad-Perez et al. 2025 recorded 11 C57BL/6 mice. Tetrodes gave 65 SUB units (28 pyramidal and spatially modulated) and 147 CA1 units in 50 × 50 and 70 × 70 cm arenas. A 64-channel probe gave 196 SUB units in an 80 × 80 cm arena. Bins were 2.5 cm with 7.5 cm smoothing, and a field was 6 contiguous bins above the mean plus SEM with a peak above 1 Hz. Results: field size was CA1 29.02 ± 0.7% of the arena vs SUB 30.0 ± 1.14% (p = 0.528, no difference). SI was CA1 0.43 vs SUB 0.32 bits/spike. Rate was SUB 1.8 vs CA1 1.2 Hz. Within-session stability was CA1 0.72 vs SUB 0.61. Tetrode sampling was mostly proximal SUB. [Abad-Perez et al. 2025, Front Neural Circuits 19:1648844, doi:10.3389/fncir.2025.1648844](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833)
  - Arithmetic: 30% of a 50 × 50 cm arena is 750 cm². 30% of a 70 × 70 cm arena is 1,470 cm². The fetched text does not say which arena the 30% refers to.
- Sun et al. 2024 imaged mice with 1-photon miniscopes in small arenas, 30 cm triangle and square, 35 cm circle. They found 68.5 ± 2.6% of subicular neurons were place cells under a 95th-percentile circular-shuffle SI criterion (66.4% in a low-wall square). Field sizes are not reported in the text I read. [Sun, Nitz, Xu & Giocomo 2024, Nature 627:821-829, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07139-z](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)

Theory relevant to the single-unit picture
- Kim et al. 2012 interpret the dense subicular code as compressing a sparse CA1 code into a distributed one. They link this to compressed sensing (Isely et al. 2011) and argue that subicular cells give up single-cell selectivity for more information per neuron. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Bennett et al. (preprint, 2024; v2 2025) model subicular boundary, corner and trajectory responses as successor features built on place-cell-like inputs. They fit SUB with longer predictive horizons than CA1 (discount factor about 0.994 vs 0.9-0.95, roughly 16-20 s vs 1-2 s). This horizon is a model inference, not a measurement. The PMC page says a peer-reviewed version appeared in Nature Communications, which I did not verify. [Bennett et al., bioRxiv doi:10.1101/2024.11.06.622306](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11580870)

### Inferences
- Where both regions were recorded in the same rats, SUB SI per spike is about 3 to 8 times lower than CA1 (Kitanishi 0.34 vs 0.94; Ledergerber 0.11 vs 0.84; Lee 0.25 vs 1.12). SI per second is roughly equal (Kitanishi 1.39 vs 1.43; Ledergerber 0.42 vs 0.47). Subicular cells spend more spikes for the same information rate.
- Two coverage measures in rats agree on about 55-80% of the environment for typical SUB cells vs about 25-45% for CA1 (Kim distal 0.69 vs CA1 0.26; Ledergerber 56% vs 26% of bins; Lee sparsity 0.78 vs 0.43). Coverage roughly doubles, which supports the user's suspicion of broad, large-scale tuning for many subicular cells.
- The mouse tetrode result (equal field size, mostly proximal SUB) and the 68.5% place-cell fraction in 30 cm arenas suggest the broad-tuning picture may be weaker in proximal SUB, in mice, or in small arenas. Field definitions also differ a lot between studies.

### Gaps
- No study I read reports subicular field size in cm or cm² with a stated threshold alongside CA1. Sharp & Green 1994, Sharp 1997 and Brotons-Mas 2017 may contain such values, but I could not read their full texts.
- Fields per cell is reported only qualitatively ("majority multiple" in Kim 2012) or as a single- vs multi-field split (Lee 2018). I found no distribution of field counts.
- Barnes 1990 methods and numbers, Sharp & Green 1994 numbers, and Anderson & O'Mara 2003 spatial numbers are unverified because only abstracts were accessible.
- Kitanishi 2021 gives place-cell proportions only as a non-significant χ² test. Decoding errors in cm are in figures only, for both Kitanishi and Ledergerber.

## 2. Vector-based cell types: BVCs, vector trace, axis-of-travel, corner, object-vector; field sizes and prevalence

### Takeaway
Boundary-anchored cells are the best-documented subicular spatial type. Estimates range from about 10% of all neurons (mouse imaging) to 24-36% (rat tetrodes, depending on classification). Most BVCs are tuned to short distances, with peaks 0-8 cm from a wall. Their fields are long, wall-length strips rather than compact blobs, and they duplicate when a barrier is inserted. Vector trace cells make up about 29% of cue-responsive cells (36% in distal SUB). Axis-of-travel cells are about 10%, and corner cells about 4-7% of SUB vs 0.6% of CA1. I found no dedicated report of object-vector cells in SUB.

### Cited findings

Boundary vector cells (BVCs)
- Lever et al. 2009: 6 rats, dorsal SUB (5.8-6.3 mm behind bregma), tetrodes. 36 putative BVCs in a 62 × 62 cm box with 50 cm walls, and "up to 24% (45 of 186)" of cells in the region were BVCs. Fields duplicated after barrier insertion in 3 BVCs tested. Firing persisted in darkness (3 cells). 14 of 17 BVCs were "largely insensitive" to wall removal (a 90 cm raised circular floor with a drop edge). Fields stayed consistent across a 62 cm square, a 79 cm circle and the 90 cm platform. CA1 place cells in the same comparison correlated r = 0.803 within the same environment but 0.053 and -0.049 across environments. Field sizes are not quantified. Short-range BVCs outnumbered long-range ones, and far-tuned BVCs had broader fields. SI was 0.25 ± 0.05 bits/spike. Global rate was 5.04 ± 0.59 Hz. 26 of 36 BVCs were in the proximal half, which the authors attribute to electrode sampling. [Lever, Burton, Jeewajee, O'Keefe & Burgess 2009, J Neurosci 29:9771-9777](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)
- BVCs were first proposed theoretically as inputs to place cells, per Lever et al. 2009, citing the Hartley et al. 2000 model. [Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)
- Stewart et al. 2014: 5 Lister Hooded rats, tetrodes. Environments were a 150 cm diameter walled circle, a 100 × 100 cm square, a 155 cm unwalled platform and a 50 cm wide barrier. 42 of 57 cells (74%) with wall-adjacent fields gained a barrier-elicited second field (criterion: at least 40% of peak rate along at least 50% of the barrier length). 46 BVCs were identified in total. All 9 testable cells repeated their field across drop gaps. Rate maps used about 3 cm bins with 5 × 5 boxcar smoothing. In the 150 cm walled circle, BVC peaks sat on average 70.74 ± 1.26 cm from the centre. [Stewart, Jeewajee, Wills, Burgess & Lever 2014, Phil Trans R Soc B 369:20120514, doi:10.1098/rstb.2012.0514](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866438)
  - Arithmetic: the radius is 75 cm, so peaks sit 75 - 70.74 = 4.3 cm from the wall, at 94% of the radius. A wall-hugging field along the whole perimeter runs about 2π × 75 = 471 cm. BVC fields usually cover only part of it, and the paper gives no field area.
- Stewart et al. also describe "boundary-off cells" that fire everywhere except where a given BVC would fire. Only "a few" were recorded, so they are rare by the paper's own account. Their firing covers most of the arena. [Stewart et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866438)
- Muessig et al. 2024: 4 adult rats gave 517 SUB neurons and 17 pups (P16-25) gave 1,080. Tetrodes. A 62.5 cm square and an 80 cm circle, both with 50 cm walls. BVCs were classified by fitting a BVC model plus a 99th-percentile shuffle and an SI threshold. Counts: 187 adult BVCs, and 74, 61 and 46 across the developmental age bins. Preferred distance was modal 0 cm, with an adult mean of 7.5 ± 0.6 cm. In squares, directions clustered within ±12° of wall orientations, while in circles they were uniform. Barriers drove firing on the far side in 92% of adult BVCs and inhibition on the near side. [Muessig et al. 2024, Nat Commun 15:982, doi:10.1038/s41467-024-45098-1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10834499)
  - Arithmetic: the adult BVC fraction is 187 / 517 = 36%. The developing total is (74 + 61 + 46) / 1,080 = 181 / 1,080 = 17%, with per-age denominators not given in the text. The mean preferred distance is 7.5 / 62.5 = 12% of the box side.
- Sun et al. 2024 (mouse miniscope): BVCs were 10.7 ± 0.9% of SUB neurons in a square and 7.2 ± 0.8% in a rectangle. Criteria: border score above 0.6, largest field covering more than 70% of the nearest wall, stability above 0.3, and significant SI. [Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)
- Simonnet & Brecht 2019: 17 of 51 spatially modulated cells (31%) were boundary cells, all of them sparsely bursting. That is 17 / 84 = 20% of cells analyzed spatially. [Simonnet & Brecht 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6510334)
- Abad-Perez et al. 2025 (mouse probe): 19 of 67 spatially modulated units were BVCs (28.4%), which is 19 / 196 = 9.7% of all units. Place cells were 36/67 (53.7%), corner cells 5/67 (7.5%) and grid-like 7/67 (10.4%). [Abad-Perez et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833)
- Frost et al. 2021: border cells were 5 of 82 units (6%) in controls. [Frost et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8318085)

Vector trace cells (VTCs)
- Poulter et al. 2021: 6 Lister Hooded rats, dorsal SUB tetrodes. Boxes were 100 × 100 × 50 cm, 150 × 150 cm and 150 × 190 cm, with cues including a 50 cm barrier, bricks, a white stripe and bottles. 253 cue-responsive cells (cue field z ≥ 1, summed z ≥ 70). 73/253 (29%) were VTCs, meaning they kept a "trace" field after the cue was removed (trace score ≥ 0.205 and overlap ≥ 0.380, 90th percentiles of shuffles). VTCs were 69/193 (36%) in distal SUB vs 3/56 (5%) in proximal SUB, and per-rat distal fractions ranged from 17% to 50%. VTC distance tuning was 18.8 ± 1.2 cm from the cue vs 12.4 ± 0.6 cm for non-trace cells. Traces lasted 0.4-2.5 h, "on the order of 3 hours". Rate maps used 2 cm bins with 10 × 10 cm boxcar smoothing. Field sizes, SI and peak rates are not in the text. [Poulter, Lee, Dachtler, Wills & Lever 2021, Nat Neurosci 24:266-275, doi:10.1038/s41593-020-00761-w](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739)
  - Arithmetic: 18.8 cm is 18.8% of the 100 cm box side, and 12.4 cm is 12.4%.

Axis-of-travel cells
- Olson et al. 2017 recorded rats on a multi-path track with six interconnected routes. A subpopulation of SUB neurons fired bimodally at head orientations about 180° apart, so each cell fired for travel in either direction along one axis. Axis tuning followed the room frame when the track rotated and largely vanished in an open circular arena. [Olson, Tongprasearth & Nitz 2017, Nat Neurosci 20:170-172, doi:10.1038/nn.4464](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27991899/)
- Prevalence was "about 10 percent of the subiculum neurons", per the UC San Diego press release. This is an estimate I could not confirm in the paper. [Kavli Foundation news](https://kavlifoundation.org/news/toeing-line-study-finds-brain-cells-signal-path-travel)
- A search snippet of the preprint states that neurons fired over "a mean of 73.9% (+/- 12.5 STD) of all track". Unverified: bioRxiv rate-limited every attempt, so I could not read the sentence in context or confirm which cells it refers to. [Olson et al. preprint, bioRxiv doi:10.1101/050641](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/050641.full.pdf)

Corner cells
- Sun et al. 2024: 10 mice with Camk2a-Cre lines, 1-photon miniscope (GCaMP), CNMF-E extraction. About 4,700 neurons per geometry and 12,550 across convex/concave sessions. Bins 1.6 cm, Gaussian SD 2 bins. Corner cells needed a corner score above the 95th percentile of 1,000 shuffles plus stability above 0.3. Concave corner cells: triangle 7.2 ± 0.9%, square 7.0 ± 0.7%, hexagon 4.2 ± 0.8%, circle about 0%. Convex corner cells were 1.4 ± 0.2%. CA1 corner cells in a square were 0.6 ± 0.1% (5,212 CA1 neurons, 12 mice), significantly lower than SUB. Concave corner cells overlapped with BVCs at only 3.5% vs 12.5% expected by chance, and convex corner cells did not overlap BVCs at all. Arenas were small, 30 cm sides. [Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)
- Abad-Perez 2025 (mouse probe): corner cells were 5/67 spatially modulated units (7.5%). [Abad-Perez et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833)

Object-vector or landmark-vector responses
- I found no paper reporting object-vector cells in SUB. Poulter et al. 2021 VTCs respond to discrete cues, bottles included, at a vector offset, and Sun et al. 2024 convex corner cells respond to discrete objects' convex corners. These are the closest documented SUB responses. [Poulter et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739); [Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)

Other types reported in SUB
- Grid-like cells: 11/82 (13%) in Frost 2021 controls. 14.3% (tetrode) and 10.4% (probe) of spatially modulated units in mice (Abad-Perez 2025). Brotons-Mas 2017 found grid-like firing in narrow-spike units it suggests are axonal. Mao et al. 2021 found 2/110 in macaque. [Frost et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8318085); [Abad-Perez et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833); [Mao et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571030)
- Head direction: SUB had a higher proportion of HD cells than CA1 in Kitanishi 2021 (χ² = 10.88). HD carried more information per second in SUB in Ledergerber 2021 (0.22 vs 0.04 bits/s). [Kitanishi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376); [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)

### Inferences
- BVC prevalence differs by classification method: barrier duplication and model-fit criteria in rats give 24-36%, while border-score criteria in mouse imaging give about 10%. Studies with the most permissive model fit (Muessig) give the highest numbers.
- Short-range BVC fields sit 0-8 cm from walls in 62-150 cm arenas, so most boundary fields are wall-hugging strips. A strip along one 62.5 cm wall that is about 10-20 cm deep would cover 10-30% of the box area. This is my rough estimate, since no paper reports BVC field area.
- VTC tuning distances (about 19 cm) exceed non-trace distances (about 12 cm), so distal subicular trace fields sit further from cues.

### Gaps
- No BVC, VTC or corner-cell paper reports field area in cm² or as a fraction of the environment.
- I could not verify the axis-cell N or fraction from the paper itself, or the 73.9% track-coverage figure.
- No dedicated SUB object-vector study was found.

## 3. Proximal vs distal, dorsal vs ventral, bursting vs regular spiking

### Takeaway
In rats, distal SUB fires faster and more diffusely than proximal SUB. The clearest numbers are from Kim 2012: spatial activity fraction 0.69 distal vs 0.31 proximal, and mean rate 10.3 vs 2.5 Hz. Distal SUB also holds most vector trace cells (36% vs 5%). Proximal SUB is CA1-like. One study disagrees on where multi-field cells concentrate. Ventral SUB fields are larger than CA1 fields, per one study I could not verify. Bursting does not predict broader tuning. Sparsely bursting cells carry more spatial information than dominantly bursting cells.

### Cited findings

Proximal vs distal
- Kim 2012 reports spatial activity fractions of 0.31 proximal, 0.54 middle and 0.69 distal (p < 10^-4), and mean rates of 2.5, 6.3 and 10.3 Hz. Information per 20 ms bin was 0.030 proximal vs 0.062 for middle and distal. The authors write that proximal SUB neurons are "more similar to canonical, sparsely firing hippocampal place cells". [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Sharp & Green 1994 report that distal cells had "slightly higher" rates, spatial signaling and field size. The effect was smaller than in Kim 2012. [Sharp & Green 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8158272/)
- Lee, Lee & Lee 2018 report that multi-field units were more common in proximal SUB and single-field units in distal SUB (Z = 2.16, p = 0.033). This runs opposite to the Kim 2012 gradient in distributedness. [Lee et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)
- Poulter 2021: VTCs were 36% of cue-responsive cells in distal SUB vs 5% in proximal SUB. [Poulter et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739)
- Lever 2009 found 26 of 36 BVCs in the proximal half, but attributes this to electrode sampling rather than a real gradient. [Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)
- Kitanishi 2021 found proximodistal differences in theta phase and ripple responses. Its fetched text reports none in spatial tuning. Theta phase by location: distal-deep 285°, distal-superficial 321°, proximal 10°. [Kitanishi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)
- Abad-Perez 2025 (mouse probe spanning about 800 µm proximodistally): bits/spike correlated with burst propensity only in the most distal bin (r = 0.66), and stability showed no proximodistal difference. [Abad-Perez et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833)
- Cembrowski et al. 2018 used RNA-seq to split SUB into two adjacent subregions. Proximal cells project to targets including nucleus accumbens and prefrontal cortex, distal cells to retrosplenial cortex and ventral hypothalamus. Distal SUB was selectively needed for encoding in spatial working memory. The classes differ in single-spike vs burst firing, and Abad-Perez 2025 cites this paper for a proximal-to-distal gradient in burstiness. I found no field-size data in the summaries I could access. [Cembrowski et al. 2018, Cell, doi:10.1016/j.cell.2018.03.031](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/04/180419131055.htm)

Bursting vs regular spiking
- Sharp & Green 1994 found no detectable difference in spatial firing between bursting and nonbursting cells. [Sharp & Green 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8158272/)
- Simonnet & Brecht 2019 split cells into 82 sparsely bursting (80%) and 20 dominantly bursting (20%). Spatially modulated: sparsely bursting 46/69 (67%) vs dominantly bursting 5/15 (33%), p = 0.022. SI for all cells: 0.19 vs 0.06 bits/spike median. All 17 boundary cells were sparsely bursting. Bursts carried 2.3 times more information than isolated spikes (0.95 vs 0.41 bits). Both types were found throughout the proximodistal axis, with no corrected difference. [Simonnet & Brecht 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6510334)
- Lever 2009: 11 of 36 BVCs were bursting (modal ISI ≤ 8 ms), with a higher global rate of 7.2 vs 4.1 Hz and stronger theta modulation. 8 of the 11 sat in the deeper half of the pyramidal layer. [Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)
- Abad-Perez 2025: in mouse SUB tetrode data, burst index vs bits/s was r = -0.326 (p = 0.09, not significant). In CA1 the same correlation was positive (r = 0.381). [Abad-Perez et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12685833)

Dorsal vs ventral
- One study recorded ventral SUB and CA1 on a 4-arm radial maze with manipulable local and distal cues. Ventral subicular fields were larger than CA1 fields. Simplifying the environment shrank subicular fields and enlarged CA1 fields. Cue rotation shifted fields or changed firing completely. Unverified: I found only the title and an abstract paraphrase in a library catalogue record. Authors, year, journal and numbers could not be confirmed. Title: "Comparison of Ventral Subicular and Hippocampal Neuron Spatial Firing Patterns in Complex and Simplified Environments". [catalogue record](https://opact.sdu.edu.tr/vufind/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-a444t-97f4fd1c23d5b58e8fce78a078eab21d8ac7ca4543e6b6f9e818a051e40d3a1d3)
- O'Mara et al. 2009 is a review. It describes SUB as giving a "relatively coarse spatial representation", with dorsal SUB weighted toward space and memory and ventral SUB toward stress, anxiety and reward. Use it only to locate primary work. [O'Mara et al. 2009, Prog Neuropsychopharmacol Biol Psychiatry 33:782-790](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22subiculum%22%20AND%20(AUTH:%22Brotons-Mas%22)&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=10)

### Inferences
- The Kim 2012 and Sharp & Green 1994 gradient in rats predicts the broadest tuning in distal SUB. Studies sampling mostly proximal SUB, such as the Abad-Perez mouse tetrodes and Lever 2009, may show more CA1-like fields.
- The Lee 2018 multi-field result goes the other way. "Multiple fields" and "high activity fraction" are different properties: a distal cell can fire everywhere at graded rates without discrete fields.

### Gaps
- No direct field-size-in-cm comparison of dorsal vs ventral SUB could be verified.
- Cembrowski 2018 full text was not read. Its in vivo tuning data, if any, are unverified.
- No proximal vs distal field-size numbers in cm exist in the papers I read.

## 4. Population level: similarity across positions and environments, remapping vs generalization, decoding, dimensionality

### Takeaway
Population results split two ways. Sharp's studies and the BVC work show subicular patterns that keep their shape across environments, stretching or shrinking with the enclosure. Kim 2012 found that subicular rate maps do change between two identical-looking tracks, but almost every cell stays active (7% active in only one environment, vs 47% in CA1), and overall rates are preserved. With 100 or more neurons, subicular populations decode position as well as or better than CA1. They have higher manifold dimensionality (about 5.2-5.8 vs 3.0-3.6) and share manifold structure across tasks more than CA1 does.

### Cited findings

Generalization across environments
- Sharp 1997: rats ran in a cylinder and a square of equal area, made visually similar for some rats and visually different for others. Subicular cells showed "very similar firing patterns" in both chambers regardless of visual similarity, while hippocampal place cells differed. Sharp proposed that the subicular pattern is "stretched to fit within the boundaries of the current environment". Abstract only, no numbers. [Sharp 1997, Behav Brain Res 85:71-92, doi:10.1016/s0166-4328(96)00165-9](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9095343/)
- Sharp 1999 (Behav Neurosci): subicular patterns in a large square were "expanded versions" of those in a smaller square inside it, while hippocampal cells tended to remap. With barriers present, cells in both areas kept the small-square pattern, so subicular patterns scale but "do not extend across barriers". Abstract only. [Sharp 1999, Behav Neurosci 113:643-662, doi:10.1037//0735-7044.113.4.643](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10495074/)
- Sharp 2006: subicular cells show "the same firing behavior across environments", for example firing in the northwest of both a striped cylinder and a gray square, and scale to environment size. Many subicular cells also carry a directional signal. Sharp describes a "rigid framework of interrelated firing fields" fitted into each enclosure. Abstract only. [Sharp 2006, Behav Brain Res 174:206-214, doi:10.1016/j.bbr.2006.05.034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859764/)
- Kim 2012, on two geometrically identical tracks: same-environment cosine similarity was higher than cross-environment similarity for 39/39 SUB neurons (p < 10^-10) and for 10/11 CA1 neurons. So SUB maps did change between environments. Mean rates across environments correlated r = 0.87 in SUB vs 0.19 in CA1, and only 4/59 (7%) SUB cells were active in just one environment vs 7/15 (47%) in CA1. For context information per added neuron, distal SUB gave 0.022 bits and CA1 0.015 bits. The authors say the SUB population is "as informative about the identity of the current environment" as CA1. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Lever 2009: BVCs "showed much less remapping than CA1 place cells" across the square, circle and drop-edge platform, but some non-BVC subicular cells remapped. [Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)
- Brotons-Mas 2010: 45% of pyramidal units were stable across light-dark-light transitions, so the rest changed. [Brotons-Mas et al. 2010](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%22subiculum%22%20AND%20(AUTH:%22Brotons-Mas%22)&resultType=core&format=json&pageSize=10)
- Ledergerber 2021 reports "no obvious remapping" between open-field foraging and the goal-directed task in either region, with no number given. [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Lee, Lee & Lee 2018 call SUB firing "more schematic". Within-epoch population correlations were higher in SUB (Z = 4.30) and between-epoch correlations lower (Z = 3.85). Shuffling field locations removed this structure. With familiar scenes, rate remapping did not differ between CA1 and SUB (p = 0.11 and 0.85). Under scene blurring, CA1 rate remapping changed (p = 0.029) and SUB did not (p = 0.21). [Lee et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6596140)
- Geva-Sagiv et al. 2016 recorded Egyptian fruit bats flying on a linear track, using vision in light or echolocation in darkness. Between modalities, subiculum neurons switched on or off while CA1 neurons shifted their fields. Fields were sharper under vision. Abstract only. Counts, track size and field sizes are unverified because the PDF failed to load twice. [Geva-Sagiv, Romani, Las & Ulanovsky 2016, Nat Neurosci 19:952-958, doi:10.1038/nn.4310](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27239936/)

Similarity between positions and repeated segments
- Ledergerber 2021: the peak of the mean population-vector autocorrelation was narrower in SUB than in CA1. The text gives no widths. [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Nakai et al. 2024 report that spatial tuning correlated between the left and right arms of a T-maze at 0.41 ± 0.13 in SUB vs 0.26 ± 0.09 in CA1 (P = 0.0016). Subicular cells fire more alike on geometrically equivalent segments. [Nakai, Kitanishi & Mizuseki 2024, Sci Adv](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)
- Olson 2017: axis-tuned cells fire on all track segments sharing an axis, which groups separate locations. The authors' phrase is "mentally grouping these different locations", quoted in the press release. [Olson et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27991899/); [Kavli Foundation news](https://kavlifoundation.org/news/toeing-line-study-finds-brain-cells-signal-path-travel)

Decoding
- Kim 2012 used information theory, not decoding. Information grew by 0.064 bits per added distal SUB neuron, 0.059 middle and 0.037 proximal, vs 0.036 for CA1, per 20 ms bin with ensembles of 1-7 neurons. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
- Ledergerber 2021 used a GLM-based joint decoder with a 400 ms window and 5 cm bins in a 150 cm box. Position decoding beat CA1 only at larger ensembles. SUB vs CA1 p-values were 0.66, 0.61 and 0.32 at 5, 20 and 50 neurons, then 0.005 at 100 and below 0.001 at 150. Errors in cm appear only in figures. Head direction decoding was better in SUB from 5 neurons up. [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
- Kitanishi 2021 used a 250 ms window with leave-one-out validation on a linear track. CA1 and SUB errors were "comparable at all population sizes" (P > 0.2). Thinning SUB spikes to the CA1 rate made SUB decoding worse. Under additive noise, SUB error grew less than CA1 error. Trajectory decoding was better in SUB. [Kitanishi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)
- Nakai et al. 2024 reanalyzed the Kitanishi dataset: 11 rats, 282 SUB and 302 CA1 cells in the T-maze. Position R² with 10 units was SUB 0.25 ± 0.11 vs CA1 0.13 ± 0.078 (P = 0.010). Thinning SUB to CA1 rates reduced its accuracy below CA1. [Nakai et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)
- Sun 2024: a naive Bayes position decoder trained on mouse SUB had larger error at corners than at the centre in the triangle and square (P < 0.0001). [Sun et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972755)

Dimensionality and manifolds
- Nakai et al. 2024 used the Grassberger-Procaccia correlation dimension. T-maze: SUB 5.24 ± 0.70 vs CA1 3.07 ± 1.32 (5.73 vs 3.01 on z-scored rates, P < 0.001). Open field: SUB 5.84 ± 0.79 vs CA1 3.61 ± 1.10. Both regions lie on low-dimensional manifolds homeomorphic to the environment. Manifold geometry was shared across rats and regions. Cross-task similarity (T-maze vs open field) was higher in SUB (P = 0.0057), meaning "common structures between different spatial tasks were observed in the SUB" while CA1 was more task-dependent. [Nakai et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10830115)
- A FENS 2024 poster (Neuropixels 2.0, rat MEC, CA1/CA3 and SUB) reports that population dimensionality rises with place-cell remapping, less so in SUB than in CA1/CA3. This is a conference abstract and not peer reviewed. Authors and numbers are unverified. [FENS 2024 poster listing](https://world-wide.org/eposter/fens-24/dimensionality-neural-sub-populations-t851pjkv)

### Inferences
- "Generalization" in SUB seems to mean that the population stays active and keeps its overall rate structure and geometric relations, such as fields scaled to the walls. It does not mean identical rate maps. Kim 2012 shows map-level differences even between identical-looking tracks. The Sharp studies rest on qualitative abstract statements, which I could not check numerically.
- Broad single-unit tuning does not mean worse population coding. At 100 or more neurons SUB decodes at least as well as CA1, and the narrower population-vector autocorrelation (Ledergerber) suggests finer population resolution. All decoding studies tie SUB's advantage to its higher firing rates.
- Higher left/right arm correlation (Nakai), axis cells (Olson) and "schematic" coding (Lee) all suggest SUB populations treat geometrically equivalent places more alike than CA1 does.

### Gaps
- No paper I read reports a subicular population-vector correlation as a function of distance between positions with numbers. Ledergerber and Nakai show it in figures only.
- No cross-environment PV correlation values for SUB vs CA1 were found in text. Sharp 1997, 1999 and 2006 could not be checked beyond abstracts.
- Decoding errors in cm are figure-only in Ledergerber and Kitanishi.

## 5. Large environments, multiple environments, and non-rodent species

### Takeaway
No subicular recording in a truly large environment (over about 4 m) turned up. The largest are a 3.3 m diameter macaque arena, a 350 cm U-track, a 234 cm linear track, and boxes up to 150 × 190 cm. Multi-environment studies exist: Sharp 1997, 1999 and 2006; Lever 2009; Kim 2012; Stewart 2014; Muessig 2024; Sun 2024. Primate SUB shows the least sparse firing of the hippocampal regions sampled, and only 5% of units are position-tuned. Human iEEG shows boundary-related theta specific to SUB.

### Cited findings
- Mao et al. 2021 recorded freely moving rhesus macaques in a cylindrical arena 3.30 m across and 2.12 m high. SUB was recorded in 2 monkeys (110 units, 27 and 83) with tetrodes or tungsten microdrives. By a traditional analysis, SUB was 5% position-tuned (HPC 26%, EC 10%) and 74% head-direction tuned. In a model with eight variables, 72% of SUB units encoded at least one (HPC 45%, EC 56%). Facing location was the best single variable in 31% of SUB units. SUB had "the least sparsity" of the three regions (Kruskal-Wallis p < 0.001), with no numeric value given. One monkey's SUB cells came mainly from lateral prosubiculum and subiculum, the other's mainly from medial presubiculum, so "SUB" here is the subicular complex. [Mao, Avila, Caziot, Laurens, Dickman & Angelaki 2021, Neuron 109:3521-3534, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2021.09.032](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571030)
  - Arithmetic: the arena floor area is π × 1.65² ≈ 8.55 m².
- Robertson et al. 1999 recorded 2 rhesus macaques, and the paper's keywords include subiculum. Averaged over cells, information was 0.64 bits about head direction, 0.27 about spatial view, 0.10 about place and 0.04 about eye position. Search snippet only. The region breakdown and N are unverified. [Robertson, Rolls, Georges-François & Panzeri 1999, Hippocampus](https://www.oxcns.org/papers/271_Robertson+Rolls++99.pdf)
- Lee et al. 2018 (human): intracranial recordings in epilepsy patients during virtual navigation. Encoding target locations near boundaries gave stronger theta than locations near the centre, and the boundary × frequency interaction was specific to the subiculum (F(2,34) = 4.71, p = 0.016). An erratum was issued in 2019. The patient count is unverified. [Lee, Miller, Watrous et al. 2018, J Neurosci 38:3265-3272](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5884460)
- Geva-Sagiv 2016 (bats) is the only non-rodent, non-primate SUB single-unit study found. See section 4. [Geva-Sagiv et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27239936/)
- Largest rodent environments with SUB recordings:
  - Kim 2012: 350 cm U-track. [Kim et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458125)
  - Kitanishi 2021: 234 cm linear track and a 118 cm zigzag maze. [Kitanishi et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7946376)
  - Poulter 2021: 150 × 190 cm box. [Poulter et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7116739)
  - Ledergerber 2021: 150 × 150 cm box. [Ledergerber et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8170370)
  - Stewart 2014: 150 cm circle and a 155 cm platform. [Stewart et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3866438)
  - Lever 2009 also tested larger variants of its box (aL, aLB), with dimensions not given in the text. [Lever et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2736390)
- Sharp 1999 is the clearest test of environment size: subicular patterns expanded from a small square to a large one, with sizes not given in the abstract. [Sharp 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10495074/)
- Two 2023-2024 UC San Diego undergraduate honors theses describe SUB recordings on a long track and SUB cells "encoding multiple locations that divide up a larger environment". These are not peer reviewed, and I did not read them. [Rehor thesis](https://cogsci.ucsd.edu/_files/undergraduates/past-honors-theses/2023-2024/Paper_Rehor,%20Evalie.pdf)
- Gauthier & Tank 2018 imaged CA1 and SUB in mice on virtual linear tracks across several virtual environments. Most cells remapped randomly, while a dedicated reward-associated population was found in both regions. I could not read the subiculum-specific field or remapping numbers because the full text was blocked. [Gauthier & Tank 2018, Neuron 99:179-193, doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2018.06.008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/7023678)

### Inferences
- The macaque data agree with the rodent data on low sparseness and weak pure-place tuning in SUB. In a 3.3 m arena, only 5% of SUB units passed a traditional place test while most encoded direction or facing location. Whether subicular fields grow with environment size beyond about 2 m is untested in rodents.

### Gaps
- No SUB recordings in large rodent environments (over about 4 m, as in Eliav et al. 2021's 200 m bat tunnel, which recorded CA1 only) were found.
- I found no rodent single-unit study that explicitly targets the prosubiculum as distinct from SUB. Mao 2021 is the only paper found that names prosubiculum as a recording site.
- Bat SUB numbers and the macaque SUB sparsity values are unverified or not reported.
