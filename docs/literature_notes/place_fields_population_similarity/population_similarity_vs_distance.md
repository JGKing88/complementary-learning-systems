# Population similarity versus spatial distance within one environment

Scope note. These notes cover how similar the population code is for two positions in the same environment as a function of their separation. Between-environment similarity (remapping) appears only as contrast. Every number below was read from the source text unless it is marked "unverified". Arithmetic that converts a length scale into a fraction of the environment is shown inline and is mine, not the authors'.

Search coverage. I checked primary full texts for Green et al. 2022, Battaglia et al. 2004, Wang, Monaco & Knierim 2020, Rubin et al. 2015, Leutgeb et al. 2004 and 2005, Tanni et al. 2022, Komorowski et al. 2013, Keinath et al. 2014, Stefanini et al. 2020, Nielson et al. 2015, Deuker et al. 2016, Peer et al. 2019, Kunz et al. 2019, Stachenfeld et al. 2017, Low et al. 2018 (preprint) and Dasbehera et al. 2025 (preprint). For Kjelstrup et al. 2008, Eliav et al. 2021, Harland et al. 2021, Morgan et al. 2011, Brunec et al. 2018, Brunec & Momennejad 2022, Royer et al. 2010, Miller et al. 2013 and Nieh et al. 2021 I could read only the abstract, a commentary or a figure-level summary. I could not open the full text of Gonzalez et al. 2019 because the PDF exceeded the fetch limit and there is no PMC copy, so nothing from it is used as a finding.

## Which studies measure PV correlation, ensemble overlap or similarity against separation within an environment, and what falloff do they report?

### Takeaway
In dorsal CA1 and CA3 of rats and mice in small environments of about 1 to 2 m, every study that plots the curve finds that population-vector (PV) correlation falls from its peak to near zero within about one place-field width. That is roughly 30 to 40 cm, or about 15 to 22% of a 1.8 to 2 m track. After that the curve is flat, with no further dependence on distance. Few papers report the curve itself. Most show a PV correlation matrix with a diagonal band, and the band width is the length scale. Very few studies quantify the far-distance baseline. The width is not uniform within an environment: it narrows at cue boundaries and near walls.

### Cited findings

Dorsal CA1 and CA3, small environments, curve or matrix reported

- Green, Tingley, Rinzel & Buzsáki 2022, "Action-driven remapping of hippocampal neuronal populations in jumping rats," PNAS 119(26):e2122141119. Rats, dorsal CA1 with silicon probes, 4 rats, 28 sessions, linear track 1.8 m. The authors z-scored each neuron's trial-averaged rate at each position, then correlated across neurons for every pair of position bins. The analysis "includes all active neurons and, therefore, is not biased by the definition of a place cell." N = 209 CA1 pyramidal cells with mean rate >0.1 Hz in at least one condition, and 132 active in control trials. N = 275 for CA3. Average PV correlation decreased to near zero within <40 cm of separation, and Fig. 3C gives a spatial scale of about 30 cm. The text does not report the far-distance value beyond "near zero". The bin size is in the SI, which I did not read. Fraction of environment: 30/180 = 0.17, and 40/180 = 0.22. [Green et al. 2022, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9245695/)
- Battaglia, Sutherland & McNaughton 2004, "Local sensory cues and place cell directionality: additional evidence of prospective coding in the hippocampus," J Neurosci 24(19):4541-4550. Rats, dorsal CA1 with tetrodes. Linear tracks were 180 x 8 cm, and circular tracks had 91 and 130 cm diameters. Bins were 4.5 cm, giving 40 bins per linear track. PVs were built from spatially selective cells, with each place-field profile normalized to a peak of 1. The authors computed Pearson correlations between PVs at locations i and j (matrices in Fig. 6, decay curves in Fig. 7). The width of the high-correlation band, meaning the distance at which PVs become uncorrelated, was "significantly smaller on the cue-rich track" than on the cue-poor track in Experiment C (2 rats, 204 putative pyramidal cells matched across tracks). In Experiment B (4 rats) the correlation "tended to decay more slowly with distance on the cue-poor track" but the difference was not significant. The text gives no functional form, no values at specific distances and no baseline, so these must be read from Fig. 7. [Battaglia et al. 2004, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6729394/)
- Wang, Monaco & Knierim 2020, "Hippocampal place cells encode local surface-texture boundaries," Curr Biol 30(8):1397-1409.e7, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2020.01.083. Rats, dorsal CA1 and CA3 with 6 to 20 tetrodes, 48 rats in total. The 1D task used a circular track of 76 cm outer diameter and 10 cm width, binned at 1 degree, smoothed with a Gaussian of SD 4.3 degrees, and normalized by each unit's peak rate. The 2D tasks used 1 x 1 m boards with 4 cm square bins and 3 cm Gaussian smoothing. Place-cell counts were CA1-STD 669, CA1-MIS 890, CA3-STD 293 and CA3-MIS 429. The authors state the expected relation directly: "if two locations are not farther than the average size of place fields (i.e., the spatial scale factor)," "the distance between them would be negatively correlated with the similarities between their neural representations", and beyond that "the representations are maximally decorrelated with no further relationship to longer distances". Median field size was 60 degrees in CA1 and 73 degrees in CA3. As a fraction of the loop, 60/360 = 0.17 and 73/360 = 0.20. In cm at the track centerline (66 cm diameter, circumference π x 66 ≈ 207 cm), that is about 35 cm and 42 cm. PV correlation "changed more abruptly with distance between locations that crossed cue boundaries", and "The diagonal bands became narrower near the locations of the local-cue boundaries (especially in CA3)". The text gives no fitted decay, no baseline distance and no baseline value. [Wang et al. 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259364/)
- Rubin et al. 2015, "Hippocampal ensemble dynamics timestamp events in long-term memory," eLife 4:e12247. Mice, CA1, one-photon calcium imaging, 5 mice, more than 1,000 cells per mouse. The tracks were a straight 96 cm track and an L-shaped track of two 48 cm arms, each divided into 24 bins of 4 cm with the last 2 bins at each end excluded. Average PV correlations between different locations along the two tracks "were low and did not change over time (One-way repeated-measures ANOVA; F<1, p = 0.44)" (Fig. 1G). PVs were built from place cells. The text gives no curve against separation and no numeric baseline. [Rubin et al. 2015, Europe PMC full text PMC4749549](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4749549/fullTextXML); [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/12247)
- Neunuebel & Knierim 2014 (CA3 and DG, circular track) and Lee, Yoganarasimha, Rao & Knierim 2004 (Nature, CA3 and CA1) show PV spatial correlation matrices with a diagonal band on a circular track. Those studies analyze the band's shift and coherence between standard and cue-mismatch sessions, which is between-condition similarity. The search summaries did not report a band width as a function of within-session distance. [Neunuebel & Knierim 2014, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3904133); [Lee et al. 2004, journal-club PDF](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/lee04a.pdf)

Large environments: local rate of change rather than a full kernel

- Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022, "State transitions in the statistically stable place cell population correspond to rate of perceptual change," Curr Biol. Rats, CA1 with tetrodes, 5 rats, 629 place cells (89 to 172 per rat). There were four environments up to 350 x 250 cm (8.75 m2), 16.4 m2 in total. Rate maps used 4 cm bins with Gaussian smoothing of SD 2 bins, and only periods with speed >10 cm/s. The paper reports no curve of PV correlation against separation. It measured the Euclidean distance between activity vectors at adjacent 1 cm steps, which is the local slope of the similarity function. For runs orthogonal to a wall, this rate of change was greatest close to the wall (Kruskal-Wallis H = 12.2, p = 0.007, n = 5), with no effect for runs parallel to the wall (H = 3.78, p = 0.29). Population change correlated with visual change at r = 0.60 (partial r = 0.51), with speed at r = -0.30 and with turning rate at r = 0.27. Field area increased with distance from walls in environments B, C and D (H = 6.82 to 10.5, p ≤ 0.015). Fields per cell rose from 1.28 in the smallest environment A to 2.28 in D. Total field count scaled linearly with area (r = 0.98), and 84.8% of cells had multiple fields across environments. [Tanni et al. 2022, Europe PMC full text PMC9616721](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)
- Harland, Contreras, Souder & Fellous 2021, "Dorsal CA1 hippocampal place cells form a multi-scale representation of megaspace," Curr Biol 31(10):2178-2190.e6, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2021.03.003. In an 18.6 m2 environment "the majority of dorsal CA1 place cells exhibited multiple place subfields of different sizes". I read only the abstract and found no PV-versus-distance analysis. [Harland et al. 2021, Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29937352%20OR%20EXT_ID:33770492%20OR%20EXT_ID:21273408%20OR%20EXT_ID:18599792&resultType=core&format=json)

Bats in a 200 m tunnel

- Eliav et al. 2021, "Multiscale representation of very large environments in the hippocampus of flying bats," Science 372, doi:10.1126/science.abg4020. Egyptian fruit bats, dorsal CA1, wireless recording in a 200 m tunnel. "Individual neurons exhibited multiple place fields of diverse sizes, ranging from <1 m to more than 30 m". Fields of one neuron could differ up to 20-fold in size. A 6 m environment showed "classical single fields". The multiscale code appeared on the first day and in lab-born bats. A theoretical decoding analysis showed advantages of the multiscale code in the number of neurons required and in decoding error. As a fraction of the tunnel, <1/200 = <0.5% and >30/200 = >15%. The N of bats and cells is unverified. PV construction in the classic remapping papers, useful for baselines

- Leutgeb et al. 2005, "Independent codes for spatial and episodic memory in hippocampal neuronal ensembles," Science 309:619-623. The authors stacked all rate maps "including those of the silent cells" into a 3D matrix, and the vector of rates at each x-y location was the PV. The sample was 10 rats with 12 tetrodes each, 330 CA3 and 487 CA1 cells. The paper compares PVs only at the same location across conditions, not across distances within one environment. Median PV correlations were 0.91 (CA3) and 0.83 (CA1) for constant cue and place, and 0.03 (CA3) and 0.25 (CA1) for room 1 versus room 2. This is between environments and is cited here only because it shows how the "unrelated" baseline differs by region when silent cells are included. [Leutgeb et al. 2005, journal-club PDF](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb05.pdf)
- Leutgeb et al. 2004, "Distinct ensemble codes in hippocampal areas CA3 and CA1," Science 305:1295-1298. The main text analyzes overlap between rooms and the time course of PV distributions. I found no within-environment PV-versus-distance analysis in the main text. The supporting online text was not checked. [Leutgeb et al. 2004, journal-club PDF](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/~kempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb04.pdf)

Related measures that are not position-to-position kernels

- Stefanini et al. 2020, "A distributed neural code in the dentate gyrus and in CA1," Neuron 107(4):703-716.e4. Mice, calcium imaging, DG in a 50 cm square arena (1,109 cells, 3 mice) and CA1 in a 50 x 28 cm arena (863 cells, 3 mice), with an 8 x 8 grid of positions. The paper has no analysis of neural distance against physical distance. Position could be decoded above chance from non-place cells alone (Mann-Whitney p < 0.001), and median decoding error was "comparable to the animal size". [Stefanini et al. 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7442694)
- A 2023 Neuron paper on object-centered coding in CA1 binned rate-map correlations (with versus without an object) by distance from the object, in five 20 cm bins. Correlation rose stepwise with distance and then saturated. This measures how much remapping depends on distance from an object, not similarity between two positions. I read only the search summary. [Object-centered population coding in CA1, Neuron 2023](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(23)00270-2)

### Inferences
- Across Green 2022, Wang 2020 and Battaglia 2004, the dorsal kernel in a 1 to 2 m environment reaches near zero at about 30 to 40 cm, close to one median field width. That is about 15 to 22% of the environment. In these data I found no support for a smooth decline that continues across the whole environment.
- The tail value depends on how the PV is built. Green 2022 z-scored each neuron across positions. For a single neuron, z-scoring makes the rates sum to zero over all bins. So the mean correlation over all position pairs is pulled toward or slightly below zero, and the flat tail should sit near zero or just below it. Leutgeb-style PVs include silent cells and are not mean-subtracted per cell. Battaglia-style PVs are normalized to peak but not z-scored. Those constructions can give a positive floor when cells differ in mean rate. This is my reading of the methods, not a reported result.
- The kernel width is not fixed within an environment. It narrows at local-cue boundaries (Wang 2020), on cue-rich tracks (Battaglia 2004) and near walls (Tanni 2022, where the population changes faster near walls and fields are larger in the interior). A single global length scale fitted to a whole environment averages over these regions.
- In large environments, multi-field cells (Tanni 2022, Harland 2021, Eliav 2021) mean two distant positions can share active cells by chance. If field placement is irregular, as reported, this would raise the far-distance floor without creating a systematic decline with distance. No paper I read measured this directly.

### Gaps
- No full text could be checked for Gonzalez, Zhang, Harutyunyan & Lois 2019, Science 365:821-825, doi:10.1126/science.aav9199. The task brief suggests it contains ensemble correlation against track distance. That is unverified.
- Ziv et al. 2013 (Nat Neurosci), Sheintuch et al. (2017, 2020) and Low et al. 2018 were not confirmed to contain a curve of PV correlation against separation. Rubin 2015 reports only that between-location PV correlations were low.
- Wills et al. 2005 and Colgin et al. 2010 measure PV correlation between morph shapes, which is between-environment. I did not find a within-environment distance analysis in them and did not open their full texts.
- None of the sources I read report a far-distance baseline as a number with its construction stated.
- The Eliav 2021 supplement, Harland 2021 and Rich, Liaw & Lee 2014 (48 m track, Science) were not checked for PV-versus-distance curves. These are the most likely places to find a kernel measured over tens of meters.
- I found no PV-versus-distance data for subiculum, lateral septum, LEC, CA2 or birds.

## Human and primate evidence: does pattern similarity track distance over large scales?

### Takeaway
Human fMRI studies find hippocampal pattern similarity graded with remembered or real distance over very large ranges: 100 m to 30 km on a log scale in Nielson 2015, and across a virtual city about 390 m wide in Deuker 2016. These studies test retrieved memories or object cues, not the current position, and fMRI voxels pool very large populations. A human single-unit study (Miller 2013) found that recall-to-encoding ensemble similarity declined monotonically across near, intermediate and far bins in a virtual town. One iEEG study found the opposite sign. I found no primate single-unit study that plots population similarity against distance.

### Cited findings
- Nielson, Smith, Sreekumar, Dennis & Sederberg 2015, "Human hippocampus represents space and time during retrieval of real-world memories," PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1507104112. Participants were 9 women, analyzed with fMRI. Cues were lifelogging images, on average 76.1 ± 5.7 images and 1,995.7 ± 261.5 image pairs per subject. Image pairs were included only if their GPS distance was between 100 m and 30 km. Distances were log10-transformed. Neural distance was 1 minus the correlation of single-trial betas, z-scored within subject. In the left anterior hippocampus (anterior third of the Harvard-Oxford mask), space gave β = 0.7064, t = 5.55, corrected P = 0.021, and time gave β = 0.3968, t = 5.89, corrected P = 0.010. The time x space interaction was β = -0.1178, corrected P = 0.038. No other ROI was significant after correction, including right anterior, middle and posterior hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus and V1. No r value and no separate short- or long-distance test are reported. The paper is inconsistent about the temporal cutoff: 15.6 h in the Methods, >16 h in Fig. S3 and "15 h" in the Discussion. [Nielson et al. 2015, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4568259)
- Deuker, Bellmund, Navarro Schröder & Doeller 2016, "An event map of memory space in the hippocampus," eLife 5:e16534. 26 participants learned 16 objects in a virtual city whose side the authors estimate "roughly translates to 390 m", assuming 1.60 m eye height. The measure was the change in pattern similarity from before to after learning (PS'). Bilateral hippocampus PS' correlated with remembered spatial distance (Z = -3.719, pFDR = 0.0005) and with objective spatial distance (Z = -2.85, pFDR = 0.02). The searchlight peak for spatial distance was at MNI 34/-17/-22, T25 = 5.16. The paper does not test the functional form and illustrates effects with low-versus-high or quartile bins. [Deuker et al. 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5053807)
- Morgan, MacEvoy, Aguirre & Epstein 2011, "Distances between real-world locations are represented in the human hippocampus," J Neurosci 31(4):1238-1245, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4667-10.2011. This is fMRI adaptation, not pattern similarity: "fMRI response levels in the left hippocampus corresponded to real-world distances between landmarks" shown on successive trials of familiar campus landmarks. Only the abstract was checked. N, the number of landmarks, the distance range and any MVPA result are unverified. [Morgan et al. 2011, Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29937352%20OR%20EXT_ID:33770492%20OR%20EXT_ID:21273408%20OR%20EXT_ID:18599792&resultType=core&format=json)
- Miller et al. 2013, "Neural activity in human hippocampal formation reveals the spatial context of retrieved memories," Science 342:1111-1114, doi:10.1126/science.1244056. Epilepsy patients carried microwires (the text refers to participants six and seven; t(5) implies 6 were included in the group test). There were 95 place-responsive cells out of 371 units (25.6%). Similarity was the rank-normalized cosine between ensemble activity during free recall and during navigation, in 500 ms windows. Similarity was highest for locations near the recalled item's delivery location, middling at intermediate distances and lowest far away. Statistics were F(2,300) = 7.6, P < 0.001 across recall events, and near versus far t(5) = 4.0, P = 0.009. The bin thresholds and town dimensions were not in the text I read. [Miller et al. 2013, Science in the Classroom annotated text](https://www.scienceintheclassroom.org/research-papers/original-gps-how-we-remember-what-happened-where)
- Kunz et al. 2019, "Hippocampal theta phases organize the reactivation of large-scale electrophysiological representations during goal-directed navigation," Sci Adv 5(7):eaav8192. 22 patients with iEEG (2,330 channels) and 16 for hippocampus-focused analyses, in a virtual arena 9,500 virtual units in diameter. The direction is the opposite of the fMRI results: "pairs of neural cue representations with subjectively similar goal locations were more distinct from each other" (t21 = -2.09, P = 0.049). [Kunz et al. 2019, Europe PMC full text PMC6609163](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC6609163/fullTextXML)
- Brunec et al. 2018, "Multiple scales of representation along the hippocampal anteroposterior axis in humans," Curr Biol 28(13):2129-2135.e6, doi:10.1016/j.cub.2018.05.016. During virtual navigation, anterior hippocampus had higher inter-voxel similarity and higher temporal autocorrelation than posterior hippocampus, which the authors read as a coarse-to-fine gradient. I read only the abstract. This is temporal smoothness of the signal during navigation, not similarity as a function of spatial distance. [Brunec et al. 2018, Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29937352%20OR%20EXT_ID:33770492%20OR%20EXT_ID:21273408%20OR%20EXT_ID:18599792&resultType=core&format=json)
- Brunec & Momennejad 2022, "Predictive representations in hippocampal and prefrontal hierarchies," J Neurosci 42(2):299-312. They measured the similarity of each point on a path to a weighted sum of its successors over different horizons, using fMRI during naturalistic navigation. "anterior PFC showed the largest predictive horizons, posterior hippocampus the smallest", with anterior hippocampus intermediate. This comes from the abstract and a review. The horizon lengths in meters or seconds are unverified. [Brunec & Momennejad, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/786434v2.full.pdf); [review describing it, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9004662)
- Peer, Ron, Monsa & Arzy 2019, "Processing of different spatial scales in the human brain," eLife 8:e47492. 19 subjects compared distances at six scales: room, building, neighborhood, city, country and continent. The authors assumed 1 m within a room and 10 m within a building, with an approximately logarithmic increase across scales. The analysis was univariate activation, not pattern similarity. "Activity shifted from small to large scales along the posterior-anterior axis of the hippocampus" (group p < 0.001), but only 6 of 19 individuals showed it. Hippocampal responses were below baseline, and a reviewer disputed the hippocampal gradient. [Peer et al. 2019, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6739872); [peer reviews](https://elifesciences.org/articles/47492/peer-reviews)
- Primates. Courellis et al. 2019 found place cells in freely moving marmosets. A macaque virtual-maze study (Gulli et al.) decoded position from population activity, but the code did not generalize across two tasks in the same environment. Neither reports similarity as a function of distance. [Courellis et al. 2019, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6922474/)

### Inferences
- The human evidence for a graded, long-range relation depends on log-distance regressors, memory retrieval and coarse spatial sampling by voxels. A graded voxel-level relation could arise from many local kernels of different widths, or from a gradient of anterior hippocampal coding. These data cannot separate those cases. The anterior bias in Nielson 2015, Brunec 2018, Brunec & Momennejad 2022 and Peer 2019 points the same way as the rodent dorsoventral field-size gradient in the next section.
- Miller 2013 has only three distance bins, so it cannot distinguish a kernel that saturates after the near bin from one that declines across the whole town.

### Gaps
- I found no primate single-unit or human single-unit study that plots PV similarity against distance with a falloff length. Kyle et al. 2015 (eLife) concerns similarity between environments and was not used.
- Exact distances and statistics for Morgan 2011 and the town size and bin thresholds for Miller 2013 are unverified.

## Smooth broad decline or drop to baseline within one field width, and differences by axis, region and environment size

### Takeaway
In dorsal CA1 and CA3, the data support a drop to baseline within about one field width, followed by a flat tail. The best-supported route to a broader kernel is the dorsoventral gradient. Single-cell spatial scale grows almost linearly from under 1 m at the dorsal pole to about 10 m at the ventral pole on an 18 m track, and ventral ensembles generalize within a context. No study I read measured a ventral PV-versus-distance curve directly. In very large environments, cells gain multiple fields of mixed sizes, which changes the expected kernel shape. That has also not been measured as a PV curve.

### Cited findings
- Kjelstrup et al. 2008, "Finite scale of spatial representation in the hippocampus," Science 321:140-143, doi:10.1126/science.1157086. Rats ran on an 18 m linear track with recordings at several dorsoventral levels. "The scale of representation increased almost linearly from <1 meter at the dorsal pole to ∼10 meters at the ventral pole". There was a similar increase in the width of the central peak of the single-cell spatial autocorrelation. "The upper limit of ∼10 m may be sufficient for the hippocampus to form detailed representations of the rat's home range." As a fraction of the track, <1/18 = <6% dorsal and 10/18 = 56% ventral. This is a single-cell scale, not a PV curve. N is unverified. [Kjelstrup et al. 2008, Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:29937352%20OR%20EXT_ID:33770492%20OR%20EXT_ID:21273408%20OR%20EXT_ID:18599792&resultType=core&format=json); [abstract text via search](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/finite-scale-of-spatial-representation-in-the-hippocampus/811915281508073473-7984)
- Hasselmo's 2008 commentary on Kjelstrup reports that dorsal neurons "fired over a mean length of 98 centimeters" on the 18 m track, which is 0.98/18 = 5.4%. Ventral neurons showed "firing fields covering distances over 10 meters". It also states that "Ventral neurons fire almost everywhere in an environment in one room, and nowhere in an identical environment in another room." This is commentary, not primary data. [Hasselmo 2008 commentary, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2590634)
- Keinath et al. 2014, "Precise spatial coding is preserved along the longitudinal hippocampal axis," Hippocampus 24(12):1533-1548. The introduction states, citing prior work, that "population activity in the DH becomes decorrelated over small distances" whereas "population activity remains correlated over much larger distances" in the ventral hippocampus. The paper itself reports no PV-versus-distance curve. The visuospatial sessions recorded 89 dorsal cells in 6 rats and 65 ventral cells in 5 rats, in 35 cm and 70 cm cylinders. Ventral fields were larger, significantly so in the 70 cm cylinder, yet location reconstruction did not differ by region (F(1,70) = 2.1, p > 0.1). Ventral cells had higher mean absolute weights on spatial PCA components (t(64) = 3.8, p < 0.001), which the authors read as a more distributed code. [Keinath et al. 2014, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627)
- Komorowski et al. 2013, "Ventral hippocampal neurons are shaped by experience to represent behaviorally relevant contexts," J Neurosci 33(18):8079-8087. Rats, CA3 at dorsal, middle and ventral levels, 11 rats (9 for overtraining). Two 37 x 37 cm contexts each had two pots in distant corners. Ensemble similarity was the cosine of z-scored rate vectors per object-sampling event. Context coding was same-context different-location similarity minus different-context similarity. After overtraining, ventral context coding exceeded dorsal (t(6) = -7.41, p < 0.001), and a region effect appeared for the same-context generalization component (F(2,8) = 37.18, p < 0.001). During learning, "the larger ventral CA3 place fields usually extended only to areas adjacent to the location of one object and seldom to the location of the other object". Ensembles had only 2 to 5 cells ventrally and 6 to 16 dorsally. [Komorowski et al. 2013, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3667351)
- Royer, Sirota, Patel & Buzsáki 2010, "Distinct representations and theta dynamics in dorsal and ventral hippocampus," J Neurosci 30(5):1777-1787, doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4681-09.2010. "Discrete place fields of dCA3 neurons evenly covered all parts of the testing environments. In contrast, vCA3 neurons rarely showed continuous two-dimensional place fields". Ventral cells distinguished open from closed arms of a radial maze and fired similarly toward shared goals. I read only the abstract. [Royer et al. 2010 record and abstract via search](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/71346)
- Jimenez et al. 2018, "Anxiety cells in a hippocampal-hypothalamic circuit," Neuron. Mice, calcium imaging. Many ventral CA1 neurons were selectively active in the open arms of the elevated plus maze, and many of the same cells were also active in the center of an open field. A review cited in search results gives 51% of vCA1 versus 35% of dCA1 neurons as open-arm selective. I did not verify that figure in the primary text. [Jimenez et al. 2018, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5877404)
- Environment size. Eliav 2021 found single fields in a 6 m environment and multiple fields from <1 m to >30 m in 200 m. Tanni 2022 found fields per cell rising from 1.28 to 2.28 as area increased to 8.75 m2. Harland 2021 found multiple subfields of mixed sizes in an 18.6 m2 arena. [Eliav et al. 2021](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/neurobiology.labs.ulanovsky/files/uploads/eliav_etal_science2021.pdf); [Tanni et al. 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9616721/fullTextXML)
- Medial entorhinal cortex. A preprint shows by simulation that a grid module with identical spacing has a PV autocorrelation that "exhibits periodicity that does not decay". With spacing variability within a module, "the population vector correlation smoothly decays as a function of distance". With σλ = 0.04 m it "decays to baseline by 2 m". The asymptotic correlation is about 0.8 in 1D and about 0.6 in 2D. In mouse MEC Neuropixels data from Campbell et al. 2021, with a VR track run "over a hundred meters", the relation was "not truly monotonic" but showed a general decreasing trend. This is a preprint, and module identity in the data was not available. [Dasbehera, Dogra & Redman 2025, arXiv 2511.08292](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08292)

Methodological caveats reported in or implied by the sources

- Smoothing and bins widen the measured central peak. Wang 2020 smoothed with a Gaussian of SD 4.3 degrees on 1 degree bins, Tanni 2022 with SD 2 bins on 4 cm bins, and Battaglia 2004 used 4.5 cm bins. Any length scale under about two smoothing SDs is not resolvable. [Wang et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259364/); [Battaglia et al. 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6729394/)
- Cell selection differs. Green 2022 used all active cells, Battaglia 2004 used spatially selective cells normalized to peak, Rubin 2015 used place cells, and Leutgeb 2005 included silent cells. [Green et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9245695/); [Leutgeb et al. 2005](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb05.pdf)
- The ventral evidence rests on very small ensembles, 2 to 5 cells in Komorowski 2013 and 65 cells in 5 rats in Keinath 2014. [Komorowski et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3667351); [Keinath et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447627)

### Inferences
- If the dorsal-to-ventral increase in single-cell scale (<1 m to ~10 m) carries over to the population, the PV kernel half-width should grow by roughly an order of magnitude along the axis. Pooling across levels would then give a kernel with a narrow peak and a long shoulder. This is an expectation from single-cell scale, not a measurement.
- Ventral similarity structure looks organized by behavioral category or context, such as open versus closed arms, goal locations or the context box, as much as by metric distance (Royer 2010, Komorowski 2013, Jimenez 2018). A "broad kernel" measured in ventral data could therefore partly reflect category membership rather than distance.
- The arithmetic of the grid preprint matters. A floor of 0.6 to 0.8 means that, in non-mean-subtracted PVs, "baseline" is far above zero. So "drops to baseline" and "drops to zero" are different claims, and they depend on the normalization.

### Gaps
- No study I read reports PV correlation against distance for ventral or intermediate hippocampus on a long track. Kjelstrup 2008's 18 m track data would be the natural source, but I verified only single-cell scales from the abstract.
- I found no direct comparison of PV kernels between CA1, CA3, DG, CA2 and subiculum in one environment. Wang 2020 notes band narrowing at boundaries was stronger in CA3.

## Theory and explicit analyses of the population similarity kernel

### Takeaway
Theory papers state the two candidate shapes explicitly. A code built from narrow, local tuning curves has representational distance that saturates beyond the tuning width. Kriegeskorte & Wei call this a "splitter" code. A successor-representation code has a kernel whose width is set by the discount factor and which follows travel distance rather than straight-line distance. Stachenfeld et al. propose a range of discount factors along the long axis. Manifold methods recover global geometry from local overlaps even when raw PV distance saturates.

### Cited findings
- Kriegeskorte & Wei 2021, "Neural tuning and representational geometry," Nat Rev Neurosci, doi:10.1038/s41583-021-00502-3. With narrow tuning, stimuli 30 degrees apart can be as far apart in representation space as stimuli 90 degrees apart, which is a "splitter" code. Wide tuning gives a "lumper" geometry, sensitive to global rather than local change. Non-uniform width or gain warps the geometry. [Kriegeskorte & Wei 2021, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09743)
- Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman 2017, "The hippocampus as a predictive map," Nat Neurosci 20:1643-1653. The SR "encodes the expected discounted future occupancy" of each state, discounted by γ, and "states that predict similar future states will have similar representations." "Hosting a range of discount factors along the hippocampal longitudinal axis provides a multi-timescale representation of space." Larger place fields "reflect the community structure of the environment". The kernel respects barriers, because "states on opposite sides of a barrier cannot occur nearby in time". On a 1D track, SR fields "skew opposite the direction of travel". [Stachenfeld et al. 2017 PDF](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs/Stachenfeld17.pdf)
- Low, Lewallen, Aronov, Nevers & Tank 2018, "Probing variability in a cognitive map using manifold inference from neural dynamics," bioRxiv preprint, doi:10.1101/418939. MIND defines distance between states by transitions, giving "a large distance if the network only transitions between them by moving through many intermediate states". In simulation with 50 place and 50 grid cells in an 88 cm box, manifold and environment distances correlated at R = 0.998. In real data from rat dorsal CA1 (4 rats, 6 sessions, 15 to 52 neurons per session), manifold and behavioral distances correlated at 0.70 ± 0.06 in random foraging and 0.55 ± 0.16 in the sound task. Distance correlations were 0.76 for MIND, 0.55 for isomap and 0.23 for PCA. Decoding error was 1.8%, 57.7% and 116.6% above raw data for MIND, isomap and PCA. The text I read does not state the recording method. [Low et al. 2018, bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/418939v1.full)
- Nieh et al. 2021, "Geometry of abstract learned knowledge in the hippocampus," Nature 595:80-84. Dorsal hippocampal population activity in a VR evidence-accumulation task "was well-described by approximately four to six latent variables". Position and evidence were "jointly mapped in an orderly manner". I read only the abstract. [Nieh et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9549979)
- Dasbehera, Dogra & Redman 2025, the grid-cell preprint above, is a theory result. Spacing variability within a module turns a periodic PV autocorrelation into one that decays over meters, and greater variability gives faster decay. [arXiv 2511.08292](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08292)

### Inferences
These are my derivations, not results from the sources.
- Gaussian fields of width σ that tile a 1D space uniformly give a PV correlation, before mean subtraction, proportional to exp(-d²/4σ²). That is a Gaussian of width √2·σ, so the kernel is wider than one field by a factor of 1.41 and has no long tail.
- For an SR on an unbiased 1D random walk with step a, solving M = (I - γT)^-1 gives rows that decay as λ^|x/a| with λ = (1 - √(1-γ²))/γ. For γ near 1 the length scale is about a/√(2(1-γ)), so γ = 0.99 gives about 7 steps. The similarity between two such rows, the convolution of two Laplace kernels, goes as (1 + d/ℓ)·exp(-d/ℓ). Its tail is exponential rather than Gaussian, and its width depends on γ and the behavioral policy.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that fits a kernel shape, Gaussian or exponential, to measured hippocampal PV correlation against distance.
- Momennejad & Howard 2018 on multiscale SR was not checked.

## Keeping within-environment similarity separate from between-environment similarity

### Takeaway
Some baselines cited for "unrelated" positions come from between-environment comparisons. Leutgeb 2005 gives a room 1 versus room 2 PV correlation of 0.03 in CA3 and 0.25 in CA1. These should not be read as the far-distance tail of a within-environment kernel. Few papers report a within-environment far-distance value.

### Cited findings
- Leutgeb 2005 compares PVs at the same location across boxes and rooms, with silent cells included: median 0.91 and 0.83 for constant cue and place, and 0.03 and 0.25 for different rooms, in CA3 and CA1 respectively. [Leutgeb et al. 2005](https://itb.biologie.hu-berlin.de/%7Ekempter/Hippocampus_Journal_Club/Articles/leutgeb05.pdf)
- Rubin 2015: "within environment PV correlations from sessions that are two weeks apart remain higher than across-environment PV correlations from the same day". [Rubin et al. 2015](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC4749549/fullTextXML)

### Inferences
- In CA1, the between-room value of 0.25 suggests that the floor for unrelated positions is not zero when silent cells and rate differences are included. I found no direct measurement of the within-environment far-distance value in CA1 to compare with it.

### Gaps
- Remapping magnitudes are covered by another researcher and are not catalogued here.
