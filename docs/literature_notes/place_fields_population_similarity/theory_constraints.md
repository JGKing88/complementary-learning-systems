# Theory of place-like single units, field size, similarity structure, and multiple or large fields

Verification tags used below. "Full text" means I read the paper body through an HTML rendering. "Abstract" means I read the abstract or publisher landing page. "Secondary" means the claim comes from a press release, blog, review, or a later paper that describes the work. "Unverified" means I could not reach the source text and the claim rests on recall; treat it as a lead to check, not a finding.

## 1. Which biological constraints make single units align with interpretable spatial variables, and what theory says about rotation invariance of similarity-only or prediction-only objectives

### Takeaway
Objectives that constrain only a population quantity (a similarity kernel, a linear readout, a prediction loss) leave the single-unit basis free up to an orthogonal transform. The theory papers break that freedom with non-negative firing plus an activity or energy budget. Whittington et al. 2023 report that these two constraints, and not sparsity alone, push each unit toward a single task factor. Whether the resulting units are place-like or grid-like depends on the similarity kernel and the occupancy range (Dorrell et al. 2023), and the condition for one-variable-per-unit modules concerns the range of the variables, not their statistical independence (Dorrell et al. 2025).

### Cited Findings

Non-negativity and energy as the symmetry-breaking constraints

- Whittington, Dorrell, Ganguli & Behrens 2023 argue that non-negative activity together with energy efficiency in both activity and weights makes neurons represent single factors of task variation. They report this across tasks and architectures, VAEs included. The mechanism in their Theorem 1 is that mixing two factors in one neuron needs a larger bias to keep the neuron non-negative over all factor values, which raises expected activity energy. At fixed variance, minimizing energy therefore favours one factor per neuron. When there is a readout, prediction rather than a variance constraint keeps the representation from collapsing. Citation: Whittington JCR, Dorrell W, Ganguli S, Behrens TEJ, 2023, "Disentanglement with biological constraints: a theory of functional cell types", ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.01768. Source: [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01768v3); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01768). Verification: full text, first half only.
- The same paper states its disentanglement proof for linear representations of independent factors, argues it holds approximately for tall random mixing matrices, and finds that nonlinear networks mix factors in early layers and disentangle them in later layers. Tasks whose factors are entangled in the world, such as objects that always sit in the same place, give entangled representations. Appendix A.6 reports that sparsity alone does not produce disentanglement, so non-negativity is the driver. Theorem 2 was corrected in v3, dated 16 Oct 2026, to assume linearity in the factors. Source: [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01768v3). Verification: full text, first half only.
- Data Whittington et al. 2023 explain. Factorized space and objects yield separate grid-cell-like and object-vector-cell-like populations, matching grid cells (Hafting et al. 2005) and object-vector cells (Høydal et al. 2019). Entangling space with reward or behaviour yields warped grid fields, matching grid distortion toward goals (Boccara et al. 2019; Butler et al. 2019). They also discuss mixed selectivity in PFC (Rigotti et al. 2013) against reports of factorized PFC populations (Bernardi et al. 2020 and others) and axis-aligned IT face cells (Chang & Tsao 2017). In the half of the text I read, place cells were not an explicit target. No single headline number; results are disentanglement scores (MIR, MIG) in figures. Source: [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01768v3). Verification: full text, first half only.
- The term "rotation invariance" does not appear in the half of Whittington et al. 2023 I read. Their argument is phrased as "a linear representation of independent factors can be mixed across neurons, so the objective alone does not fix which neuron codes what". The rotation framing is a paraphrase. Source: [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01768v3). Verification: full text, first half only.
- Dorrell, Hsu, Hollingsworth, Lee, Wu, Finn, Latham, Behrens & Whittington 2025 refine the condition. In non-negative, energy-efficient optimal linear autoencoders, neurons split into single-variable modules when the joint support of the source variables meets a spread condition. The authors describe this condition as much weaker than mutual independence. They test predictions in nonlinear feedforward and recurrent networks and in entorhinal recordings that carry both spatial and reward information. Citation: Dorrell W et al., 2025, "Range, not independence, drives modularity in biologically inspired representations", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.06232. Source: [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/c07d71ff0bc042e4b9acd626a79597fa-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06232v2). Verification: abstract.

Grid-like units from minimal constraints, and how the kernel decides place-like versus grid-like

- Dorrell, Latham, Behrens & Whittington 2023 combine a functional separation loss, non-negativity, bounded activity, and "actionability". The separation loss minimizes the average Gaussian similarity exp(-|g(x)-g(x')|^2/2σ^2) between codes, weighted by χ(x,x') = 1 - exp(-|x-x'|^2/2l^2) and by an occupancy density p(x) of lengthscale L. Bounded activity fixes each neuron's mean-square rate to 1. Actionability requires a matrix T(Δx) with g(x+Δx) = T(Δx)g(x), which by representation theory forces each code to be a constant plus sines and cosines of a few frequencies. Citation: Dorrell W, Latham PE, Behrens TEJ, Whittington JCR, 2023, "Actionable neural representations: grid cells from minimal constraints", ICLR 2023, arXiv:2209.15563. Source: [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15563); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15563). Verification: full text, first half.
- In Dorrell et al. 2023, the χ kernel alone favours low frequencies, and its optimum in periodic 2D space is the lowest-frequency, place-cell-like code; the authors link this to Sengupta et al. 2018. Adding the occupancy density p(x) adds a bias toward high frequencies. Together the two terms favour an annulus of frequencies, and hexagonal grids are the densest packing of that annulus. Non-negativity favours harmonically related frequencies within a module, and separation favours non-harmonic frequencies across modules, which the authors call the "harmonic tussle" and use to explain multiple modules. Larger neural width σ gives one module and smaller σ gives several. Larger l pushes harder toward hexagons; with l = 0.1, 64-neuron modules were not hexagonal while 100-neuron modules stayed hexagonal. Source: [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15563). Verification: full text.
- Predictions of Dorrell et al. 2023 for grid data. Neurons per module scale as N ∝ (ν/μ)^2, with ν the lattice lengthscale and μ the field width; a "rudimentary" comparison with Stensola et al. 2012 module data is described as favourable. Modules should sit at small relative orientation offsets of about 3 to 8 degrees, with the frequency-overlap minimum near 4 degrees. Aligned or 30-degree offsets are poor codes. In square rooms the optimal lattice is slightly non-hexagonal, which the authors link to grid shearing (Stensola et al. 2015). In circular rooms the optimal scales fall at zeros of a Bessel function and lattices stay hexagonal. When a square room is squashed by 2/3, only grids optimal in both geometries, such as frequency 6π/L, should stay stable. The model does not explain field-to-field intensity variability (Dunn et al. 2017) or lattice bending in trapezoids (Krupic et al. 2015). Source: [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15563). Verification: full text.
- Sorscher, Mel, Ganguli & Ocko 2019 cast the learning dynamics of networks trained to path integrate as a pattern-forming system. They state that symmetries in how space is represented predict the firing fields learned, prove that a non-negativity constraint on firing rates is the symmetry-breaking mechanism that favours hexagonal fields, and find that when several grid maps are learned the optimum is a hierarchy of maps with increasing length scales. Citation: Sorscher B, Mel G, Ganguli S, Ocko S, 2019, "A unified theory for the origin of grid cells through the lens of pattern formation", NeurIPS 32. Source: [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/6e7d5d259be7bf56ed79029c4e621f44-Abstract.html). Verification: abstract.
- Sorscher, Mel, Ocko, Giocomo & Ganguli 2023 report that non-negative firing rates and center-surround (difference-of-Gaussians) place-cell readouts are both essential for hexagonal grids in trained path-integrating RNNs, and that trained networks fit neural recordings better than hand-designed mechanistic models. Citation: Sorscher B, Mel GC, Ocko SA, Giocomo LM, Ganguli S, 2023, "A unified theory for the computational and mechanistic origins of grid cells", Neuron 111:121-137, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2022.10.003. Source: [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10513491). Verification: abstract-level summary. Unverified details: that grid period scales with the readout field width, and that dropping non-negativity gives square, banded, or irregular lattices; I could not parse the PDF.
- Dordek, Soudry, Meir & Derdikman 2016 train a single feedforward layer from place-cell-like inputs to grid-like outputs with a generalized Hebbian (Oja-type, PCA-like) rule. With non-negative feedforward weights the output converges to a hexagonal lattice. Without non-negativity it converges to a square lattice. The spacing ratio between the first two modules is about 1.4, which the authors call consistent with experiments. The mechanism works only if place cells excite and never inhibit grid cells, and the model predicts that inactivating place cells removes hexagonal grid patterns. Citation: Dordek Y, Soudry D, Meir R, Derdikman D, 2016, "Extracting grid cell characteristics from place cell inputs using non-negative principal component analysis", eLife 5:e10094, DOI 10.7554/eLife.10094. Source: [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/10094); [PMC4841785](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4841785). Verification: abstract. Unverified: the requirement for zero-mean or difference-of-Gaussians inputs.
- Kropff & Treves 2008 show that triangular grids can self-organize in a single cell from firing-rate adaptation plus Hebbian learning on slowly varying spatial inputs, with no designed network connectivity. Alignment of grids across the population comes from collateral interactions between grid units. Citation: Kropff E, Treves A, 2008, "The emergence of grid cells: intelligent design or just adaptation?", Hippocampus 18(12):1256-1269, DOI 10.1002/hipo.20520. Source: [SISSA repository](https://iris.sissa.it/handle/20.500.11767/12573); [ModelDB citation](https://modeldb.science/citations/135812). Verification: abstract and secondary description.

Localized place-like fields from similarity preservation, and explicit rotation invariance

- Sengupta, Pehlevan, Tepper, Genkin & Chklovskii 2018 show analytically that, for data on symmetric manifolds, the optimum of a similarity-preserving objective with rectifying (non-negative) neurons has localized receptive fields that tile the manifold. Simulations on standard datasets reproduce the tiling. The authors present this as a model for place cells. NeurIPS reviewers questioned the claim that it learns arbitrary manifolds and called the MNIST and rotated-object experiments preliminary. Citation: Sengupta AM, Pehlevan C, Tepper M, Genkin A, Chklovskii DB, 2018, "Manifold-tiling localized receptive fields are optimal in similarity-preserving neural networks", NeurIPS 31. Source: [NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/hash/ee14c41e92ec5c97b54cf9b74e25bd99-Abstract.html); [reviews](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/file/ee14c41e92ec5c97b54cf9b74e25bd99-Reviews.html). Verification: abstract and reviews.
- Pettersen, Rogge & Lepperød 2024 train networks on an objective that maps closeness in space to closeness in representation and get place-like units. They state the objective is invariant to orthogonal transformations. Applying an orthogonal transform to a trained map gives a new map with the same similarity structure that is decorrelated from the original, which they equate with global remapping. Adding context to the objective yields context-dependent remapping, and a recurrent version also develops band-cell-like units. Citation: Pettersen M, Rogge F, Lepperød ME, 2024, "Learning place cell representations and context-dependent remapping", NeurIPS 2024. Source: [NeurIPS 2024 poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96470); [paper PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/0098a92f5f4e2d96c6db471e0c5507a8-Paper-Conference.pdf). Verification: abstract. Unverified: the field-count statistics of the learned units and which constraint picks out place-like axes.
- Kriegeskorte & Wei 2021 argue that tuning determines representational geometry but not the reverse: changing tuning usually changes geometry, and different sets of tuning curves can give the same geometry. Geometry, defined by all pairwise distances between population patterns, determines Fisher information, mutual information, and ideal-observer performance. They list as open questions why the brain develops one set of tuning functions over another and how best to quantify geometry. Citation: Kriegeskorte N, Wei XX, 2021, "Neural tuning and representational geometry", Nature Reviews Neuroscience 22(11):703-718, DOI 10.1038/s41583-021-00502-3. Source: [arXiv 2104.09743](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2104.09743); [Kriegeskorte lab PDF](https://kriegeskortelab.zuckermaninstitute.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/KriegeskorteWei_2021.pdf). Verification: abstract.

### Inferences
- Across these papers, the task objective fixes a population-level object (the similarity kernel in Sengupta et al. 2018, Pettersen et al. 2024 and Dorrell et al. 2023; the readout covariance in Sorscher et al. and Dordek et al.), and a separate per-unit constraint fixes the unit basis. Kriegeskorte & Wei 2021 state the general version: geometry does not determine tuning.
- An orthogonal rotation of a non-negative code generally produces negative rates, so non-negativity removes most of the rotational freedom. Positive rescaling and permutation of units remain free in all of these formulations. This is my reading of the math, not a statement any paper made in the text I read.
- The kernel and range set what the units look like. In Dorrell et al. 2023 a similarity kernel alone gives place-like units, and adding a finite occupancy range gives grids. In Dorrell et al. 2025 the range of the variables, not their independence, decides whether units become single-variable. Both point to the environment's extent relative to the kernel width as a variable that changes single-unit tuning even when the objective is unchanged.

### Gaps
- I could not parse the Sorscher et al. 2023 Neuron PDF or the second half of Whittington et al. 2023, so the quantitative link between readout width and grid scale, and any explicit place-cell results in Whittington et al., are unverified.
- None of the abstracts I read say whether a similarity-plus-non-negativity objective predicts the number of fields per place-like unit in large environments. Pettersen et al. 2024 may contain this; I could not check.
- Whether sparsity alone suffices is contested in principle. Whittington et al. 2023 report it does not, while Sengupta et al. 2018 get localized fields from rectification plus similarity matching without an explicit energy term. I found no paper that compares the two directly.

## 2. Normative accounts of place fields and what they predict for field size, field number, and the population similarity kernel

### Takeaway
The successor representation predicts that fields reflect expected future occupancy, so they skew backward along habitual routes and spread within well-connected regions but not past them, with grid cells as its low-dimensional eigenvector basis. Memory-compression accounts predict place-like fields as a by-product of compressing correlated sensory input, with field fluctuations that carry recent history. Optimal-coding analyses predict that single-field place codes gain resolution only linearly with neuron number and that the best field width depends on the dimension of the encoded space. Distance-preserving accounts derive hexagonal grids, not place fields, from local isometry.

### Cited Findings
- Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman 2017 propose that place cells encode a predictive representation, the successor representation (SR), in which a cell's firing is proportional to the expected discounted number of future visits to its state given the current state. The value function factors into the inner product of the reward function and the SR. Data explained: the backward skew of place fields that develops after repeated travel in one direction (Mehta et al. 2000 is the usual data reference). Fields expand within a cluster of strongly connected states but not beyond it. Grid cells encode the eigenvectors of the SR matrix, a low-dimensional basis that can suppress noise, ordered from coarse to fine. Citation: Stachenfeld KL, Botvinick MM, Gershman SJ, 2017, "The hippocampus as a predictive map", Nature Neuroscience 20:1643-1653, DOI 10.1038/nn.4650. Source: [bioRxiv 097170](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/097170v2.full.pdf); [Sainsbury Wellcome Q&A with Stachenfeld](https://www.sainsburywellcome.org/qa/hippocampus-predictive-map). Verification: abstract and author interview. Unverified, from recall: the SR account of fields at barriers and detours (Tolman-style mazes), field over-representation near goals (Hollup et al. 2001), field size growing with the discount factor along the dorsoventral axis (Kjelstrup et al. 2008), and grid fragmentation in hairpin or compartmentalized mazes (Derdikman et al. 2009). The bioRxiv full text returned HTTP 429 twice.
- Benna & Fusi 2021 propose that the hippocampus is a memory device that compresses episodes by exploiting correlations between sensory experiences, and that place cells may be a consequence of this compression. A simple compressing network produces place-cell-like units. The model predicts that place-cell activity is variable and that place-field fluctuations encode the recent history of sensory experience, which is offered as an account of non-spatial modulation and the reported instability of place fields. Citation: Benna MK, Fusi S, 2021, "Place cells may simply be memory cells: memory compression leads to spatial tuning and history dependence", PNAS 118(51):e2018422118, DOI 10.1073/pnas.2018422118. Source: [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10338047); [Columbia preprint PDF](https://ctn.zuckermaninstitute.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/content/Publications/2019/Benna%20%28Fusi%29%2C%20Are%20Place%20Cells%20Just%20Memory%20Cells_Memory%20Compression%20Leads%20to%20Spatial%20Tuning%20and%20History%20Dependence.pdf). Verification: abstract. Unverified: any prediction about field size or number of fields per cell in large environments.
- Mathis, Herz & Stemmler 2012 (PRL) state that a labelled-line place code has spatial resolution that scales only linearly with the number of neurons, while a nested cascade of periodic codes at different scales, each refining the coarser one, has resolution exponential in neuron number. Citation: Mathis A, Herz AVM, Stemmler MB, 2012, "Resolution of nested neuronal representations can be exponential in the number of neurons", Physical Review Letters 109:018103. Source: [LMU repository](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/14814/); [Santa Fe summary page](https://centre.santafe.edu/thermocomp/Resolution_of_nested_neuronal_representations_can_be_exponential_in_the_number_of_neurons). Verification: abstract and author thesis summary.
- Mathis, Herz & Stemmler 2012 (Neural Computation) compare place and grid codes with spiking noise, derive optimal parameters for both, and find that optimal grid codes need lattices of several spacings with resolution set by the spacing ratios. A conference abstract from the group reports lower distortion for grid codes than for the best place codes in 1D. Citation: Mathis A, Herz AVM, Stemmler M, 2012, "Optimal population codes for space: grid cells outperform place cells", Neural Computation 24(9):2280-2317, DOI 10.1162/neco_a_00319. Source: [LMU repository](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/15117/). Verification: abstract. Unverified: the derived optimal place-field width as a function of neuron number and environment size.
- Zhang & Sejnowski 1999 show that how tuning width affects coding accuracy depends on the dimension D of the encoded variable. Fisher information per neuron scales as σ^(D-2) for equal-width tuning, so narrow fields help in 1D, width does not matter in 2D, and broad fields help above 2D. Sharpening always raises Fisher information per spike, which they tie to energy efficiency. A commentary in the same issue (Pouget, Deneve, Ducom & Latham 1999) notes that the Fisher-optimal width shrinks toward order 1/N and questions whether Fisher information is the right measure. Citation: Zhang K, Sejnowski TJ, 1999, "Neuronal tuning: to sharpen or broaden?", Neural Computation 11(1):75-84, DOI 10.1162/089976699300016809. Source: [ML Anthology record](https://mlanthology.org/neco/1999/zhang1999neco-neuronal); [ESANN 1999 paper citing the σ^(D-2) result](https://www.esann.org/sites/default/files/proceedings/legacy/es1999-254.pdf). Verification: abstract plus secondary citation of the scaling law.
- Recanatesi, Farrell, Lajoie, Deneve, Rigotti & Shea-Brown 2021 train an RNN to predict upcoming observations and find low-dimensional but nonlinearly transformed representations that map the latent structure of the environment, with linearly decodable latent variables. In a spatial navigation task the network develops place-related activations similar to hippocampal recordings. They contrast this "predictive" solution, which extracts the latent space, with a "combinatorial" solution that does not, and give mathematical arguments for why prediction yields the latent map. Citation: Recanatesi S, Farrell M, Lajoie G, Deneve S, Rigotti M, Shea-Brown E, 2021, "Predictive learning as a network mechanism for extracting low-dimensional latent space representations", Nature Communications 12:1417, DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-21696-1. Source: [PMC7930246](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7930246); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/411ac526db244399bdd114924abfd060). Verification: abstract. Unverified: whether the place-like units have single or multiple fields and how field width relates to prediction horizon.
- Xu, Gao, Zhang, Wei & Wu 2025 test the conformal isometry hypothesis: the grid population code maps 2D space so that local distances are preserved up to a scale factor. They argue the hexagonal flat torus is the maximally distance-preserving embedding and study a single grid module with an explicit metric, without place-cell readouts. A third-party review reports that they modulate the velocity input so the RNN satisfies conformal isometry by construction. The earlier formulation is Xu et al. 2022, "Conformal isometry of Lie group representation in recurrent network of grid cells", NeurIPS 2022 workshop, building on Gao et al. 2019 and 2021. Citation: Xu D, Gao R, Zhang WH, Wei XX, Wu YN, 2025, "On conformal isometry of grid cells: learning distance-preserving position embedding", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2405.16865. Source: [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/4c751ed6cacd53971a1183dcd7821d8c-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16865v4). Verification: abstract and secondary review.
- Dorrell et al. 2023, covered under question 1, make the similarity kernel explicit: the χ kernel with lengthscale l is the term that alone yields place-like codes. Their predicted neurons-per-module relation N ∝ (ν/μ)^2 ties field width μ to lattice scale ν. Source: [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15563). Verification: full text.

### Inferences
- These accounts predict different things about field number. The SR ties fields to the transition structure, so a cell's fields follow connectivity and policy. Compression accounts tie them to input correlations. Optimal-coding accounts predict that single-field place codes become inefficient as the environment grows, because resolution grows only linearly with neuron number.
- For a 2D arena, Zhang & Sejnowski's σ^(D-2) result means Fisher information per neuron does not favour any particular field width, so in 2D field width has to be set by something else, such as energy per spike, the range of the environment, or the kernel in a similarity objective.
- The distance-preserving accounts fix the population metric, a similarity kernel in all but name, and derive grids. Under Dorrell et al. 2023 the same kind of kernel term without an occupancy range gives place-like codes. Which of the two you get depends on the ratio of kernel width to environment size.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the full texts of Stachenfeld et al. 2017, Benna & Fusi 2021, Mathis et al. 2012, or Recanatesi et al. 2021, so their quantitative predictions for place-field width and field count are not in these notes.
- I did not find a normative paper in this set that predicts the exact shape of the place-cell population similarity kernel, for example population-vector correlation versus distance, and compares it to data. The SR predicts a policy-dependent kernel, but I could not verify any quantitative comparison.
- Recent predictive-learning work on place cells, such as Levenstein et al. 2024 (bioRxiv, "Sequential predictive learning is a unifying theory for hippocampal representation and replay"), was not checked.

## 3. Multi-field and multi-scale theory: models of gamma-Poisson field statistics, the multiscale bat fields, and when multiple or large fields are efficient

### Takeaway
Rich et al. 2014 found that each CA1 cell lays fields down like a Poisson process with its own rate, and that rates vary across cells so that field counts follow a gamma-Poisson (negative binomial) distribution. Lee et al. 2020 showed the per-cell propensity is stable across environments and months and tied to excitability. Mainali et al. 2025 explain field statistics across rats and bats, in 1D, 2D and 3D, with a thresholded random Gaussian process, which implies largely random input to CA1. Eliav et al. 2021 found multi-field, multiscale fields in a 200 m tunnel and show by decoding that this code beats single-field codes in large environments but not small ones.

### Cited Findings
- Rich, Liaw & Lee 2014 recorded CA1 in rats on a novel 48 m track. Within a cell, field locations fit a spatial Poisson process with a fixed per-cell rate, a very good fit for cells with 6 or more fields. At the population level, a single Poisson rate failed because field counts were overdispersed. A negative binomial, equivalent to a gamma-Poisson mixture, fit best, with r = 0.57 and p = 0.14 reported in a secondary summary. The propensity to form fields is highly skewed across cells, and cells appeared to place fields independently. Citation: Rich PD, Liaw HP, Lee AK, 2014, "Large environments reveal the statistical structure governing hippocampal representations", Science 345(6198):814-817, DOI 10.1126/science.1255635. Source: [Caltech-hosted paper PDF](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf); [supplement](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/Rich-2014-supp.pdf); [summary by R. Schaeffer giving r, p](https://rylanschaeffer.github.io/kernel_papers/rich_science_2014_large_environments.html). Verification: abstract plus secondary summary. The r and p values come from the secondary summary and should be checked against the paper.
- A later review states that in CA1 the number of fields a cell expresses on a large track is unrelated to its in-field firing rate. Source: [eScholarship review](https://escholarship.org/content/qt0c52n677/qt0c52n677.pdf?t=ru3geh). Verification: secondary search snippet; authors and title of the review not confirmed.
- Lee, Briguglio, Cohen, Romani & Lee 2020 used longitudinal calcium imaging in mice across several large virtual environments with different reward contingencies and fit a single probabilistic model of CA1 built on one variable, field propensity. Propensity sets the number of fields per unit distance, varies about tenfold between neighbouring cells, stays the same across environments and over months, correlates with intrinsic excitability in intracellular recordings, and predicts reward-related activity. Citation: Lee JS, Briguglio JJ, Cohen JD, Romani S, Lee AK, 2020, "The statistical structure of the hippocampal code for space as a function of time, context, and value", Cell 183(3):620-635, DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.024. Source: [PubMed 33035454](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33035454/); [PMC7708714](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7708714); [Janelia news](https://www.janelia.org/news/virtual-landscapes-reveal-how-mouse-brains-map-the-world). Verification: abstract and press release. Volume and page numbers are from recall.
- Mainali, Azeredo da Silveira & Burak 2025 propose that place fields arise from thresholding one realization of a random Gaussian process. The model captures field arrangement statistics and predicts field shapes and topologies, and they verify these predictions in bats and rodents, in 1D, 2D and 3D, in small and large environments. They conclude that common mechanisms underlie the diverse field statistics and that synaptic projections to CA1 are predominantly random. A seminar summary by Mainali adds that the statistics are universal across species but depend on environment size and dimensionality. Citation: Mainali N, Azeredo da Silveira R, Burak Y, 2025, "Universal statistics of hippocampal place fields across species and dimensionalities", Neuron 113(7):1110-1120.e3, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2025.01.017. Source: [IOB news page](https://iob.ch/?p=9924); [HUJI CRIS record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/universal-statistics-of-hippocampal-place-fields-across-species-a/). Verification: abstract. Unverified: which datasets were used (likely Rich et al. 2014 and Eliav et al. 2021, not confirmed) and whether the model reproduces the gamma-distributed propensity through cell-specific thresholds or input gain.
- Eliav, Maimon, and colleagues in Ulanovsky's lab recorded dorsal CA1 in Egyptian fruit bats flying a 200 m tunnel with wireless electrophysiology. Single neurons had multiple fields of very different sizes, from under 1 m to over 30 m, and fields of the same neuron differed up to 20-fold in size. In a 6 m control environment the same kind of cells showed classical single fields. The multiscale code was present from the first day and in lab-born bats with no prior large-space experience. A theoretical decoding analysis found the multiscale code more precise than single-field codes in very large spaces, with no significant difference in small environments. Interacting attractor networks and feedforward models reproduced the multiscale code. Citation: Eliav T, Maimon SR, Aljadeff J, Tsodyks M, Ginosar G, Las L, Ulanovsky N, 2021, "Multiscale representation of very large environments in the hippocampus of flying bats", Science 372(6545):eabg4020, DOI 10.1126/science.abg4020. Source: [Weizmann-hosted PDF](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/neurobiology.labs.ulanovsky/files/uploads/eliav_etal_science2021.pdf); [IUCC CRIS record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/multiscale-representation-of-very-large-environments-in-the-hippo/). Verification: abstract and search-snippet summaries. The full author list, issue number, and the figure of roughly 20- to 100-fold fewer neurons needed are from recall or snippets and are unverified.
- Press coverage of Eliav et al. 2021 states that a single-field code would need roughly 10^13 to 10^15 neurons to cover multi-kilometre bat routes, against about 10^5 hippocampal neurons, and that the multi-field multiscale code removes this gap. Source: [News-Medical press article](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20210601/Study-reveals-a-novel-neuronal-code-for-spatial-perception.aspx). Verification: secondary, press only. Do not cite these numbers without checking the paper.
- The efficiency argument for periodic multiple fields comes from the grid-code literature. Mathis et al. 2012 show nested multi-scale periodic codes reach resolution exponential in neuron number against linear for single-field place codes. Sorscher et al. 2019 and Dorrell et al. 2023 find that learning several maps yields a hierarchy of scales. Sources: [Mathis et al. PRL, LMU](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/14814/); [Sorscher et al. 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/6e7d5d259be7bf56ed79029c4e621f44-Abstract.html); [Dorrell et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.15563). Verification: abstract, plus full text for Dorrell.

### Inferences
- Two kinds of explanation exist for multi-field, multi-size place codes. Normative decoding arguments (Eliav et al. 2021, Mathis et al. 2012) say multiple fields at several scales are efficient in large spaces. The statistical account of Mainali et al. 2025 says the same field statistics follow from thresholding random input, with no optimization. The two do not conflict on the data, since random input could be the mechanism that happens to produce an efficient code. They do differ on whether field placement carries structure beyond chance.
- The Rich et al. 2014 and Lee et al. 2020 results separate two levels. Field placement within a cell looks random (Poisson), while the rate of field formation is a stable, cell-specific trait. Any theory of multiple fields has to produce both.
- Field count in large environments grows with environment size, as Rich et al. 2014 and Eliav et al. 2021 both show, so whether a cell looks "single-field" depends on the size of the arena relative to its propensity.

### Gaps
- I found no paper in this search that derives the gamma shape of the propensity distribution from an optimality principle. Rich et al. 2014 and Lee et al. 2020 fit it, and Mainali et al. 2025 may reproduce it through random input, but I could not confirm the mechanism.
- Other models of multiple fields in large spaces that I did not check include Hedrick & Zhang 2016 (J Neurophysiol, "Megamap", attractor network for large spaces), Neher, Azizi & Cheng 2017 (PLoS One, grid-to-place models with realistic field sizes), Fenton et al. 2008 (J Neurosci) and Park, Dvorak & Fenton 2011 (PLoS One) data on multiple fields in larger arenas, Harland et al. 2021 (Current Biology, multiscale CA1 fields in a rat megaspace), and Tanni, de Cothi & Barry 2022 (Current Biology, field count and propensity across environment sizes). These are leads, not verified findings.
- Eliav et al. 2021 decoding numbers and the details of their attractor and feedforward models could not be read from the source.

## 4. Learned spatial representations in trained networks and what they claim about neural data

### Takeaway
Banino et al. 2018 and Cueva & Wei 2018 reported grid-like units in networks trained to path integrate, and Sorscher et al. explained this through the place-cell readout plus non-negativity. Schaeffer, Khona & Fiete 2022 found that grid-like units appear only for narrow, hand-picked readout and hyperparameter choices, so their emergence says more about the modeller's choices than about the brain. Their 2023 self-supervised framework produces multiple grid modules without supervised position readouts.

### Cited Findings
- Banino et al. 2018 trained a recurrent network to path integrate in an artificial agent and report grid-like units, hexagonal even in square environments but heterogeneous. They report that these units supported vector-based navigation and shortcuts. Citation: Banino A, Barry C, Uria B, et al., 2018, "Vector-based navigation using grid-like representations in artificial agents", Nature 557:429-433, DOI 10.1038/s41586-018-0102-6. Source: [later paper describing Banino et al., arXiv 2310.19192](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19192v2). Verification: secondary only; PubMed and Nature pages would not load. Unverified, from recall: training with supervised place-cell and head-direction-cell targets, and any reported grid scale ratio.
- Cueva & Wei 2018 trained an RNN on velocity inputs to perform 2D localization and found grid-like, border-like and band-like units, which they argue may be a natural solution for space representation in recurrent circuits, with boundary responses supporting error correction. A later paper notes that their grids were square in square environments, while biological grids are hexagonal. Citation: Cueva CJ, Wei XX, 2018, "Emergence of grid-like representations by training recurrent neural networks to perform spatial localization", ICLR 2018, arXiv:1803.07770. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07770v1); [later paper noting square lattices](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19192v2). Verification: abstract plus secondary.
- Sorscher et al. 2023 claim that trained networks fit neural recordings better than hand-designed mechanistic models, and that non-negativity and center-surround readouts are essential for hexagonal grids. Source: [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10513491). Verification: abstract-level. The datasets and fit metric are unverified.
- Schaeffer, Khona & Fiete 2022 report that most networks trained to path integrate do not develop grid cells, that grid-like units appear only under a narrow set of design choices, and that their emergence in earlier work was post hoc, reflecting architectures and hyperparameters chosen to get grids. They call this an informal "no free lunch" result for neuroscience: such models need substantial added inductive bias. Citation: Schaeffer R, Khona M, Fiete IR, 2022, "No free lunch from deep learning in neuroscience: a case study through models of the entorhinal-hippocampal circuit", NeurIPS 2022. Source: [NeurIPS paper PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/66808849a9f5d8e2d00dbdc844de6333-Paper-Conference.pdf); [ICML 2022 workshop page](https://icml.cc/virtual/2022/19912). Verification: abstract. Unverified, from recall: that the decisive choices are the difference-of-Gaussians readout and its width, and that the readout width sets the grid period.
- Schaeffer, Khona, Ma, Eyzaguirre, Koyejo & Fiete 2023 (preprint, arXiv) conclude that the "unified theory" of grid-cell origins is at best an occasionally suggestive description of trained deep path integrators. Citation: "Disentangling fact from grid cell fiction in trained deep path integrators", arXiv:2312.03954, 2023, preprint. Source: [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2312.03954). Verification: secondary snippet; author list unverified.
- Schaeffer, Khona, Ma, Eyzaguirre, Koyejo & Fiete 2023 propose a self-supervised framework of data, augmentations, losses and architecture with no supervised position information and no engineered readout. Networks trained with it develop multiple grid modules and generalize outside the training distribution. A third-party summary calls the framework Separation-Invariance-Capacity. Citation: Schaeffer R et al., 2023, "Self-supervised learning of representations for space generates multi-modular grid cells", NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2311.02316. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02316v1); [NeurIPS poster page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/72628). Verification: abstract.

### Inferences
- The debate between Sorscher et al. and Schaeffer et al. is about where the grid comes from. Both groups agree that the readout's spatial structure and non-negativity matter. Sorscher et al. treat these as a principled theory, while Schaeffer et al. treat them as modeller-supplied bias that the path-integration task alone does not provide.
- The trained-network papers show the same point as question 1. Representations trained toward a fixed population target, such as place-cell readouts, inherit their single-unit shapes from the target's similarity structure plus the unit-level constraints, not from the navigation task.

### Gaps
- I could not verify what any of these papers claim quantitatively about fit to neural data, such as grid score distributions, scale ratios, or module counts compared against recordings.
- Nayebi et al. 2021 (NeurIPS, "Explaining heterogeneity in medial entorhinal cortex with task-driven neural networks"), which compares trained networks to MEC recordings, was not checked.
- Open disagreements to carry forward: whether grid emergence in path integrators is principled (Sorscher et al. 2019, 2023) or tuned (Schaeffer et al. 2022, 2023 preprint); whether non-negativity plus energy (Whittington et al. 2023) or rectification plus similarity matching alone (Sengupta et al. 2018) is the minimal recipe for interpretable units; whether the optimal field width in 2D is set by Fisher information (Zhang & Sejnowski 1999) or must come from another cost (Pouget et al. 1999 commentary); and whether multi-field codes are optimized (Eliav et al. 2021) or random (Mainali et al. 2025).
