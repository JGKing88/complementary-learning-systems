# Audit of the mouse field-width sweep

Audit date 2026-11-21. I checked `sweep_fields_mouse.csv` (91 rows, 52 studies) against the papers, not against the sweep's notes. I re-read every one of the 72 rows marked `verified_in = this sweep`. I spot-checked 8 of the 19 note rows, from 5 studies. Full text came from the sweep's downloads (Europe PMC JATS XML converted to text, supplements, the Lee 2020 bioRxiv PDF) and from the earlier note downloads in `tmp/fields_mouse/dl/`. Figure values are my own reads: I measured Chen 2018 Fig. 3F and Sheintuch 2023 Fig. S2 in the raster images, recomputed the Hainmueller 2018 CDF crossings from the saved pixel masks, re-extracted the Lee 2020 Fig. 1G bars from the PDF vectors with PyMuPDF, and recomputed the Sosa 2025 counts from the source-data sheet. I recomputed every derived number. Scripts are in the job's `tmp/audit_sweep_mouse/scripts/`.

Verdicts are confirmed, corrected, unverifiable or dropped. "Corrected" means at least one cell of the row should change. Each change is in `audit_sweep_fields_mouse_corrections.csv` with the old value, the new value and the evidence. In quotes below, en dashes and minus signs are written as hyphens and multiplication signs as x.

## Corrections that change what the heat maps or the count table show

1. Kim 2023, four DG rows. The paper prints "pixel size ~1.3 cm2" for a 64 x 48 grid. Square pixels of 1.3 cm2 make a 73 x 55 cm grid, which cannot hold the 60 cm squares or the 69 cm circle the mice ran in. Any square grid that holds a 60 cm square needs pixels of at least 1.56 cm2. GoodSmith 2022, from the same lab, mouse line and 64 x 48 grid, states "~1.4 x 1.4 cm" pixels. Reading 1.3 cm as the side length moves the widths from 17.5, 19.3, 16.1 and 22.1 cm to 20.0, 22.0, 18.3 and 25.2 cm (14% wider). At 1.4 cm sides they would be 21.5, 23.7, 19.7 and 27.1 cm. This is an inference from geometry, so the notes keep the range. The granule against mossy-cell ratios do not change.
2. Gu 2023. The 31.1 cm width is per field over all fields, not the highest-peak field per cell. The paper counts 367 fields in 163 CB- place cells and runs the size test on n = 54 and 367 fields. That also gives a count the sweep missed, 2.25 fields per place cell over both running directions. The summary page lists Gu among studies that "keep only the highest-peak field"; that is wrong.
3. Quigley 2023. The units come from "hippocampal electrodes" on a drive with tetrodes aimed at both CA1 and CA3. The paper never assigns the place cells to CA1. The row should stay out of CA1-only maps and the CA1 count table, or carry a pooled-region flag.
4. Chockanathan 2024, both rows. The authors ran no field detection and report no place cells. A fields_per_cell of 0 would enter the count table as a measured count, so the cell should be blank. The null stays in value_reported.

The other corrections are labels and n values: Chen 2018 error bars (about ±1.5% and ±1.8%, not ±0.3%) and n = 7 CA1 mice; the Chen 2025 count is from day 5; Grijseels states familiarity; Sloin does not; Radvansky has 122 fields, not 120; Sartorato's place cells come from 5 mice; Zaremba defines mean ± s.e.m. and imaged 6 WT mice; Mao's per-field label is unclear; Danielson imaged on a fabric belt; Krishnan's 605 are fields; Geiller states the dorsal level; Dragoi gives a count for the disjunct session; the Senzai open-field rule is complete in the text; the Zhao familiar track was recorded before the novel one.

## Systematic problems

- Units taken at face value. The sweep copied the Kim 2023 pixel area without checking that the grid could hold the arena. Boublil 2026's assumed cm2 passes the same check (see its row), and the Donegan pixels were correctly left unconverted.
- Same cells in more than one row. Kim 2023 has a per-field and a summed row for the same 34 granule and 106 mossy cells. Chen 2018 has the same 154 cells in a real and a VR row. Zhao 2014 has four rows from the same mice on different days, and Dragoi 2013 two rows from the same three control mice. Go 2021's track and arena rows are 3 mice each from one lab, probably the same mice. A heat map should take one row per study, condition and statistic, or weight by study.
- `per_field_or_per_cell` is free text with 24 different strings. The heat maps need a fixed set: per field, largest field, summed, or unclear.
- Same-study comparisons on the summary page claim more tests than exist. "Four find no significant difference" between CA3 and CA1 counts Boublil and Hussaini, but neither paper tests CA3 against CA1; both test knockout against control within a region. Only Hainmueller (n.s., Dunn's test) and Sheintuch (U = 12, p = 0.06) test it, plus Dong (CA3 wider).
- The page says Dragoi "opened a second arm and the count rose from 1.5 to 1.9". The 1.5 is from the first-ever track in naive mice (DnRun1) and the 1.9 from a later L-shaped track, so novelty and experience change along with size. A novel isolated track in the same experienced mice gave 1.76 (DisjRun1).
- The page writes Zhao 2014 as "Novel 1 m track days 1, 2, 3, then the familiar 2 m track". The familiar track was recorded first. The widths are right; the order is not.

## Duplicates and double counting

- kim2023_1 and kim2023_2 are the same 34 granule cells; kim2023_3 and kim2023_4 are the same 106 mossy cells. One is per field, the other summed. Use one per map.
- chen2018_1 and chen2018_2 are the same 154 place cells in a real and a virtual square.
- zhao2014_1 to zhao2014_4 come from the same 5 to 7 control mice on four days. Cells probably overlap across days.
- dragoi2013_1 and dragoi2013_2 are the same three control mice (CT2-4) in two conditions.
- go2021_1 and go2021_2 are 3 mice each from one lab; the paper does not say whether they are the same mice.
