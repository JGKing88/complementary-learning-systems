## 1. Dorsoventral gradient: field size, fields per cell and spatial information along the long axis

### Takeaway
The largest fields in any single-unit study come from the dorsal CA1 of bats flying a 200 m tunnel, with individual fields up to 32 m (Eliav et al. 2021). Relative to the environment, the largest fields are rodent ventral CA3 fields. Kjelstrup et al. 2008 report a scale of about 10 m at the ventral pole on an 18 m track, which is about 56% of the track. In rodents, field size grows from dorsal to ventral. At the same time, fewer ventral cells have clear place fields and ventral spatial information per spike is lower (Jung 1994; Maurer 2005; Royer 2010; Keinath 2014). Whether this lowers population-level precision is disputed: Keinath et al. 2014 found ventral decoding accuracy comparable to dorsal for populations of the same size.

### Inferences
- The two lists rank differently. By absolute size the largest fields are bat dorsal CA1 fields of up to 32 m in a 200 m tunnel. Relative to the environment, the largest are rodent ventral CA3 fields of about 10 m on an 18 m track, roughly 56% of the track. No bat field exceeded 16% of its tunnel. [Der from Eliav 2021 and Kjelstrup 2008]
- Kjelstrup's 10 m figure is bounded by an 18 m track. It describes a "scale" of representation, and the per-level means are much smaller, at 5.52 m for the ventral group. The largest ventral field possible on that track is close to the track length, so the true ventral maximum in an unbounded environment is unknown. This is an inference about the ceiling effect, not a reported result.
- In mice the ventral enlargement is modest and depends on context: ventral fields were larger, but the field count per cell did not differ, and Keinath et al. found population precision comparable at matched population size. In rats, Jung 1994, Maurer 2005 and Royer 2010 all report fewer or less discrete ventral fields. Species, the exact ventral level, CA1 vs CA3, and 1D vs 2D tasks differ across these studies, so the difference cannot be attributed to any single factor.
- Royer 2010's vCA3 cells fired on multiple arms with similar goal-related patterns. In that maze, a "field count" for ventral cells partly measures generalization across arms, not separate spatial fields.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Kjelstrup 2008 full text or supplement. Field definition, bin size, cell counts per level, the exact dorsoventral coordinates, and the single largest field length are unverified. The 0.98, 3.73 and 5.52 m means and "21 rats" are from a secondary summary only.
- Jung 1994 numbers are missing: percentage of dorsal vs ventral cells with fields, field area, selectivity values, chamber size. The J Neurosci page returned 403 and the PMC PDF did not load as text.
- Maurer 2005 numbers are missing: field length dorsal vs middle, track length, N cells.
- Keinath 2014 reports mean field sizes and information values only in figures.
- I found no study giving field counts per cell in ventral hippocampus in a large environment. The ventral fields-per-cell question in large spaces is open in the sources I reached.

## 2. Subregions: CA1, CA3, dentate gyrus, CA2, proximodistal and deep-superficial axes, and environment size

### Takeaway
In the same rats and environments, dorsal CA1 fields are larger than CA3 fields: open-field medians were 1,775 vs 1,275 cm², and linear-track fields 75 vs 50 cm. CA3 fields carry more information per spike (Mizuseki et al. 2012). CA2 fields were 24.5% larger than CA1 fields, with more fields per cell and less spatial information (Mankin et al. 2015). In the dentate gyrus, granule cells are sparse and nearly single-field, about 1.0 field per active cell, while hilar mossy cells average 2.2 fields per active cell (GoodSmith et al. 2017; Senzai & Buzsáki 2017). Distal CA1 cells have more fields than proximal CA1 cells without larger fields (Henriksen et al. 2010). Proximal CA3 has the smallest, sharpest fields along the CA3 to CA2 axis (Lu et al. 2015). Field counts depend strongly on environment size. In small arenas most cells have one field. In 2 to 19 m² boxes and on 48 m tracks, CA1, CA3 and dentate gyrus cells average 3 to 6 fields each (Fenton 2008; Park 2011; Rich 2014; Harland 2021).

### Inferences
- With environment held constant in the same animals, the ordering of field size in dorsal hippocampus is CA3 < CA1 < CA2. Distal CA3 and CA2 fields are larger than proximal CA3 fields. Field count per cell is higher in CA2, distal CA1 and mossy cells. These rankings come from different labs and arenas and should not be merged into one numerical scale. [Inference from Mizuseki 2012, Mankin 2015, Lu 2015, Henriksen 2010]
- Park 2011's DG cells averaged 5.35 fields in the box, more than CA3. Park did not separate granule from mossy cells. GoodSmith and Senzai later showed that mossy cells are the multi-field DG population, so Park's DG sample likely includes mossy cells. This is my reading, not stated by Park.
- Fenton's per-field proportions imply that summed coverage per cell changes less than field count. 1.3 fields x 0.27 = 0.35 of the cylinder vs 3.4 x 0.073 = 0.25 of the chamber floor [Der]. Harland reports the same pattern directly: summed subfield area per cell did not depend on subfield number. Eliav's bat data showed average per-neuron coverage of 0.15 in the 200 m tunnel.
- Field size grows sublinearly with environment size. Fenton's chamber was 5.8 times the cylinder's area (21,000/3,632) but fields grew only about 30%. The exponent is ln 1.3 / ln 5.8 = 0.15 in area terms [Der]. Eliav et al. fitted an exponent of 0.3 for 1D field size vs track length from their 6 m and 200 m bat data.

## 3. Related structures with spatially tuned cells

### Takeaway
Lateral septum place fields on a T maze are as long as CA1 fields, 28.8 vs 29.1 cm, with a similar number per cell, about 1.7 vs 1.5. Fewer LS cells have fields, 26.5% vs 63.0%, and LS spatial information is about half of CA1's (Wirtshafter & Wilson 2020). About two thirds of medial entorhinal spatial cells are non-grid spatial cells (Diehl 2017), but their field sizes and counts were not in the text I read. Lateral entorhinal cells have weak spatial specificity (Hargreaves 2005). Place-like, head-direction and border-like cells exist in nucleus reuniens and the anterior and midline thalamus (Jankowski 2015). Retrosplenial cortex has place-cell-like cells with narrow fields in head-fixed mice (Mao 2017). Medial prefrontal place fields cluster at goals (Hok 2005), though another lab found very few place-specific cells in mPFC (Jung 1998). I found no quantitative evidence that any of these structures has fields larger than ventral hippocampus.

## 4. Other species: bats, nonhuman primates, humans, birds

### Takeaway
Bats hold the absolute record. Dorsal CA1 fields reached 0.6 to 32 m in a 200 m tunnel, with on average about 4.9 fields per cell per flight direction and only 12.2% of neurons single-field. In a 6 m section of the same tunnel, fields were classical single fields averaging about 1.3 m (Eliav et al. 2021). Primate place cells are fewer: 14.1% of units on a marmoset L-track, and 26% of macaque hippocampal neurons by a classical criterion but only 7% after controlling for facing location and other variables. Field sizes in physical units were not stated in the text I read (Courellis 2019; Mao 2021). Human single-neuron studies use virtual environments and report place-responsive cell counts, not field sizes in physical units. Human fMRI shows coarser, more autocorrelated representation in anterior hippocampus. In food-caching tufted titmice, 60% of excitatory hippocampal cells are place cells vs 27% in zebra finches, with an anterior pole gradient. Barn owls have 27% place cells in hippocampus and 48% in the adjacent hyperpallium.

### Inferences
- Bat field count per cell, at about 4 to 5 per direction over 200 m, is similar to rodent dorsal CA1 counts in 2 to 19 m² boxes: 3.4 to 5.9 per cell in Fenton 2008 and Park 2011. Eliav's statement that rodent counts are "much smaller" refers to the 1D large-track literature; it is not obvious against the 2D box data. The bat field-size range spans a factor of about 50, from 0.6 to 32 m, and individual neurons span up to 20-fold. Harland's rat megaspace subfields span about 150-fold in area, 0.023 to 3.46 m², which is about 12-fold in equal-area diameter: sqrt(3.46/0.023) = 12.3 [Der].
- Per-neuron coverage converges across species and sizes in the few studies that report it. Bat coverage was 15% of the tunnel per neuron. Rat dorsal CA1 coverage, from Fenton's per-field proportions, was 25 to 35% in boxes [Der]. Harland reports summed subfield area per cell independent of subfield count.
- For primates, classical place-cell percentages overstate pure place coding when gaze or facing location is not modeled; see Mao 2021's drop from 26% to 7%. Field-size comparisons across primates and rodents should only use studies that control for view and facing location.
- Human single-unit work is all virtual-reality based with no physical scale, so field size in metres or as a fraction of a real environment is not available from these sources. The fMRI granularity results are indirect and do not measure field size.

### Gaps
- Field sizes in metres for Yartsev & Ulanovsky 2013, Ulanovsky & Moss 2007, Courellis 2019, Mao 2021, Payne 2021 and Agarwal 2023 were not in the text I could read.
- I found no recent intracranial study of freely walking humans that reports place-field size. A Europe PMC search for 2021 to 2026 human single-neuron studies with walking or ambulatory designs returned zero hits under my query terms. This may reflect my query.
- Primary Rolls spatial-view papers were not read. View-field extent was not obtained.
- I did not verify whether Payne et al. 2021 also recorded black-capped chickadees. The abstract names only tufted titmice and zebra finches.
- Other species I did not cover with sources: quail, pigeon, systematic mouse vs rat comparisons, and fish.

## 5. How field definitions differ, and how much they affect comparisons

### Takeaway
Thresholds, minimum sizes, bin sizes, smoothing and inclusion rules differ several-fold across these studies. Some choices cap field size directly, for example Mankin's exclusion of fields of 25% of the enclosure or more. Others make fields look larger, for example Fenton's and Park's rate above 0 Hz threshold. Field size in 1D studies is a length in one running direction, while 2D studies report area, so the two cannot be compared without conversion. Environment size changes both field size and count: fields grow sublinearly with environment size, and counts per cell rise from about one to three to six. Cross-study rankings are therefore reliable only within a study.

#### Effects of definitions documented in the sources
- Royer 2010 used a 3 times wider smoothing kernel for ventral zigzag cells than for dorsal cells, 15 vs 5 pixels. This smooths ventral maps more and can enlarge measured ventral fields. [FT, Royer 2010]
- Mankin 2015 excluded cells unless their fields were smaller than 25% of the enclosure in at least one session. A population with broad fields, such as ventral or CA2 cells, is partly removed by this rule. [FT, Mankin 2015]
- Fenton 2008 and Park 2011 define a field as any contiguous area firing above 0 AP/s. This includes the low-rate skirt of a field and makes absolute field sizes larger than those from 10 to 20% of peak thresholds. [FT, Fenton 2008; Park 2011]
- Mizuseki 2012 uses a 10% of peak edge where most others use 20%. This makes its fields larger at the edges. [FT]
- Eliav 2021 measured field size from 5 to 95% of in-field spike positions to undo the smearing from rate-map smoothing. The supplement repeats the analysis with 10% and 30% edges, 0 to 100%, 10 to 90% and 20 to 80% spike percentiles, and 25% or 30% lap criteria. I did not extract whether each variant changed the size range. [FT]
- Harland 2021 shows that a fixed 1 Hz threshold left 45 of 383 cells without fields, compared with the cell-specific threshold. [FT]
- Mao 2021 shows that the classical place-cell criterion gave 26% of hippocampal cells, while a model controlling for facing location, head direction and other variables gave 7%. [FT]
- Rate maps per running direction: Eliav reports 4.9 fields per direction [Der], while 2D studies report fields per cell over the whole arena. Kjelstrup and Mizuseki track lengths are also per-direction measures. In 1D, a cell active in both directions counts separate directional fields in some studies, like Courellis with 64 bidirectional cells. [Inference]
- Converting 1D to 2D: a 2D field of area A corresponds to a circle of diameter 2 x sqrt(A/π). Mizuseki's CA1 open-field median of 1,775 cm² corresponds to 47.5 cm, which is smaller than its 75 cm linear-track field length in the same cells' region. 1D and 2D sizes are not interchangeable even within a study. [Der]

#### Disagreements between studies
- Ventral spatial coding. Jung 1994 found fewer ventral place cells and lower resolution. Poucet 1994 found ventral place cells very similar to dorsal ones. Keinath 2014 found population precision preserved in mouse ventral CA1. Royer 2010 found vCA3 rarely had continuous 2D fields and generalized across arms. [Abs and FT as above]
- Ventral fields per cell. Maurer 2005 says the probability of a field in a given environment falls toward the temporal pole. Keinath 2014 found no difference in field count between dorsal and ventral CA1 in visuospatial trials. Royer 2010 found vCA3 cells fired on multiple arms. [Abs and FT]
- Is scale a dorsoventral property or a per-cell property? Kjelstrup 2008 frames scale as a long-axis gradient. Harland 2021 and Eliav 2021 both find a wide range of field sizes within single dorsal CA1 neurons in large environments, and Harland states the gradient concept "is challenged". These are not direct contradictions, since Kjelstrup measured mean size by level and the others measured within-cell spread. [Abs and FT]
- Fields per cell in large environments. Park 2011 reports more fields per cell in CA1 than CA3 in a large box, 5.89 vs 4.96. Mizuseki 2012 reports multi-arm firing more common in CA3 than CA1 in a radial maze. Both report CA1 fields larger than CA3. [FT]
- DG field count. Park 2011 reports DG cells averaged 5.35 fields in a 1.8 x 1.4 m box. GoodSmith 2017 and Senzai 2017 report granule cells at about one field and mossy cells at two or more. Cell-type mixing in Park's DG sample may explain this. [FT, inference noted in section 2]
- Prefrontal place fields. Hok 2005 reports a substantial proportion of PL/IL cells with fields; Jung 1998 reports very few place-specific mPFC cells. [Abs]

### Inferences
- Within-study comparisons are the most reliable evidence: Mizuseki 2012 for CA1 vs CA3, Mankin 2015 for CA2, Henriksen 2010 for proximodistal CA1, GoodSmith 2017 and Senzai 2017 for DG cell types, Wirtshafter 2020 for LS vs CA1, Payne 2021 for bird species, Agarwal 2023 for owl regions, and Eliav 2021 for 6 m vs 200 m.
- Fractions of the environment computed across studies mix environment-size effects with regional effects. A dorsal CA1 field is 27% of a 68 cm cylinder (Fenton), 30% of a 2.5 m track (Mizuseki), and at most 16% of a 200 m bat tunnel (Eliav). The fraction falls as environments grow, because fields grow sublinearly. Ventral CA3's 10 m out of 18 m is the clear outlier toward large fractions. [Der and inference]

### Gaps
- Few studies report how field-size estimates change under alternative thresholds. Eliav and Harland ran such controls, but I did not extract their quantitative outcomes.
- Bin and smoothing parameters were not obtained for Kjelstrup 2008, Jung 1994, Maurer 2005, Rich 2014, Payne 2021 or Agarwal 2023.
