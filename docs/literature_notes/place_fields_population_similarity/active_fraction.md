# Fraction of hippocampal principal cells active in an environment, silent cells, and sparseness

Verification tags used below. [full text] means I read the number in the paper's full text (PMC author manuscript, open-access article, or author-hosted PDF). [abstract] means the number is in the PubMed abstract. [snippet] means the number came from a search-engine extract of the paper's text that I could not open myself, so treat it as unverified. [secondary] means the number comes from a later paper or review describing the original, not from the original.

Full citations are given at first mention. Later mentions use author and year with the same link.

## 1. What fraction of principal cells have place fields in a given environment, by region and method?

### Takeaway
In a standard small environment, roughly 20-50% of dorsal CA1 pyramidal cells have place fields, and the rest are silent or nearly silent. This estimate rests on tetrodes with sleep- or anesthesia-based detection of silent cells, Arc imaging (~35-40% of CA1), and calcium imaging (~15-30%). CA3 is sparser (~18-30% per environment), dentate granule cells are much sparser (~2-10% per environment), and hilar mossy cells are dense (~88% per environment). The reported fraction depends heavily on the denominator (all cells versus cells that fired) and on the place-cell criterion. One CA1 dataset gives 74.7% versus 37.3% in the same open field, depending on whether a coherence criterion is added.

### Cited findings

#### CA1, tetrodes and wires
- Under barbiturate anesthesia, Thompson and Best isolated pyramidal units in rats and later recorded them in freely moving animals. "Nearly two-thirds" of these units were behaviorally silent in the awake rat in the test environment. Reanesthetization and antidromic stimulation reactivated the silent cells, which confirmed they were still being recorded. Of the spontaneously active pyramidal cells, more than 92% were place cells, meaning they had spatially specific firing in at least one environment. Many place cells had low or no firing in other, dissimilar environments. The abstract gives no cell counts, N rats, environment dimensions or rate criteria, and the PMC full text is a scanned PDF I could not read. [abstract] Thompson LT, Best PJ (1989). Place cells and silent cells in the hippocampus of freely-behaving rats. J Neurosci 9(7):2382-2390. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-07-02382.1989](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-07-02382.1989), PMID 2746333.
- Karlsson and Frank recorded CA1 and CA3 principal cells in rats with tetrodes in novel and familiar environments. In any given environment, "less than half of the neurons in areas CA1 and CA3 are active whereas the rest are essentially silent." CA1, but not CA3, produced about twice as many spikes in novel compared with familiar environments. [abstract] Karlsson MP, Frank LM (2008). Network dynamics underlying the formation of sparse, informative representations in the hippocampus. J Neurosci 28:14271-14281. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4261-08.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4261-08.2008).
- Rich, Liaw and Lee summarize the earlier literature this way. In typical laboratory environments, with tracks shorter than 5 m or open arenas smaller than 1 m², about 20-50% of dorsal CA1 pyramidal neurons are recruited as place cells, and most of them have a single field. [full text, the paper's own literature summary] Rich PD, Liaw HP, Lee AK (2014). Large environments reveal the statistical structure governing hippocampal representations. Science 345(6198):814-817. [doi:10.1126/science.1255635](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255635), [author-hosted PDF](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf).
- Mizuseki et al. recorded dorsal CA1 (3,670 pyramidal cells, 13 rats) and CA3 (2,203 cells, 6 of those rats) with silicon probes in 9 rats and tetrodes in 4. The fraction of cells with fields depended strongly on the criterion. [full text] Mizuseki K, Royer S, Diba K, Buzsáki G (2012). Activity dynamics and behavioral correlates of CA3 and CA1 hippocampal pyramidal neurons. Hippocampus 22(8):1659-1680. [doi:10.1002/hipo.22002](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22002), PMC3718552.
  - In the open field, 200 × 100 cm or 180 × 180 cm, 74.7% of CA1 and 59.6% of CA3 cells had fields under a rate-only criterion. With a spatial-coherence criterion added, the figures were 37.3% for CA1 and 37.9% for CA3.
  - On a 250 cm linear track, 64.2% of CA1 and 31.0% of CA3 cells had fields under the rate-only criterion, and 48.0% and 16.8% with coherence added.
  - Median open-field spatial information was 1.31 bits/spike in CA1 and 1.77 in CA3. On the track it was 0.83 in CA1 and 1.57 in CA3.
  - Mean firing rate during running was 0.88 ± 1.23 Hz in CA1 and 0.50 ± 0.78 Hz in CA3.
  - I could not confirm from the text whether the denominator includes cells silent on the maze.
- Fenton et al. recorded rat CA1 pyramidal cells in a standard cylinder and in a chamber about 6 times larger. Of the active pyramidal cells, 64% (307/477) were place cells in the cylinder. In the chamber, a significantly higher 72% (322/448) were (p < 0.001). Note the denominator: these are cells that were active, not all isolated cells. [snippet from the J Neurosci PDF; the chamber-size ratio is in the abstract] Fenton AA, Kao HY, Neymotin SA, Olypher A, Vayntrub Y, Lytton WW, Ludvig N (2008). Unmasking the CA1 ensemble place code by exposures to small and large environments: more place cells and multiple, irregularly arranged, and expanded place fields in the larger space. J Neurosci 28(44):11250-11262. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008), PMC2695947.
- Talpir et al. (mouse dorsal CA1) say published estimates range "between 20% and 90%" of recorded hippocampal populations being place cells. They argue, by fitting data against simulated datasets with different place-cell fractions, that in a familiar environment all active pyramidal cells are place cells. In a novel environment the fraction is lower. They found that many "non-place cells" in novel environments are position-modulated but have unstable fields. [abstract] Talpir I, Sheintuch L, Rubin A, Ziv Y (2025). All active hippocampal pyramidal cells are place cells. iScience 28(6):112489. [doi:10.1016/j.isci.2025.112489](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.112489).

#### CA1 and CA3, immediate-early gene (Arc) imaging
- Guzowski et al. used Arc catFISH in rats after two sequential explorations. The fraction of Arc-expressing CA1 neurons matched predictions from ensemble recordings, and the abstract gives no percentage. [abstract] Guzowski JF, McNaughton BL, Barnes CA, Worley PF (1999). Environment-specific expression of the immediate-early gene Arc in hippocampal neuronal ensembles. Nat Neurosci 2(12):1120-1124. [doi:10.1038/16046](https://doi.org/10.1038/16046).
- The same group later reported that about 40% of CA1 pyramidal cells express Arc after spatial exploration, "similar to the proportion" with place fields in electrophysiology. [abstract] Chawla MK et al. (2005). Sparse, environmentally selective expression of Arc RNA in the upper blade of the rodent fascia dentata by brief spatial experience. Hippocampus 15(5):579-586. [doi:10.1002/hipo.20091](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20091).
- Arc/H1a catFISH in rats gave about 18% of CA3 versus about 35% of CA1 neurons active for one context, so the context representation is sparser in CA3. [abstract] Vazdarjanova A, Guzowski JF (2004). Differences in hippocampal neuronal population responses to modifications of an environmental context: evidence for distinct, yet complementary, functions of CA3 and CA1 ensembles. J Neurosci 24(29):6489-6496. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0350-04.2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0350-04.2004).

#### CA1, calcium imaging
- Ziv et al. imaged mouse CA1 through a head-mounted miniature microscope with GCaMP3, tracking 515-1,040 pyramidal cells per mouse in 4 mice. Mice ran on an 84 cm × 4.5 cm linear track over 10 sessions in 45 days. [full text] Ziv Y et al. (2013). Long-term dynamics of CA1 hippocampal place codes. Nat Neurosci 16(3):264-266. [doi:10.1038/nn.3329](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329), PMC3784308.
  - In each session, 31 ± 1% of the tracked cells were active.
  - About 20% of cells had a place field in either running direction, about 12 ± 1% per direction. A place cell was defined as having significant mutual information between Ca²⁺ events and position against 10,000 shuffles, p ≤ 0.05.
  - The mean field covered about 27% of the track, with a median width of 24 cm.
  - The denominator is cells detected as active in at least one session. Cells that never produced a detectable transient are invisible to this method.
- Dombeck et al. used two-photon imaging of CA1 with GCaMP3 in head-fixed mice on a 180 cm virtual linear track. About 80-100 neurons were visible per field of view, with 17 ± 9 place cells per field, so "~15-20% of the neurons were place cells." Across 47 datasets they found 808 place cells, of which 735 had one field and 73 had two. Mean optical field width was 50 ± 19 cm. Place fields were defined by significant transients at a 5% false-positive rate, field width over 18 cm, in-field mean more than 3 times the out-of-field mean, and a bootstrap test, p < 0.05. [full text] Dombeck DA, Harvey CD, Tian L, Looger LL, Tank DW (2010). Functional imaging of hippocampal place cells at cellular resolution during virtual navigation. Nat Neurosci 13(11):1433-1440. [doi:10.1038/nn.2648](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2648), PMC2967725.

#### CA1, whole-cell
- Epsztein, Brecht and Lee made whole-cell recordings in 9 rats exploring a novel O-shaped arena for 7.9 ± 2.3 min. Of the 9 cells that could be classified, 4 were place cells and 5 were silent. By direction, 5 directions had place fields and 7 were silent. All place-field directions fired above 1.46 Hz and all silent directions below 1.02 Hz. An expanded set had 11 active and 7 non-active cells. [full text, author PDF] Epsztein J, Brecht M, Lee AK (2011). Intracellular determinants of hippocampal CA1 place and silent cell activity in a novel environment. Neuron 70(1):109-120. [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.006), [author PDF](https://www.inmed.fr/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/11-epsztein_brecht_lee_2011.pdf). The cell properties are in section 4.

#### CA3
- In GoodSmith et al., about 29% of CA3 cells were active per environment, and 6/28 CA3 cells were silent in all 4 environments. Details are under the dentate gyrus entry below. [full text]
- In Alme et al. (CA3, 11 rooms), 342 well-isolated cells in 7 rats were included and 210 were active in at least one room, using a 0.10 Hz threshold. Details are in section 4. [snippet]
- In bats, 58.6% of CA3 pyramidal cells (268/457) were significant place cells across 130-200 m tunnels, versus 76.3% in CA1. Details are in sections 2 and 5. [full text]

#### Dentate gyrus: granule cells and mossy cells
- GoodSmith et al. recorded rats with tetrodes in 4 rooms, each with a different platform about 107-117 cm across. They also identified cells juxtacellularly in other rats. [full text] GoodSmith D, Chen X, Wang C, Kim SH, Song H, Burgalossi A, Christian KM, Knierim JJ (2017). Spatial representations of granule cells and mossy cells of the dentate gyrus. Neuron 93(3):677-690. [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026), PMC5300955.
  - A cell counted as active if it fired at least 75 spikes in a 15-20 min session, had spatial information above 0.5 bits/spike, and beat 100 shuffles at p < 0.01.
  - Cells had to fire in both baseline sleep sessions, so cells silent on the platforms were still counted.
  - Mean fraction active per environment was 9% for granule-cell-layer cells, 88% for hilar (putative mossy) cells and 29% for CA3. The discussion states that fewer than 10% of granule cells had a field in a given environment.
  - Silent in all 4 environments were 25/37 granule-layer cells, 1/23 hilar cells and 6/28 CA3 cells.
  - The mean number of environments (out of 4) with a field was 0.35 ± 0.09 for granule cells, 3.52 ± 0.21 for hilar cells and 1.14 ± 0.18 for CA3.
  - Fields per active cell were 1.04 ± 0.04 for granule cells, 2.26 ± 0.14 for hilar cells and 1.19 ± 0.07 for CA3.
  - Sleep firing rates were 0.10 ± 0.02 Hz for granule cells, 0.70 ± 0.11 Hz for hilar cells and 0.13 ± 0.02 Hz for CA3.
  - A median "sparseness ratio" was reported as 0.05 (IQR 0-0.18) for granule cells and 0.96 (0.83-1.0) for hilar cells. I did not confirm how this ratio is defined.
  - The initial tetrode set came from 4, 5 and 2 rats for the granule, hilar and CA3 groups. All 8 juxtacellularly identified granule cells had a single field.
- Senzai and Buzsáki recorded mice with silicon probes: 586 putative granule cells and 500 putative mossy cells in 16 mice. Mossy-cell identity was validated by optogenetic tagging. Mice ran a T-maze (65 cm central arm, two 102 cm side arms) and 46 × 46 cm open fields. "Most granule cells had no place field or just one and only a small fraction (6.6% of recorded cells) had two or more." By contrast, 57.8% of mossy cells and 36.1% of CA3 cells had two or more fields. On the T-maze, a field needed to be at least 8.3 cm wide with firing above 1 Hz. The text did not give the overall fraction of granule cells with any field. [full text] Senzai Y, Buzsáki G (2017). Neuron 93:691-704, PMC5293146 ([PMC record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5293146)). The title, "Physiological properties and behavioral correlates of hippocampal granule cells and mossy cells", is from my memory and I did not verify it.
- Jung and McNaughton recorded units in the rat dentate granular layer on an 8-arm maze. "The majority (88%) of cells in this layer have mean rates below 0.5 Hz," with minimum rates of 0.01 Hz or less. [abstract] Jung MW, McNaughton BL (1993). Spatial selectivity of unit activity in the hippocampal granular layer. Hippocampus 3:165-182. [doi:10.1002/hipo.450030209](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.450030209).
- With Arc imaging in the dentate gyrus, the upper blade showed about a 4-fold rise in Arc-positive granule cells over cage controls after exploration, and the lower blade showed little behavior-induced Arc. [abstract] (Chawla 2005, [doi:10.1002/hipo.20091](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20091)). A figure of "~2% of granule cells" Arc-positive after exploration appears in search extracts of later papers citing this work. [secondary] I did not verify it in Chawla 2005 itself.
- Alme et al. (2010) exposed rats to different environments over many weeks and then tested all of them in one Arc catFISH session. Only a small subset of granule cells responded, and the authors estimate that "possibly 90-95%" of granule cells are effectively retired. [abstract] Alme CB, Buzzetti RA, Marrone DF, Leutgeb JK, Chawla MK, Schaner MJ, Bohanick JD, Khoboko T, Leutgeb S, Moser EI, Moser MB, McNaughton BL, Barnes CA (2010). Hippocampal granule cells opt for early retirement. Hippocampus 20(10):1109-1123. [doi:10.1002/hipo.20810](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20810).
- Leutgeb et al. (2007) found that small changes in the shape of the environment substantially altered correlated activity among place-modulated granule cells. The abstract gives no fraction active. [abstract] Leutgeb JK, Leutgeb S, Moser MB, Moser EI (2007). Pattern separation in the dentate gyrus and CA3 of the hippocampus. Science 315(5814):961-966. [doi:10.1126/science.1135801](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1135801).
- Two-photon imaging of mouse granule cells showed about one Ca²⁺ event per 2-5 min per cell, so granule cells are sparsely active. [abstract] Pilz GA et al. (2016). Functional imaging of dentate granule cells in the adult mouse hippocampus. J Neurosci 36. [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3065-15.2016](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3065-15.2016).
- In two-photon imaging, young adult-born granule cells fire at a higher rate and are less spatially tuned than mature granule cells. The abstract gives no numbers. [abstract] Danielson NB et al. (2016). Distinct contribution of adult-born hippocampal granule cells to context encoding. Neuron. [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.02.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.02.019).
- Park, Dvorak and Fenton recorded a "DG" group of 58 cells, all from 1 rat, alongside CA1 (104 cells, 3 rats) and CA3 (52 cells, 1 rat). The environments were a 76 cm cylinder and a 1.8 × 1.4 m box with 5.6 times the area. "Active" meant firing above 0.1 AP/s. Among active cells, 63.9% (23/36) of DG cells had a single field in the cylinder and 8.2% (4/49) in the box. [full text] Park E, Dvorak D, Fenton AA (2011). Ensemble place codes in hippocampus: CA1, CA3, and dentate gyrus place cells have multiple place fields in large environments. PLoS ONE 6(7):e22349. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0022349](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0022349).

#### Methodological caveats reported in the sources
- Extracellular methods detect only cells that spike, so studies that count silent cells need an independent way to find them. Thompson and Best used barbiturate anesthesia and antidromic activation. Rich et al. counted only neurons isolated in sleep periods flanking behavior, "to ensure accurate counts of silent cells". GoodSmith et al. required firing in both baseline sleep sessions. [abstract] (Thompson and Best 1989, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-07-02382.1989)); [full text] (Rich 2014, [PDF](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf)); [full text] (GoodSmith 2017, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026)).
- Several published fractions use active cells as the denominator. Examples are Fenton 2008 (64% and 72% of active cells) and Park 2011 (single-field percentages among active cells). These are not comparable to fractions of all isolated cells. [snippet/full text, sources above]
- The criterion changes the answer by a factor of about 2. Mizuseki 2012 reports 74.7% versus 37.3% of CA1 cells with fields in the same open-field data, depending on whether spatial coherence was required. [full text] ([doi:10.1002/hipo.22002](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22002)).
- Calcium-imaging denominators are cells that showed a transient at least once, and GCaMP3 misses some low-rate firing. Ziv 2013's 31% active per session is a fraction of cells detected across 10 sessions. [full text] ([doi:10.1038/nn.3329](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329)).
- Arc catFISH reports cells that transcribed Arc during a roughly 5-min exploration window. Chawla 2005 notes that the ~40% Arc-positive CA1 fraction matches the electrophysiological place-cell fraction. [abstract] ([doi:10.1002/hipo.20091](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.20091)).
- Earlier tetrode "dentate" datasets probably mixed granule cells with hilar mossy cells, which have very different activity. GoodSmith 2017 and Senzai and Buzsáki 2017 both separated the two cell types and found granule cells sparse and mossy cells dense. [full text, sources above]

### Inferences
- The three main methods roughly agree for dorsal CA1 in a single standard environment. Tetrodes with silent-cell detection give about 1/3 active (Thompson and Best: about 2/3 silent). Arc gives about 35-40%. Imaging gives about 15-31%, depending on whether one counts "active" or "place cell". Agreement is looser than the numbers suggest, because each method's denominator differs.
- Region ordering for the fraction active per standard environment is consistent across studies: mossy cells (~88%) > CA1 (~35-50%) > CA3 (~18-30%) > granule cells (~2-10%). CA2 may be at or above CA1, based on one study [snippet].
- Park 2011's "DG" active fractions in the cylinder and box (36/58 and 49/58 active) are far above the less than 10% for identified granule cells in GoodSmith 2017. A likely explanation is that the 2011 sample included hilar mossy cells. This is my inference, and the paper does not say so.

### Gaps
- Thompson and Best 1989. I could not get exact counts, N rats, environment dimensions or rate criteria, because the full text is a scanned PDF behind a publisher block.
- Guzowski 1999's own CA1 percentage. The ~40% comes from Chawla 2005 and later papers by the same group.
- Wilson and McNaughton 1993. The abstract gives only 73-148 simultaneously recorded cells, and I found no fraction active.
- Leutgeb 2007. No granule-cell active fraction in the abstract, and the full text was not accessible.
- Epsztein 2011 and Lee, Lin and Lee 2012 are not designed to estimate population fractions, because their N is too small. Lee 2012's N cells and N rats were not retrieved.
- Subiculum and ventral CA1. I found no numeric fraction of cells with fields in the abstracts I could access.
- CA2 numbers come only from a search extract of Mankin 2015 and need checking against the paper.

## 2. How does recruitment grow with environment size, and does the fraction of active cells saturate?

### Takeaway
Recruitment keeps rising with environment size without a hard ceiling, but it rises slowly, roughly with the logarithm of track length. In rat dorsal CA1 on a 48 m track, about 65% of cells were recruited (range 46-77% across rats), short of the ~100% an equal-propensity model predicts. The extrapolation reaches 90% at about 500 m. Cells already active add more fields rather than the population recruiting proportionally more cells. In bats flying 200 m tunnels, more than 80% of tetrode-recorded CA1 and CA3 pyramidal cells were place cells. CA1 cells there carried many fields, while CA3 cells mostly had one.

### Cited findings
- Rich et al. recorded 253 putative dorsal CA1 pyramidal cells in 5 rats on an entirely novel track. The track was extended in stages to 3, 10, 22 and 48 m, and the analysis covered only the newly added sections in each stage. Only cells isolated in flanking sleep were analyzed, so silent cells were counted. [full text] (Rich 2014, [doi:10.1126/science.1255635](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255635), [PDF](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf))
  - "The recruitment of neurons into the representation reached only ~65% at 48 m (range 46 to 77%...), greatly undershooting the equal-rate Poisson prediction." About 35% of cells stayed silent.
  - The number of fields per cell was overdispersed relative to a Poisson process with equal rates across cells. It was well fit by a negative binomial distribution, with parameters reported as 0.57 and 0.14. The parameter symbols were lost in my text extraction. A negative binomial arises when each cell is a Poisson process whose rate is drawn from a gamma distribution.
  - The predicted recruitment curve under this gamma-Poisson model is a Lomax, or Pareto type II, distribution. It matched the data (Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic 0.05, p = 0.48). Its extrapolation "predicts 90% recruitment at ~500 m."
  - For the 61/253 cells with 6 or more fields, field locations were consistent with a uniform spatial Poisson process and inter-field intervals with an exponential distribution.
  - Field density was uniform along the track and not affected by local track features or speed. Field locations were uncorrelated between cells, and recruitment was memoryless from any starting point.
  - With the observed gamma-distributed propensities, the model predicts that the proportion of cells with exactly one field stays at about 5-10% over a wide range of environment sizes.
  - "Different criteria for place field detection did not change the results (fig. S15)."
- Lee et al. used longitudinal calcium imaging of mouse CA1 in several large virtual environments. Each cell's field propensity, its number of fields per unit space, was broadly distributed, with "many low, and some very high, propensity cells." Propensity "varied ~10-fold between adjacent cells," and it was preserved across distinct environments and over months. [abstract] Lee JS, Briguglio JJ, Cohen JD, Romani S, Lee AK (2020). The statistical structure of the hippocampal code for space as a function of time, context, and value. Cell 183. [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.024).
- Fenton et al. compared CA1 in a cylinder with a chamber about 6 times larger. The share of active cells that were place cells rose from 64% (307/477) to 72% (322/448), p < 0.001. Single-field place cells fell from 72% in the cylinder to 11% in the chamber, and fields were enlarged and irregularly arranged. Of the 227 cells examined in both sessions, 138 (61%) were place cells in both. [snippet; the chamber-size ratio is in the abstract] (Fenton 2008, [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2862-08.2008))
- Park, Dvorak and Fenton compared a 76 cm cylinder with a 1.8 × 1.4 m box of 5.6 times the area. The active subset (rate > 0.1 AP/s) was larger in the box in every region, p < 0.001. [full text] (Park 2011, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0022349](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0022349))
  - Active cells were CA1 56 versus 78 of 104, CA3 22 versus 41 of 52, and DG 36 versus 49 of 58, for the cylinder and box respectively. These counts are my reading of the single-field denominators, so treat them as derived.
  - Among active cells, the single-field share fell from 60.7% to 17.9% in CA1, from 81.8% to 29% in CA3, and from 63.9% to 8.2% in DG.
  - Mean fields per cell in the box were 5.89 ± 0.40 in CA1, 4.96 ± 0.56 in CA3 and 5.35 ± 0.38 in DG.
- Harland et al. recorded 539 dorsal CA1 place cells in 5 rats. The environments were a "megaspace" of 5.3 × 3.5 m (18.6 m²) and a small arena of 1.8 × 1.2 m (2.2 m²), plus 8.2 m² and 0.54 m² arenas in other rats. [full text, bioRxiv preprint version] Harland B, Contreras M, Souder M, Fellous JM (2021). Dorsal CA1 hippocampal place cells form a multi-scale representation of megaspace. Curr Biol 31(10). [doi:10.1016/j.cub.2021.03.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.03.003). Preprint: [doi:10.1101/2021.02.15.431172](https://doi.org/10.1101/2021.02.15.431172).
  - The analysis kept only place cells active in all three sessions (Small 1, Megaspace, Small 2), 383 of 539 (71%). This design cannot measure recruitment.
  - In the megaspace, 82% of cells had 2-5 subfields. In the small arena, 91% had 1-2.
  - Subfield number grew linearly with environment size (R² = 0.98). Summed subfield area grew with size as well and was uncorrelated with subfield number (r = 0.027).
  - Average firing rate did not differ between the small arena and the megaspace.
- Eliav et al. made wireless recordings of dorsal CA1 in wild-born Egyptian fruit bats flying a 200 m tunnel. Place field sizes ranged from 0.6 to 32 m, and the fields of a single neuron differed up to 20-fold in size. This multifield, multiscale code was present from the first day and was similar in lab-born bats never exposed to large spaces. [abstract] Eliav T, Maimon SR, Aljadeff J, Tsodyks M, Ginosar G, Las L, Ulanovsky N (2021). Multiscale representation of very large environments in the hippocampus of flying bats. Science 372(6545):eabg4020. [doi:10.1126/science.abg4020](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg4020). A search extract of a lab-hosted copy mentions "196 place cells" and "235 of 235 cells, 100%" in a figure caption, without context. [snippet, unverified]
- Maimon et al. recorded bats with tetrodes and wireless loggers, 810 CA1 and 457 CA3 putative pyramidal cells in 15 bats, with simultaneous CA1 and CA3 recordings in 5. Tunnels were 6, 15, 130, 180 and 200 m long. A place cell needed significant spatial information against 1,000 spike shuffles (above the 99th percentile), more than 0.25 bits/spike, a minimum spike count per direction, and at least one significant field. [full text] Maimon SR, Eliav T, Aljadeff J, Shalev A, Gronich Y, Finger NM, Eveland KE, Moss CF, Las L, Ulanovsky N (2026). Sparse-to-dense coding transformation between hippocampal areas CA3 and CA1. Nature 655(8125):1242-1251. [doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0), PMC13421320.
  - Pooled over the 130-200 m tunnels, 76.3% (618/810) of CA1 and 58.6% (268/457) of CA3 cells were place cells. In the 200 m tunnel, "more than 80%" were place cells in both regions. CA3 had a lower proportion than CA1 at every size.
  - In the large tunnels, single-field cell-directions were 29.6% (292/986) in CA1 versus 73.7% (250/339) in CA3. Five or more fields occurred in 31.9% of CA1 place cells versus 0.7% of CA3 place cells.
  - In the 6 m and 15 m tunnels, cells in both regions mostly had one field.
  - CA1 field number rose with environment size while CA3 field number stayed low. Field size grew with environment size in both regions.
- Kjelstrup et al. found that on an 18 m track, rat CA3 field size scales from under 1 m dorsally to about 10 m ventrally. Larger environments therefore engage different dorsoventral levels at different spatial scales. [abstract] (Kjelstrup 2008, [doi:10.1126/science.1157086](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1157086))

### Inferences
- I checked the reported negative-binomial parameters against the silent fraction. Reading them as r = 0.57 and p = 0.14, with P(0 fields) = p^r, gives 0.14^0.57 ≈ 0.33. That matches the observed ~35% silent at 48 m, and the implied mean is about 3.5 fields per cell over 48 m. The other common parameterization would give about 92% silent, which contradicts the data. This is my arithmetic, not a statement in the paper.
- Two kinds of evidence suggest recruitment slows but does not saturate. Rich 2014 gives about 65% at 48 m with a Pareto-type tail. The bat data give above 80% at 200 m in tetrode samples. The bat denominators probably exclude cells silent throughout recording, so they are not directly comparable to Rich's sleep-verified denominators. That exclusion is my inference, since the methods excerpt did not describe silent-cell handling.
- Across Fenton 2008, Park 2011, Harland 2021, Rich 2014 and Maimon 2026, larger spaces are covered mainly by more fields per already-active CA1 cell. The added fields are irregularly spaced and of mixed size. New recruits are a smaller part of the increase.

### Gaps
- The Rich 2014 supplementary figures were not accessed: fig. S7B (per-rat recruitment), fig. S8 (silent cells forming fields elsewhere) and fig. S17 (propensity correlated across environments). I also lack the exact recruitment percentages at 3, 10 and 22 m.
- Eliav 2021's fraction of recorded cells that were place cells, and its multifield percentage, were not verified from the full text.
- Harland 2021 cannot answer the recruitment question because of its inclusion criterion.

## 3. Population sparseness at a location, and reported lifetime or Treves-Rolls sparseness

### Takeaway
I found no study that directly reports "fraction of all cells active at one location" as a primary measure. Back-of-envelope estimates from CA1 imaging data come to a few percent of cells per location and running direction. Published single-cell sparseness measures consistently put CA3 sparser than CA1 and granule cells sparsest. I could not retrieve region-by-region numeric Skaggs or Treves-Rolls sparsity values from primary text.

### Cited findings
- In Ziv et al. (mouse CA1, 84 cm track), about 12 ± 1% of tracked cells per session had a field in a given running direction. Fields averaged about 27% of track length, with a median of 24 cm. Each field showed Ca²⁺ activity on only 17 ± 14% of passes (n = 1,656 fields). [full text] (Ziv 2013, [doi:10.1038/nn.3329](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329))
- In Dombeck et al. (mouse CA1, 180 cm virtual track), about 15-20% of imaged neurons were place cells. Mean field width was 50 ± 19 cm, and 91% of place cells (735/808) had one field. [full text] (Dombeck 2010, [doi:10.1038/nn.2648](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2648))
- In Mizuseki et al. (rat, 250 cm track), with rate plus coherence criteria, 48.0% of CA1 and 16.8% of CA3 cells had fields. Mean field size was 75 cm in CA1 and 50 cm in CA3. Spatial information was 0.83 bits/spike in CA1 and 1.57 in CA3. [full text] (Mizuseki 2012, [doi:10.1002/hipo.22002](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22002))
- In bats in large tunnels, CA3 maps had higher spatial information (p = 10⁻⁷²), lower spatial coverage (p = 9 × 10⁻³¹) and sparser maps (p = 7 × 10⁻⁵⁹) than CA1. The paper reports a per-map sparsity score and no population fraction active per location. [full text] (Maimon 2026, [doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0))
- GoodSmith et al. give fields per active cell of 1.04 for granule cells, 1.19 for CA3 and 2.26 for hilar cells, and a median "sparseness ratio" of 0.05 for granule cells versus 0.96 for hilar cells. I did not verify the ratio's definition. [full text] (GoodSmith 2017, [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026))
- In Arc imaging, about 18% of CA3 versus about 35% of CA1 neurons were active per context. [abstract] (Vazdarjanova and Guzowski 2004, [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0350-04.2004](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0350-04.2004))
- In the theory literature, Treves and Rolls describe place representations as very sparse in dentate granule cells, still sparse in CA3 and fairly sparse in CA1. In their framework, sparseness "a" is the population measure. The snippet gives no measured values. [snippet] Treves A, Rolls ET (1994). Computational analysis of the role of the hippocampus in memory. Hippocampus 4:374-391. [PDF](https://oxcns.org/papers/186_Treves+Rolls94.pdf).
- Ensemble-level sparsity is also experience-dependent. Karlsson and Frank report that CA1, but not CA3, produced about twice as many spikes in novel than familiar environments, and their title frames this as the formation of sparse representations with experience. [abstract] (Karlsson and Frank 2008, [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4261-08.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4261-08.2008))

### Inferences
- Rough population sparseness per location and direction, multiplying the place-cell fraction by the mean field coverage:
  - Ziv 2013: 0.12 × 0.27 ≈ 3% of detected CA1 cells have a field covering a given point in a given direction. On any single pass only about 17% of those fields actually show Ca²⁺ activity, so the fraction is lower still.
  - Dombeck 2010: 0.15-0.20 × 50/180 ≈ 4-6% of imaged cells.
  - Mizuseki 2012 track: CA1 ≈ 0.48 × 75/250 ≈ 14% and CA3 ≈ 0.17 × 50/250 ≈ 3%, assuming one field per cell. I did not check whether these fractions are per direction.

  These are my calculations, not reported values. They rest on uniform field coverage and ignore multiple fields, field overlap and directionality.
- For granule cells, combining under 10% active per environment with about one small field per active cell gives a population sparseness per location well below 1%. This is an inference with no direct measurement found.

### Gaps
- Region-by-region Skaggs sparsity and Treves-Rolls lifetime sparseness values were not retrieved from primary text. Candidates to check are Skaggs et al. 1996 Hippocampus, Jung and McNaughton 1993, Leutgeb 2004 and 2007, Neunuebel and Knierim 2012, and GoodSmith 2017 Table S1.
- I found no direct measure of "fraction of all principal cells firing at one location" with silent cells counted in the denominator.

## 4. Silent cells: what makes a cell silent, how stable is silence, and how many cells are active in at least one of many environments?

### Takeaway
Silence is a property of the cell-environment pair, not a permanent cell state. Many cells silent in one environment have fields in another, and depolarizing a silent CA1 cell during behavior can unmask a place field. A cell's excitability and its pre-exposure activity bias whether it becomes a place cell, and the bias carries over across environments. Even across 11 rooms, a substantial minority of CA3 cells (about 39%) stayed silent. Multi-room cells were rare, and fields of cells active in several rooms were uncorrelated across rooms.

### Cited findings
- Epsztein et al. compared place cells and silent cells in their whole-cell CA1 recordings. [full text, author PDF] (Epsztein 2011, [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2011.03.006))
  - The difference in subthreshold field, measured as peak minus baseline, was 12.8 ± 2.8 mV in place-field directions versus 2.9 ± 0.3 mV in silent directions, p = 0.024. In the expanded set it was 11.5 ± 1.6 mV in active versus 3.2 ± 0.5 mV in non-active cells, p = 0.00032.
  - AP threshold was lower in place cells, about 54.9 ± 1.5 versus 46.2 ± 1.5 mV below zero, p = 0.0049. The minus signs were lost in text extraction and are my reading.
  - Baseline Vm did not differ significantly, at -65.5 ± 2.2 versus -59.2 ± 1.8 mV (p = 0.059). The signs are again my reading.
  - Before any maze experience, in the anesthetized animal, future place cells fired more of their APs in bursts in response to depolarizing current steps: 0.80 ± 0.15 versus 0.24 ± 0.02, p = 0.033. Pre-exploration AP threshold did not differ significantly.
- In Lee, Lin and Lee (whole-cell, freely moving rats), "a small, spatially uniform depolarization" of a silent CA1 cell's somatic membrane potential caused the sudden, reversible appearance of spatially tuned subthreshold responses and place-field spiking. [abstract] Lee D, Lin BJ, Lee AK (2012). Hippocampal place fields emerge upon single-cell manipulation of excitability during behavior. Science 337(6096):849-853. [doi:10.1126/science.1221489](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1221489).
- Rich et al. report two links between pre-existing activity and later fields. [full text] (Rich 2014, [PDF](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~jkenny/nb250c/papers/rich_14.pdf))
  - Slow-wave-sleep firing rates before the animal experienced the maze were moderately correlated with the later number of fields per cell, ρ = 0.45. The authors take this as evidence that pre-existing differences contribute to field propensity.
  - Some of the ~35% of cells silent on the 48 m track formed clear place fields in a second novel environment (fig. S8). The text also mentions "correlated field propensities (fig. S17) across multiple environments".
- Lee et al. (2020) found that field propensity is preserved across distinct environments and over months in mouse CA1. [abstract] (Lee 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.024))
- Thompson and Best found that many place cells "had low or no firing" in other, dissimilar environments, and that silent cells could be reactivated by reanesthetization and antidromic stimulation. [abstract] (Thompson and Best 1989, [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-07-02382.1989](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.09-07-02382.1989))
- Alme et al. recorded rat CA3 with tetrodes across 11 recording boxes in 11 different rooms, giving 55 pairwise room comparisons per animal. Ten rooms were novel and one familiar. Maps were highly correlated on repeat visits to the same room and orthogonal across rooms. [abstract] Alme CB, Miao C, Jezek K, Treves A, Moser EI, Moser MB (2014). Place cells in the hippocampus: eleven maps for eleven rooms. PNAS 111(52):18428-18435. [doi:10.1073/pnas.1421056111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1421056111), PMC4284589. I could not open the full text, so the counts below come from search extracts and need checking.
  - 342 well-isolated cells were analyzed, and 210 were active in at least one room under a 0.10 Hz threshold, about 30 active cells per rat. [snippet, consistent with the abstract's "average of 30 active cells per animal"]
  - The mean number of active place cells per room was 46.1 ± 8.6 summed over rats, or 6.62 ± 0.7 per rat. [snippet]
  - 6% (21/342) of all cells were active in 6 or more rooms, and fewer than 3% (10/342) in 8 or more. [snippet]
  - The number of rats is 7, according to a reanalysis of the dataset. [secondary] Jain B, Elliott S. Correlation across environments encoded by hippocampal place cells. arXiv:2012.14559, [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.14559) (preprint).
  - A bioRxiv preprint reanalyzing the dataset reports, at a 0.1 Hz threshold, 103 cells active in only one room, 44 in two rooms, 21 in six or more, 10 in eight or more, and 2 cells in distal-most CA3 active in all 11 rooms. [snippet, preprint] "Grid to place cell connectivity in eleven different rooms" / "Minimum grid cells for place cell activity in eleven enclosures", bioRxiv [10.1101/2024.11.15.623794](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.15.623794v3.full). The authors were not captured.
- In GoodSmith et al., across 4 rooms, 25/37 granule-layer cells (68%) were silent in all 4, and only one granule cell was active in more than one room. Among CA3 cells, 6/28 (21%) were silent in all 4. Among hilar mossy cells, 17/23 were active in all 4 and only 1/23 was silent in all. Rate-map correlations across rooms were at chance in all three groups, so every group remapped. [full text] (GoodSmith 2017, [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.12.026))
- Ziv et al. followed cells over days in the same environment. Of cells active in at least one of 10 sessions over 45 days, 57 ± 1% were active in only 1 or 2 sessions and 2.8 ± 0.3% in all 10. Overlap of the active set between two sessions was about 60% at 5 days apart and about 40% at 30 days. Overlap of the place-cell set was about 25% at 5 days and about 15% at 30 days. Cells that recurred kept nearly the same field location, with a median shift of 3.5 cm against a 24 cm median field width. [full text] (Ziv 2013, [doi:10.1038/nn.3329](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3329))
- Mankin et al. found that CA2 population activity patterns became progressively dissimilar over hours to days, more than they differed between spatial contexts. [abstract] (Mankin 2015, [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.12.001))
- Firing rates of hippocampal and entorhinal neurons follow a "lognormal-like" distribution that is preserved across brain states. The authors title this as a "preconfigured" distribution. [abstract] Mizuseki K, Buzsáki G (2013). Preconfigured, skewed distribution of firing rates in the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex. Cell Rep. [doi:10.1016/j.celrep.2013.07.039](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2013.07.039).

### Inferences
- From the Alme 2014 snippet counts, if all 342 cells were recorded in all 11 rooms, the mean per-room active fraction is about 46.1/342 ≈ 13% of all cells. That is about 22% of the cells active somewhere. That cells were held across all rooms is my assumption and is not verified.
- I tested these counts against a model where each cell activates independently in each room with p ≈ 0.135. That model predicts about 80% of cells active in at least one room, against the observed 61% (210/342). It predicts about 0.15% active in 6 or more rooms, against the observed 6%. The observed distribution therefore has both more never-active cells and more multi-room cells than independent activation predicts. This fits the heterogeneous, cross-environment-correlated propensity described by Rich 2014 and Lee 2020. These are my calculations on snippet-level numbers.
- Within a single environment across days, Ziv 2013's imaging data show high turnover of which cells are detectably active. "Silence" measured with calcium imaging in one session is therefore a weak predictor of silence in the next session. The detection threshold of GCaMP3 limits this conclusion.
- The whole-cell data (Epsztein 2011, Lee 2012) point to silent CA1 cells receiving spatially tuned input that stays below threshold. Lee 2012 showed that depolarization unmasks tuned responses. Silence therefore reflects excitability and threshold more than an absence of spatial input.

### Gaps
- Alme 2014 per-room percentages, the full distribution of rooms per cell, and the box dimensions were not verified from the paper.
- The size of the cross-environment correlation in propensity (Rich 2014 fig. S17, Lee 2020) was not retrieved.
- Thompson and Best's count of cells silent in one environment but active in another was not retrieved.
- Cohen, Bolstad and Lee 2017 (eLife, whole-cell CA1 in novel and familiar virtual environments, [doi:10.7554/eLife.23040](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.23040)) was located but not read, so its place versus silent fractions are missing.

## 5. Species differences: bats, primates, humans, birds

### Takeaway
Bats show rodent-like CA1 place cells, and in very large flight tunnels a large majority of tetrode-recorded CA1 and CA3 cells are place cells. CA1 is multi-field there and CA3 mostly single-field. Human and primate data use different behaviors, often virtual navigation or view-based tasks, and report lower fractions of pure place cells, around 10-25% in human hippocampus according to secondary sources. Food-caching birds have more place cells than non-caching birds. I could not verify most primate and bird percentages from primary text.

### Cited findings
- Ulanovsky and Moss recorded CA1 in crawling Egyptian fruit bats. 47 of 64 non-diffuse cells (73%) were classed as place cells. I could not confirm the behavioral condition or denominator. [snippet from a lab-hosted PDF] Ulanovsky N, Moss CF (2007). Hippocampal cellular and network activity in freely moving echolocating bats. Nat Neurosci 10. [doi:10.1038/nn1829](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn1829), [lab PDF](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/brain-sciences/labs/ulanovsky/sites/neurobiology.labs.ulanovsky/files/uploads/ulanovsky_moss_natneurosci2007.pdf).
- In flying bats in 3D space, more than 90% of neurons encoded all three axes with similar resolution. The abstract gives no place-cell fraction. [abstract] Yartsev MM, Ulanovsky N (2013). Representation of three-dimensional space in the hippocampus of flying bats. Science 340. [doi:10.1126/science.1235338](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1235338).
- In flying bats in 130-200 m tunnels, 76.3% of CA1 and 58.6% of CA3 pyramidal cells were place cells, and more than 80% of both in the 200 m tunnel. [full text] (Maimon 2026, [doi:10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0)). In a 200 m tunnel, CA1 fields ranged from 0.6 to 32 m. [abstract] (Eliav 2021, [doi:10.1126/science.abg4020](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg4020))
- Ekstrom et al. recorded 317 neurons in epilepsy patients navigating a virtual town as taxi drivers: 67 in hippocampus, 54 in parahippocampal region, 111 in amygdala and 85 in frontal lobes. [abstract for N=317; region counts from a press summary, snippet] Ekstrom AD, Kahana MJ, Caplan JB, Fields TA, Isham EA, Newman EL, Fried I (2003). Cellular networks underlying human spatial navigation. Nature 425. [doi:10.1038/nature01964](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01964).
  - Secondary sources report that 26% of all recorded cells responded to place and about 12% to view. About 11% were "pure" place cells, and 24% of hippocampal neurons were pure place cells, with an average of 1.7 non-contiguous fields. [secondary, unverified]
  - One secondary source gives "95 of 371" as the denominator, which conflicts with the 317 neurons in the abstract.
- Rhesus macaques freely foraging in an open environment were recorded with head and eye tracking. "Most neurons encoded a mixture of spatial variables," including view as well as position. The abstract gives no place-cell fraction. [abstract] Mao D et al. (2021). Spatial modulation of hippocampal activity in freely moving macaques. Neuron. [doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2021.09.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2021.09.032).
- Freely moving marmosets had place cells in hippocampus, and theta appeared in bouts of about 0.4 s. The abstract gives no cell counts or fractions. [abstract] Courellis HS et al. (2019). Spatial encoding in primate hippocampus during free navigation. PLoS Biol. [doi:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000546](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3000546).
- Hippocampal place cells were recorded in tufted titmice, a food-caching species, and in zebra finches, which do not cache. In the non-caching species, spatial firing was less informative and "exhibited by fewer neurons." The abstract gives no percentages. [abstract] Payne HL, Lynch GF, Aronov D (2021). Neural representations of space in the hippocampus of a food-caching bird. Science 373. [doi:10.1126/science.abg2009](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abg2009).
- A later chickadee study reportedly found 56% place cells, 2,462 of 4,366 units in 5 birds. [snippet, unverified] Chettih SN et al. (2024). Barcoding of episodic memories in the hippocampus of a food-caching bird. Cell. The author list and DOI are from memory and unverified: [cell.com landing page](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674(24)00235-6).

### Inferences
- Human, primate and bird "place cell" fractions are not directly comparable to rodent numbers. Human recordings are from clinical electrodes during virtual navigation. Primate data often show view or mixed selectivity. Bird and bat percentages come from tetrode or probe samples of cells that fired, and the papers I accessed say little about how silent cells were counted.

### Gaps
- Primate fractions (Mao 2021, Courellis 2019, Rolls' spatial view cell work) were not retrieved.
- Bird percentages from Payne 2021, and verification of the Chettih 2024 figure, are missing.
- Ekstrom 2003 percentages need verification against the Nature paper.
- Eliav 2021's own place-cell fraction is not verified.
- I found no bird or primate study that counts silent cells or tests recruitment against environment size.
