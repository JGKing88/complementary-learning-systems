# Full-text check of three subiculum papers

These notes record what the full texts of Sharp & Green 1994, Olson, Tongprasearth & Nitz 2017 and Lever et al. 2009 report. The facts come from the page-marked text extraction, with tables and figure labels checked against page images where the text layer was garbled. Values marked "figure-read" come from a figure and are approximate. Values marked "derived" are my arithmetic. Only real recordings are listed. Where a paper frames a result as a test of a theory, only the measurement is kept.

Page numbers are journal pages. For Sharp & Green the PDF page is the journal page minus 2338. For Lever et al. the PDF page is the journal page minus 9770. Olson et al. print pages 170 to 172 (PDF pp. 1 to 3), and the Online Methods are unnumbered PDF pp. 4 to 6.

## Part 1. Verified facts

### Sharp & Green 1994, J Neurosci 14:2339-2356

Design and sample

- Animals and cells. 99 cells from 28 hemispheres of 21 female Long-Evans rats. p. 2343, "Data were collected from a total of 99 cells in 28 hemispheres of 21 rats."
- Electrodes. Two drivable six-wire bundles of 25 µm nichrome wire, one per hemisphere. These were not tetrodes. p. 2341.
- Sampling. Every isolated cell was kept, spatial or not. p. 2353, "every cell that could be electrically isolated was included in the data set, regardless of whether it appeared to have a spatial component".
- Main environment. A grey cylinder 76 cm in diameter and 51 cm high, with one white card covering 100° of arc, inside a circular curtain 137 cm across. p. 2341, "76-cm-diameter, 51-cm-high, cylindrical chamber".
- Second environment. A grey rectangle 39 × 55.5 cm with 51 cm walls and a white card on one end wall. p. 2341, "a 39-cm-wide, 55.5-cm-long, 51-cm-high rectangular apparatus".
- Sessions. Standard sessions lasted 20 to 40 min of pellet chasing, and manipulation sessions lasted 15 to 20 min. pp. 2341-2342.
- Rate maps. Pixels of 2.9 × 2.9 cm, about 540 pixels across the cylinder floor [derived]. p. 2342.

How broad the firing was

- Field size definition. It is the share of visited pixels with any firing, so the threshold is zero. p. 2342, "The size of the firing field for a given cell was calculated as the percentage of visited pixels in which the rate was above zero." A pixel counts if the cell fired one spike there, so the value also depends on session length and rate [my reading].
- Mean field size for all 99 cells was 0.84. p. 2344, "The average field size (percentage of cylinder pixels in which the rate was above zero) for the subicular cells was 0.84". Checked on the page image.
- Field size by region, mean ± SE (Table 1, p. 2345, checked on the page image). Distal 0.88 ± 0.02 (N = 52), proximal 0.78 ± 0.04 (N = 34), hippocampo-subicular border 0.71 ± 0.09 (N = 13). The 99-cell mean therefore includes 13 border-zone cells.
- Field size by spike-train type, mean ± SE (Table 2, p. 2348, checked on the page image). Bursters 0.70 ± 0.04 (N = 29), nonbursters 0.79 ± 0.04 (N = 35), "depolarized bursters" 0.97 ± 0.01 (N = 12), theta cells (putative interneurons) 0.99 ± 0.003 (N = 10). 13 cells were unclassified (p. 2348). Bursters and nonbursters together average 0.75 [derived].
- Field size differed by type, F = 11.25, p < 0.001. Bursters and nonbursters each differed from depolarized bursters and theta cells, and from each other. p. 2350.
- Fields per cell were not counted. p. 2344, "each cell had one or more 'hot spots' in which the firing rate was greater than average". p. 2353, subicular fields "were much larger, and they often contained more than one region of high firing".
- The hippocampal contrast is qualitative, and no hippocampal cells were recorded in this study. p. 2345, "The hippocampal cells are typically silent throughout much of the enclosure, with a relatively small, unitary region of rapid firing in one location."
- Spatial coherence. This is the correlation of each pixel's rate with the mean of its 8 neighbours, after removing the head-direction contribution (p. 2342). Mean R = 0.48 ± 0.02, 83 of 99 cells significant at p < 0.01, mean R² = 0.26, range -0.04 to 0.92. p. 2344, "Eighty-three of the 99 cells showed significant (p < 0.01) R values".
- The most spatial cells. p. 2351, "as much as 80% or more of the total pixel-to-pixel variance could be explained by spatial location".
- Comparison with hippocampus. Mean coherence was 0.48 against 0.80 for hippocampal place cells in the same apparatus reported by Kubie et al. 1990, and 11% of subicular cells were at or above 0.80. The hippocampal sample kept only cells that appeared to have a place field. p. 2353, "some subicular cells (11%) whose spatial coherence value was at or above the hippocampal complex spike cell average".
- Spatial information, sparsity and selectivity indices were not computed. The authors note that Barnes et al. 1990 found "negligible values" on their spatial selectivity score for subicular cells on an eight-arm maze. p. 2353.

Rates

- Mean rate of all cells was 9.05 ± 1.41 Hz. p. 2343. This includes the 10 theta cells at 37 Hz.
- Overall rate by type, mean ± SE (Table 2, p. 2348). Bursters 3.91 ± 0.71 Hz, nonbursters 4.08 ± 0.72 Hz, depolarized bursters 13.73 ± 1.82 Hz, theta cells 37.32 ± 2.81 Hz. Mean rate within nonzero pixels was 5.64, 6.46, 21.98 and 51.28 Hz. Bursters and nonbursters together average 4.0 Hz [derived].
- Peak rates are not reported. The Figure 2 legend gives only each example map's mean and SD pixel rate (p. 2344).

Head direction

- 60 of 98 cells had a significant head-direction effect (ANOVA, F > 2.64, p < 0.01). p. 2345.
- Mean ω² was 0.01 ± 0.002, range 0 to 0.13. p. 2345, "head direction was a reliable but very small influence on firing rate".

Proximal against distal

- Table 1, mean ± SE, distal / proximal / border (p. 2345, checked on the page image). Overall rate 11.16 ± 1.77 / 6.26 ± 1.41 / 8.23 ± 3.46 Hz. Rate in nonzero pixels 16.21 ± 2.41 / 9.01 ± 1.97 / 11.27 ± 4.98 Hz. Spatial coherence 0.59 ± 0.03 / 0.38 ± 0.04 / 0.30 ± 0.06. Field size 0.88 / 0.78 / 0.71. N 52 / 34 / 13.
- With electrode-track means as samples (16 distal and 9 proximal tracks, border excluded), no difference was significant. Coherence gave t = 1.41, p < 0.20 and field size t = 1.27, p < 0.25, df = 23. p. 2345.
- With cells as samples (df = 84), coherence gave t = 4.46, p < 0.001, rate in nonzero pixels t = 2.13, p < 0.05, and field size t = 2.94, p < 0.01. p. 2346. The overall-rate difference was not significant either way. p. 2353, "These differences were statistically significant for the first three measures, but only when assessed using the data from each individual cell".
- Rate and coherence were uncorrelated within each region, R = -0.19 distal and R = 0.08 proximal. pp. 2347-2348.
- The distal advantage held within types (Table 3, p. 2350, checked on the page image). Bursters had coherence 0.63 distal against 0.39 proximal and field size 0.80 against 0.69. Nonbursters had coherence 0.55 against 0.34 and field size 0.83 against 0.72.
- 10 of the 12 depolarized bursters were distal. p. 2350.

Stability and population-level results

- Repeated sessions. For 7 cells, the mean pixel-by-pixel correlation between first and repeated sessions was 0.49 ± 0.08. A rate-matched different cell from the same electrode track gave -0.06 ± 0.06. p. 2345 (checked on the page image).
- Neighbouring cells. 4 pairs recorded simultaneously on one wire had a mean map correlation of 0.06, range -0.27 to 0.37. 37 same-wire pairs from different sessions had a mean of 0.0003, range -0.20 to 0.22. p. 2351, "there was no suggestion that cells located along the same vertical strip of subiculum had related spatial patterns".
- There is no population-vector, decoding or ensemble analysis.

Cross-environment tests, 8 cells

- When the card and entry point rotated together, each cell's pattern rotated with them. p. 2351.
- After direct transfer from the rotated cylinder into the rectangle, each cell fired over much of both boxes with a location-related pattern. 5 of 8 appeared to repeat the cylinder pattern, in the same room orientation, judged by eye only. p. 2351, "No attempt was made to assess this similarity quantitatively; however, of the eight cells tested in this series of manipulations, five appeared to show this replication of field as assessed by visual inspection."
- After return to the standard cylinder, all but one cell rotated back. p. 2351.
- Caveats from the authors. Hippocampal studies of two boxes placed the rat in its home cage between sessions, whereas here it moved directly, and the two boxes may not have been distinct enough. pp. 2354-2355.

### Olson, Tongprasearth & Nitz 2017, Nat Neurosci 20:170-172

- Animals and cells. 3 adult male Sprague-Dawley rats and 542 subicular neurons (81, 321 and 140 per rat). Online Methods, PDF p. 4. PDF p. 5, "No neurons were excluded from analysis, even if activity was minimal."
- Track. A triple-T track with 8 cm wide paths, 1.6 × 1.25 m overall, raised 20 cm, with 2 cm edges and open view of distal cues. PDF p. 4, "8-cm-wide pathways, overall 1.6 m × 1.25 m in length and width". There were four partially overlapping outbound routes from one start to four goals and two return paths (Fig. 1a legend, p. 171). Each outbound path was 140 cm, with turns at 51, 87 and 118 cm (PDF p. 4).
- Arena. A circular wall-less arena 60 cm across, placed 20 cm above the centre of the track. The first session of rat NS14 (17 neurons) used a 30 cm pot with 22 cm walls instead. PDF p. 4.
- Session. 3 to 10 min in the arena, as about 5 min of directed runs and about 5 min of free foraging, analysed together. Then about 80 rewarded track runs, about 45 min in all. PDF p. 4.
- Rate maps. Samples below 3 cm/s or above 20 rad/s angular velocity were dropped, with 2 cm bins and a 2 cm Gaussian. PDF p. 5.
- Axis-tuned share. 47 of 542, which is 8.7% [derived]. p. 170, "The 47 neurons (of 542 tested) meeting these criteria were strongly tuned to a specific axis of travel on the track."
- Criteria. The best cross-validated von Mises mixture had to be second order, with over 50% less error than a uniform fit and a further 20% over first order. Firing at the model maxima had to be at least twice that at the minima. The spatial-independence score had to exceed 50%. p. 170; PDF p. 6.
- Figure 2e appears to show 114 track neurons best fit by a second-order model before the rate and independence criteria [figure-read from vector labels, assignment uncertain].
- Tuning strength. Mean circular variance was 0.27 rad ± 0.08 SD. The peak-to-minimum ratio averaged 4.65 ± 2.23 SD after one outlier of 90.4 was removed. Peaks sat about 180° apart, and preferred directions were uniform (Hodges-Ajne, N = 94, P = 0.9182). p. 170.
- The 73.9% figure is in the published paper. p. 170, "Mean spatial independence of axis-tuned firing was also well above criterion (73.9% ± 12.5, s.d.)." The definition is on PDF p. 6, "For all 2 cm × 2 cm spatial bin locations with at least 10 samples in a peak 10° bin orientation, the neuron was considered active if its mean rate at that position and orientation was at least 50% of its overall mean rate for that orientation. If more than 50% of the viable locations for both peaks of a neuron were active, the neuron was considered to meet this criterion."
- What 73.9% means. It is the mean, over the 47 axis-tuned cells and their two preferred directions, of the share of well-sampled track positions where the cell fired at least half its direction-specific mean rate while the rat faced that direction. It is not the share of the track where a cell fires, it applies only to the selected cells, and cells needed more than 50% to be selected, so the selection pushes the mean up. The Figure 2a inset gives 74.5% for the larger peak and 73.3% for the smaller, whose mean is 73.9% [figure-read from vector labels, column assignment inferred from label positions]. The example neuron 174 scored 79.7% and 79.4%.
- Example rates. Neuron 174 peaked at 33.5 and 45.7 Hz, and across the 47 cells the smaller peak averaged 0.53 of the larger [figure-read, Fig. 2a].
- Arena. No neuron met the axis criteria in the arena. 8 neurons had a moderate single-direction bias on both the track and the arena. For the 47 cells, arena peak-to-minimum ratios were lower (P = 1.79 × 10⁻¹¹) and circular variance higher (P = 2.35 × 10⁻¹⁸), while spatial independence did not change (P = 0.07). p. 170, "consistent with axis-tuned firing on the track and the broad spatial tuning typical of subiculum arena data", citing Sharp & Green 1994.
- Place-like and boundary-like shares in the arena. p. 170, "some subiculum cells exhibited spatially specific firing ... akin to that of place cells in CA1 (~29%) and BVCs in subiculum (~21%)." Despite the wording, both percentages are shares of subicular cells. Each arena map was correlated with idealized templates, 2D Gaussians with SD 5 to 11 cm and boundary-distance templates at 9 distances and 72 directions, and r > 0.4 counted as a match, a cutoff "chosen by visual inspection". The analysis covered the 354 of 542 cells with at least 250 arena spikes (PDF p. 5). The main text does not give the denominator for 29% and 21%. The counts are in Supplementary Figs. 3 and 4, which I did not read.
- 7 of the 47 axis cells, 14.9%, had boundary-like arena firing. p. 171.
- Arena spatial information, information per spike and coherence were computed for the 354 cells, but values appear only in the supplementary figures. PDF p. 5.
- Track rotation. 170 neurons were recorded with a 90° rotation of the track. All 26 axis cells kept their tuning fixed to the room. Tuning curves correlated at 0.76 ± 0.16 in room coordinates against -0.41 ± 0.22 in track coordinates, P = 6.55 × 10⁻¹⁰. p. 171.
- Axis tuning persisted in darkness (Supplementary Fig. 8, not read). p. 171.
- No CA1 cells were recorded, and there is no population decoding or population-vector similarity. The only correlations are single-cell tuning-curve correlations across the track rotation.

### Lever et al. 2009, J Neurosci 29:9771-9777

- Animals. 6 rats with tetrodes in dorsal subiculum, 5.8 to 6.3 mm behind bregma. pp. 9771, 9775.
- Trials were 10 min of foraging. p. 9772.
- Environment a. A 62 × 62 cm square with 50 cm walls of beige "morph box" material, in a black-curtained arena with a white cue card. pp. 9772, 9774, "62 × 62 × 50-cm-high beige square box".
- Environment b. A circular wooden light-grey enclosure 79 cm across with 50 cm walls. p. 9774.
- Environment c. The 90 cm raised circular floor of a and b, bounded by a drop with no walls. p. 9774.
- Environment d. A 39 cm square holding platform with 5 cm ridges, 2 m south of a. p. 9774.
- Other configurations. A circle and a diamond made of the morph box, used for 7 cells. Larger squares aL and aLB, the latter with a barrier, whose sizes are not given in the main text. Two raised rectangular platforms, either together or 13 cm apart. pp. 9772, 9774.
- 36 putative boundary vector cells. p. 9772.
- Prevalence. p. 9772, "in trials in which 45 recordings of BVC-like firing fields were made (9 of the 36 BVCs being resampled on subsequent days), 141 non-BVC firing fields were also recorded. Thus, up to 24% (45 of 186) of cells in this region are BVCs." The authors call it an upper bound. The 45 counts recordings, not distinct cells, and the 186 counts recorded firing fields. The paper does not say how silent cells were treated.
- Field size. Not quantified. Peak rates after smoothing are printed on each map. Across the 36 maps of Figure 2 they range from 1.6 to 28.6 Hz, median about 11 Hz and mean about 12 Hz [figure-read from the page image, summary derived]. p. 9773.
- Qualitative field shape. p. 9772, "The long axes of firing fields reflected the curvature of the boundaries, tending to be straight in square and curved in circular environments." The grouping of Figure 2 by apparent preferred distance is "Purely to illustrate" (p. 9772), and cells grouped as far-tuned "have firing fields that are broader". On p. 9777 the authors say that short-range cells being more numerous "appears to be true of our subicular recordings (Fig 2)". Neither claim comes with counts.
- Rates. Global rate 5.04 ± 0.59 Hz, range 0.56 to 13.3 Hz. 11 bursting cells averaged 7.2 ± 1.3 Hz and 25 nonbursting cells 4.1 ± 0.6 Hz. p. 9776.
- Location against direction, for the 24 cells tested in circular environments. p. 9775, "Estimated mutual information between firing rate and location was 0.25 ± 0.05 bits per spike (mean ± SEM) and 0.05 ± 0.01 bits per spike between firing rate and direction."
- Directional selectivity (peak over mean rate across 60 bins) was 1.56 ± 0.05, against 2.87 ± 0.20 for CA1 place cells (n = 46) and 6.91 ± 0.05 for presubicular head-direction cells (n = 45), both from Cacucci et al. 2004 "recorded and analyzed similarly". p. 9775.
- Location. 26 of 36 cells were in the proximal half, which the authors attribute to electrode sampling. p. 9775.
- Cells tested beyond environment a. Of 24, 17 were tested in b and c, 7 in the morph circle and diamond, 11 on platform d, and 3 in larger environments with and without a barrier. p. 9772.
- Barrier. A second field appeared in 3 cells (cells 2a, 4a and 1a, Fig. 3D,E). Cell 1a also gained a second field when the two platforms were pulled 13 cm apart, the same over 4 voluntary and 5 carried crossings. p. 9772.
- Square to walled circle (a to b). This "produced strong remapping in CA1 PCs without disrupting BVC firing". p. 9776. No count or correlation is given.
- Square to wall-less platform (a to c). p. 9776, "However 14 of 17 BVCs were primarily insensitive even to this change." Figure 3B shows 3 cells "which have more diffuse firing in Envt c" (p. 9774). These are probably the other 3 of the 17 [my inference].
- Holding platform. All 11 cells were BVC-like there, "notwithstanding the more diffuse firing pattern". p. 9773.
- Darkness. 3 cells were recorded in complete darkness and kept boundary firing. pp. 9773-9774.
- Other subicular cells. p. 9776, the change from a to c "also elicited remapping in many of the non-BVC subicular cells recorded simultaneously with BVCs (data not shown)".
- CA1 comparison. 5 rats, 4 of them with simultaneous CA1 and subicular recordings. Mean bin-by-bin correlations were 0.803 between two trials in a, 0.053 between a and b, and -0.049 between a and c. p. 9772, "(C. Lever, unpublished observation)". The minus sign was checked against the PDF glyph.
- No correlation values are given for the boundary cells across environments, and there is no population analysis.
- The phrase "showed much less remapping than CA1 place cells" does not occur in the main text. The closest sentence is on p. 9776, "the data strongly suggest that subicular BVCs remap less than PCs in response to the same environmental differences".

### Change 7. Line 175, replace the Olson et al. sentences, upgrading [abstract], [secondary] and [unverified, preprint] to [full text] (same section)

Corrections. The full text gives 47 of 542 neurons, 8.7%, which replaces the press release's "about 10%". The 73.9% figure is in the published paper, but it is a direction-conditioned selection score for the 47 axis cells and not the share of the track a cell fires over. "Groups separate locations" is the press release's framing. The paper says the firing "was largely independent of room location". New values are the track and arena sizes, tuning strength, the arena result, the rotation result and the arena place-like and boundary-like shares.

Old text:

```
Axis-of-travel cells fire for travel in either direction along one axis of a multi-path track, which groups separate locations [abstract] ([Olson, Tongprasearth & Nitz 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27991899/)). A press release gives them as about 10% of subicular neurons [secondary] ([Kavli Foundation news](https://kavlifoundation.org/news/toeing-line-study-finds-brain-cells-signal-path-travel)), and a preprint snippet says neurons fired over a mean of 73.9% of the track [unverified, preprint] ([Olson et al. preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/050641.full.pdf)).
```

New text:

```
Axis-tuned cells fire for travel in either direction along one axis of a multi-path track, so one cell fires on separate segments that share that axis. In 3 rats on a 160 × 125 cm track with 8 cm paths, 47 of 542 subicular neurons, 8.7%, met the criteria, and their firing at the two tuning peaks averaged 4.65 ± 2.23 times their firing at the minima. No neuron met them in a 60 cm wall-less arena above the track. When the track was rotated 90°, all 26 tested axis cells kept their tuning fixed to the room, with tuning curves correlating at 0.76 in room coordinates against -0.41 in track coordinates.
```
