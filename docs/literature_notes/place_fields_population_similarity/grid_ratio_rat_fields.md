# Rat place-field widths by region and dorsoventral level, for the field-width over grid-spacing table

Checked on 2026-11-20. Real neural data only, rat only. This note supplies the rows of a table whose columns are MEC grid modules and whose cells are field width divided by grid spacing. The grid-spacing columns are in `grid_ratio_rat_grid.md`.

Status tags used below:

- [full text]: quoted from the paper or its supplement, which I read as a PDF or XML.
- [full text, WebFetch]: read from the PMC article page through the WebFetch tool. That tool returns verbatim fragments of at most 125 characters, so some quotes are fragments, marked with "...". I could not read these pages directly because PMC served a reCAPTCHA to plain downloads.
- [abstract]: only the abstract was reachable.
- [figure-read]: read from a figure. Each row says how.
- [derived]: my arithmetic, shown.
- [secondary]: a later paper's reading of the original. Not checked against the original.
- [unverified]: seen only in a search-engine summary. Not usable as a value.

Width rules. Linear-track field length is used as is. For an open-field area A, the width is the equal-area circle diameter 2 x sqrt(A/π), tagged [derived], and the area is kept. Field definitions differ a lot between labs, and the edge rule moves widths by tens of percent (see "Field definitions" below).

## Corrections to the draft rows

1. Diba & Buzsáki 2008, 52 cm. This value pools dorsal CA1 and CA3. The paper recorded 1,750 fields, 1,062 in CA1 and 688 in CA3, found no regional difference and pooled them: "No intrahippocampal regional differences could be seen for the measures we calculated" ... "so all data are hereby pooled" [full text, WebFetch]. The track was 170 cm long and 6.2 cm wide, and rats were trained on it before implantation. Field size was the stretch of track with firing above an absolute 2 Hz, not a fraction of peak. The 52 cm is a mean with no SD or SEM. The paper does not say which fields enter the mean. It sits right after the 1,147 fields that persisted on the shortened track, so it is most likely those. Relabel the row "dorsal CA1 and CA3 pooled".
2. Mizuseki et al. 2012, 75 cm CA1 and 50 cm CA3 on a 2.5 m track. Confirmed. The text calls them means, but the parallel open-field sentence calls its value a median and the tests are rank-sum tests, so the statistic is uncertain. The field edge is at 10% of the cell's peak rate. That is lower than the 20% rule used by most others, so these fields come out wider. Maps were made per running direction. The track was 250 x 7 cm. The paper does not say whether the track was familiar.
3. Rich et al. 2014, 65 ± 43 cm. Confirmed as mean ± SD (supplement, Fig. S14 legend). The track was novel. Fields were measured only on newly added sections after 3 to 5 laps. The edge rule was an absolute 2 Hz. Fields from the two directions were merged when they overlapped by at least 50%.
4. Kjelstrup et al. 2008, 0.98 ± 0.03, 3.73 ± 0.43 and 5.52 ± 0.54 m. Confirmed as mean ± SEM, all CA3. Details the draft lacked:
   - Levels are fractions of the CA3 long axis: dorsal 7 to 22%, intermediate 40 to 70%, ventral 70 to 100%. The dorsal group does not include the first 7% of the axis.
   - N is 111 cells in 5 rats, 37 cells in 3 rats and 61 cells in 4 rats. That is 12 rats, out of 21 rats recorded overall.
   - Field rule: "spatially stable contiguous regions with firing above 20% of the peak rate".
   - Fig. 4 describes the per-cell measure as "the average width of the largest place field of individual cells".
   - The paper gives no CA1 numbers. Every value is CA3.
   - The "~10 meters" in the abstract is the scale at the ventral pole. It is not the ventral mean, and "Few fields were longer than 10 m".
5. Kjelstrup et al. 2002 has no place-field data. It is a lesion study, "Reduced fear expression after lesions of the ventral hippocampus", PNAS 99:10825-10830, PMID 12149439. Kjelstrup 2008 cites it (ref. 21) only for lesion effects on defensive behaviour.
6. Jung et al. 1994 did not record at the ventral pole in the sense used by Kjelstrup. Kjelstrup 2008, note 29, says "Previous studies have demonstrated place fields in intermediate parts of the hippocampus, at approximately 30 to 45% of the dorsal-to-ventral axis (13, 14)", where refs. 13 and 14 are Jung 1994 and Maurer 2005 [full text, Kjelstrup 2008 p. 143]. So the "ventral" sites of Jung 1994 and the "middle" sites of Maurer 2005 belong in an intermediate row, not a ventral one.

### Environment size

Fields grow with the environment, more slowly than the environment does. This matters for the ratio table because the intermediate and ventral numbers all come from an 18 m track.

- Shortening a familiar track from 170 to 100 cm, a 41% cut, shrank mean field size from 52 to 42 cm, a 19% cut (rows 1, 2).
- Going from a 76 cm cylinder to a 1.8 x 1.4 m box, 5.6 times the floor area, raised the largest CA1 field per cell from about 1,019 to 2,626 cm², or 36 to 58 cm in diameter (rows 15, 16). Pooled over all fields, Park et al. found no change: "field size was not different in the cylinder and box (F1,1316 = 0.01 p = 0.93)" [full text].
- Dorsal CA3 is 34 to 50 cm in 2 m mazes (rows 4, 8, 10) and 98 cm on the 18 m track (row 19). Part of that gap is the 7 to 22% level, part is the track, and part is the field rule. The data cannot separate them.
- Dorsal CA1 on a novel 48 m track is 65 cm (row 11), not far above the 2.5 m track's 75 cm under a looser edge rule (row 3).

One way to place intermediate and ventral levels in a standard environment is to scale a dorsal standard value by Kjelstrup's within-study ratios. Those ratios are 3.73/0.98 = 3.8 (intermediate) and 5.52/0.98 = 5.6 (ventral) for per-cell means, and 3.71/0.81 = 4.6 and 6.66/0.81 = 8.2 for population half-widths [derived]. This assumes the gradient does not depend on environment size. Nothing I read tests that assumption. The standard-environment evidence points to smaller ratios: about 1.65 for intermediate CA1 on a 1.3 m track (row 14), 1.39 for ventral CA3 on radial arms (row 25), and about 2.1 in coverage for ventral CA3 in a 2 m² box (rows 26, 27). Those three are all [figure-read], [secondary] or ratio-only. [Inference]

| Kjelstrup 2008 | 20% of peak, "spatially stable" | Not in main text | Not in main text |
| Royer 2010 | 20% of peak, all pixels (coverage) | None | 200 x 200 pixel map; Gaussian half-width 5 pixels |
| Komorowski 2013 | 20% of peak | 50 bins of 2 cm (200 cm²) | 2 cm; 5 cm Gaussian weighting |

The 10% edge of Mizuseki and the above-zero edge of Park give wider fields than a 20% edge. Rich's and Diba's absolute 2 Hz edge widens fields of high-rate cells and narrows those of low-rate cells. Wirtshafter's mean + 1 SD edge is the strictest here. [Inference]
